# Zi'Dara: notes for Claude

This repo is the master copy of the rules for **Zi'Dara**, Mo's tabletop roleplaying game (Mo runs Dice Goblin in Auckland and playtests three times a week). The website (zidara.world), the printed book, the reference sheets and the character tools are all built from `content/`. The repo is private.

## Where things are

- `content/rules/*.md`: the rulebook, one chapter per file. Front matter gives `title`, `id`, `order` (book order, so `03a` is 3.2 and `03b` is 3.5), `part`, `partNumber`, `source` and `updated`. `_parts.yaml` describes the five parts.
- `content/changes.md`: the public change log, newest first, one entry per rules change, dated by when it went to the table.
- `content/learn.md`: the Learn deck (`/learn/`), the core rules as 40 cards in book order, one idea to a card, each saying which section it teaches. `scripts/learn.mjs` reads it for the build and the tests.
- `content/figures/`: SVG figures the chapters use.
- `notes/to-confirm.md`: calls Claude made to fill gaps in Mo's rules, waiting for Mo's yes or no.
- `notes/website-brief.md`: what Mo wants from the website (look, companions, live play, worlds, order). Read it before any site work.
- `notes/companion-plan.md`: the agreed first version of the character companion, and what comes after it.
- `notes/worlds-plan.md`: Mo's answers for worlds and maps (27 Oct) and its stages, the faction board first, then the faction turn, places and people, and maps and her pins.
- `notes/builder-plan.md`: the world builder (28 Oct): Mo's calls, the brief, how its tables are written, and its stages, each built with the one prompt at the end of the plan.
- `notes/sheets-plan.md`: the agreed plan for the fillable two-page character sheet and the separate player reference.
- `notes/design-review.md`: Claude's look at the live site on 24 Oct (why it feels bland, and the approach). `notes/design-plan.md`: the design pass that came from it, step by step, with where each step's got to.
- `notes/design-26oct.md`: Mo's 26 Oct asks and picks for the site (real die shapes in the Struck look, the character and the builder on a phone, the Learn deck and look-up pages, the paper sheets), and the plan for each piece.
- `notes/reading-28oct.md`: Mo's 28 Oct pick for reading the book on a phone (chapters open on their contents, a section at a time with a swipe, and places, threats and sheets drawn as the game's pieces), and what was built.
- `notes/playtest-sheets/`: Mo's "What's changed" playtest sheets (text), the primary source for each change.
- `tools/check_terms.py`: flags retired rules words (`--links` checks every `](#id)` link against the chapters' ids).
- `tools/voice_check.py`: flags em dashes, semicolons, exclamation marks, stiff phrases and filler, and counts contractions.
- `tools/build_book.py` and `tools/book_template.html`: build a one-page reading copy of the whole book (`python3 tools/build_book.py OUT.html`). Rebuild it after every rules change.
- `tools/import_book.py`: the one-off import of the 2 Oct HTML book. Don't run it again.
- `content/world-tables/`: the world builder's tables (`notes/builder-plan.md`): `core.yaml` (everything that isn't genre, Building a Faction's and Building Your World's own rows among it), `regions.yaml` (the five regions), `genres.yaml` (the eras, twists, named genres and frames' mixes) and a pack for each era and twist (`eras/medieval.yaml`, `eras/renaissance.yaml` and `twists/magic.yaml` so far), read and checked against the book by `scripts/world-tables.mjs` into `public/world-tables.json`. Write them with plain apostrophes, since the build makes them curly. Mo reads and judges them on `/deal/`. `npm run deal` (`scripts/deal-world.mjs`) deals boards as text for a recipe and a seed (`--deck` plays a deck at random), and `tests/world-pack/` is a small made-up pack for the engine's own checks, never shipped.
- `scripts/build-sheets.mjs` (`npm run sheets`): builds the two-page fillable character sheet and the four-page player reference into `public/sheets/`, from `scripts/sheets/` and Appendix D, drawn with the kit in its Paper theme (Piece 4 of `notes/design-26oct.md`), and writes `src/data/sheets.json` for the Sheets page (`/sheets/`). Workers Builds can't run Chromium, so the PDFs are committed. Rebuild them after any change to Appendix D, `scripts/sheets/`, the kit (`src/kit/`, `src/styles/kit.css`) or `src/companion/dice.js`, since `tests/check-site.mjs` fails when a sheet's behind any of them. If Playwright can't download Chromium, set `PW_CHROMIUM=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1243/chrome-linux64/chrome`.

## The rules

- **The chapters are the truth.** When Mo sends a new sheet, newest wins: his latest wording replaces anything older, and everything he didn't change stays.
- When a sheet leaves a gap the book has to fill, make the smallest call in the shape of the existing rules, write it in, and add it to `notes/to-confirm.md`. When Mo marks something as still open, don't invent an answer: say only what's settled.
- The six crew (Vesk, Bram, Dorrie, Isolde, Wren, Ash) and their sheets are in `03b-title-ideas-and-the-crew.md` (`#s-pregens`). Every example uses their real numbers, archetypes and threads, and every number has to add up. The GM is "she".
- The character companion reads those six sheets straight off the chapter, so keep each sheet's labels and shapes (`**Set actions:** Sneak 3 (d6): her first Title, *move unseen* · …`). A line on a set action is a Title (`her first Title`), a permission in italics (an attuned thing's too, by its permission), or something they carry (`her picks`), and never a trait (since 27 Oct a trait's never a step). The build stops if it can't read a line, if a trait's on a set action, if a plain thing's on more than one or a permission on more than two, if a trait's type isn't the list's, or if a set action's steps or die don't add up.

## Changing a rule

1. Change every chapter where the rule lives or is summarised: the main chapter, then The Core or Building if it's touched, the quick reference (Appendix D), every lever (Appendix C), the glossary and the index, and the Learn deck (`content/learn.md`). Once the change log entry's written (step 2), the build names every card whose section it touches. Check each one against the chapter, keep it to 40 to 120 words with the crew's real dice, then set `checked` at the top of the deck to the entry's date. The deck teaches the chapters and never changes a rule.
2. Add an entry to the top of `content/changes.md`: the date, what changed in plain words, what it replaced, and links to the sections.
3. If you made a call, add it to `notes/to-confirm.md`.
4. Run `python3 -I tools/check_terms.py`, `python3 -I tools/check_terms.py --links` and `python3 -I tools/voice_check.py FILE --lines` on what you changed. Add newly retired words to `RETIRED` in `check_terms.py`.
5. If Appendix D changed, rebuild the sheets (`npm run sheets`). Then rebuild and check the site (`npm run build`, then `node tests/check-site.mjs`, which fails when a sheet's behind its sources).
6. Work on a branch and commit. `main` is what the live site deploys from, so only merge to `main` when Mo says so.

## Keeping usage low

Every chat comes out of Mo's usage, so get the most done for the least.
- One stage or piece of work per chat. Finish by giving Mo the prompt for the next one, since every step in a long chat costs more than the same step in a fresh one.
- Grep, then read only the lines you need. Don't read long files whole (`app.js`, `builder.js`, a chapter) or re-read what you've just edited.
- While you work, run only the part of the tests that covers your change (`ZI_ONLY=5,5b node tests/check-companion.mjs` runs just those parts of it). Run the full `tests/check-site.mjs`, `tests/check-companion.mjs`, `tests/check-accounts.mjs` and `tests/check-world-engine.mjs` once before you finish, since each full run is long.
- Screenshots at 1x and the size of the screen, and only when you need to judge how something looks.
- The independent check is one agent on Sonnet that reviews the diff and reports in a few hundred words, since an open-ended review can cost as much as the build.
- Don't narrate between steps, and ask Mo only about the decisions that are his.

## The voice

Write as Mo, teaching at the table with dice in hand. Use the `zi-voice` skill when it's available.
- Contractions always, plain sentences of mixed length, and a quick example with real names and real dice after a rule.
- NZ spelling (armour, colour, favour, defence, centre).
- Never em dashes, semicolons or exclamation marks in prose. No slogans, "not X, it's Y", rhythm triplets, punchline closers, mood-setting openers or filler words.
- No "Mo's note" boxes or designer notes. The chapters say how the game works, never what changed (that's the change log's job).
- Don't name other games, books, films or shows.
- Digits for dice and steps (a d8, 3 steps, 1 Aura, 1 Resolve).

## The Markdown

- Headings carry ids: `## Heading {#s-some-id}`. Keep every id that survives, because links point at them. Archetypes are `#a-name`, Title ideas `#k-name`.
- Boxes are directives: `:::example[Title]`, `:::rule`, `:::note`, `:::quote`, `:::sheet[Name]{#id}`, `:::threat[Name]`, `:::place`, each closed with `:::`.
- Pipe tables only, with ` · ` between items in a cell. Links are `[text](#id)`.
- Permissions, tags, Catches, conditions and quoted threads in *italics*.

- Telegraphs on phones (stage 8): the live table. `src/worker/table.js` is a Durable Object class, `Table`, one object for each campaign by name (`TABLE.getByName("table:<campaign>")`), holding its rounds of telegraphs in its own SQLite, so a round outlasts a dropped connection, a redeploy and the object sleeping. `/api/campaigns/<id>/table` with `Upgrade: websocket` hands the phone's socket to it (from the site's own pages only), and it pushes every change down that socket. Changes go up as `POST` (`{ dev, ops }`, numbered per phone so one sent twice counts once), and the Worker checks who's playing whom against D1 every time. The socket only carries the table down, plus the phone's `ping`, which the object answers without waking. `src/companion/table-rules.js` is the rules both sides apply (a round, a telegraph and how it went: stopped, landed then and there, landed at the end, held or through, one action a round, intercepts and the scene). `src/companion/table.js` draws it: the GM's round at the top of her campaign page (built as kit cards on her phone, sent, one more sent mid-round, one taken back, the round ended, That fight's over), and each player's on their Play tab, answered with the roll card taking the telegraph's die (`openAction(i, tele)`) or rolled with When it lands once it's landed (`openDefence(pre)`), the rest of the round face up underneath. An answer or a When it lands is written into the character with the change that rolled it (`state.tele`, `noteTable()` in `app.js`), so Undo takes it back from the GM too, and it goes up when there's a signal (`zi-table`). One action a round: an answer, putting it into whoever answers a friend's telegraph (a combo, whose steps go into that answer and who moves with its roll), a roll at a stake or an event, the ground or something else, kept in the character by round (`state.tele.x`) and sent as one `acts` record, so Undo takes it back too.
