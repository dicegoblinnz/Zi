::::card[Did you succeed?]{#l-succeed teaches=the-core}
You play someone who's properly good at something, like a thief who can open any lock in the city. Everything your character can do is a **permission**, a short line on your sheet. If it's on there you can do it, no questions asked, and every permission that fits what you're doing makes your die bigger. Then the question's simple: did you succeed? Roll against whatever's in your way, and if you beat the number, you did.

:::example
Vesk's got *pick a mechanical lock* on her sheet, so nobody asks whether she can open the strongbox. Her Locks is a d10 and the strongbox is a d6. She rolls 7 against its 4, and it's open.
:::
::::

::::card[Titles and permissions]{#l-permissions teaches=s-permissions}
You start with two **Titles**. A Title's who you are, real and specific, like *the best cat burglar in the city*. Your permissions go on your Titles, three on each to start and six at most. A permission's something you can do or something you're good at. It can be one word, like *Quick*, or a doing word with how, what or where, like *fight with a sword*.

:::example
Bram's first Title is *The wall that held the harbour gate*, and on it he's got *hold the line*, *guard one person* and *lift what two men can't*.
:::
::::

::::card[Every line's a step]{#l-lines teaches=how-permissions-stack}
Your Title's a step on anything it fits, and so is a plain item: a sword's a step in a sword fight. Some traits are a step too. Each of these is a **line** on your sheet, the same as a permission. Every line that fits what you're doing is a **step**, and it counts once a roll. The same permission can't sit on one set action twice, so to stack, use different words, like *Deceptive* and *Sly*.

:::example
Vesk's going over a roof without a sound. Her first Title fits, so does *move unseen*, and so does Silent Step, her trait that's +1 to moving unseen. That's 3 lines, so with the action it's 4 steps.
:::
::::

::::card[Set actions]{#l-set-actions teaches=s-set-actions}
Your sheet does the counting before you play, and that's what **set actions** are for. Defend, Fight, Move, Sneak, Study and Talk are on every sheet, and you add your own for the things you do all the time. A set action's steps are 1 for the action and 1 for every line on it. Titles and items go on every set action they fit, but a permission goes on two at most, and Defend counts as one.

:::example
Vesk's Locks has her first Title, *pick a mechanical lock*, *know how a lock's built* and her picks on it. That's four lines and the action, so it's 5 steps, a d10.
:::
::::

::::card[Building a roll]{#l-building teaches=s-building-die}
Every roll goes the same way. Say what you're doing and how, and pick the set action that fits. Start from its steps, and leave out any line that doesn't fit this time. Anything else that fits costs 1 [Aura](#s-aura) each to bring in, and each one's a step. Then turn your steps into dice and roll.

:::example
Vesk's going over Lord Vane's roof with her Sneak, 4 steps, a d8. Her grappling line fits too, but it's on her Move, so 1 Aura brings it in: 5 steps, a d10. Bram swings at a smuggler running for the door, but he isn't holding any line, so he leaves *hold the line* out: 3 steps, a d6.
:::
::::

::::card[Steps into dice]{#l-dice teaches=steps-into-dice}
Your die is your steps × 2, up to a d12, so 1 step's a d2, 4 steps a d8 and 6 steps a d12. Every 6 steps gives you another d12, and whatever's left over makes one more die. No die you build is ever bigger than a d12, so getting stronger means throwing more dice. With nothing on it at all, the action on its own is 1 step, a d2.

:::example
Wren's Fight is 5 steps, a d10. When Bram [barks an order](#leading-and-inspiring) and hands her 2 more steps, it's 7, so she throws a d12 and a d2.
:::
::::

::::card[Beat the number]{#l-roll teaches=s-the-roll}
Most of the time you're rolling against something that pushes back, like a guard, a lock or a storm. Roll your dice and theirs too, in another colour, and compare. **Beat it** and you did it. A **tie** goes to whoever's defending, and if you're the one acting, 1 [Aura](#s-aura) wins it. **Roll under** and you didn't. Whenever one of your dice rolls its top face, its highest number, you get 1 Aura back.

:::example
Vesk's Locks rolls 6 against the study door's d6, and the door rolls 6 as well. She's the one acting, so she pays 1 Aura to win the tie, and she's in.
:::
::::

::::card[When a miss matters]{#l-miss teaches=s-miss}
A miss is just a miss, unless something's pushing back or time's running out. Then it costs you something, a [consequence](#s-consequences), and the GM names it: your body, your gear, your Confidence, your dreams, the crew's Goal or the clock. If you'd rather pay something else that fits and costs about as much, offer it.

:::example
Picking a lock with all night and nobody around, Vesk's d10 can come up short and it costs her nothing. With the guard coming up the stairs, it's a different story.
:::
::::

::::card[Every die gets an intent]{#l-intents teaches=s-intents}
Before you roll, give every die an **intent**: what it's trying to do, and who or what it's aimed at. Whatever you gave the die, that's what happens if it wins. A target rolls its defence once a round, and each of your dice has to beat that one roll. Put more than one die on the same intent and that's **advantage**: your best die counts.

:::example
Bram and Wren throw three d6s at the guard captain: one to disarm him, one to floor him and one to make him give up. His d6 rolls 4. Theirs come up 5, 2 and 4, so he's disarmed, he keeps his feet, and the tie's his, so he won't give up.
:::
::::

::::card[Splitting]{#l-splitting teaches=s-splitting}
You can **split** your steps into more, smaller dice. Every action in the roll gives you one split, so one action can turn a d8 into two d4s, or a d2 and a d6. Dice past 6 steps are already separate, so they don't use up your splits. Your dice all have to go at things that belong to the same job.

:::example
Vesk's at the study door with her Locks, 5 steps, and she splits once: a d6 for the lock and a d4 to keep it quiet. The lock rolls 3 and her d6 comes up 5, so it opens. Her d4 lands on 4 against the guard's 3, and he never looks up.
:::
::::

::::card[Going beyond]{#l-beyond teaches=s-going-beyond}
Every permission does what it says for free, magic or not, the same as a sword. **Going beyond** what it normally does costs 1 [Resolve](#s-resolve). It doesn't add a step: you spend the Resolve and the permission does the bigger thing. If you have to make a case for why it's normal, it's going beyond, so spend the Resolve and keep playing.

:::example
Isolde speaks with the recently dead for free. Calling up the mason who built the Vane vault, a hundred years dead, is going beyond, so that's 1 Resolve, and she's down to 1.
:::
::::

::::card[Aura]{#l-aura teaches=s-aura}
**Aura** is your character's luck and flair, and it pays for the small stuff. You start every scene full, and your maximum's 5 unless something on your sheet changes it. Spend 1 Aura to bring in something that isn't on your set action, to win a tie when you're the one acting, to [intercept](#s-intercept) for a friend, or to use [your archetype](#s-how-archetypes). Already full? Give it to someone else in the scene.

:::example
Bram's holding the corridor with his Fight, 4 steps, a d8. He pays 1 Aura to bring the chandelier down on the Warden, and it's a step, so he rolls a d10.
:::
::::

::::card[Resolve]{#l-resolve teaches=s-resolve}
**Resolve** is your character pushing themselves, so it pays for the big stuff. You've got as much as you've got Titles, so 2 to start. Spend 1 to take an extra action, to roll again after a miss, to go beyond what a permission does, or on your archetype's Resolve spend. It also holds your ground against [fear](#s-fear-gets-through) and lets you act when you're [shaken](#s-confidence). You get 1 back at the end of every scene, and all of it after downtime.

:::example
Ash spends 1 Resolve and puts an extra action into her Fight. The same set action twice adds 1, so it's 5 steps instead of 4, and her d8 becomes a d10.
:::
::::

::::card[Catches and trouble]{#l-catches teaches=s-trouble}
A **Catch** is trouble written on your sheet, from an [attuned thing](#s-attunement) or a [Scar](#s-blaze). Letting it bite is up to you: say how it causes trouble, the GM runs with it, and you take 1 Aura. A big bite, one that changes the scene or costs you something you wanted, gets you 1 Resolve instead. A [hostile thread](#s-bonds) bites the same way, and with no Catch you can still bring real trouble on yourself for 1 Aura.

:::example
Vesk owes the fence. Halfway across a warehouse roof, her player lets that thread bite: the fence's boy is under the drainpipe, and he wants paying tonight. Vesk takes 1 Aura.
:::
::::

::::card[Working together]{#l-combo teaches=s-teamwork}
There's no special action for helping. Put your action into the same thing a friend's doing, and that's a **combo**: add your set actions together and roll once, between you. Nobody pays Aura for it, but it has to be one thing in the story, and you've got to be able to join in from where you are. Every action in the roll gives you a split.

:::example
Vesk's at the study door with her Locks, 5 steps, and Dorrie keeps the guard talking with her Talk, 4. That's 9 steps from two actions, and they roll a d8 for the lock, a d6 to keep it quiet and a d4 to keep the guard talking.
:::
::::

::::card[Studying something]{#l-study teaches=s-study}
**Study** is an intent like any other. Build it from whatever fits how you're looking, usually your Study set action, and roll against the target's Challenge. Win, and pick one. Ask something specific, like *what's holding it together?*, and the GM reveals the [hidden tag](#s-hidden) that answers it, if there is one, which is how you find weak points. Or ask one question about it, and she answers it truthfully.

:::example
Isolde's Study is 4 steps, a d8, with *know the history of anything old* on it. She studies the old construct and rolls 6 against its 5, so she asks what's holding it together and gets the hidden tag that answers it.
:::
::::

::::card[Archetypes]{#l-archetypes teaches=s-title}
Every Title is an **archetype** too, like Guardian or Shadow, and each archetype has a mechanic only it can do. A [Guardian](#a-guardian) intercepts for free for every friend within his stride. On most archetypes, 1 Aura puts +2 on one die of the mechanic, once you've rolled and know you need it, and the same archetype twice makes it +3. A die the +2 takes to its top face, or past it, doesn't give you Aura back.

:::example
Vesk's a Shadow twice over, so 1 Aura gets her +3 to hide. Her Sneak's d8 comes up 5 against the cellar guard's 7, and the +3 makes it 8, so he walks right past.
:::
::::

::::card[What you carry]{#l-carry teaches=s-carrying}
You carry 3 **equipment** (what you wear or wield), 3 **utility** items (tools and things you use up) and a **keepsake**, and your **bag** holds 10 more. [Attuned things](#s-attunement) are on top of all that. Nothing in your bag can be used till you've pulled it out, which takes your action, but putting something away is free. Plain items are just what they are, so a sword's a step when it fits.

:::example
A few sessions in, Wren's utility is full when the trail runs into a cave. She puts her snare kit away in her pack for free, and spends her action pulling out the torch bundle.
:::
::::

::::card[Attuned things]{#l-attuned teaches=s-attunement}
When you **attune** something, it gets permissions of its own and grows with you. You start with one, at Awakened, with one permission, and you can give it its [Catch](#s-trouble) now for a second. It counts through its permissions, and each one goes on two set actions at most, like any other. Attuned things don't take up room in what you carry, and they can't be broken.

:::example
Vesk's grappling line is Awakened with *climb or swing anywhere you can throw a hook*, so it's 1 step on her Move. With her first Title and *run the rooftops*, that's 4 steps, a d8.
:::
::::

::::card[Bonds and threads]{#l-threads teaches=s-bonds}
A **bond** is usually someone who matters to your character, and each **thread** is one truth between you. A bond thread's something good, like a rescue or a shared past. A hostile thread's something gone bad, like a debt or a grudge. Any thread can be strained, which means it's nearly snapped. You start with two bond threads with the crew, and writing a hostile one gives you room for a third bond thread.

:::example
Vesk's bond thread with Bram is *He once carried me out of a fire. I have never said thank you.* Her thread with the fence, *I owe a debt I would rather not discuss*, is hostile.
:::
::::

::::card[Leaning on a bond]{#l-lean teaches=s-bonds}
A bond thread's something you can lean on. Pay 1 Aura to borrow one of their permissions for your roll. Or lean on them in the story, like calling in a favour, and take **1 Resolve**. It's free, and the thread's strained until you make it right with them. Strain it again before it's mended and it snaps. You can't borrow through a strained thread or lean on a hostile one, but a hostile thread bites like a Catch.

:::example
Vesk's on Vane's roof with no Resolve left, so she whistles for Dorrie to pull the guards back down. She takes 1 Resolve, and their thread's strained on both sheets till she gives Dorrie the pick of the haul.
:::
::::

::::card[Your Aspiration]{#l-aspiration teaches=s-aspiration}
Your **Aspiration** is one thing you want. It starts at d2 and climbs as you chase it: when something you do really moves it along, roll against its die, and beat it to climb one. A setback steps it back down, never below d2. When it climbs past d12, it resolves: you take a new archetype, name it with a Title, and write a new Aspiration at d2.

:::example
Vesk's *Own the score of a lifetime* is on d2 when she cracks a strongbox in the Low Market. Her Locks, a d10, rolls 7 against its 1, so it climbs to d4.
:::
::::

::::card[Confidence]{#l-confidence teaches=s-confidence}
**Confidence** is your nerve, and your defence against fear, lies and pressure. It starts every scene on d6 and moves a size at a time. It goes up for every telegraph your defence stops and every action where one of your dice wins. It goes down every time your [wound die](#s-blaze) steps up and for every action where all your dice miss. When your armour or something you carry takes a blow for you, it doesn't move.

:::example
Vesk starts the fight on d6. Her Fight, a d4, rolls 3 against a guard's 2, so she's on d8. Next round her Locks misses the cell door, 3 against 6, and she's back on d6.
:::
::::

::::card[Shaken and fired up]{#l-shaken teaches=s-confidence}
At d2 you're **shaken**. You can't take an action at what the scene's about unless you spend 1 Resolve, so otherwise your action goes on pulling yourself together, and that brings you back up to d4. At d12 you're **fired up**: cash it in for an extra action, or to step your wound die down a size, and it drops back to d6.

:::example
The brute's roar takes Bram down to d2, so he's shaken. He spends 1 Resolve to swing at the brute anyway. His Fight's d8 rolls 7 against the brute's 5, so the hammer lands and he's back up to d4.
:::
::::

::::card[Armour]{#l-armour teaches=s-armour}
Armour hasn't got a die of its own. Each piece of plain armour you wear is a line on your Defend, and so is a shield. When a blow gets through, your armour can take it instead of you: *not the Hurt, my shield takes it.* The piece is crossed out till it's mended, and your Confidence doesn't move.

:::example
Bram's Defend is his first Title, his plate and his shield, so with the action it's 4 steps, a d8. A spear gets through and he offers his plate instead of the Hurt, so his Defend's 3 steps, a d6, till it's mended.
:::
::::

::::card[The round]{#l-round teaches=s-round}
When things get dangerous and timing matters, play moves into **rounds**. First the GM telegraphs everything that's coming this round, face up. Then you act, in any order you like, one action each. There's no initiative, so talk it through and make a plan. At the end of the round, every telegraph nobody answered lands, and every condition steps down a size.

:::example
The GM puts a Warden's spear at Isolde on the table, a d10, and the maid on the stair slipping off to wake the house, a d4. Wren and Bram take on the spear, and Vesk goes after the maid.
:::
::::

::::card[Telegraphs]{#l-telegraphs teaches=s-telegraphs}
A **telegraph** is the GM saying out loud what's about to happen, before anything's rolled. It always tells you what it's doing, who or what it's aimed at, and how hard, in dice. Telegraphs are honest, so the GM never hides a size or a target. A threat aims one die at each of you, unless a tag says it can do more. Running doesn't dodge a telegraph aimed at you. To stop it, [answer it](#s-answering).

:::example
"The brute's going to bring his club down on Isolde. That's a d8." Isolde's Defend is a d2, so Wren and Bram talk about answering it before anyone rolls.
:::
::::

::::card[Your action]{#l-action teaches=s-actions}
Every round you get **one action**. You can act with a roll from a set action, answer a telegraph, [Defend](#s-defend), move further than your stride, lead if you're a Leader, pull something out of your bag, break a [condition](#s-conditions) or take a breath. Your stride's free, and so are a few words and putting something away. To help a friend, put your action into what they're doing.

:::example
Bram crosses the hall on his stride, which is free, and spends his action going at the guard on the door with his Fight, a d8.
:::
::::

::::card[Answering a telegraph]{#l-answer teaches=s-answering}
To **answer** a telegraph, spend your action and build a roll from the set action that fits how you're stopping it, then roll against its dice. Win and it's stopped. Lose and it lands at once, with no defence roll, and a tie's a loss unless you pay 1 Aura. You can answer for someone else, and friends can put their actions in.

:::example
The brute's club is coming at Isolde, a d8. Wren puts an arrow in his arm and Bram gets his hammer under the club, 9 steps between them, a d12 and a d6. The club rolls 6 and the d12 comes up 8, so it never lands.
:::
::::

::::card[When it lands]{#l-lands teaches=s-defending}
Whatever nobody answers lands at the end of the round, and it's down to your defence, rolled once: your Defend against blows, and your Confidence against fear, lies and pressure. Each die you roll stops one telegraph it beats or ties, because ties go to you. Everything else gets through, and it costs you something.

:::example
Two guards telegraph at Vesk, a d8 and a d6. Her Defend's d4 rolls 3. The d8 rolls 6 and the d6 rolls 3, so her 3 stops the d6, and the d8 gets through.
:::
::::

::::card[Defend]{#l-defend teaches=s-defend}
**Defend** takes your action. When the telegraphs land, every line on your Defend counts twice, and you can split it once, so you've got more dice to stop more blows. Your Confidence rolls as it is. With nothing on your Defend it's a d2 either way, so answer the blow instead.

:::example
Bram Defends, so the three lines on his Defend count twice, and with the action that's 7 steps, a d12 and a d2. He splits the d12 into two d6s, so he's got three dice for three blows.
:::
::::

::::card[Intercept]{#l-intercept teaches=s-intercept}
When a telegraph's aimed at a friend within your stride, you can **intercept** it for 1 Aura and no action, any time before the telegraphs land. Take as many of the dice aimed at them as you like, and they come at you instead. It's 1 Aura for each friend, and a Guardian does it for free. Fear, lies and pressure are inside their head, so nobody can step in front of those.

:::example
The brute's club is coming down at Isolde, and her Defend's a d2. Vesk's right beside her, so she pays 1 Aura and takes it. Her Defend's d4 rolls 4 against the club's 3, and it's stopped.
:::
::::

::::card[Consequences]{#l-cost teaches=s-consequences}
When a blow gets through, the GM names what it costs you, picking one that fits. It might be a **Hurt**, which puts your wound die up a size and your Confidence down one. It might be a broken item, your [Aspiration](#s-aspiration) or the [Party Goal](#s-goal) stepping back down, or a [clock](#s-stakes). You can offer another that fits and costs about as much.

:::example
A guard's blade gets through, and the GM names a Hurt for Vesk. Her Aspiration's on d4, so she offers that instead, and it steps back down to d2.
:::
::::

::::card[When fear gets through]{#l-fear teaches=s-fear-gets-through}
When fear, lies or pressure get through your Confidence, the GM doesn't name the cost. Each one that gets through drops your Confidence a size, or you spend 1 Resolve and hold your ground, and it's your call which. Nothing takes your Confidence below d2, so on d2 it's a Hurt instead, or 1 Resolve.

:::example
Dorrie's on d10 when the duke's sister cuts her down in front of the room, a d10 at her Confidence. Dorrie rolls 2 against its 6, so it gets through. She spends 1 Resolve to hold her ground and stays on d10.
:::
::::

::::card[Threats and tags]{#l-threats teaches=s-challenges}
A threat comes down to two things: a **Challenge** and a handful of **tags**. The Challenge is its defence, a die twice its number, so Challenge 3 is a d6, and it never steps down. Tags are what's true about it, like *Armoured* or *Loyal*. The ones that keep it going are its health: every win strips one, and strip the last and it falls.

:::example
A vault door's Challenge 4, and *Oak*, *Iron Bars* and *Warded* keep it shut. That's three wins against its d8 to break it.
:::
::::

::::card[Conditions]{#l-conditions teaches=s-conditions}
A **condition**'s a tag carrying a die. It can be against you, like *Burning d8*, or for you, like *Warded d8*, and its die is the die that placed it. While it lasts it's a step against you or for you, and you can spend it for its one big effect. At the end of every round it steps down a size, and past d2 it's gone.

:::example
Ash's Fight, a d8, beats a Warden with the intent to set it alight, so it's *Burning d8*. That lasts four rounds, unless someone spends it or puts it out first.
:::
::::

::::card[Your wound die]{#l-wounds teaches=s-blaze}
Your **wound die** is how hurt you are. It starts at d2, and every Hurt puts it up a size and your Confidence down a size with it. Take a Hurt at d12 and you Blaze. At the end of every scene you rest: roll the Confidence you finished on against your wound die, and beat it to bring your wound down a size. Downtime clears it.

:::example
Vesk walks out of the job on d8 Confidence with a d6 wound. Her d8 rolls 5 against its 4, so she starts the next scene on a d4 wound.
:::
::::

::::card[Blaze and Scars]{#l-blaze teaches=s-blaze}
When you **Blaze**, declare something extraordinary and it happens, no roll. You can choose for your character to die. If you don't, they leave the scene, and the wound becomes a **Scar**: a Catch, like *a spear through the shoulder*, that bites like any other. Each Scar starts your wound die a size bigger, and nothing heals you below it.

:::example
Bram's on a d12 wound when an axe gets through, so he Blazes. He declares he holds the gate alone till the crew's clear, and he does. He doesn't die, so he leaves the scene with a Scar, and his wound die never goes below d4 now.
:::
::::

::::card[Healing]{#l-healing teaches=s-healing}
You don't have to wait for downtime, but only a [Healer](#a-healer) makes a **heal roll**: an action rolled against your patient's wound die, where each die that beats it steps it down a size. Anyone can do a **quick heal**: pay 1 Aura and spend your action to step one person's wound die down a size, no roll. Nothing heals anyone below their Scars.

:::example
The crew's got no Healer, so Dorrie spends 1 Aura and her action to bring Bram's wound die down from a d6 to a d4.
:::
::::

::::card[Downtime]{#l-downtime teaches=your-slots}
After every session there's a downtime, where the world moves and you heal and grow. Every player gets two personal slots, for things like a new permission, mending armour or chasing your Aspiration. The crew gets one action between you, and a slot you put into it makes everything count twice. When downtime's over, all your Resolve comes back, and your wound die goes back to d2, or to your Scars.

:::example
Bram spends one slot at the smithy to mend his plate. His other slot puts *fight two at once* on his first Title.
:::
::::
