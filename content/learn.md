::::card[Did you succeed?]{#l-succeed teaches=the-core}
You play someone who's properly good at something, like a thief who can open any lock in the city. Every roll's one of your three **Roots**, Body, Mind or Soul, rolled whole. The better you are, the bigger the Root, and the bigger your dice. Then the question's simple: did you succeed? Roll against whatever's in your way, and if you beat the number, you did.

:::example
Vesk's at the strongbox with her Body, 11 steps: a d12 and a d10. Its d6 rolls 4, and her 9 opens it.
:::
::::

::::card[Roots]{#l-roots teaches=s-roots}
You've got three **Roots**, and every roll is one of them. **Body**'s what your hands and feet do, like climbing or swinging a hammer. **Mind**'s what goes on in your head, like noticing or lying. **Soul**'s who you are, like giving orders, holding your nerve or magic that pours out of you. Pick the one that fits *how* you're doing it, not what you're after. A Root's number is its dice, and it's [your points](#s-tracks) too.

:::example
Dorrie can get past the gate guard with her Mind, a story he can't pick holes in, 7 steps, or her Soul, a smile and a bit of nerve, 6 steps. It's the same door either way, so she lies.
:::
::::

::::card[Titles and permissions]{#l-permissions teaches=s-permissions}
A **Title**'s who you are, real and specific, like *the best cat burglar in the city*, and it puts 5 on your Roots, spread however you like. Your **permissions** go on your Titles, six at most. A permission's a doing word with how, what or where, like *pick a mechanical lock*, and it puts 1 on the Root that fits how you do it. Nothing else adds to a Root.

:::example
Bram's first Title, *The wall that held the harbour gate*, is all Body, so that's 5 on his Body. On it, *hold the line* is Body too, so that's 1 more.
:::
::::

::::card[How it adds up]{#l-lines teaches=how-permissions-stack}
A roll's **one Root, at full**, and nothing else on your sheet is counted: not your Titles, permissions, archetypes or what you carry. Your permissions say what you can do, and they're what lets you bring another Root in. An awakening that fits is 2 steps. Points you've spent or lost never shrink the dice, so a Root rolls its whole number every time.

:::example
Vesk's Body is 11: 5 from her first Title, 2 from her second and four permissions. Over Lord Vane's roof it's her Body and her grappling line's *Anywhere I can throw a hook*, 13 steps, two d12s and a d2.
:::
::::

::::card[Building a roll]{#l-building teaches=s-building-die}
Every roll goes the same way. Say what you're doing and how, and pick the Root that fits how. If a permission explains how another Root gets in on it, you can **bring it in** for a point of that Root or 3 [Aura](#s-aura), all of it. Add 2 for an awakening that fits. Going beyond what something normally does is 1 Aura. Then turn your steps into dice and roll.

:::example
Bram's chasing a smuggler down the quay, his Body, 9 steps. He bellows at the dockhands to grab the man, which is *bark an order that gets obeyed*, so he pays a point of Soul and brings it in: 13 steps, two d12s and a d2.
:::
::::

::::card[Steps into dice]{#l-dice teaches=steps-into-dice}
Your die is your steps × 2, up to a d12, so 1 step's a d2, 4 steps a d8 and 6 steps a d12. Every 6 steps gives you another d12, and whatever's left over makes one more die. No die you build is ever bigger than a d12, so getting stronger means throwing more dice. Every roll's at least 1 step, a d2, even with a Root that's 0.

:::example
Wren's shot from cover is her Body and *Long shot*, 10 steps, a d12 and a d8. When Bram [barks an order](#leading-and-inspiring) and hands her 4 steps, it's 14, two d12s and a d4.
:::
::::

::::card[Beat the number]{#l-roll teaches=s-the-roll}
Roll your dice and theirs too, in another colour, and compare. **Beat it** and you did it. A **tie** goes to whoever's defending, and if you're the one acting, 1 Aura wins it. **Roll under** and you didn't, or pay a point of Soul to roll again. **Every die of yours that wins gives you 1 Aura, and so does every top face**, win or lose, your defence included.

:::example
Ash throws fire at two guards, 13 steps, two d12s and a d2: a d12 at each, and the d2 at the first. Their d6s roll 4 and 5. Her d12s come up 9 and 12, a top face, and the d2 lands on 2, another, so that's 4 Aura.
:::
::::

::::card[When a miss matters]{#l-miss teaches=s-miss}
A miss is just a miss, unless something's pushing back or time's running out. Then it costs you something, a [consequence](#s-consequences), and the GM names it: a hit on your Body, your Mind or your Soul, your gear, your dreams, the crew's Goal or the clock. If you'd rather pay something else that fits and costs about as much, offer it.

:::example
Picking a lock with all night and nobody around, Vesk's d12 and her d10 can both come up short and it costs her nothing. With the guard coming up the stairs, it's a different story.
:::
::::

::::card[Every die gets an intent]{#l-intents teaches=s-intents}
Before you roll, give every die an **intent**: what it's trying to do, and who or what it's aimed at. Whatever you gave the die, that's what happens if it wins. A target rolls its defence once a round, and each of your dice has to beat that one roll. Put more than one die on the same intent and that's **advantage**: your best die counts.

:::example
Bram and Wren throw three d12s at the guard captain: one to disarm him, one to floor him and one to make him give up. His d10 rolls 6. Theirs come up 9, 4 and 6, so he's disarmed, he keeps his feet, and the tie's his, so he won't give up.
:::
::::

::::card[Splitting]{#l-splitting teaches=s-splitting}
You can **split** your steps into as many dice as you like, any sizes, as long as they add up to your steps and none's bigger than a d12. Every die's an intent, so splitting is how you do more than one thing with your action. Small dice fish for Aura with their top faces, and they lose more often.

:::example
Vesk's at the study door with her Body, 11 steps, a d12 and a d10. She splits them four ways: a d8 for the lock, a d6 to keep it quiet, a d4 to listen for anyone coming and a d4 to leave no scratch.
:::
::::

::::card[Going beyond]{#l-beyond teaches=s-going-beyond}
Every permission does what it says for free, magic or not, the same as a sword. **Going beyond** what something normally does costs 1 [Aura](#s-aura). It doesn't add a step: you spend the Aura and it does the bigger thing. If you have to make a case for why it's normal, it's going beyond, so spend the Aura and keep playing.

:::example
Isolde speaks with the recently dead for free. Calling up the mason who built the Vane vault, a hundred years dead, is going beyond, so that's 1 Aura, and she still rolls her Soul, 7 steps, a d12 and a d2, to get him talking.
:::
::::

::::card[Aura]{#l-aura teaches=s-aura}
**Aura** is your luck and flair. You start every scene full, and your maximum's 5. Spend 3 to bring a Root in, or 1 for an ability, 2 more steps on an awakening, going beyond, winning a tie when you're acting, or an [intercept](#s-intercept). While you're acting it can go past your maximum, and what's over is gone once you're done, so spend it first.

:::example
Ash starts on 5. She puts 2 on *Fire without a word* before she throws, 4 more steps, so she's on 3. Her fire goes at three guards, all three dice win and one lands on its top face, so she's on 7 while she's acting, and back to 5 when she's done.
:::
::::

::::card[Your points]{#l-tracks teaches=s-tracks}
A Root's number is three things at once: its **dice**, how many **hits** it can take, and what you **spend** to push. Every hit that gets through takes a point off the Root it was at, and your dice never shrink as you're hurt. A Root at 0 that's hit again is **wounded**: Bloodied, Frayed or Hollow. At the end of every scene, roll each Root that's down, unsplit, and get back what you roll.

:::example
A spear gets through to Vesk's Body, so she's on 9 of the 10 she can hold, and she still defends with a d12 and a d10. At the end of the scene her Body rolls 7, so she's full again.
:::
::::

::::card[Pushing yourself]{#l-paying teaches=s-tracks}
Your points pay for things too. **A point of a Root** brings it into a roll, and **a point of Soul rolls again** after a miss. When a hit gets through, **2 for 1** takes it on another Root instead, for 2 points of that one, if it's got them. Pushing never hurts you: you can't spend what you haven't got, so spending down to 0 only leaves you exposed.

:::example
A spear gets through to Bram's Body. He takes it without a flinch and pays 2 points of his Soul, so his Soul's on 2 and his Body stays on 7.
:::
::::

::::card[Catches and trouble]{#l-catches teaches=s-trouble}
A **Catch** is trouble written on your sheet, from an [attuned thing](#s-attunement) or a [Scar](#s-blaze). Letting it bite is up to you: say how it causes trouble, the GM runs with it, and you take 1 Aura. A big bite, one that changes the scene or costs you something you wanted, gets you a point of Soul back instead. A [hostile thread](#s-bonds) bites the same way, and with no Catch you can still bring real trouble on yourself for 1 Aura.

:::example
Bram's Soul is down to 2 when his old captain turns up at the inn and names him, and the room turns on the crew. That's a big bite, so his Soul's on 3.
:::
::::

::::card[Working together]{#l-combo teaches=s-teamwork}
There's no special action for helping. Put your action into the same thing a friend's doing, and that's a **combo**, rolled once between you. Every action in it brings the Root that fits how they're helping, all of it. Nobody pays Aura for it, but it has to be one thing in the story, and you've got to be able to join in.

:::example
Vesk's at the study door with her Body, 11 steps, and Dorrie keeps the guard talking with her Mind, 7 more. That's 18 steps between them, three d12s: one for the lock, one to keep it quiet and one for the guard.
:::
::::

::::card[Studying something]{#l-study teaches=s-study}
**Study** is an intent like any other. Roll the Root that fits how you're looking, usually your Mind, against the target's Challenge. Win, and pick one. Ask something specific, like *what's holding it together?*, and the GM reveals the [hidden tag](#s-hidden) that answers it, if there is one. Or ask one question about it, and she answers it truthfully.

:::example
Isolde studies the old construct with her Mind, 9 steps, a d12 and a d6. Its d8 rolls 4 and her d12 comes up 9, so she asks what's holding it together and gets the hidden tag that answers it.
:::
::::

::::card[Archetypes]{#l-archetypes teaches=s-step-archetypes}
An **archetype**, like Guardian or Shadow, is its own pick, apart from your Titles, and it's 5 points. Each one has a **mechanic** only it can do, which is free, a **Trait** that's always true of you, and one **ability** to start, your pick, paid for in [Aura](#s-aura). You can take the same one twice, and its Push gets bigger. It's never counted on a roll.

:::example
Bram's a Guardian and a Leader. As a Guardian he intercepts for free for every friend within his stride, and as a Leader he hands out the steps of his orders, his Soul, 4 steps, a d8.
:::
::::

::::card[What you carry]{#l-carry teaches=s-carrying}
You carry 3 **equipment** (what you wear or wield), 3 **utility** items (tools and things you use up) and a **keepsake**, and your **bag** holds 10 more. Each is a point when you make your character, bar the keepsake and the bag. [Attuned things](#s-attunement) are on top of all that. Pulling something out of your bag takes your action, and putting it away's free. A plain item does what it is, and it's never a step.

:::example
A few sessions in, Wren's utility is full when the trail runs into a cave. She puts her snare kit away in her pack for free, and spends her action pulling out the torch bundle.
:::
::::

::::card[Attuned things]{#l-attuned teaches=s-attunement}
When you **attune** something, it's bound to you and it grows with you. It's 5 points, it starts at Awakened, and it comes with one **awakening**, something it does. Give it its [Catch](#s-trouble) and it gets a second, free. Each awakening locks a point of the Root it fits for as long as it's attuned, so that Root holds a point less, though it still rolls whole. It can't be broken.

:::example
Vesk's grappling line has *Anywhere I can throw a hook* (Body), so her Body's 11 holds 10. She still rolls all 11, and over a roof it's 13 steps, two d12s and a d2.
:::
::::

::::card[Awakenings]{#l-awakenings teaches=s-attunement}
An **awakening**'s 2 steps on any roll it fits, and every Aura you put in it is 2 more, so it's where Aura turns into dice. A few do something no roll could, like letting an arrow reach anything you can see, and they just do it. Nobody else can put Aura in your awakening, bar a [Maker](#a-maker) who can touch it.

:::example
Wren's shooting at a lookout behind a hedge with her Body and *Long shot*, 10 steps. She puts 1 Aura in for 2 more, so it's 12 steps, two d12s, and *Hit anything I can see* means the hedge doesn't help him.
:::
::::

::::card[Bonds and threads]{#l-threads teaches=s-bonds}
A **bond** is usually someone who matters to your character, and each **thread** is one truth between you. A bond thread's something good, like a rescue or a shared past. A hostile thread's something gone bad, like a debt or a grudge. Any thread can be strained, which means it's nearly snapped. Each person's one bond, with all your threads with them under it. You start with two bond threads with the crew, and writing a hostile one gives you room for a third bond thread.

:::example
Vesk's bond with Dorrie holds two threads, one in her words and one in Dorrie's. Her thread with the fence, *I owe a debt I would rather not discuss*, is hostile.
:::
::::

::::card[Leaning on a bond]{#l-lean teaches=s-bonds}
A bond thread's something you can lean on. Pay 1 Aura to borrow one thing off their sheet, like a permission. Or lean on them in the story, like calling in a favour, and you get **a point of Soul back**. It's free, and the thread's strained until you make it right with them. Strain it again before it's mended and it snaps. You can't borrow through a strained thread or lean on a hostile one.

:::example
Dorrie's Soul is down to 3 on Vane's stair, so she whistles for Vesk to pull the guards away. She's on 4, and their thread's strained on both sheets till she gives Vesk the pick of the haul.
:::
::::

::::card[Your Aspiration]{#l-aspiration teaches=s-aspiration}
Your **Aspiration** is one thing you want. It starts at d2 and climbs as you chase it: when something you do really moves it along, roll against its die, and beat it to climb one. A setback steps it back down, never below d2. When it climbs past d12, it resolves: you get what you were after, take a new Title, archetype or attuned thing with the one it comes with, and write a new Aspiration at d2.

:::example
Vesk's *Own the score of a lifetime* is on d2 when she cracks a strongbox in the Low Market. Her Body's d12 and d10 roll 7 and 2 against its 1, so it climbs to d4.
:::
::::

::::card[Armour]{#l-armour teaches=s-armour}
Armour sits on one of your Roots: leathers, plate and shields on your Body, court clothes or a disguise you trust on your Mind, and a holy symbol or a ward on your Soul. When a hit gets through to that Root, it can take it instead: tick one of its boxes and you keep the point. **Common**, **Fine** and **Masterwork** have 1, 2 and 3 boxes, and it breaks when the last one goes.

:::example
A spear gets through to Bram's Body, and he puts it on his Fine plate. That's one of its two boxes, and his Body stays on 7.
:::
::::

::::card[Abilities]{#l-abilities teaches=s-abilities}
Every archetype comes with one ability, and more are a point each. Each one costs **1 Aura** every time you use it, and they stack, so pay again and you get it again. **Push** is the one most have: +2 to one die on your mechanic, once you've rolled, and +3 with the same archetype twice. A die it takes to its top face doesn't pay Aura.

:::example
Vesk's a Shadow twice over, and her best die to hide comes up 5 against the cellar guard's 7. She pays 1 Aura for her Push, +3, so it's 8 and he walks right past.
:::
::::

::::card[The round]{#l-round teaches=s-round}
When things get dangerous and timing matters, play moves into **rounds**. First the GM telegraphs everything that's coming this round, face up. Then you act, in any order you like, one action each. There's no initiative, so talk it through and make a plan. At the end of the round, every telegraph nobody answered lands, and every condition steps down a size.

:::example
The GM puts a Warden's spear at Isolde's Body on the table, a d10, and the maid on the stair slipping off to wake the house, a d4. Wren and Bram take on the spear, and Vesk goes after the maid.
:::
::::

::::card[Telegraphs]{#l-telegraphs teaches=s-telegraphs}
A **telegraph** is the GM saying out loud what's about to happen, before anything's rolled: what it's doing, who or what it's aimed at, and how hard, in dice, and how many hits when it's more than one. At one of you, it says which Root: blows at your Body, fear and lies at your Mind, despair and curses at your Soul. A threat aims one die at each of you. Running doesn't dodge one, so to stop it, [answer it](#s-answering).

:::example
"The brute's going to bring his club down on Isolde. That's a d8 at her Body." Her Body's 0, so it'd leave her Bloodied, and Wren and Bram talk about answering it before anyone rolls.
:::
::::

::::card[Your action]{#l-action teaches=s-actions}
Every round you get **one action**, and nothing gets you another. Usually it's a roll, with an intent on every die, so one roll can go at a threat, answer a telegraph, break a [condition](#s-conditions) and get you further than your stride. Or it's [Defend](#s-defend), a lead if you're a Leader, pulling something out of your bag, or taking a breath. Your stride's free.

:::example
Bram crosses the hall on his stride, which is free, and goes at the guard on the door with his Body and *Ring the bell*, 11 steps: a d12 and a d10, one to disarm him and one to put him down.
:::
::::

::::card[Answering a telegraph]{#l-answer teaches=s-answering}
To **answer** a telegraph, give it one of your dice or more, from a Root that fits how you're stopping it, and roll against its dice. Win and it's stopped. Lose and it lands at once, with no defence roll, and a tie's a loss unless you pay 1 Aura. You can answer for someone else.

:::example
The brute's club is coming at Isolde, a d8. Wren puts an arrow in his arm with her Body and *Long shot*, 10 steps, and Bram gets his hammer under the club, bringing his Body and *Ring the bell*, 11 more. That's 21 steps, three d12s and a d6. The club rolls 6 and a d12 comes up 8, so it never lands.
:::
::::

::::card[When it lands]{#l-lands teaches=s-defending}
Whatever nobody answers lands at the end of the round. Roll each Root that's got something landing on it, once, at full, split however you like: your Body against what's at your Body, and the same for your Mind and your Soul. Each die stops one telegraph it beats or ties, because ties go to you. Everything else gets through, and it's a hit.

:::example
Two guards telegraph at Vesk's Body, a d8 and a d6. She rolls her Body, 11 steps, as a d12 and a d10, and gets 5 and 2. The d8 rolls 6 and the d6 rolls 3, so her 5 stops the d6, and the d8 gets through.
:::
::::

::::card[Defend]{#l-defend teaches=s-defend}
**Defend** takes your action. When the telegraphs land, roll a Root that fits how you're defending, at full, on top of the Root they're landing on. Each die stops one telegraph it beats or ties, as long as how you're defending fits it, so a shield won't stop a lie. Split it however you like, so you've got more dice for more blows.

:::example
Bram Defends behind his shield. When they land, his Body's 9 steps roll as a d12 and a d6, and his Defend's another 9 steps, a d12 and a d6, so he's got four dice for four blows.
:::
::::

::::card[Intercept]{#l-intercept teaches=s-intercept}
When a telegraph's aimed at a friend's Body and they're within your stride, you can **intercept** it for 1 Aura and no action. Take as many of the dice aimed at them as you like, and they come at your Body instead. It's 1 Aura for each friend, and a Guardian does it for free. Nobody can step in front of what's at a friend's Mind or Soul.

:::example
The brute's club is coming down at Isolde, and her Body's 0. Vesk's right beside her, so she pays 1 Aura and takes it. Her Body's d12 and d10 roll 9 and 4 against the club's 3, so it's stopped, and the win pays her Aura back.
:::
::::

::::card[Consequences]{#l-cost teaches=s-consequences}
When a blow gets through, the GM names what it costs you, picking one that fits. Usually it's a **hit**, a point off the Root it was aimed at. It might be a box on your [armour](#s-armour), something you carry breaking, your [Aspiration](#s-aspiration) or the [Party Goal](#s-goal) stepping back down, or a [clock](#s-stakes). You can offer another that fits and costs about as much, and **2 for 1** always fits, if the other Root's got the points.

:::example
A guard's blade gets through, and the GM names a point of Vesk's Body. Her Aspiration's on d4, so she offers that instead, and it steps back down to d2.
:::
::::

::::card[When fear gets through]{#l-fear teaches=s-fear-gets-through}
Fear, lies and pressure get through to your Mind, and despair, temptation and curses get through to your Soul. They cost you the way a blade does: a point off that Root, unless your armour there takes it or you take it 2 for 1. If one had a specific intent, like *Frightened*, that's what happens when it gets through, unless you take it as a hit instead.

:::example
A Warden telegraphs *Frightened* at Wren's Mind, a d8. Her Mind rolls a d12 and a d4, 3 and 2, against its 6, so it gets through. She'd rather not spend her next action running, so she takes a point instead, and her Mind's on 7.
:::
::::

::::card[Threats and tags]{#l-threats teaches=s-challenges}
A threat comes down to two things: a **Challenge** and a handful of **tags**. The Challenge is its defence, a die twice its number, so Challenge 3 is a d6, and it never steps down. Tags are what's true about it, like *Armoured* or *Loyal*. The ones that keep it going are its health: every win strips one, and strip the last and it falls.

:::example
A vault door's Challenge 4, and *Oak*, *Iron Bars* and *Warded* keep it shut. That's three wins against its d8 to break it.
:::
::::

::::card[Conditions]{#l-conditions teaches=s-conditions}
A **condition**'s a tag carrying a die. It can be against you, like *Burning d8*, or for you, like *Warded d8*, and its die is the die that placed it. While it lasts it's a step against you or for you, and you can spend it for its one big effect. At the end of every round it steps down a size, and past d2 it's gone.

:::example
Ash's fire beats a Warden with a d12 meant to set it alight, so it's *Burning d12*. That lasts six rounds, unless someone spends it or puts it out first.
:::
::::

::::card[Wounds, Scars and the Blaze]{#l-blaze teaches=s-blaze}
A hit on a Root with no points left **wounds** it: **Bloodied** for your Body, **Frayed** for your Mind and **Hollow** for your Soul. A wounded Root heals only to half its maximum till downtime. Another hit there at 0 is a **Scar**, a Catch that locks a point for good, and you leave the scene. While you're wounded you can take the **Blaze** on your turn: the Scar, something extraordinary, then out, or die if you choose.

:::example
Bram's Body is Bloodied when the Warden comes for the gate. On his turn he Blazes and holds it alone till the crew's clear, and he does. His Body takes a Scar, so it holds 6 now.
:::
::::

::::card[Healing]{#l-healing teaches=s-healing}
At the end of every scene, roll each Root that's down, unsplit, and get back what it rolls. Only a [Healer](#a-healer) makes a **heal roll**: a Root unsplit, and the patient gets back what it rolls, across any of their Roots. Every heal they take gives them a **Toll**, and the next heal loses 5 for each one. Anyone can **quick heal**: 1 Aura and your action, a point back, no roll.

:::example
The crew's got no Healer, so Dorrie spends 1 Aura and her action to get a point of Bram's Body back, and he's on 4.
:::
::::

::::card[Downtime]{#l-downtime teaches=your-slots}
After every session there's a downtime, where the world moves and you heal and grow. It starts with everything back: every Root full, every wound and all your Toll gone. Every player gets two personal slots, for things like a new permission or ability, mending armour or chasing your Aspiration. The crew gets one action between you, and a slot you put into it makes everything count twice.

:::example
Bram's back on 7 of his Body as downtime starts. He spends one slot at the smithy to mend his plate, and his other slot puts *fight two at once* on his first Title, so his Body's 10 now, a d12 and a d8.
:::
::::
