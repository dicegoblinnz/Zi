**What you need to play.** Two to six players and a GM. Polyhedral dice (d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12), a few d2s (a coin works), and three d20s for *The Oracle*, or one d20 rolled three times. A grid map helps, but you don't need one. Paper and pencils. A session runs two to four hours.

I'm Mo. I run Dice Goblin, a game store in Auckland, and I made this game.

I like characters who kick ass and take names. So I built a game about competent people who stack the odds in their favour. Being good at one thing helps you borrow from another, and before you know it you're standing toe to toe with the best in the room.

Every rule in here has been through real games at a real table. Plenty of rules got thrown out along the way, and what you're holding is the stuff that survived.

Zi'Dara starts with *Zi*, the Sumerian word for soul. That's a nod to where this game started.

## If you've never played a roleplaying game {#s-new-to-rpgs}

Here's the short version. You and a few friends tell a story together. One of you is the GM, who plays the world: the places, the people and the trouble. Everyone else plays one character each, and together they're a crew that's good at what it does. The GM sets up a situation, you say what your character does, and when it's not certain whether it works, you roll dice to find out.

Nobody wins or loses the way they would in a board game. A session runs a few hours, and a run of sessions with the same crew is a campaign.

## Coming from another roleplaying game? {#s-from-dnd}

You'll pick it up fast, but a few things work differently. You build your character on points, and most characters start with two **Titles** that say who you are, like *the best cat burglar in the city*. Everything else your character can do is a permission written on one of them, so your Titles and permissions say what you're actually good at, and they build up your three [Roots](#s-roots), Body, Mind and Soul. You take [archetypes](#s-how-archetypes) as well, like Guardian or Shadow, and each one has something only it can do. There's no skill list. A roll's one Root and a step for every thing on your sheet that fits what you're doing, so the better you are at something, the bigger your dice. You don't roll a d20 to see if it works either. Your Roots are counted before you sit down, everything that fits stacks on top, and past a d12 you throw more dice. Most threats don't have hit points. They have tags, and every win strips one, though the big ones carry multipliers, like *Armoured ×4*, which takes four wins. There's no initiative. The GM shows you everything that's coming first, then everyone takes one action, in any order. If you want a proper side-by-side, there's a free guide called *Crossing Over*.

## What it looks like at the table {#s-at-the-table}

Don't worry about the words yet. *The Core* and *Conflict* explain all of them.

:::example[Over the wall]
**GM:** Vesk, you're halfway over the manor wall. The gate guard hasn't seen you yet, but his dog's spotted Bram at the gate, and he's reaching for the bell rope. That's a d6 at the bell, and if it lands, the whole house wakes up. Bram, the dog's already coming for you. That's a d8 at your Body.

**Vesk:** He's not ringing that bell. I swing down off the wall on my grappling line, land on him from behind and drag him away from the rope. That's my Body, my first Title, *move unseen*, Shadow twice, my grappling line, *Anywhere I can throw a hook* and my boots, 15 steps. Two d12s and a d6, all on the bell.

**GM:** Fair enough. Roll his d6 as well.

**Vesk:** 9 and 4 on the d12s, and 6 on my d6. That's a top face, so with the win that's 2 Aura. His d6 is a 6.

**GM:** You land on him before his hand's on the rope. Nobody's heard a thing.

**Bram:** I'm getting the gate open. The dog can have a go at my plate.

At the end of the round the dog's d8 rolls a 7 against Bram's Stamina, a d12, which rolls a 3. It gets through.

**GM:** It gets its teeth into your arm. That's a size of Stamina.

**Bram:** My Stamina's a d10, then. I keep hauling on the gate.

**GM:** It hangs off your arm, and you don't even slow down.
:::

## What's in this book {#whats-in-this-book}

This book is the whole game. You don't need anything else.

There are twenty chapters. The first eight teach you to play. The next six are about playing together over a campaign. The last six are for the GM: running the game, then her tools for building threats, the world around the crew and its factions, the Oracle, and what to do when something's not working. After that come the appendices, a glossary and an index.

There are worked examples all the way through: a character built from scratch, a whole fight, a week of downtime, a threat built start to finish, one character's whole career and a crew's whole career. If you want to play tonight, *Title Ideas and the Crew* has twenty-nine Title ideas to build from in ten minutes, roles and schools of magic, each with the archetypes it suits, and six ready-made characters. All eighteen archetypes are in *The Archetypes*.

## Where to start {#where-to-start}

**If you're a player**, read *The Core*, *Building Your Character* and *Conflict*, plus your own archetypes in *The Archetypes*. That's enough to sit down and play tonight.

**If you're the GM**, read the lot, in order. Everyone skips *The Party Sheet*, so don't. *Running the Game* is the chapter you'll keep coming back to.

**If you're all new to Zi'Dara**, start with the free starter adventure, *Ironjaw*. It teaches the whole table the game while you play it, and the GM only needs to read it through once.

**If you've been away for a while**, read *The Core* again, skim the *Quick Reference* at the back, then jump back into whatever chapter you were up to.

## A word on the rules {#a-word-on-the-rules}

Every rule in here is a rule, but a rule's only worth keeping if it makes your table better. If one gets in the way, play a session without it. If the game's better for it, bin it. It's your table.
