You play someone who's properly good at something, like a thief who can open any lock in the city or a fighter nobody wants to meet in a narrow corridor. Being good means you stack the odds in your favour before anyone touches a die.

Every roll's one of your three **Roots**, Body, Mind or Soul, whichever fits how you're doing it, and you roll all of it. Your Titles build your Roots up, and so do your **permissions**, short lines like *pick a mechanical lock* or *throw fire*. A Root's number is its dice, its health and what you've got to spend, so the better you get at something, the harder you are to put down.

The tactics come from bringing in a second Root. When one of your permissions explains how your Mind gets in on a fight, pay for it, and your Mind's dice go on the roll with your Body's. Then split your dice however you like and give every one of them a job.

At the end of the day the question is simple: **did you succeed?** You roll against whatever's in your way, and if you beat the number, you did.

## Roots {#s-roots}

You've got three Roots, and every roll starts from one of them. Your Titles and permissions build them: **a Title puts 5 on your Roots**, spread however you like, and **a permission puts 1 on its Root**. Nothing else adds to a Root.

| Root | What it is | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Body** | what your hands and feet do | swinging a hammer, climbing, sneaking, lifting, picking a lock |
| **Mind** | what goes on in your head | noticing, remembering, working something out, lying, seeing through a lie |
| **Soul** | who you are | giving orders, inspiring, frightening someone, holding your nerve, magic that pours out of you |

Pick the Root that fits *how* you're doing it, not what you're after. Dorrie can get past the gate guard with her Mind, a story he can't pick holes in, or with her Soul, a smile and a bit of nerve. It's the same door either way, and she'll go with whichever she's better at. Her Mind's 7, a d12 and a d2, and her Soul's 6, a d12, so she lies.

**A Root's number does three jobs.** It's the steps of dice you roll with it, it's how many hits it can take, and it's what you spend when you push yourself. Isolde's Mind is 9, so she rolls a d12 and a d6 with it, and she's got 9 points of Mind to lose to lies and pressure, or to spend. Getting stronger makes you sturdier. There's more in [Your Points](#s-tracks).

## Titles and Permissions {#s-permissions}

A **Title** is who you are, a role or a school of magic, real and specific: *the best cat burglar in the city*, *Captain of the Guard*, *Master of Illusions*. **Each Title puts 5 on your Roots, spread however you like**, and it comes with one permission, so it's 6 on your Roots in all. Vesk's *The best cat burglar in the city* is all hands and feet, so it's Body 5. Her *Raised by the Guild* is mostly what she learned there, with a bit of knife work, so it's Mind 3 and Body 2. A Title holds six permissions at most.

A **permission** is something you can do, written as a doing word with how, what or where: *pick a mechanical lock*, *fight in a shield wall*, *throw fire*. **Each permission puts 1 on its Root**, the one that fits how you do it, and it has to fit its Title. *Pick a mechanical lock* is Body. A good one comes up a few times a session. Magic stays narrow: *throw fire*, not *fire magic*, because a magic permission is what lets you do that magic at all, and it does what it says for free. There's more in [Writing permissions](#s-writing-permissions).

**A permission's a permission and nothing more.** It says what you can do, and it's what lets you bring a second Root into a roll (see [Bringing In a Root](#s-bring)). You never count them, so there's nothing to hunt for when you pick up your dice. One word for what kind of person you are, like *Strong* or *Deceptive*, isn't a permission, because that's what your Roots are for.

The rest of your sheet doesn't touch your Roots, and it isn't counted on a roll either. Your **archetypes**, like Guardian or Shadow, are separate from your Titles. Each one's got something only it can do, a Trait that's always true of you, and abilities you pay for in Aura. They're all in [The Archetypes](#s-how-archetypes). Your **attuned thing** is something personal that's bound to you, and each of its **awakenings** locks a point of the Root it fits and puts 2 steps on a roll it fits (see [Attunement](#s-attunement)). Everything you carry does what it is: a sword cuts, a rope gets you up a wall and a lockpick opens locks.

[How a roll adds up](#how-permissions-stack) has Vesk's Roots, and everything that goes on a roll.

### How a roll adds up {#how-permissions-stack}

-   **One Root a roll, at full.** Pick the one that fits how you're doing it. Points you've spent, lost or locked never make its dice smaller.
-   **Another Root, when a permission explains it**, for 1 point of that Root or 3 Aura, and all of it goes on too (see [Bringing In a Root](#s-bring)).
-   **2 steps for an awakening that fits**, and 2 more for every Aura you put in it.
-   **And nothing else.** Your Titles, permissions, archetypes, abilities and items aren't counted. A condition that's on you can still be a step for you or against you (see [Conditions](#s-conditions)).
-   **Nothing comes in levels.** A permission you picked up last week does as much as one you started with.

| Vesk's | Where it comes from | Steps and points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | 5 from her first Title, 2 from her second, *move unseen*, *run the rooftops*, *pick a mechanical lock*, *fight dirty with a knife* | 11 |
| Mind | 3 from her second Title, *know how a lock's built*, *find a buyer for anything* | 5 |
| Soul | nothing yet | 0 |

So picking a lock is her Body, 11 steps, a d12 and a d10, and so is climbing, sneaking and knife work. Working out how a vault door's built before she touches it is her Mind, 5 steps, a d10. Her Soul's 0, so a rousing speech would be a d2, and that's fine. When she's at the vault door itself, *know how a lock's built* brings her Mind in for a point of it, and that's 16 steps, two d12s and a d8.

## Steps and Dice {#s-building-die}

When things get tense you've got **one action** a round, and nothing gets you another (see [Conflict](#s-actions)). Outside a round it's the same: one roll for each go at something.

Every roll goes the same way:

1.  Say what you're doing and how, and pick the **Root** that fits how. All of it goes on the roll.
2.  If a permission explains how another Root gets in on it, **bring that Root in** for 1 point of it or 3 Aura, and all of it goes on as well.
3.  Put in any **awakening** that fits, 2 steps, and 2 more for every Aura you put in it.
4.  Going beyond what something normally does is **1 Aura** (see [Going Beyond](#s-going-beyond)).
5.  Turn your steps into dice, split them however you like, and give every die an intent.
6.  Roll. Then, if you need it, pay Aura for an ability, like your Push (see [Abilities](#s-abilities)).

:::example[Building a roll]
**One Root:** Vesk's going over Lord Vane's roof without a sound. That's her Body, 11 steps, and her grappling line's awakening, *Anywhere I can throw a hook*, fits a climb like that, so it's 13: two d12s and a d2.

**Bringing one in:** Bram's chasing a smuggler down the quay, his Body, 9 steps, a d12 and a d6. Halfway down he bellows at the dockhands to grab the man, which is *bark an order that gets obeyed*, so he pays a point of Soul and his Soul comes in, 4 more. That's 13 steps, two d12s and a d2.
:::

:::rule
**No arguing at the table.** Pick the Root that fits how you're doing it. Bringing a second Root in takes a permission that explains how it gets in on what you're doing, so say how, pay for it, and roll. The GM can still say no, but that should be rare, mostly for when a player's being cheeky. It keeps the game moving and it keeps your character competent.
:::

### Steps into dice {#steps-into-dice}

:::quote
Your die is your steps × 2, up to a d12. Every six steps gives you another d12, and whatever's left over makes one more die.
:::

| Steps | Dice |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | d2 |
| 2 | d4 |
| 3 | d6 |
| 4 | d8 |
| 5 | d10 |
| 6 | d12 |
| 7 | d12 + d2 |
| 8 | d12 + d4 |
| 9 | d12 + d6 |
| 10 | d12 + d8 |
| 11 | d12 + d10 |
| 12 | 2d12 |
| 18 | 3d12 |
| 24 | 4d12 |

:::rule
**No die you build is ever bigger than a d12.** A gifted beginner can build a d12 on a good day, and so can a master. The difference is how many of them you throw, and rolls get big when you bring Roots in: Vesk at the vault door with her Body and her Mind is two d12s and a d8 before she's put a single Aura in. In Zi'Dara, getting stronger means throwing more dice.
:::

**A Root of 0?** You can still have a go. Every roll's at least 1 step, a d2. It's a long shot, but long shots do land.

## Bringing In a Root {#s-bring}

This is where you get clever. When something you can do explains how a second Root gets in on what you're doing, **bring that Root in**, and all of it goes on the roll.

-   **Name the permission** that explains it, and say how. Bram's swinging his hammer at the guard captain, and *spot who's in charge* is how his Mind gets in on it: he's going for the man giving the orders, where it'll hurt the rest of them most.
-   **Pay for it**: 1 point of the Root you're bringing in, or 3 Aura. Bram pays a point of Mind.
-   **All of it goes on.** His Body's 9 and his Mind's 3, so it's 12 steps, two d12s. Points you've spent or lost never make a Root smaller on a roll, so his Mind's still 3 steps on the next one, even with a point gone.
-   **Bring in both** if you've got the permissions for it, each one paid for on its own.
-   **Any roll will do**, your defence and a heal included. It's said as you build the roll, before anyone rolls, and it's in for that roll only.

Spending a point or 3 Aura is a real choice. A point comes out of what that Root can take, and 3 Aura's most of what you've got. Either way it's said out loud, and the whole table hears the clever bit.

:::example[Bringing in at the table]
Isolde's reading the dead order's script off a crypt wall, her Mind, 9 steps, a d12 and a d6. The script's a ward, and *draw a ward the dead can't cross* is how her Soul knows one when it sees it, so she pays a point of Soul and brings it in: 16 steps, two d12s and a d8.

Wren's lining up a shot on the archer in the bell tower, her Body, 8 steps. She's read the ground the whole way in, so *read the land for an ambush* brings her Mind in. The Warden's howl left her Mind on 1 point, and spending it would leave nothing between her and the next one, so she pays 3 Aura instead. Her Mind's 8 steps however few points it's got left, so that's 16, and her longbow's *Long shot* fits too, 2 more: 18 steps, three d12s.
:::

## The Roll {#s-the-roll}

Most of the time you're rolling against something that pushes back: a guard, a lock, a magistrate, a storm. You roll your dice, you roll theirs too in another colour, and you compare. The GM almost never touches a die in Zi'Dara. She's busy running everything else.

-   **Beat it** and you did it.
-   **Tie** and it goes to whoever's defending: you when something comes at you, him when you go at the guard. If you're the one acting, even to stop something coming at you, you can pay **1 Aura** to win the tie. The GM never spends Aura, and there's no Aura in downtime (see [Downtime](#s-downtime-rolls)).
-   **Roll under** and you didn't. Try something else, or pay **a point of Soul** to roll again (see [Your Points](#s-tracks)).

**A target rolls its defence once a round.** The first time anyone goes at a threat in a round, roll its die, and that roll stands against everyone who goes at it till the round's over. It's different for you when the telegraphs land: you roll your Body once, at full, against everything landing at your Body, your Mind once against everything at your Mind, and your Soul once against everything at your Soul, and each die stops one telegraph (see [When It Lands](#s-defending)). Spend your action to [Defend](#s-defend) and you roll a Root on top.

A wall you need to climb or a book you've got to get through before dawn doesn't fight back, but it's still a **Challenge**, and you still roll its die and have to beat it. A Challenge is a die twice its number, so Challenge 2 is a d4 and Challenge 6 is a d12. Threats have **tags** too, like *Armoured* or *Loyal*, and your wins strip them off. A win can also leave a **condition**, like *Burning*, that carries your die. Both are in [Conflict](#s-challenges).

**Every die of yours that wins gives you 1 Aura, and so does every die that lands on its top face, win or lose.** A die that beats three men at once still pays once, and a die that wins on its top face pays twice. It counts for every roll you make, your defence and your recovery included. A summon's dice don't pay, and nor does a die something added to after you rolled, a number an Oracle swapped in or a die a Gambler flipped. Small dice hit their top face more often, so you'll see players split off a tiny die they don't really need, just to fish for Aura. It still needs an intent, and a d2 that's got to beat a d8 hardly ever does. That's the trade, and it's theirs to make.

### When a miss matters {#s-miss}

A miss is just a miss, unless something's pushing back or time's running out. Then it **costs** you something, a **consequence**, and **the GM names it**: a hit on your Body, your Mind or your Soul, your gear, your dreams, the crew's Goal, or the clock. If you'd rather pay something else that fits and costs about as much, offer it (see [Conflict](#s-consequences)). Picking a lock with all night and nobody around costs nothing if it goes wrong. With the guard coming up the stairs, it's a different story.

## Intents: What Each Die Is For {#s-intents}

Before you roll, give **every die an intent**: what it's trying to do, and who or what it's aimed at. Say it out loud. *This d10 is for the lock. This d6 keeps it quiet.* An intent can be anything the story allows: hurt it, disarm it, pin it, shove it off the stairs, grab the key off its belt, find out what it's hiding, stop the knife coming at you. Whatever you gave the die, that's what happens if it wins.

**Every die's as good as an action.** You've only got one action a round, so more dice is how you do more. One roll can answer a telegraph, go at a threat and get you across the room, as long as the Root you're rolling fits how you're doing each of them.

### Several dice, several intents {#several-dice-several-intents}

Put three dice on one man and each one can try something different. He'll roll his defence once this round, and **each of your dice has to beat that one roll**. A threat defends with its Challenge die.

### Several dice, one intent {#several-dice-one-intent}

Put more than one die on the **same** intent and that's **advantage**: your **best** die counts. **Dice never add together**, except when you move, when a [Warrior's](#a-warrior) dice explode, and when an ability adds to a die.

### Past six steps: more dice, more intents {#past-six-steps-more-dice-more-intents}

Every die you get past six steps can have **its own intent**, and that's what big rolls are for. Two dice at the same Warden are two chances to strip two tags. You can still put them on one intent for advantage, but a d12 and a d4 doing two different things is nearly always worth more than the same two dice on one intent.

:::example[Three dice on the guard captain]
Bram's holding the barricade, and the guard captain's coming over it. Bram swings with his Body, 9 steps, and *Ring the bell* fits his hammer, 2 more. He's going for the man giving the orders, so *spot who's in charge* brings his Mind in for a point of Mind, 3 more. That's 14 steps, two d12s and a d4: *one to knock the sword out of his hand, one to put him on the floor, and the d4 to make him give up.*

The captain's Challenge 5, and it's the first time anyone's gone at him this round, so he rolls his d10 once: 6. Bram's dice come up 9, 6 and 4. The sword goes skittering, the second d12's a tie, and the d4 was never going to beat a 6, though it landed on its top face. Bram could pay 1 Aura to win the tie, but he lets it go. So the captain's disarmed, still standing, and he won't yield. One die won and the d4 hit its top face, so that's 2 Aura for Bram.
:::

### Splitting: shaping your dice {#s-splitting}

You can **split** your steps into more, smaller dice, as many as you like and whatever sizes you like, as long as they add up to your steps. Vesk's Body is 11 steps, and that can be a d12 and a d10, or a d8, a d8 and a d6, or eleven d2s if she wants to fish for Aura with every one of them. There's no limit, and nobody's counting.

The catch is that small dice lose. A d2 against a d8 hardly ever wins, so the more you split, the more you miss. Splitting's how you do several things at once, and how you decide what matters most.

Every die can go at something different, as long as the Root you're rolling fits how you're doing it and you can reach it from where you are. If a die would be doing something your Root's got nothing to do with, like a rousing speech in the middle of Vesk's run across the rooftops, bring the right Root in for it, or leave it.

:::example[Splitting at the study door]
Vesk's at the study door, and picking its lock is her Body, 11 steps. *Know how a lock's built* brings her Mind in for a point of Mind, and it's 16. That's two d12s and a d8 as it comes, and she splits it four ways: a d12 for the lock, a d8 to keep it quiet so the guard at the foot of the stairs hears nothing, a d6 to listen for anyone else coming, and a d6 to leave the lock without a scratch on it.

The lock's d8 rolls 5, and her d12 opens it with a 7. Her d8 lands on 8, a top face, against the guard's 3. Her first d6 has got nothing to beat but the quiet house, and the GM calls it a Challenge 1, a d2, which her 4 beats. Her last d6 comes up 2 against the lock's 5, so there'll be a scratch for someone to find. Three dice won, and the 8 was a top face as well, so that's 4 Aura.
:::

### Area: one die for how far {#s-area}

If you're an [Elementalist](#a-elementalist), one of your dice can be your **area** instead of an intent. It doesn't cost Aura. Say what shape it is and roll it with the rest. Everyone the story puts in it defends against your other die on its own, and whatever you beat, your intent happens to it. Nobody else makes a die their area.

-   **Burst:** out from a point in every direction, as many squares as you rolled.
-   **Cone:** out from you the way you're facing, as many squares long as you rolled, and as wide at the end as it is long.
-   **Line:** straight out from you, twice as many squares as you rolled.

**You can always make it smaller** than you rolled. The story decides what's in it, and a ball of fire catches friend and foe alike. Without a grid, 1 or 2 is everyone close to the target, 3 to 5 is everyone within a few steps, and 6 or more is the whole room.

Threats use the same shapes for their areas. **Sweep** weapons go at everyone within your reach for free, because that's what the weapon does.

**A ward or a blessing** goes on one friend. Nothing pushes back, so you don't roll it, and the die you built becomes the condition's die on them. Split it, and the second die can put it on another friend you can touch. There's more in [Wards and blessings](#s-wards).

:::example[An area at the table]
The guardroom door opens on five men. Ash is an Elementalist, and she wants them gone more than she wants them burnt: her Soul, 11 steps, a d12 and a d10. The d10's her burst, which doesn't cost her anything. It rolls 3, three squares out, and they're all in it. Her d12 rolls 7, once, and each man rolls his own d6 defence. Not one of them gets near it, and they run for it. Her d12 won, so that's 1 Aura, once, however many men it beat.

With the dead coming up through the crypt floor, Isolde draws a ward with her Soul, 7 steps, and splits it into a d8 and a d6. Nothing pushes back, so nothing's rolled, and no Aura comes back from it. Bram's holding the stair, so the d8 makes him *Warded d8*, and Vesk's right beside her, so the d6 makes her *Warded d6*.
:::

## Going Beyond {#s-going-beyond}

Every permission, magic or not, does what it says for free, the same as a sword. You need a magic permission or a magic item to do magic at all, and once you've got one, it works like anything else.

**Going beyond** what something normally does costs **1 Aura**, for a sword or a spell. It doesn't add a step. You spend the Aura and it does the bigger thing.

-   Phasing is free until you phase through a wall. That's 1 Aura.
-   A master of combat fights for free. An aimed shot that takes out the tag you choose costs 1 Aura.
-   A necromancer raising the dead to attack or carry something, as part of their own action, is free.
-   Isolde speaks with the recently dead for free. Calling up someone who died a hundred years ago is going beyond, so that's 1 Aura.

Anything that works on its own, like a summoned beast or a raised servant acting for you, is **summoning**, and only a [Summoner](#a-summoner) can do that (see [Summoning](#s-summoning)).

**If you have to make a case for why it's normal, it's going beyond.** Spend the Aura and keep playing.

## Aura {#s-aura}

**Aura** is your character's luck and flair, the bit of soul they put into a moment when skill alone won't get them there. It comes and goes all scene, and it's what you spend when you want more than your Roots give you.

-   **You start every scene full.** Your maximum's **5**, or more with the Aura Boon (see [Boons](#s-boons)). A scene is one place and one problem, and the next one starts when that problem's sorted or the crew moves on. There's no Aura in downtime.
-   **Every die of yours that wins gives you 1 back, and so does every top face** (see [The Roll](#s-the-roll)).
-   **While you're acting, it can go past your maximum.** Once you've finished acting for the round, anything over that's gone, so spend it first. When it comes in while you're not acting, like off your defence, it stops at your maximum, and you can give the rest to a friend in the scene.

**Spend it to:**

-   **Bring a Root in**, for **3**, when a permission explains it and you'd rather keep that Root's points (see [Bringing In a Root](#s-bring)).
-   **Put more into an awakening**: **1** for 2 more steps on a roll it fits, as many as you like (see [Attunement](#s-attunement)).
-   **Pay for an ability**, **1** each time you use it (see [Abilities](#s-abilities)).
-   **Borrow** one thing off a friend's sheet through a bond thread you share, for **1** (see [Bonds and threads](#s-bonds)).
-   **Go beyond** what something normally does, for **1**.
-   **Win a tie** when you're the one acting, for **1**.
-   **Intercept** for a friend within your stride, for **1**: take any of the blows aimed at their Body, and they come at you instead (see [Intercept](#s-intercept)). A [Guardian](#a-guardian) does it for free. Fear and despair are inside your friend's head, so nobody can step in front of those.
-   **Quick heal** someone, for **1** and your action (see [Healing](#s-healing)), or keep a Catch quiet this time, for **1**.

### Getting it back {#getting-it-back}

-   **Win and roll top faces.** That's where most of it comes from.
-   **Let a Catch bite**, as often as you like, and take 1 Aura each time. A hostile thread bites the same way. A big bite gets you a point of Soul back instead. No Catch? Bring real trouble on yourself anyway and take 1 Aura. There's more in [Catches and Trouble](#s-trouble).
-   **A great setup that doesn't land.** You planned it, it was cool, and the dice said no. The GM can give you 1 Aura for it.
-   **Boons** like *Beat a threat* and *In your element* (see [Boons](#s-boons)).

:::example[Aura at one table]
**Spent:** Wren's shooting from the gallery at the archer and the two men beside him. That's her Body, 8 steps, and her longbow's *Long shot* fits, 2 more. She's on 5, and she puts 2 into the bow for 4 steps more, so it's 14 steps, two d12s and a d4, and she's on 3.

**Past her maximum:** All three of her dice win, and one lands on its top face, so she's on 7 while she's acting. The brute's club is coming at Isolde beside her, and Isolde's Body is 0, so Wren pays 1 to intercept it and she's on 6. When she's done for the round, the 1 over her maximum is gone.

**Got back:** Crossing the market, Vesk's player says a watch sergeant knows her face from the posters. The GM has him shout for the watch, and Vesk takes 1 Aura.
:::

## Your Points {#s-tracks}

Your Roots are what keep you standing. **A Root's got as many points as its number**, less any that are locked, and a new character starts with all of them. Those points are what it can take, and what you spend when you push yourself.

-   **Hits take points.** A telegraph that gets through to your Body takes a point of Body, and the same goes for your Mind and your Soul. A vulnerability or a deadly threat makes it 2 hits or more (see [Hits and Consequences](#s-consequences)).
-   **You spend them to push.** Bringing a Root in costs a point of it, and rolling again after a miss costs a point of Soul. You can't spend points you haven't got.
-   **Your dice never shrink.** Spent, lost or locked, a Root's still its whole number on a roll. Vesk on 2 points of Body still rolls 11 steps, a d12 and a d10.
-   **Locked points.** Each awakening locks a point of the Root it fits for as long as it's attuned, and each Scar locks one for good. A locked point's still dice, but it's not a point you can have. What's left is that Root's **maximum**. Vesk's grappling line locks a point of her Body, so her Body's 11 and its maximum's 10.
-   **At 0 you're exposed.** The next hit on a Root with no points left wounds it (see [Wounds, Scars and the Blaze](#s-blaze)). A Root that's 0 to start with has nothing to lose, so its first hit wounds it: a curse that gets through to Vesk's Soul leaves her Hollow straight away.
-   **They come back.** At the end of every scene, roll each Root that's below its maximum, unsplit, and get back what you roll. Downtime puts them all back (see [Healing](#s-healing)).

:::rule
**Pushing never hurts you.** Spending your points is pushing yourself, and it can't wound you or Scar you, because you can't spend points you haven't got. Spend a Root down to 0 and all you've done is leave yourself exposed: the next hit on it wounds you.
:::

**Roll again for a point of Soul.** Reroll a roll you just failed, all your dice or just one, your choice. The threat's dice stay where they landed. It works in downtime too, where there's no Aura.

**2 for 1.** When a hit gets through, you can take it on another Root instead, for 2 points of it, as long as it's got them. Bram takes the spear standing up and unflinching, and it costs him 2 points of Soul instead of a point of Body.

:::example[Bram's points in one fight]
Bram's Body is 9, and his war hammer's two awakenings lock 2 of it, so he starts the fight on 7 points of Body. A spear gets through in round one, and he's on 6. In round two *spot who's in charge* brings his Mind in for a point of Mind, so his Mind's on 2, and his swing's still 12 steps, two d12s. Two blows get through in round three, so his Body's on 4, and when one more spear gets through in round four he takes it 2 for 1 on his Soul, so his Soul's on 2 and his Body stays on 4.

At the end of the scene his Body rolls a d12 and a d6, 5 and 2, and that's more than enough to put it back on 7, its maximum. His Mind rolls a 4 on its d6 and goes back to 3, and his Soul rolls a 3 on its d8 and goes back to 4.
:::

## Catches and Trouble {#s-trouble}

A **Catch** is trouble written on your sheet, ready to go. It comes from an attuned thing (see [Attunement](#s-attunement)), or from a Scar (see [Wounds, Scars and the Blaze](#s-blaze)). It's got to be something that can bring real trouble into a scene, something the GM can run with. If it'd never come up, it isn't a Catch.

Trouble's yours to bring. Letting your Catches bite is up to you, and the GM doesn't hand out trouble. She makes things go wrong in all the usual ways, like telegraphs and the cost of a miss.

-   **Let it bite**, as often as you like. Say how it causes trouble, the GM runs with it, and you take 1 Aura. Every bite has to bring real trouble.
-   **A big bite** changes the scene or costs you something you wanted. You get **a point of Soul back** for it instead of the Aura, as far as its maximum. It's your call when.
-   **A hostile thread** bites the same way as a Catch, for 1 Aura, or a point of Soul for a big bite (see [Bonds and threads](#s-bonds)).
-   **No Catch?** Bring real trouble on yourself in the story anyway, and take 1 Aura.

:::example[Trouble at the table]
**A hostile thread:** Vesk owes the fence, and it's a hostile thread on her sheet. Halfway across a Guild warehouse roof, her player lets it bite: the fence's boy is under the drainpipe, and he wants paying tonight. The GM runs with it, and now there's a debt collector shouting under the watchman's window. Vesk takes 1 Aura, the same as she would for a Catch.

**A big bite:** Bram's Soul is down to 2 after two rerolls at the gate, and his attuned war hammer has the Catch *Marked*. At the harbour, his player lets it bite in a big way: the Vane Estate's man on the quay knows that hammer from across the water, and the boat the crew spent a week arranging leaves without them. That costs them something they wanted, so Bram gets a point of Soul back instead of the Aura, and he's on 3.
:::

## Working Together {#s-teamwork}

There's no special action for helping. To help a friend, put your action into the same thing they're doing. That's a **combo**: two or more actions going into the same thing at the same time, rolled once between you.

-   **Every action brings its Root.** Whoever rolls brings theirs, and every friend who puts their action in brings the Root that fits how they're helping, all of it, the same as if they were rolling it on their own.
-   **Each of you can bring a Root in on top**, with your own permission and your own point or 3 Aura, and an awakening of yours that fits is 2 steps as usual.
-   **Nobody pays Aura for the combo itself**, but it has to be one thing in the story, and you've got to be able to join in from where you are. Any number of friends can join one roll.
-   **Whoever rolls gets the Aura** for every die that wins and every top face, friends' dice included.
-   **Joining a friend's roll** takes your action, and brings any steps a Leader's handed you as well.

:::example[Back at the study door]
Vesk's at the study door, and picking its lock is her Body, 11 steps. Dorrie joins in, spinning the guard a story at the foot of the stairs, and brings her Mind, 7 more. That's 18 steps between the two of them: a d12 for the lock, a d12 to keep it quiet and a d12 to keep the guard talking.
:::

### Leading and inspiring {#leading-and-inspiring}

Only a [Leader](#a-leader) leads. An order or a rallying song is your action: build it from the Root that fits, then hand its steps out however you like to friends who can hear or see you. They add them to their own rolls this round, and those steps don't have to fit anything. A lead can be a combo too, with friends putting their actions in before you hand it out.

:::example[Bram barks orders]
Bram's a Leader, and he barks orders from the doorway with his Soul, 4 steps. He gives 2 to Vesk and 2 to Wren, so this round Vesk's run over the roof goes from 13 steps to 15, two d12s and a d6, and Wren's shot from cover goes from 10 to 12, two d12s.
:::

## Studying Something {#s-study}

Most things worth fighting have more going on than they show. **Study** is an intent like any other. Roll the Root that fits how you're looking, usually your Mind, against the target's Challenge: Isolde going over an old construct, or Dorrie reading a nervous witness. **Win, and pick one:**

-   **A hidden tag.** The GM reveals one. Ask something specific, like *what's holding it together?*, and she reveals the tag that answers it, if there is one. It's how you find weak points, and the thing a threat wants that could end the fight without a blow.
-   **A straight answer.** Ask one question about it, and the GM answers it truthfully.

Study works on people, places, stakes and whole scenes, and it finds what's really there.

If you're an [Oracle](#a-oracle) with *Foretold*, you can make something true instead. When you win a Study, pay 1 Aura and **introduce a truth**: something that wasn't obvious to anyone else. Say it, and it's true from now on, and anyone in the crew can bring a Root in on it this scene, like a permission they've all got. Isolde wins her Study on the brute, pays for Foretold and says his left knee never healed right. Now it didn't, and anyone in the crew can go for it this scene.
