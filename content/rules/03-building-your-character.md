:::quote
Your whole character fits in a few plain sentences and some numbers.
:::

You build your character with **35 points**. Here's what they buy:

| What you buy | Points | What it comes with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Title | 5 | 5 on your Roots, spread however you like, and one permission |
| An archetype | 5 | its mechanic, its Trait and one ability |
| An attuned thing | 5 | the thing itself at tier 2, and one awakening |
| Another permission | 1 | 1 on its Root |
| Another ability | 1 | one more off its archetype's list |
| An item | 1 | it comes at tier 2 |
| A tier up on an item | 1 | tier 3 at most when you make your character |

An awakening's a point as well, but what you attune starts out with room for the one it comes with and one more for its Catch, so the rest come as it grows (see [Attunement](#s-attunement)). Your keepsake, your Resources, your threads, your answers in Connect and your Aspiration cost nothing.

**I'd recommend at least one Title, one archetype and one attuned thing**, but the choice is yours, so build what you like. All six of the [ready-made characters](#s-pregens) went for two Titles, two archetypes and one attuned thing. Most characters start on 35. For a one-shot with characters further along, or to catch someone up with the rest of the crew, the GM can start you on more.

Here's how it goes, step by step:

1.  **Titles**: who you are, a role or a school of magic. Each one's 5 on your Roots, and it comes with a permission.
2.  **Archetypes**, like Guardian or Shadow. Each one comes with its Trait and an ability.
3.  **Permissions** on your Titles, each one 1 on its Root.
4.  **Your Roots**, added up.
5.  **Items:** six at most, each with its tier, and your keepsake on top. You've got 3 Resources as well, and you don't write those down till you need them.
6.  **Attunement**: each attuned thing starts at Awakened, with its awakening, which locks a point of the Root it fits.
7.  **Your numbers**, and **two bond threads** with the crew, or three if you write a hostile thread as well.
8.  **A few answers** that tie you to the rest of the crew, and they're where your bond threads come from.
9.  **An Aspiration**, with nothing in it yet.

You start with every Root full, a stride of 3, an Aura maximum of 5 and 3 Resources. Your armour soaks on whichever Root it's on.

In a hurry? The [Quick Build](#s-quick) gets you a character in ten minutes, or you can pick one of the [six ready-made characters](#s-pregens) and play. If you've got a character in your head but not the words for it yet, start from the [Title ideas](#s-title-ideas): example Titles and permissions, and the archetypes that suit them.

**Those are your starting numbers, not limits.** You earn more Titles, archetypes, attuned things, permissions, abilities and threads as you play, and there's no cap on Titles, Roots or threads. A Title can't hold more than six permissions, though, an archetype six abilities and an attuned thing six awakenings, and you [carry](#s-carrying) six items at most.

## Step 1: Titles {#s-title}

A **Title** is who you are: a role or a school of magic, real and specific. *The best cat burglar in the city*, *Captain of the Guard* and *Master of Illusions* are all Titles. Each one's **5 points**.

**Each Title puts 5 on your Roots, spread however you like**: all 5 on one, 3 on one and 2 on another, or any mix you like. Put them where the Title lives. Vesk's *The best cat burglar in the city* is all hands and feet, so it's Body 5. Her *Raised by the Guild* is mostly what she learned growing up there, with a bit of knife work, so it's Mind 3 and Body 2. **Every Title comes with one permission** (see [Step 3](#s-writing-permissions)). Your Roots are your dice, your health and what you spend, so every Title you earn makes you harder to put down.

One role or one school per Title. *Archmage* is too wide, so pick the school you're known for. A Title that could mean anything tells the table nothing, but one that says exactly what your character's good at, and how they got that way, does all the work.

:::example
**Written:** *Master of all trades.*\
**GM asks:** the best at what?\
**Player:** "Surviving. They grew up on the streets."\
**Title:** *Street survivor.*

**Written:** *Knight.*\
**GM asks:** sworn to what, trained by whom, good at what specifically?\
**Title:** *Knight of the Broken Oath.* Now the character's got a whole backstory in five words.
:::

### Four good Titles {#four-good-titles}

-   *The best cat burglar in the city.* Specific and boastful, and it suggests there's a rival somewhere.
-   *The last student of a dead order.* Specific and lonely, and it hands you a whole history.
-   *A doctor who stopped believing in medicine.* Specific and conflicted. It tells you how they think.
-   *Master of Illusions.* One school of magic, so the table knows straight away what you're good at.

### Three bad Titles {#three-bad-titles}

-   *Adventurer.* It means nothing, because adventure's what the whole game is about.
-   *Warrior.* That's an archetype, and you still need a Title to say what kind. A duellist and a sergeant in a shield wall can both be Warriors, and they don't do the same things, so nobody at the table knows which one you are.
-   *Good at everything.* Nobody is, and it doesn't tell the table what you're actually good at.

### What a Title holds {#what-a-title-holds}

**A Title holds its spread and its permissions.** It comes with one permission, more are a point each, and it holds six at most. There's no Catch and no Burden on a Title: Catches come from [attunement](#s-attunement), and Burdens only ever come on attuned things.

:::sheet[Bram's first Title]
**Title:** *The wall that held the harbour gate.*

**Roots:** Body 5.

**Permissions:** *hold the line* (Body) · *guard one person* (Body) · *lift what two men can't* (Body).

**Room to grow:** three more permissions.
:::

That's 7 points: 5 for the Title, which came with *hold the line*, and 1 each for the other two.

**Titles are the biggest growth in the game.** When your Aspiration comes good, a new Title is one of the things you can take. It comes with its 5 for your Roots and one permission, and Grow fills it from there. It counts the same way your first ones do, and it raises the stakes for the whole table: the GM's [threat budget](#s-threat-budget) grows as the crew earns them.

## Step 2: Archetypes {#s-step-archetypes}

An **archetype**, like Guardian, Healer or Shadow, is the kind of character you are at the table. Each one's **5 points**, and it comes with three things:

-   **Its mechanic**, something only it can do. A Guardian intercepts blows for free for every friend within his stride, and a Hunter marks a creature so everyone who acts against it rolls her Mark die as well.
-   **Its Trait**, something that's always true of you because of it. Most cost nothing, and the few that cost Aura or come once a scene say so.
-   **One ability**, your pick off its list, Push included. You pay for abilities in Aura when you use them, more are a point each, and an archetype holds six at most.

There are seventeen, and you'll find them all in [The Archetypes](#s-how-archetypes).

Your archetypes don't belong to your Titles. Bram's a Guardian and a Leader, and his Titles are *The wall that held the harbour gate* and *A sergeant who lost his stripes*. **You can take the same archetype twice**, like Vesk, for 5 points each time. Each one comes with its own ability, so she's got Push and Vanish, and her Push is +3 instead of +2. Your archetypes don't give you more actions: everyone gets one a round, and more dice is how you do more.

If you're not sure which ones you're after, the table in [Picking Your Archetypes](#s-picking-archetypes) says what each one's for, and every [Title idea](#s-title-ideas) says which ones suit it.

## Step 3: Permissions {#s-writing-permissions}

A **permission** is something your character can do. Each Title comes with one, more are **a point each**, and they have to fit inside the Title they're on. **Each permission puts 1 on its Root**, the one that fits how you do it, and you write it next to it: *pick a mechanical lock* (Body). It's also what lets you bring another Root into a roll: name the permission that says why, and pay for it (see [Bringing In a Root](#s-bring)). So it's worth a couple of minutes to get right. Here's how I write them.

### Every part of your sheet does a different job {#kinds-of-word}

| On your sheet | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | who you are, a name, and 5 on your Roots | *The best cat burglar in the city* |
| Root | how you're doing it, Body, Mind or Soul, and your dice, your health and what you spend there | Body |
| Permission | something you can do, 1 on its Root, and what lets you bring a Root into a roll | *pick a mechanical lock* |
| Catch | trouble the GM can run with, from an attuned thing | *Wanted* · *Loud* |
| Scar | what a hit at your worst took from you, written as something you can't do, and a point of its Root locked | *can't see on my left* |

If what you've written reads like a name, like *Combat master*, it's a Title. If it's something you can do, like *pick a mechanical lock*, it's a permission. If it's one word for what kind of person you are, like *Strong* or *Deceptive*, that's what your Roots are for, so build the Root instead.

### Two parts {#two-parts}

A permission's got **a doing word and the part that says how, what or where**:

-   *fight* and *with a war hammer* makes *fight with a war hammer*
-   *spot* and *who's in charge* makes *spot who's in charge*
-   *read* and *dead languages* makes *read dead languages*

### Check the size {#check-the-size}

Picture the moment you'd use it, then ask how often it'd come up. A good permission comes up a few times a session. If it'd come up once a campaign, it's too narrow. A wide one's fine, as long as it fits its Title and its Root.

| Too narrow | About right |
| --- | --- |
| *duel Vane's captain* | *fight with a sword* |
| *lie to Vane's steward* | *lie to someone's face* |
| *lift Vane's portcullis* | *lift what two men can't* |
| *hide in Vane's wine cellar* | *move unseen* |
| *light Vane's candles from across the room* | *throw fire* |

**Magic's the one that can be too wide.** A magic permission is what lets you do that magic at all, and it does what it says for free, so *fire magic* would do anything fire can. Write the magic you actually do, like *throw fire*.

### Fixing the usual ones {#fixing-the-usual-ones}

-   **Combat master** reads like a Title. As permissions it's things like *fight with a sword* and *fight two at once*.
-   **Fire magic** becomes *throw fire*, *feel every flame in a room* or *put a fire out with a word*.
-   **Lie to Vane's steward** becomes *lie to someone's face*. The steward's one man, and you'll be lying to plenty more.
-   **Deceptive** is one word for what kind of person you are. Build your Mind, and write what you actually do, like *lie to someone's face* (Mind).

### How they work {#how-permissions-work}

-   **It's got to fit inside its Title.** *Raised by the Guild* can have *know how a lock's built*, but not *throw fire*.
-   **It's got to fit its Root.** *Pick a mechanical lock* is Body, because it's your hands doing it. *Know how a lock's built* is Mind, because it's in your head.
-   **Magic needs a magic permission**, or a magic item. Without one you can't do magic at all. With one, it does what it says for free, and pushing it past that is [going beyond](#s-going-beyond), for 1 Aura.
-   **Took one you never use?** You're not stuck with it. In downtime you can swap any of your permissions for others that fit your Title, as long as it makes sense for your character, and your Roots change with it.

### The quick test {#the-quick-test}

1.  Is it something you can do, a doing word with how, what or where?
2.  Can you picture the moment you'd use it?
3.  Would it come up a few times a session?
4.  Does it fit inside your Title, and the Root you've put it on?

Four yeses and it's a permission.

## Step 4: Roots {#s-step-roots}

Now add them up. **Each Root is what your Titles put on it, and 1 for every permission on it.** Write your three Roots. Nothing else goes in.

That number does three jobs at once. It's **the steps you roll**, since a roll's one Root at full, so a Body of 9 is a d12 and a d6. It's **your health** there: every hit that gets through to it takes a point, and once it's got none left, the next one wounds it. And it's **what you spend** to bring it into a roll. So the stronger you get, the harder you are to put down.

:::rule
**A roll** is one Root, the one that fits how you're doing it, at full, whatever it's got left. **Bring another Root in** by naming the permission that justifies it and paying 1 point from that Root, or 3 Aura, and all of it goes on the roll too (see [Bringing In a Root](#s-bring)). An awakening's 2 steps on any roll it fits, and so is one [item](#s-items) that fits, as many steps as its tier.
:::

| Bram's | Where it comes from | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | 5 from his first Title, *hold the line*, *guard one person*, *lift what two men can't*, *fight in a shield wall* | 9 |
| Mind | 2 from his second Title, *spot who's in charge* | 3 |
| Soul | 3 from his second Title, *bark an order that gets obeyed* | 4 |

When the telegraphs land at his Body he rolls it, 9 steps, and his shield's a guard at tier 2, so getting out of the way is 11, a d12 and a d10. Swinging his war hammer is his Body, the hammer's tier 2 and *Ring the bell*'s 2 more, 13, two d12s and a d2. Barking an order is his Soul, 4 steps, a d8, so for now he's a better wall than he is a sergeant. When an order really has to land, he brings his Body in on *fight in a shield wall*, since a man who's held one knows exactly what to shout. That's a point of his Body, and 13 steps, two d12s and a d2.

:::example[Growing a Root]
Two Grows later, Bram's added *fight two at once* and *fight in tight spaces* to his first Title, both Body. His Body's 11 now, so a doorway with two guards coming is a d12 and a d10, and with his war hammer and *Ring the bell* it's 15, two d12s and a d6. That's 2 more points to lose before a hit can wound him, and 2 more to spend.
:::

## Step 5: Items {#s-items}

An **item** is real gear you've got equipped: a weapon, a shield, armour, or a tool good enough to matter, like a grappling hook, a set of picks, a spyglass or a focus. **You carry six at most.** Each one's **a point**, and it comes at **tier 2**. One more point puts it up a tier, and tier 3's the most you start with. What you attune is one of your items too, and it counts among your six. Your **keepsake** is free and on top of the six. The small stuff, like rope, a lantern or a smoke bomb, is a [Resource](#s-resources), and you say you've got it when you need it.

:::rule
**When an item fits what you're doing, its tier's that many steps on top of your Root.** One item a roll, the one doing the work. A Root you bring in brings its Root and nothing else, never another item.
:::

Vesk picking a lock is her Body, 11 steps, and her set of picks at tier 3 is 3 more, so that's 14, two d12s and a d4. She can't put her knife on it as well, because it's one item a roll and the picks are doing the work. Say Bram's Body 9 and a tier 4 sword: that's 13 steps, two d12s and a d2, or any split he likes.

### Tiers {#s-item-tiers}

An item's **tier** is how many steps it adds, from 1 to 6, so tier 6 is a d12's worth. Each tier's got a name, and you can write it before the item. This is the one table the names come from.

| Tier | Name | Steps | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Plain | 1 | Rough or improvised: a chair leg, a borrowed bow. |
| 2 | Trusty | 2 | Good gear that does the job, and what an item comes at. |
| 3 | Proven | 3 | Tried and known for it, and the best you can start with. |
| 4 | Storied | 4 | Made by a master, and it costs real money. |
| 5 | Sung | 5 | Out of the story, with a material. |
| 6 | Mythic | 6 | Out of the story, with a material, and there's only one. |

How an item gets past tier 3 is in [Upgrading gear](#upgrading-gear): Grow in downtime takes one to tier 3, money buys tier 4, and tiers 5 and 6 only come out of the story. **Aura never buys a tier.** The tier's the thing itself, and your Aura goes on what it always has.

### Kinds of item {#s-item-kinds}

Every item's one kind, and its kind says what its tier does.

| Kind | What its tier does | For example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon | Its tier on a roll it fits, and it keeps its [properties](#s-weapon-props). | A sword · a longbow · a war hammer |
| Tool | Its tier on a roll it fits. | A set of picks · a grappling line · a spyglass · a focus, like an ember stone |
| Guard | Its tier only on a roll **avoiding harm**: the roll when the telegraphs land, a [Defend](#s-defend), or an answer that's only getting out of the way. Never anything else. | A shield · a buckler · a parrying dagger · a cloak that blurs you |
| Armour | No steps. It [soaks](#s-armour) its tier on its Root. | Worn leathers · plate · court clothes (Mind) · a holy symbol (Soul) |
| Pack | No steps. It gives you more [Resources](#s-resources), half its tier, rounded up. | A quartermaster's pack · a bandolier · a surgeon's roll |
| Vessel | No steps. It holds its tier in [charge](#s-vessels). | A lantern of captured light · a prayer string · a reliquary |

**Dodging uses Body.** Getting out of the way of a blow is your Body when it lands, at full, and a guard's tier on top. Bram's shield is a guard at tier 2, so when a Warden's spear lands at his Body he rolls 9 and 2, 11 steps, a d12 and a d10. Swinging his hammer with it on his arm is the hammer's tier and never the shield's.

### What you carry {#s-carrying}

:::rule
**Six items at most**, the ones you've attuned among them, each with its kind and tier. Your **keepsake**'s free and on top. Your **Resources** are 3, and you say them when you need them.
:::

-   **An attuned thing's one of your six.** It's still an item with its tier, and it can't break (see [Attunement](#s-attunement)).
-   **Picking something up** in play makes it one of your six, so if you've got six already, put one down first. Something you grab in the middle of a fight, like a chair leg or a bottle, is tier 1.
-   **Past six**, it stays behind, or with someone you trust.
-   **When something breaks**, it's one of your items, and it's crossed out till it's mended. Soaking never breaks armour, but the GM can name "something breaks" as a consequence. Attuned things can't break (see [Broken items](#recovering-armour)).

:::example[Wren's six]
Wren's got her longbow at tier 3, attuned, her hooded cloak at tier 3, a hunting knife at tier 3 and a snare kit at tier 2, which is 4 of her six. Her keepsake's a torn map, and it's on top. She's got no rations or rope on her sheet. When the trail runs into a cave and she wants a torch, her player says she's got one, and ticks one of her 3 Resources.

At the bottom of the cave there's a crossbow on a dead man. She takes it, and it's her fifth item.
:::

### What a written item looks like {#what-a-written-item-looks-like}

:::sheet
**Items:** a sword (weapon, tier 3) · a spear (weapon, tier 2, Reach, Thrown) · worn leathers (armour, Body, tier 2) · a buckler (guard, tier 2) · a quartermaster's pack (pack, tier 2) · the ring that was his (tool, tier 2, attuned) · 6 of 6.

**Keepsake:** A letter you never sent.

**Resources:** 4.

**Attuned (Awakened):** The ring that was his. **Awakenings:** *Bear the family seal* (Soul) · *Pass as a Vane* (Mind), for its Catch, *Marked*.
:::

That's all an item is: one line with its kind and its tier in brackets, a weapon's properties after them, and the Root a piece of armour soaks on. There aren't any hidden properties, and nothing else adds to a roll. The sword's tier 3, so it's 2 points, and the rest are a point each, bar the ring, which is the attuned thing's 5. The pack's tier 2, so it's 1 Resource more, and that's 4.

### Weapons and their properties {#s-weapon-props}

A weapon's tier is its steps on a roll it fits. Its **properties** say what it lets you do that bare hands can't. They're what make a spear a spear, and they come free with the weapon, at any tier.

| Property | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Reach** | Strike at 2 units, not just the square next to you. |
| **Thrown** | Throw it, across a room. Then it's on the floor until someone picks it up. |
| **Ranged** | Shoot it, across a room or down a street. |
| **Long** | Shoot it further than anything else: across a town square, or a field. |
| **Grapple** | Grab and hold with it, as far as it reaches. A win can put *Grabbed* on the target. |
| **Sweep** | One die can go at everyone within your reach. Each of them rolls its own defence. |
| **Piercing** | *Armoured* doesn't count as a tag keeping a threat going, whatever its multiplier, and even when it comes first. |
| **Breaker** | *Shield* doesn't count as a tag keeping a threat going, whatever its multiplier. Wooden doors, barricades and furniture go down in one win. |
| **Charge** | If you moved 3 units or more in a straight line this round, it adds a step to your first die. |
| **Concealed** | It hides on your body. Nobody finds it without searching hard. |
| **Quick** | Drawing it costs no action. |
| **Two-Handed** | It needs both hands. No shield or second weapon while you use it. |
| **Reload** | After it shoots, it takes an action to load again. |

| Weapon | Properties |
| --- | --- |
| Bare hands | Grapple, Quick |
| Dagger or knife | Thrown, Concealed, Quick |
| Throwing knives | Thrown, Concealed, Quick |
| Sword | None (it needs nothing: that's what it's for) |
| Rapier | Piercing |
| Stiletto | Piercing, Concealed |
| Hand axe | Breaker, Thrown |
| Mace, club or war hammer | Breaker |
| Great axe or maul | Breaker, Sweep, Two-Handed |
| Spear | Reach, Thrown |
| Polearm, glaive or halberd | Reach, Sweep, Two-Handed |
| Lance | Reach, Charge |
| Staff | Reach, Two-Handed |
| Whip | Reach, Grapple |
| Net | Grapple, Thrown |
| Sling | Ranged, Concealed |
| Shortbow | Ranged, Two-Handed |
| Longbow | Long, Two-Handed |
| Hand crossbow | Ranged, Piercing, Reload |
| Crossbow | Long, Piercing, Reload, Two-Handed |
| Improvised weapon | Whatever the fiction gives it. A chair leg is a club, and a broken bottle is a knife that isn't Concealed. It's tier 1. |

Bare hands aren't an item, so they're never steps. How far a bow or a thrown weapon reaches is the story's call, and the properties say what kind of distance it likes. Write them in the brackets after its tier. If your world has pistols or stranger things, build them the same way: a pistol is *Ranged, Piercing, Reload*, and everyone nearby hears it go off. There's more on using all of this in [Conflict](#s-weapons-tactics).

### Resources {#s-resources}

**Everyone's got 3 Resources.** A Resource is something ordinary you happen to have, and you say it the moment you need it: "as it happens, I've got rope on me", or "I've got a flask for this". You don't list them ahead of time. Say it, tick one, and it's real. It's yours from then on, the way anything ordinary is, and something you use up, like a smoke bomb, a flask or a bribe, is gone once it's used.

-   **What it can be:** anything ordinary you could have on you, or have sorted earlier, that fits who you are. It's never magic and never better than ordinary. The GM can say no to one that doesn't fit the scene: "you came straight from the bath house".
-   **It does what the thing does**, the way a permission does. Rope lets you climb down a well, and a lantern lets you see what's at the bottom. It's never steps and it never soaks, so a weapon or a tool good enough to add steps is an item.
-   **They all come back in downtime**, when you restock. The GM can give you one back in the middle of a session when the story restocks you, like a market, a quartermaster or a friend's cellar.
-   **More Resources** come from a [pack](#s-item-kinds), half its tier rounded up, and from the abilities that say so.
-   **A purse of gold** is a Resource, about a month's wages (see [Coin and Kit](#what-you-can-afford)).

These are *your* Resources. The crew's resources on the [party sheet](#s-party-resources) are something else, what the crew lives on and pays its assets with.

:::example[A smoke bomb at the table]
The corridor's filling up with Iron Wardens. Vesk's player says she's got a smoke bomb on her belt, and ticks one of her 3 Resources: a burst of smoke one square out, for one round.

She throws it and the GM marks the smoke on the map. Everything the Wardens aim into it comes in a step weaker. Bram drags Isolde out of the corridor and Vesk follows. The smoke bomb's gone, and she's got 2 Resources left till downtime.

Down in the vault she says she's got 50 feet of rope, and ticks another. The rope's hers now, so she's still got it on the way back up, and 1 Resource left.
:::

## Step 6: Attunement {#s-attunement}

When you **attune** something, it's bound to you, and it grows with you. It's always something personal: Vesk's grappling line, Bram's war hammer, Isolde's chalk and salt. An attuned thing's **5 points**, and it takes an attunement slot. What you attune starts at **Awakened**.

**What you attune is one of your items**, and it counts among your six. The 5 points are the thing itself at tier 2 and its first awakening, and a tier up's a point, to tier 3 at most when you make your character. Attuning it never changes its tier. It's magic, and it can't be broken.

-   **It comes with one awakening.** An **awakening** is something the thing does, written the way you'd write a permission, with the Root it fits after it: *Anywhere I can throw a hook* (Body). Give the thing its Catch and it gets a second awakening, free. That's all an Awakened thing holds, so the rest come as it grows (see [Three stages](#attunement-stages)).
-   **Each awakening locks a point of its Root** for as long as it's attuned. Vesk's Body is 11, and with her hook attuned she can hold 10 of it. A locked point isn't there to lose or to spend, but it never shrinks your dice, so she still rolls all 11.
-   **It's 2 steps on any roll it fits.** As you roll, **each Aura you spend on it is 2 more**. An attuned item that fits adds its tier and its awakening's steps, so Vesk over Lord Vane's roof is her Body's 11, her grappling line's tier 2 and its awakening's 2, 15 steps, and with 2 Aura on the awakening that's 19, three d12s and a d2.
-   **It does what it says, too.** Wren's *Hit anything I can see* lets her arrows reach anything she can see, the way a permission lets you do something, and it's 2 steps on those shots. Pushing it past what it says is [going beyond](#s-going-beyond), the same as anything else.

**It needs a point to lock.** If the Root it fits hasn't got one free, the awakening does nothing till it has. Attuned things don't touch your Aura, so your maximum's 5 however many you've got, bar a Burden that says otherwise. Aura goes on an awakening's steps, never on the thing's tier.

-   **Take the same awakening twice** and it locks two points, and it's 4 steps on a roll it fits.
-   **Only you can spend Aura on it**, or a [Maker](#a-maker) who can touch it.

Write each awakening the way you'd write a permission, as something the thing itself does, and give it the Root it fits. A grappling line climbs and swings, which is Body, and a ring of command makes people listen, which is Soul.

### Three stages {#attunement-stages}

An attuned thing grows in three stages. **Each new stage needs a material**, found in the story or bought, and **every new awakening takes a downtime slot**. Each stage can take on more, for a price:

| Stage | Awakenings | It can add | In all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awakened | 1 | 1 more with a Catch | 2 |
| Tempered | 2 | 2 more with a Burden (the Catch becomes the Burden) | 4 |
| Perfected | 3 | 3 more with a Burden and a Catch | 6 |

That's six awakenings at most, with one Catch and one Burden, and each one locks its point, so a thing that's grown a long way takes a lot of you with it. You can't take a Burden before Tempered, so there aren't any when you make your character. What's a material? Whatever makes sense for the thing, like steel from a vault door your picks have beaten.

A **Burden** is a permanent cost from the list in [Burdens](#s-boons), like *Bad Knee* (stride −2). It's always on, and it's worth two awakenings on that thing.

**You can hand an attuned thing to a friend** with a free attunement slot and room among their six items. Its tier, its awakenings, its Catch and its Burden go with it. Its points come unlocked on you, and its awakenings lock theirs instead.

If you're a [Summoner](#a-summoner), your summoning attunement holds souls, one to each awakening, on the same three stages, and it takes an attunement slot like anything else you attune (see [Summoning](#s-summoning)). A soul doesn't lock a point. Some attuned things come out of the story already Tempered or Perfected, and you'll find them, and how to write your own, in [Attuned things from the story](#s-rare).

### Catch ideas: 12 {#catches-12}

A **Catch** is trouble that comes with an attuned thing. It has to be something that can bring real trouble into a scene, something the GM can run with, and if it'd never come up, it isn't a Catch. You can let it bite as often as you like for 1 Aura, as long as it brings real trouble every time, and 1 Aura on your own roll keeps it quiet (see [Getting it back](#getting-it-back)). Letting it bite is always your call. When it bites in a big way, one that changes the scene or costs you something you wanted, you get a point of Soul back instead of the Aura (see [Catches and Trouble](#s-trouble)).

| \# | Catch | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Loud** | Everyone knows where you are. |
| 2 | **Heavy** | Your stride's 1 unit shorter for each thing you've got that's *Heavy*. |
| 3 | **Fragile** | It can't break, but when a consequence breaks one of your items, the GM can name this instead. It goes dormant (its awakenings don't work, though they still lock their points) until you mend it in downtime. |
| 4 | **Fussy** | It's good for 3 uses. Each awakening you use takes one, and a downtime slot of upkeep puts them back. |
| 5 | **Marked** | You can be identified by it from a distance. |
| 6 | **Cursed** | When you let it bite, it puts a condition on you, at a d6. Agree which condition when you attune it. |
| 7 | **Wanted** | Someone else wants it, and knows you have it. Let it bite and they come for it. |
| 8 | **Slow** | Its awakenings take a round to wake. Say you're readying it on one roll, and they work from your next. |
| 9 | **Conspicuous** | Can't be hidden. |
| 10 | **Bright** | Can't be used in darkness. |
| 11 | **Narrow** | Only useful in specific circumstances (aquatic, urban, indoors). |
| 12 | **Delicate** | A 1 on any die where it's used makes it dormant until the end of the scene. |

:::example[Wren's longbow]
Wren attunes her longbow when she makes her character, for 5 points, and puts it up to tier 3 for 1 more. It's Awakened with one awakening, *Long shot* (Body), 2 steps on every roll she makes with it, and it locks a point of her Body. She gives it its Catch now, *Wanted* (the Duke's foresters want it back, and they know she's got it), and that buys it a second awakening for nothing: *Hit anything I can see* (Body), so her arrows reach anything she can see, however far, and cover doesn't help it. That's another point of her Body locked, so she can hold 6 of its 8. Shooting from cover is her Body's 8, the bow's tier 3 and *Long shot*'s 2, 13 steps, two d12s and a d2, and at something a long way off *Hit anything I can see* is 2 more, 15, two d12s and a d6.

In the market at Thornby, Wren's player lets *Wanted* bite: one of the foresters knows the bow, and the GM has him follow her out of the market with two friends. Wren takes 1 Aura.

If she wants the bow Tempered one day, she'll need a material first, and the string off the Duke's own bow would do nicely. After that, every new awakening on it takes a downtime slot.
:::

## Step 7: Your numbers and bonds {#s-starting-numbers}

Your [Aura](#s-aura) maximum is 5, and you're full at the start of every scene. Aura's what you spend on the small stuff, like winning a tie or going beyond, and on the bigger things: bringing a Root into a roll for 3, your archetype's abilities, and more steps from your awakenings.

**Every Root starts full**, less the points your awakenings lock. Its points are your health there: each hit that gets through to it takes one, and when it's got none left, the next hit **wounds** it, *Bloodied* for your Body, *Frayed* for your Mind and *Hollow* for your Soul. When the telegraphs land, the Root they're at is what you roll to stop them. At the end of every scene each Root rolls, unsplit, and gets back what it rolls, and downtime puts everything back. It's all in [Wounds and Scars](#s-blaze) and [Healing](#s-healing).

Your stride is 3: that's how far you move each round without spending an action (see [Movement and Range](#s-movement)). Your armour soaks its tier on the Root it's on, and how that works is in [Armour and soak](#s-armour). You've got your 3 [Resources](#s-resources), and any vessel you've got starts empty.

### Bonds and threads {#s-bonds}

A **bond** is usually a person who matters to your character, in the crew or out of it. It can be a faction, a place or a cause too. Each **thread** is one truth between you, and it's one of two kinds. A **bond thread** is something good, like a rescue, a secret you keep for each other or a shared past. A **hostile thread** is something that's gone bad, like a debt you can't pay, a grudge or a betrayal. You can have both kinds with the same person, and as many threads as the story gives you. With no thread at all, you're neutral.

**Each person's one bond.** Write their name once, and every thread you've got with them goes under it. A second thread with Dorrie goes under Dorrie, not on a new line with her name on it again.

Any thread can be **strained**, which means it's nearly snapped. So a single thread is always one of these: a bond, a bond strained, a hostile thread strained, or hostile, and with no thread you're neutral.

**You start with two bond threads** with the crew, from your answers in [Connect](#s-connect), and they don't cost any points. Write a hostile thread as well, with anyone, and you've got room for a third bond thread. Three bond threads is the most you start with. With two players, one of your two bond threads goes to someone outside the crew, and playing alone, both do.

Every thread's a hook the GM can pull, and a bond thread's something you can lean on, too:

-   **Borrow through a bond thread.** 1 Aura borrows one thing off their sheet for your roll, and it does there what it does for them, so one of their permissions can justify bringing a Root of yours in, which you still pay for. It doesn't strain anything. Through a faction, it's one of its assets. A place or a cause has nothing to lend, but you can still strain a thread with it. You can't borrow through a strained thread until it's mended.
-   **Strain a bond thread.** Lean on them in the story, like calling in the favour, using their name, dragging them into it or using the secret, and you get **a point of Soul back**. It's free, it doesn't take an action, and the thread's now **strained**.
-   **Mend it.** Make it right with them, by roleplaying it, doing them a favour or paying them, whatever suits them. That happens in downtime or in a scene with them, not in the middle of the fight where you strained it. Then it's whole again.
-   **Strain it again before it's mended and it snaps.** You still get the point of Soul, but cross the thread out. When the last thread goes, so does the bond.

**A hostile thread bites like a Catch.** Let it bring you real trouble and take 1 Aura, or a point of Soul for a big bite, one that changes the scene or costs you something you wanted (see [Catches and Trouble](#s-trouble)). You can't borrow through it, and you can't strain it for Soul. Doing right by them strains it instead, and doing right by them again snaps it, and the grudge is over. Wrong them again before it snaps, and it's whole again.

:::example[Vesk's threads]
*Dorrie: we have pulled a dozen jobs. I trust her with my life.* (bond)

*Bram: he once carried me out of a fire. I have never said thank you.* (bond)

*The fence: I owe a debt I would rather not discuss.* (hostile)

The first two come from her answers in Connect. The fence is outside the crew, and writing a hostile thread with him gives her room for a third bond thread.
:::

**A thread with someone in the crew goes on both your sheets**, and it counts for both of you, so agree on it together. It stays in the words of whoever wrote it, and it's theirs. If either of you strains it, it's strained for both. A thread with a faction, or with someone outside the crew, only goes one way: it's yours, and theirs toward you is a different thread.

New threads come from [Grow](#grow) in downtime, and when the crew's [Party Goal](#s-goal) is done, everyone writes one to someone the Goal brought into their life.

:::example[Straining a thread]
Dorrie's in the courtyard at Vane's with her Soul down to 2, and the steward's started asking who invited her. She signals up to Vesk on the roof to drop a slate into the next yard and pull him off, and Vesk does, so now Vesk's the one with the whole household looking for her. That's leaning on their thread, *We have pulled a dozen jobs. I trust her with my life.* Dorrie gets a point of Soul back, so it's 3, and the thread's strained on both their sheets. After the job she gives Vesk the pick of the haul, and it's mended.

Vesk's thread with the fence, *I owe a debt I would rather not discuss*, is hostile, so she can't lean on it for Soul. She brings the fence a cut of the haul anyway. That's doing right by him, so the thread's strained, and one more good turn will snap it and clear the debt.
:::

### Playing the strain {#s-playing-the-strain}

This is for when someone in the crew strains a thread with your character, so it stays honest both ways.

The thread gives the reason. The strain's about that one truth, so nobody has to invent a grudge.

| The thread | Strained, it sounds like | It's settled when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A rescue (bond) | "I can't keep pulling you out." | you save them, or finally say thank you, and it's mended |
| A secret (bond) | "You swore you'd keep that." | you trust them with one of yours, and it's mended |
| A shared past (bond) | "That's not who we are any more." | you go back to it together, and it's mended |
| A debt you owe (hostile) | "Don't think this makes us square." | you pay them the rest, and it snaps |

Say how it shows, and what would make it right. Play that and nothing bigger, and once they've made it right, let it go.

Play a strained bond thread by what's left between you. With two or more bond threads still whole, it's a grumble and a "you owe me". With one, it's a real problem and you'll say so. With none, you're cold until they make it right, and one more pull snaps it.

A hostile thread goes the other way. It's strained because they've done right by you, so let it soften you a bit, and if they do it again, it snaps and whatever was between you is over.

It never stops you working together in the moment. You still combo and still intercept for each other.

:::example[Vesk and Bram]
Vesk's bond thread with Bram is *He once carried me out of a fire. I have never said thank you.* She's halfway up a wall with the watch coming and yells for him to haul her up again, so she strains it. Her Soul's 0, so there's no point for her to get back, but she needs him, and that's what a strain's for. Bram's player says he's starting to feel like her ladder, and she can fix it by saying thank you. It was their only thread, so none are whole now, and he's cold with her until she makes it right. At the bar afterwards she finally says it. The thread's mended, and she rewrites it now that it's said.
:::

## Step 8: Connect {#s-connect}

Before you play, tie the crew together. Go round the table with these ten questions. Everyone answers **at least two**, and names another player's character in each answer. If the table's enjoying it, answer all ten.

1.  Who in the crew do you already know, and how?
2.  Who do you have unfinished business with?
3.  Who in the crew do you trust most, and why?
4.  Who owes you, and what do they owe?
5.  Who saved your life, or nearly got you killed?
6.  Which crewmate do you know a secret about?
7.  Who do you worry about when things go wrong?
8.  Who would you never want to let down?
9.  What job did you pull together that went badly?
10. Why are you still with this crew?

Your answers are where your threads come from. Turn two of them into your **bond threads**, and an answer that's gone bad, like business you never finished or a job that went wrong, can be your **hostile thread**. A thread with someone in the crew goes on both your sheets, so agree on it together (see [Bonds and threads](#s-bonds)). The GM uses the rest of your answers to put you in each other's way.

:::example[Bram's answers]
Bram answers three. He already knows Isolde, because he's guarded her since she was twelve, and that becomes his first bond thread: *I swore to her father I would keep her alive.* He trusts Wren most, because they held the same wall in the border war, and that's his second: *We held the same wall in the border war.* His unfinished business is with Dorrie. The harbour job went wrong and she walked away clean, so that one's gone bad, and it's his hostile thread: *I do not trust her, and she knows it.*
:::

## Step 9: Aspiration {#s-aspiration}

Your **Aspiration** is one thing you want, and it takes **5 points** to get there. There's no roll for it. You put the points in yourself, so you get it when you've decided it's worth the time.

-   **Put a point in.** Every downtime slot you spend on it is a point in it, the same way a slot buys a permission or an ability. Both your slots can go on it, and the crew's action can put a point in anyone's.
-   **A setback** takes a point back off it, never below 0.

When it's got all 5, it **comes good**: you get what you were after, and you take **a new Title**, **a new archetype** or **a new attuned thing**, your pick, with the permission, ability or awakening it comes with. That's what 5 points buy when you make a character, so the points you put in are what you get back. Then write a new Aspiration, with nothing in it yet.

Your Aspiration is also somewhere a consequence can land. When a blow or a failure costs you, you can take a point off your Aspiration instead of taking it on your Roots or your gear, so the fight costs you your dream instead of your skin. You can't do it while it's got nothing in it.

The crew's [Party Goal](#s-goal) is different. It's a die, and it climbs with rolls.

:::example[Vesk's Aspiration, session by session]
**Session 1:** *Own the score of a lifetime*, with nothing in it yet.

**Downtime:** she puts a slot in it, so it's 1 of 5.

**Session 2:** She gets run out of a warehouse. That's a setback, so it's back to nothing.

**Sessions 3 to 6:** She puts a slot in it most downtimes, and the other one goes on mending whatever the last job broke. By session 6 it's got 4.

**Session 7:** She robs the Vane Estate, and in the downtime after, her slot's its fifth point. It comes good. Vesk takes a third Title, *The woman who has never been seen*, with its 5 for her Roots and one permission. Her new Aspiration, *Find out who the fence really is*, starts with nothing in it.
:::

### A good Aspiration {#s-good-aspiration}

When your Aspiration comes good, you get what you wrote, so make it something you could have in an arc or two. Vesk's first one took her seven sessions. A good one passes all four of these:

-   **It's one thing.** Wren has to find the thing before she can put it down, and Ash will either burn the Ember Court down or take it back, but each of them is after one thing. If you want two things, write the first, and the other can be your next Aspiration.
-   **You'd know the day it's done.** There's a moment you could point at, like Vesk walking out of the vault or Bram getting his rank back. *Be rich* hasn't got one, so when it comes good, nobody knows what you've got.
-   **It's about someone or something in the world.** That's what lets the GM put it within reach. Dorrie's *Get a seat at the table that ruined my family* points at the people who did it, so the GM knows who to put in her way.
-   **The story goes on after it.** You get it, and then everyone else gets to react. *Never get caught again* would mean nothing could ever catch Vesk, so leave out words like *never* and *can't*.

Each of these has a version that's too big or too vague, then what the crew wrote:

-   *Be the best thief there's ever been* has no day it's done. *Own the score of a lifetime* is one job, and Vesk will know it when she's pulled it.
-   *Be a hero again* could mean anything. *Earn back the rank they stripped from me* says what Bram's after and who has to give it back.
-   *Bring my order back* is a whole campaign. *Find out who burned my order's library* is where Isolde starts.
-   *Kill the thing that took my brother and make sure it never happens again* is two things, and the second one never ends. *Find the thing that took my brother, and put it down* keeps the first.

If yours is too big or too vague, the GM says so, and you work it out together before it goes on your sheet. Usually that means keeping the first piece of it, and the rest makes a good next Aspiration for when this one comes good.

## Armour and soak {#s-armour}

Every piece of armour is an item that sits on one of your Roots, and it soaks hits for you there.

-   **Body armour** is worn leathers, a chain shirt, plate or a helmet. It's Body unless it says otherwise.
-   **Mind armour** is what keeps your head straight, and it counts while it fits the scene: court clothes at court, a guard's uniform in the barracks, a disguise you trust.
-   **Soul armour** is what holds you together, like a holy symbol, a ward stitched into your coat or a relic of your order.

A shield's a [guard](#s-item-kinds), so it's steps when you're getting out of the way, and it doesn't soak.

:::rule
**A hit takes its points from the armour on that Root first**, a point of soak for each point, then from the Root. It's extra health you're wearing, and it's all back at the end of every scene.
:::

### How much it soaks {#s-armour-quality}

**Armour soaks as many points as its tier**, every scene. Worn leathers at tier 2 soak 2, and plate at tier 3 soaks 3. Wear two pieces on the same Root and they both soak, one after the other.

-   **2 for 1.** A hit you take on another Root is 2 points there, and that Root's armour soaks them first.
-   **More hits.** A [vulnerability](#s-vulnerable)'s hit, or a deadly threat's 2 hits or more, are a point each, so each takes a point of soak.
-   **It's back in full at the end of every scene.** Soaking never breaks armour. The GM can still name "something breaks" as a consequence instead of a hit, and then it's crossed out till it's mended.
-   **Wounds and Scars** only come once a Root's soak and its points are both gone.
-   **Attuned armour** soaks like any armour, and it can't break.

:::example[Armour at the table]
Bram's wearing his plate at tier 3 and a helmet at tier 2, both on his Body, so he's got 5 to soak before a hit takes a point of the 7 his Body holds.

Round 1: a Warden's spear gets through. His plate soaks it, so it's got 2 left, and his Body's still 7.

Round 3: the Warden captain's deadly, and her blade gets through for 2 hits. That's 2 points, and the plate soaks both. It's got nothing left this scene, so the helmet's next.

Round 4: the captain's sneer gets through at his Mind, and he'd rather not lose a point of the 3 he's got there, so he takes it on his Body instead. That's 2 points on his Body, and the helmet soaks both, so his Body's still 7. The next one that gets through takes a point.

When the scene ends, the plate's 3 and the helmet's 2 are back.
:::

### Broken items {#recovering-armour}

An item only breaks when the GM names it as a consequence: the bowstring snaps, the shield splits, the picks bend in the lock. It's crossed out, so it's no use to you till it's mended, and mending it in downtime takes a slot and usually a purse of gold (see [Mend](#recover)). Attuned things can't break.

## Vessels {#s-vessels}

A **vessel** is an item that holds charge, like a lantern of captured light, a prayer string, a battery-coil or a reliquary. **It holds as much charge as its tier**, so a tier 3 vessel holds 3, and it keeps what's in it between scenes and through downtime.

-   **Fill it with Aura you'd lose.** That's the Aura past your maximum when you finish acting, and Aura that comes in while you're not acting and would stop at your maximum, if you'd rather keep it than give it to a friend. Any of it can go in instead. The Aura you've got left when a scene ends isn't lost, since you start the next one full, so it stays out.
-   **Or fill it with points.** Any time in a scene, with no action, a point from any Root is a charge. That's pushing, so it can leave you exposed, and it never wounds you. You can't do it at a scene's end or in downtime.
-   **A point in a vessel stays held there** till its charge is spent, the way an awakening locks one. The Root can't get it back at a scene's end or in downtime while it's in the vessel. When you spend charge, the points go first, and a point spent is just a point spent, which comes back the usual way.
-   **Spend it only on your own** abilities and your own awakenings' extra steps, a charge for each Aura they'd cost, and never on a signature at 5.
-   **It isn't Aura.** It never counts towards your maximum, you can't hand it to a friend, and it never pays for bringing in a Root, a tie, an intercept, a quick heal, going beyond or borrowing.

It only takes Aura you'd have lost, it never turns back into Aura, and it can't hold more than its tier, so once it's full, the Aura past your maximum goes the way it always did.

:::example[Ash's coal horn]
Ash carries a coal horn at tier 3, a cow's horn of banked coals, so it holds 3. She's on 3 Aura when she throws fire at the Iron Wardens, and her dice pay her 4 while she's acting, so she's on 7. When she's finished acting the 2 past her 5 would go, so they go in the horn instead, and it's 2 of 3.

Two rounds later she's got the captain cornered. She puts a point of her Soul in, no action, and the horn's full. That point's held in the horn, so her Soul's maximum is 9 till it's spent, with the point her ember stone locks. Throwing fire is her Soul's 11, her ember stone's tier 2 and *Fire without a word*'s 2, 15 steps, and she spends 2 charge on the awakening for 4 more, 19, three d12s and a d2. Her point of Soul goes first, so her Soul's back to a maximum of 10, and it'll come back at the scene's end the usual way. She's still got 1 charge for her Push.
:::

## Burdens {#s-boons}

A **Burden** is a permanent cost, and it only ever comes on an attuned thing. Titles never take them. A Burden's worth two awakenings on that thing, and a thing can't take one before it's Tempered (see [Attunement](#s-attunement)).

-   **Give them names from the story:** *Bad Knee* (stride −2), *Others First* (each Root gets 2 less back at the end of a scene).
-   **A Burden is always on**, and it's got to cost you something you actually use. A Burden on something you haven't got, like locking a point of a Root that's 0, doesn't count.

| Lever | Burden |
| --- | --- |
| Toughness | a point of one of your Roots is locked, the way a Scar locks it |
| Recovery | each Root gets 2 less back at the end of a scene |
| Aura | −2 to your maximum |
| Stride | −2 |
| Getting around | difficult ground costs you 3 a square |
| Acting | you say your action before the GM telegraphs, and you're stuck with it |
| Ties | you lose every tie, even defending, and can't pay to win one |
| Armour | your armour soaks 1 less, each piece |
| Conditions | one condition doesn't fade on you, and stays until someone breaks it with an action |
| Defend | you can't Defend as your action |
| Intercept | you can't intercept |
| Kit | you carry five items, not six |
| Resources | you've got one fewer Resource |

Nothing trades a permission for something that's always on. What's always true of you comes from your archetypes' Traits, and the rest is what you can do. For the Defend and Intercept levers, see [Defend](#s-defend) and [Intercept](#s-intercept).

:::example[A Burden for two awakenings]
Say Bram's war hammer is Tempered one day, after a material from the harbour gate. He wants two more awakenings on it, so it takes a Burden: *Weighs on Him*, a point of his Soul locked the way a Scar locks it. His Soul's 4, so he'll feel it every time he barks an order.
:::

## A Complete Character, Start to Finish {#a-complete-character-start-to-finish}

:::quote
Here's what it looks like to build a character, and every choice here is a real one.
:::

### Step 1: Titles {#step-1-title}

The player wants a thief called Vesk, and he's got 35 points to build her with. For her first Title he starts to write *Thief*. The GM pushes back: "The best at what? A thief who does what?" He has a think and comes up with *The best cat burglar in the city.* It's all hands and feet, so all 5 go on her Body.

For the second Title he asks himself where she learned it all, and writes *Raised by the Guild*. It's a real role, and it hands the GM a whole Guild to play with. It's mostly what she knows, so it's Mind 3, and Body 2 for the knife work they taught her. Two Titles are 10 points, so he's got 25 left.

### Step 2: Archetypes {#step-2-archetypes}

He picks the Shadow. When she gets into hiding, the dice she hid with go into her shadow pool, the things she noticed while nobody was looking, and she can add them to any roll after that. He takes it twice, for 10 points, and each one comes with an ability: Push, +2 on her roll to hide or on a shadow die for 1 Aura, and Vanish, hidden again with no roll for 1 Aura. She's taken Shadow twice, so her Push is +3, and she's got the Shadow's Trait, *Any Shadow Will Do*. That leaves 15.

### Step 3: Permissions {#step-3-permissions}

Each Title came with a permission. He writes *hide in Vane's wine cellar* first, and the GM points at the size table: that'd come up once a campaign. *Move unseen* is about right, so that's her first Title's, and *know how a lock's built* is her second's. Then he buys four more, a point each, so she's got three on each Title:

-   *The best cat burglar in the city:* *move unseen* (Body), *run the rooftops* (Body) and *pick a mechanical lock* (Body).
-   *Raised by the Guild:* *know how a lock's built* (Mind), *fight dirty with a knife* (Body) and *find a buyer for anything* (Mind).

Every one passes the quick test. *Know how a lock's built* is all about locks, but it's the kind of thing you learn growing up in the Guild, so it fits inside her second Title, and it's in her head, so it's her Mind. That's 11 left.

### Step 4: Roots {#step-5-roots}

He adds them up:

| Vesk's | Steps |
| --- | --- |
| Body | 11: 5 from her first Title, 2 from her second, and four permissions |
| Mind | 5: 3 from her second Title, and two permissions |
| Soul | 0 |

So sneaking across a roof is her Body, 11 steps, a d12 and a d10. When she gets into hiding, those dice go in her shadow pool. Her Soul's 0, so a roll on it is a d2, and anything that gets through to her Soul wounds her straight away, unless she takes it on her Body for 2 points. The player's happy with that. She's a loner.

### Step 5: Her items {#step-4-items-three}

Every item's a point, and it comes at tier 2. A point more puts it up to tier 3.

-   **Worn leathers**, armour on her Body at tier 2, so they soak 2. That's a point.
-   **A knife**, a weapon at tier 2, for a point.
-   **Soft-soled boots**, a tool at tier 3, for 2 points. Sneaking across a roof in them is her Body and the boots, 14 steps, two d12s and a d4.
-   **A set of picks**, a tool at tier 3, for 2 points. Picking a lock with them is 14 too.
-   **Keepsake:** the first coin she ever stole, free.

That's 4 of her six items and 6 points, so she's got 5 left. She hasn't written down a purse of gold or a smoke bomb. They're the sort of thing she'll say she's got when she needs them, with her 3 Resources.

### Step 6: Attunement {#step-6-attunement}

5 points is one attuned thing, and he picks her grappling line. It's a tool at tier 2, and it's her fifth item. It starts at Awakened, with one awakening, *Anywhere I can throw a hook*. It's Body, so it locks a point of her Body, and she can hold 10 of its 11. It's 2 steps on every roll she makes with the line, and 2 more for each Aura she spends on it.

So over Lord Vane's roof it all comes together: her Body's 11, the line's tier 2 and her hook's 2, 15 steps, two d12s and a d6, and with 2 Aura on the hook it's 19, three d12s and a d2.

He could give the line its Catch now for a second awakening, free, but there's no trouble he wants on it yet, so he leaves it. It can't be broken. That's all 35 points spent, and she's got room for one more item when she picks something up.

### Step 7: Numbers and bonds {#step-6-bonds}

She starts with the usual numbers: Aura 5, 3 Resources, and every Root full but the point her hook locks, so that's 10 on her Body and 5 on her Mind, and her Soul's 0. Her stride's 3, and her worn leathers soak 2 on her Body every scene, so a hit there takes their soak before it takes a point.

Her threads cost nothing, and they come from her answers to the crew questions, so he writes them in the next step.

### Step 8: Connect {#step-7-connect}

He answers two of the crew questions:

-   *Who in the crew do you already know, and how?* Dorrie. We have pulled a dozen jobs together.
-   *Who do you have unfinished business with?* Bram. He carried me out of a fire and I never thanked him.

Now she's got a reason to be in the crew, and both answers become her bond threads. Then he writes a hostile thread with someone outside the crew, the fence she owes, and that gives her room for a third bond thread when he thinks of one.

-   *Dorrie: we have pulled a dozen jobs. I trust her with my life.* (bond)
-   *Bram: he once carried me out of a fire. I have never said thank you.* (bond)
-   *The fence: I owe a debt I would rather not discuss.* (hostile)

Her threads with Dorrie and Bram go on their sheets as well, in her words. The rest of the crew write theirs, and three of them are with Vesk, so they go on her sheet too, in their own words:

-   *Dorrie: we came up on the same streets. She never asks why.* (Dorrie's, bond)
-   *Isolde: she stole the book that started all this.* (Isolde's, hostile)
-   *Wren: I owe her for the night she hid me from the watch.* (Wren's, bond)

That's six threads on five bonds. Both of Dorrie's go under her name, on the one bond, and Isolde's is hostile.

### Step 9: Aspiration {#step-8-aspiration}

*Own the score of a lifetime.* It starts with nothing in it.
