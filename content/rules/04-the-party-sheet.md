:::quote
Everything the crew wants, owns and owes, out on the table where everyone can see it.
:::

The party sheet is two A4 sheets, printed double-sided. The front of each one is a tile of the **faction board**, and the backs are the **crew page** and the **downtime page**. Lay the tiles side by side in the middle of the table. Any edge joins any edge: two tiles make an A3 board, four make A2, and you can keep going.

Each tile has six seats, one for every faction that matters, with room for its dice, its agendas, its assets and resources, its face, its secret once you find it, and its threads. There aren't any lines printed between the seats. The crew draws its own, between any seat and any other, because the crew decides who's tied to who. If you need more seats, print the **Faction Sheet**, which is the board on one page, as many times as you like.

Here's what lives on it:

-   **The crew.** Its name, its Reputations, its Power, resources and assets, and the people it relies on, with how it grows and what an extra resource can buy.
-   **The Party Goal**, and any **projects** on the go.
-   **Aspirations.** Every player's, where everyone can see them.
-   **The faction board.** Every power that matters: its dice, its agendas, and its threads toward the crew.
-   **What has changed.** One line for every agenda that happened.
-   **Party holdings.** Things the crew owns together.
-   **Crew assets.** All twelve, so the table can pick one when the crew grows.

The downtime page has the order downtime runs in, how downtime rolls work, the crew's moves on a faction, the faction turn and its d6, and what happens when a faction comes for the crew, so you don't have to look them up.

## Things That Climb {#s-climb}

Most dice in Zi'Dara count down. A scene clock, a stake clock, an event, Supplies and the alarm all step down, and past d2, whatever they were counting to happens. Four things on the party sheet count up instead: the **Goal**, every **Aspiration**, every **project** and every faction's **agenda**. They start low and climb as someone works at them, so the first steps come easy and the last ones are hard work.

:::rule
**Roll against its die. Beat it and it climbs one. Past d12, it's done.** One roll moves it one step at most, so more dice on it is advantage, and your best die counts. A setback steps it down one, but never below d2.
:::

-   **In a scene**, an action aimed at it rolls against its die, when the GM says it's within reach. That's usually once or twice a session, when a scene's really about it.
-   **In downtime**, the crew's action or a personal slot spent on it is one roll.
-   **Whoever does it builds the roll.** In a scene it's an action like any other, built from the set action that fits. In downtime it's 1 step for the crew's action or the slot, plus everything on their sheet that fits what they're doing about it.
-   **Ties go to the die you're climbing.** In a scene you can pay 1 Aura to win one, the same as always. In downtime there's no Aura, so see [Rolling in downtime](#s-downtime-rolls).

## The Party Goal {#s-goal}

The Goal is what the crew's after this arc, like *clear Dorrie's name*, *free the Bound* or *own the Low Market*. It starts at **d2** and climbs.

-   **It climbs** when someone chases it and wins: an action in a scene when the GM says the Goal's within reach, or in downtime, the crew's action or a personal slot someone puts toward it.
-   **It steps back down** one when a consequence lands on it, or when the crew takes a real setback. It never goes below d2.
-   **When it climbs past d12, the crew's done it.** Play the moment out. Then the crew grows (see [How the crew grows](#s-crew-grows)), everyone writes one new [thread](#s-bonds), to someone the Goal brought into their life, and the table writes the next Goal, at d2.

:::example[Clearing Dorrie's name]
**Session 1.** The crew corners the forger who's been signing Dorrie's hand on letters, and the GM says it out loud: the Goal's within reach. The proof's locked in his desk, so Vesk goes at it with her Locks: her first Title, *pick a mechanical lock*, *know how a lock's built* and her picks, with the action 5 steps, a d10. She rolls 6 against the Goal's d2, which rolls a 2, and it climbs to d4.

**Downtime.** The crew's action goes on getting the proof in front of the right magistrate, and Dorrie's plainly the right person for it, so she rolls her own permissions. That's 1 step for the action, plus what fits: her first Title, *talk my way past a door* and *read a room in a glance*. She's on 4 steps, a d8, against the d4. It's 3 against 3, and the tie goes to the Goal. She spends 1 Resolve to roll again and gets a 7, so it's d6.

**Sessions 2 to 5.** It climbs slower as it goes, and a bad night at the magistrate's knocks it back a step. By session 5 it's at d12, and the last roll is Dorrie standing up in court. She wins it, the Goal climbs past d12, and the crew's done it.
:::

## Projects {#s-projects}

A **project** is anything the crew, or one of them, sets out to build or bring about over time: a ring of informants in the Low Market, a forged charter, a ship refitted, the salt carters turned against the Vane Estate. Write one line for what it is, and one for what happens when it's done.

-   **It starts at d2**, like the Goal. If the crew's already partway there, start it higher: a d6, or a d10 when one more push would do it.
-   **Work it the same way.** An action that chases it when it's within reach, or in downtime the crew's action or a personal slot, rolls against its die.
-   **Past d12, it's done**, and what you wrote happens.
-   **Keep two or three on the go**, at most. Any more and none of them gets anywhere.

Projects are how a small crew takes on a big faction. You can't go straight at the Vane Estate's Power without the crew's action and a personal slot on the same move (see [Factions](#s-faction-crew)), but you can start *Turn the salt carters against the Estate*, and write that when it's done, the Estate loses its *Salt Contracts*. The crew's action can work it, and so can anyone's personal slot, and every roll at it is one the Estate doesn't see coming.

## The Crew {#s-party-faction}

The crew's a faction too, on the same card as the others. It starts small, and it grows the way a character does.

:::sheet[A new crew]
**Name** Whatever it gets called, once it gets called something.

**Reputations** None yet.

**Power** d4: 1 step, plus *Four or five of us and a back room*.

**Resources** *Odd jobs*.

**Assets** *Four or five of us and a back room* (+1 Power).

**Holdings** Whatever it starts the campaign with.

**People it relies on** The fence, the landlord, the cousin with a boat.
:::

-   **Power** is muscle, reach, and how many people turn up when the crew calls. It's 1 step, plus 1 for each asset that adds Power, so a new crew's a d4. It's the die the crew rolls when it moves on a faction, plus a step for every Reputation and bond thread that fits the roll, and its people are threats with a Challenge to match: a d6 Power sends Challenge 3.
-   **Resources** are permissions for where the crew's money comes from, one line each, like *Odd jobs*. Every asset needs one paying for it, every downtime, and one that isn't paying for anything is an extra resource (see [Extra Resources](#s-crew-wealth)). A purse of gold is still each character's own, a utility item that's worth about a month's wages.
-   **Assets** are permissions for what the crew can do, one line each, and each one says exactly what it does. An asset that's people who'll turn up when the crew calls, like *Muscle* or *The Dock Lads*, adds Power. One with nothing paying for it stops counting till something does. There are twelve below, or write your own in the same shape.
-   **Reputations** are what the city says about the crew, one line each: *The ones who beat the forger's court.* A Reputation works like a Title for the crew. It's a step on any crew roll it fits, and the crew earns one every time a Goal's done, and keeps them all.
-   **Holdings** are the big things the crew owns together (see [Party Holdings](#s-party-resources)).

A new crew's one resource pays for its one asset, the four or five of them and their back room, so there's no extra resource yet.

The crew doesn't need the three faction [bonds](#s-faction-bonds): its people are the characters, and its Reputations do the job of Public Sentiment. Its threads with factions go on the faction board. The rest works the same as any faction's (see [Resources and Assets](#s-wealth)), and its moves on the factions are in [Factions](#s-faction-crew).

### How big it is {#s-crew-size}

The die's 1 step, plus the assets that add Power, so a crew with *Muscle* as well as its back room is a d6.

| Power | What the crew is |
| --- | --- |
| d4 | A crew with no name. Four or five people and a back room. |
| d6 | A name, and fifty hands who'll come when they're called. |
| d8 | A guild, or something like one: a building, people on the books, a seat at some tables. |
| d10 | A power in the city. Other factions plan around it. |
| d12 | One of the great powers. |

### Crew Assets {#s-crew-tags}

Here are twelve to pick from. There are plenty more in [Appendix B](#faction-strength), for the crew or any faction, or you can write your own in the same shape. Each one needs a resource paying for it, like any asset, and does exactly what it says.

| Asset | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Informants** | Once a downtime, ask the GM one question about a faction, and get a straight answer. |
| **Muscle** | +1 Power, and once a session the crew's people join in for one scene, with as many steps as half its Power die (a d6 is 3). |
| **Safehouses** | Once a downtime, one of the crew lies low without spending a slot. |
| **Fences** | Once a downtime, one of the crew gets something through them without spending a slot. |
| **Friends at Court** | A step on any crew roll to Influence a faction's face. |
| **Influential** | A step on any crew roll at a faction whose threads toward the crew aren't mostly hostile. |
| **Rooted** | Once a session, anyone in the crew being chased through the crew's own streets gets away, with no roll. |
| **A Ship** | The crew's own ship. A voyage on it needs no Supplies clock, and while nothing's paying for it, it stays tied up in harbour. |
| **Smugglers' Roads** | A journey on the crew's own roads starts with its first stage already behind it. |
| **A Workshop** | Once a downtime, mend a broken item without spending a slot. |
| **Healers** | Once a session, between two scenes, everyone in the crew steps their wound die down a size. |
| **Old Debts** | Once a session, someone the crew once helped turns up and does one thing for them. |

### Extra Resources {#s-crew-wealth}

An **extra resource** is one that isn't paying for an asset. It's spent each downtime, not saved, so each one buys one of these between one downtime and the next:

-   **A new asset of the crew's own.** It takes the crew's action to build (that's Establish), and the extra resource pays for it from then on.
-   **Another faction's action.** Pay a faction and it spends its one action that downtime where the crew says, rolled with its own dice. A faction that's hostile to the crew won't take its money, and the money buys the action, not the win.
-   **An asset or a resource** from a faction that'll sell, which means one that isn't hostile to the crew. It changes hands, and an asset needs a resource paying for it from then on.
-   **A tie** won on a downtime roll (see [Rolling in downtime](#s-downtime-rolls)).
-   **Anything too big for a purse**, in a session: passage for twenty, a house, a magistrate's goodwill. When the money's what does it, it counts as a permission on the roll.

It can't buy the crew another action. The crew gets one a downtime, like every faction, and nothing buys any faction more than that.

### How the crew grows {#s-crew-grows}

-   **When the Goal's done**, the crew earns a new Reputation and picks **two** of these: a new resource, a new asset (it'll need something to pay for it), or a new holding.
-   **When it takes something big** (a building, a ship, a street) **or a faction it's been fighting falls**, it picks one.
-   **A big job can pay a resource.** When it will, the GM says so when she offers it.
-   **It shrinks** when a faction takes or destroys its assets or resources.
-   **Rebuild** with the crew's action. That's Establish, and it brings back an asset or a resource the crew's lost. An asset still needs something paying for it.
-   **A brand-new resource** comes from the Goal, a big job or a project, or off a faction, by Acquiring it or buying it with an extra resource. Establish only builds back one the crew's lost.
-   **Its action is its turn.** The crew doesn't take a faction turn on top of its one action a downtime.

There's a whole campaign of it, from a crew with no name to a power in the Low Market, in [The Crew, Session 1 to 20](#s-crew-1-20).

## Party Holdings {#s-party-resources}

Big things, like a horse, a cart, a small boat or a house, are **party holdings** rather than items. The story decides whether you've got access to them, and the crew keeps track of them together. A holding counts as a line on any roll it fits for whoever's using it: a fast horse in a chase, or a safehouse when the crew needs to disappear. It isn't on anyone's set action, so bringing it into a roll costs 1 Aura, the same as bringing in the ground. A holding isn't an asset, so nothing has to pay for it.

:::example
In session 3 the crew steals a horse and cart from a Vane warehouse. It goes on the party sheet as a holding: *"A horse and cart."*

In session 6 they leave it in a stable and forget all about it. In session 9 they come back to the city and need to get out fast. The horse and cart are still on the party sheet, and nothing's happened to them, so they're right where the crew left them.
:::
