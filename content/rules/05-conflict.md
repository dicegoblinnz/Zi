:::quote
Every fight in this game runs on one engine, whether you're fighting with blades, with words or against the clock.
:::

Most of the time the game's just a conversation. The GM describes what's going on, you say what you do, and the dice come out when something's uncertain. When things get dangerous, when something's pushing back and *timing* matters, play moves into **rounds**. A round is a beat in the action, not a set number of seconds. A sword fight, a shouting match in a council chamber and a race against a burning fuse all run the same way.

## The Round {#s-round}

1.  **The GM telegraphs.** Everything that's coming this round goes on the table, face up: what it's doing, who it's aimed at, and how hard, in dice.
2.  **The players act**, in any order they like, one action each. Talk it through and make a plan. There's no initiative.
3.  **The round ends.** Every telegraph nobody answered **lands**. Clocks and events nobody worked step down, and every condition steps down one size. Then the GM telegraphs the next round.

Everything else in this chapter is what happens inside a round.

## Telegraphs {#s-telegraphs}

A **telegraph** is the GM saying out loud what's about to happen, before anything's rolled. It always tells you three things:

-   **What it's doing** (its intent). *Pin Bram against the wall.* *Slip up the stair to wake the house.* *Slam the vault door.*
-   **Who or what it's aimed at**: a character, several characters, a stake or a place.
-   **How hard** (its dice). *A d10.*

:::example
**GM:** Round one. The left Warden's going to close in and pin Bram against the wall, that's a d10. The right Warden drives its spear at Isolde, also a d10, and plants itself in the vault doorway, another d10. The maid on the stair is going to slip away and wake the house. That one's a d4, and it's only got one answer: if it lands, the household clock drops two sizes.
:::

Nothing's been rolled yet. Now that you know exactly what's coming, it's up to you what to do about it.

-   **A threat telegraphs with its Challenge die.** That's as hard as it pushes. A glancing blow can be smaller, and a tag can say it hits harder. How many telegraphs it makes is up to the story and the budget.
-   **One die at each person.** A threat can telegraph at several of you, but it only aims one die at each, unless a tag says it can do more, like *Flurry*. To spread you thin, the GM aims at more of you, not more dice at one.
-   **Anything can telegraph.** It can be a threat, the ground (the fire spreads to the stair), an event (the bell starts to ring), or something the GM's just decided the scene needs. If it's on the table, it came with a die.
-   **The GM has a budget.** It works out at about 5 steps a round for each of you, so about a d10 each, and she can split it into smaller telegraphs at more of you. Within it, threats can do as much as the story allows. The details are in [Running the Game](#s-budget), and you never need to track it.
-   **Telegraphs are honest.** The GM never hides a telegraph's size or its target.
-   **Running doesn't dodge.** A telegraph aimed at you lands on you at the end of the round, wherever you've gone. It's already coming. To stop it, answer it. A telegraph aimed at a place, like the burning stair or the doorway under the chandelier, only hits whoever's in it when it lands, so getting out of there gets you clear.
-   **Some telegraphs have only one answer.** If the GM says so when she telegraphs it, that's what happens if it lands, and there's no trading it for something else.
-   **The round can change.** When something you do sets off something new, like the guard seeing you and shouting, the GM can put a new telegraph on the table, as long as someone's still got an action to answer it with. She never adds one after the last player has acted.

## Your Action {#s-actions}

Every round you get **one action**. It can:

-   **Act.** Build a roll from your set action and throw it: strike, trick, talk, climb, pick a lock, work a stake, strip a tag, study something, or heal someone if you're a [Healer](#a-healer).
-   **Answer a telegraph**: stop it before it lands.
-   **Defend**: every line on your Defend counts twice when the telegraphs land, and so does your Confidence, so you can stop more of what's coming. See [Defend](#s-defend).
-   **Move** further than your stride. See [Movement and Range](#s-movement).
-   **Lead**, if you're a [Leader](#a-leader): an order or a rallying song, with its steps handed out to friends who can hear or see you.
-   **Pull something out of your bag.**
-   **Break a condition**, yours or a friend's. Beat its die and it's gone.
-   **Take a breath.** Your Confidence comes up a size, as far as d6. See [Confidence](#s-confidence).

Some things are free: your stride (before or after your action, or between two), a few words, dropping something or putting it away. [Intercepting](#s-intercept) for a friend costs 1 Aura but no action, and a Guardian does it for free.

There's no special action for helping. To help a friend, put your action into the same thing they're doing. That's a **combo**: you add your [set actions](#s-set-actions) together and one of you rolls the lot. It doesn't cost Aura, it has to be one thing in the story, and every action in it gives you a split. Any number of friends can pile into the same roll. There's one under [Answering a Telegraph](#s-answering), and the rest is in [Working Together](#s-teamwork).

### Extra actions {#s-extra-actions}

Now and then you'll get more than one action in a round:

-   **Hold it.** Do nothing this round apart from your stride, and you've got two actions next round.
-   **Spend 1 Resolve** for an extra action this round. See [Resolve](#s-resolve).
-   **Come out of hiding.** Whoever you are, coming out of hiding gives you an extra action. It's one extra action, even if you're *Unseen* as well.
-   **Cash in your Confidence.** At d12 you're fired up, and you can cash it in for an extra action. Your Confidence drops back to d6. See [Confidence](#s-confidence).
-   **A trait that says so**, once a scene: *Second Wind* gives you an extra action, and *Fast* gives you a free Move action.
-   **The Extra action Boon** gives you one extra action a scene. See [Boons and Burdens](#s-boons).

An extra action can be its own roll, or it can go into the same roll as a combo. Put it into the same set action again and that's 1 more step. Put a different set action in and it brings its lines with it as a new approach, like Fight with Sneak for a sneak attack, and anything that's on both counts once.

:::example[Extra actions]
**Holding:** Vesk spends round two crossing the gallery on her stride and nothing else. In round three she's got two actions, and she puts both into the vault door. Her Locks twice is 5 + 1 = 6 steps, a d12.

**Resolve:** Ash spends 1 Resolve for an extra action and puts it into her Fight as well, so it's 5 steps instead of 4.

**Out of hiding:** Vesk's been up in the rafters since the guards came in. She drops on the one below her, and coming out of hiding gives her an extra action. She puts it in with her action as a new approach, her Sneak and her Fight together. Nothing's on both, so that's 4 + 2 = 6 steps, a d12, for one knife out of the dark.
:::

You can't keep your action back to use later in *this* round once the telegraphs start landing, because there are no last-moment reactions in Zi'Dara. Everything you might react to is already on the table, so you deal with it on your turn.

## Answering a Telegraph {#s-answering}

To **answer** a telegraph, spend your action and build a roll from the set action that fits the *way* you're stopping it: dodge it, parry it, shove the brute's arm aside, shout the crowd down, slam the door before it slams on you, or shoot the archer first. Then roll against the telegraph's dice. Friends can put their actions in too, and then it's a combo with one roll between you.

-   **Win, and it's stopped.** It doesn't land.
-   **Lose, and it lands at once**, right there and then, and nobody rolls a defence against it. A tie's a loss, unless you pay 1 Aura.

Answering's a gamble. It lets you throw everything you've got at a problem instead of trusting your Defend, but if the answer fails, you've walked straight into it.

-   **You can answer for someone else.** Get your shield in front of the spear, drag your friend out of the way, or talk over the man shouting at her. If you answered by putting yourself in the way and you lose, it lands on *you*. If you'd rather it came at you, [intercept](#s-intercept) it instead.
-   **Several dice on the same answer** are advantage: your best die counts.
-   **Taking the source down answers everything it telegraphed.** If the archer falls before the end of the round, his arrow never flies. Strip a tag and whatever it gave a telegraph goes with it, so if you take its *Reach*, the spear can't reach you.
-   **Telegraphs aimed at a stake, a place or a clock** have no defence to roll. If nobody answers them, they happen.

:::example[Answering together]
The brute telegraphs his club at Isolde, a d8, and her Defend's a d2. Wren answers it for her with an arrow into his arm, and Bram puts his action into the same thing, getting his hammer under the club. Wren's Fight is 5 steps and Bram's is 4, so that's 9 steps from two actions, a d12 and a d6, and they put both on the club. It rolls 6 and the d12 comes up 8, so it never lands. If they'd lost, it'd have landed on Isolde there and then.
:::

## When It Lands {#s-defending}

You're never going to answer everything. Whatever nobody answers **lands at the end of the round**. If you don't Defend against it either, you're **letting it ride**, and it's down to your defence, rolled once:

-   **Your Defend**, rolled once, against the blows, blades, falling rocks and teeth.
-   **Your Confidence**, rolled once, against the fear, lies, pressure and anything else that works on the mind.

**Each die you roll stops one telegraph.** Roll them with the telegraphs' dice, then say which telegraph each of yours stops. It has to beat it or tie it, because **ties go to you**. Everything else gets through, whatever it rolled, and it costs you something. The GM names what a blow costs you, and you choose what fear, lies and pressure cost (see [Consequences](#s-consequences)).

:::example[One roll against two blows]
Two guards telegraph at Vesk this round, a d8 and a d6. Nobody answers either, so she lets them ride. At the end of the round she rolls her Defend once, a d4 for her worn leathers, and gets a 3. The d8 rolls 6 and the d6 rolls 3. Her 3 stops the d6, because the tie's hers, and the d8 gets through. If she'd rolled a 1, both would have.
:::

:::rule{#s-past-d12}
**Past 6 steps.** A defence past 6 steps is more than one die, the same as any roll, and each of those dice stops one telegraph. When Bram Defends, his Defend's 7 steps, a d12 and a d2, so he's got two dice to stop two telegraphs with. Your Confidence tops out at d12, but it counts twice when you Defend, so a d12 is 12 steps, two d12s.
:::

**Fear is a telegraph.** The GM telegraphs fear, lies and pressure like any attack, say a dragon's roar as a d10 at everyone's Confidence, and you deal with it the usual way: answer it, Defend or let it ride. That's your fear check, and there isn't another one.

**Your Confidence swings with it.** Stop all of it in a round, every bit of fear, lies and pressure that came at you, and your Confidence goes up a size. Taking a threat down sends it up a size too (in a combo, that's everyone who put an action in). If nothing's aimed at you, it doesn't move. How far it can swing, from shaken at d2 to fired up at d12, is in [Confidence](#s-confidence).

**Threats get one roll a round too.** The first time anyone goes at a threat in a round, roll its Challenge die, and that roll stands till the round's over. Everyone who goes at it after that has to beat that same number, whatever die they're rolling and whatever it's for. If Bram rolls a 4 for the guard captain when he swings at him, that's the 4 Wren has to beat when she shoots the captain later in the round. A *Layered* threat rolls all its dice the first time it's attacked in the round, and its best die is the roll that stands. A stake works the same way. So a threat's one roll stands against every die anyone throws at it that round, where each of your defence dice stops one telegraph.

### Defend {#s-defend}

**Defend** takes your action. When the telegraphs land at the end of the round, **every line on your Defend counts twice, and so does your Confidence**, and you can split each of them once. More dice means more telegraphs stopped. Then you choose which die stops which. A condition's step, like *Warded*, still counts once.

Confidence counts twice by its steps: a d6 is 3 steps, so it's 6 when you Defend, and you can split that into two d6s.

With nothing on your Defend, it's a d2 either way, so Defending won't help you against blows. Answer the blow instead, or get a Guardian in front of it. It still doubles your Confidence.

It's what you do when a lot's coming at you, which is often once you've [intercepted](#s-intercept) for your friends. It only covers what lands at the end of the round, though, so a failed answer still lands at once, with no defence roll.

:::example[Bram Defends]
A brute and two of his mates telegraph a d8 each at Wren. She's within Bram's stride, so he intercepts all three, which is free for a Guardian, and Defends. When they land, the three lines on his Defend (his first Title, his plate and his shield) count twice, so with the action it's 7 steps, a d12 and a d2. He uses his split to make the d12 two d6s. The d8s come up 7, 5 and 2, and Bram rolls 6, 3 and 1. He puts the 6 on the 5 and the 3 on the 2, so only the 7 gets through. The 6 is a d6's top face, so he gets 1 Aura back. If he'd let them ride, he'd have rolled his Defend once, a d8, and stopped one of them at most.

Isolde's Defend is a d2, so Defending does nothing for her against blows. It still doubles her Confidence, though. Her d8 is 4 steps, which makes 8 when she Defends: a d12 and a d4. That's two telegraphs at her Confidence she can stop instead of one, or three if she splits the d12.
:::

### Intercept {#s-intercept}

When a telegraph's aimed at a friend within your stride, you can step in and **intercept** it. It costs **1 Aura** and no action, and you can do it any time before the telegraphs land. Take as many of the dice aimed at them as you like, from as many enemies as you like, and they come at you instead. Your own defence handles them the usual way, and one die stops one, so if you've taken a lot, you'll want to Defend.

-   **One friend for each Aura.** Stepping in for two friends is 2 Aura.
-   **A [Guardian](#a-guardian) intercepts for free**, for every friend within their stride.
-   **Fear, lies and pressure too.** Intercept them and your Confidence handles them, and if they get through, you choose what they cost.
-   **The GM's people can intercept too**, without Aura, like the bodyguard who throws himself in front of the knife meant for his lord. She says so before you roll, and your die has to beat his defence instead.
-   **A summon intercepts for free**, and one hit takes it out (see [Summoning](#s-summoning)).

:::example[Intercepting]
The brute's club is coming down at Isolde again, a d8, and her Defend's a d2. Vesk's right beside her, so she spends 1 Aura and intercepts it. It doesn't take her action, so she still gets her knife into the brute. At the end of the round the club comes at Vesk, and she rolls her Defend, a d4: 4 against the club's 3, so it's stopped. That's the d4's top face, so she gets her Aura back.
:::

There's a Burden for it in [Boons and Burdens](#s-boons): you can't intercept. There's no Boon, because intercepting for free is the Guardian's.

## Consequences: When Something Lands {#s-consequences}

When a telegraph lands on you, or when a miss costs you something because it mattered, a **consequence** lands. Fear, lies and pressure have their own rule, because when they get through, you choose (see [When fear gets through](#s-fear-gets-through)). For everything else, **the GM names it** from this list, picking one that fits:

-   **Take a Hurt.** Your wound die goes up a size. If it's already a d12, you Blaze. See [Wounds, Blaze and Scars](#s-blaze).
-   **Step your Confidence down** one size, for a miss where it fits, like a bluff that falls apart in front of the whole room.
-   **Break an item.** Something you carry breaks: cross it out until it's mended. Armour and shields are the usual ones. Your attuned things are magic, and they can't be broken.
-   **Step your Aspiration back down** one size, never below d2. The blow costs you your dream instead of your body.
-   **Step the Party Goal back down** one size, never below d2.
-   **Tick a clock**: step one of the scene's clocks, or one of the campaign's, down one.
-   **Something else** the table agrees fits.

**You can offer another.** If a different one fits the story and costs about as much, say so, and it lands there instead: *not the Hurt, my shield takes it.* It has to fit, though: a blade can't dent your Confidence, and an insult can't break your shield.

If the telegraph's intent was something specific, like *pin you*, *grab the painting* or *knock you off the roof*, that's what happens. You can usually offer a consequence instead: *I hold onto the painting and take the blow.* The only exception is a telegraph the GM said has only one answer.

### When fear gets through {#s-fear-gets-through}

When fear, lies or pressure get through your Confidence, the GM doesn't name the cost. **You choose**, for each one that gets through:

-   **Let it shake you.** Your Confidence drops a size.
-   **Bury it.** Take a Hurt, and your Confidence stays where it is.
-   **Hold your ground.** Spend 1 Resolve, and neither happens.

**Nothing takes your Confidence below d2.** Anything that would is a Hurt instead, or 1 Resolve to hold your ground. At d2 you're shaken, and you won't go at a threat, in a fight or face to face, unless you spend 1 Resolve each time you do.

If a telegraph at your Confidence had a specific intent, like *Frightened* or *Taunted*, that's what happens when it gets through, unless you trade it for a cost the usual way. The cost you trade it for is one of those three.

:::example[When fear gets through]
**On d2:** Vesk's down to d2 and shaken when the guard captain's threat gets through as well. Nothing takes her below d2, so it's a Hurt instead, or 1 Resolve to hold her ground. Her wound die's already a d8, so she spends the Resolve.

**Something specific:** A Warden's empty helm turns on Wren, and the GM telegraphs *Frightened* at her, a d8 at her Confidence. Nobody answers it, so it rides, and at the end of the round her d6 rolls 3 against its 6. She'd be *Frightened d8*, with her next action spent getting away, so she trades it: she buries it and takes a Hurt.
:::

### Player versus Player {#s-pvp}

The rules are the same when one player goes at another. Both dice hit the table, ties go to the defender, and each defence die stops one attack, the same as against a telegraph. Whoever throws the punch spends the action and names the cost, and whoever takes it can offer another. When it's fear, lies or pressure that gets through, whoever it got through to chooses what it costs, the same as with anyone else.

## Threats {#s-challenges}

A threat is anything that pushes back: a person, a beast, a mob, a storm, a rumour. Every threat comes down to two things, a **Challenge** and a handful of **tags**.

| Challenge | Die | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | d4 | A nuisance. |
| 3 | d6 | A real fight. |
| 4 | d8 | A capable opponent. |
| 5 | d10 | A serious threat. |
| 6 | d12 | The top of the scale. |

**The Challenge is the threat's defence.** It's the die you've got to beat, and it never steps down. There's no Challenge above 6, so anything bigger is a Challenge 6 with more tags, and **the tags are what make a boss a boss**.

### Tags {#tags}

A tag is something true about a threat that the whole table can see, use and play to: *Armoured, Reach, Loyal, Calls for Help, Bound to a Place*. Tags don't make its dice bigger. They tell you what the threat is, what it does and what you've got to get through, and a tag only changes the dice when it says so. Every tag is one of three kinds:

-   **How it comes at you.** *Reach, Fast, Venom, Fear.* These are what its telegraphs can do to you.
-   **What keeps it going.** *Armoured, Shield, Flies, Regrows.* These are its **health**: every win strips one, and a big one with a [multiplier](#s-tough) takes a win for each.
-   **What drives it.** *Loyal, Greedy, Scared, Code of Honour.* These are what it wants, fears and loves, and they're how you talk to it, buy it, scare it or turn it.

**Its defence against every intent is its Challenge die.** A Challenge 5 defends against a sword at a d10, and against a bribe at a d10. It rolls that die once a round, the first time anyone goes at it, and what's different from one intent to the next is what a win takes away.

### Tags are its health {#s-staying}

Threats in Zi'Dara don't have hit points. Count a threat's **keeps-it-going tags** and their [multipliers](#s-tough), and that's how many wins it takes to bring it down the long way. Every win strips a tag or ticks one off a multiplier. **Strip the last and it falls.** A threat with none at all falls to one good win.

-   A **mook** has none or one, so one good hit does it.
-   A **named** threat has two, which makes for a proper fight.
-   A **boss** has three or more, some hidden, a weak point, and multipliers on the big ones.

:::rule{#s-layered}
**Layered.** Special encounters stack their dice. A threat or a stake that's *Layered* rolls one Challenge die for every tag it's got left keeping it going, and its best die counts. A tag with a multiplier is still one tag here, so *Armoured ×4* is one die. It rolls them the first time it's attacked in a round, and that best die stands for the rest of the round. Strip a tag and it rolls one fewer from the next round on. Save it for the doors, wards and bosses that should feel like a wall, not for every guard.
:::

:::example[The enchanted door]
A vault door is Challenge 4, and three tags keep it shut: *Oak*, *Iron Bars* and *Warded*. That's three wins against its d8 to break it. Make it *Layered* and it rolls 3d8 the first time someone goes at it in a round, and keeps the best. Once a tag's gone it's 2d8 the next round, and the last tag only has a single d8 behind it.
:::

### Multipliers {#s-tough}

Some tags take more than one win to strip, and they carry a **multiplier**: *Armoured ×4* takes four wins, *Shield ×3* takes three and *Sword ×2* takes two. A tag with no multiplier goes in one win, the same as always. Every win ticks one off, and the GM says what's left out loud: *"Armoured ×2 left."* [Vulnerabilities](#s-vulnerable) tick it down faster. A multiplier never changes the threat's dice. It's only how many wins the tag takes.

-   **Most threats don't need them.** They're for bosses and the few things built to last. The usual multiplier is the threat's Challenge, on the tag that matters most to it. It can go higher when the whole fight's about that tag, and it's less on the small stuff.
-   **A named tag only ticks to what fits it.** Blades and hammers tick *Natural Armour*, shaming a captain in front of his men ticks *Commanding Presence*, and bribes and threats tick a crowd that's *Loyal ×3*.
-   **Some come first.** A tag marked *first*, like *Natural Armour ×5 (first)*, has to go before anything under it can be stripped.
-   **Every die can tick it.** Call several dice at it, each as its own intent. It rolls its defence once that round, and every die that beats that roll ticks one off.
-   **Openings and weak points ignore multipliers.** A weak point still ends the threat at once. An opening ends a Mook or a Named threat, and a Boss loses two whole tags, multipliers and all.
-   **Piercing still gets through.** A *Piercing* weapon ignores *Armoured*, whatever its multiplier, even when it comes first.
-   **Conditions tick too.** A spent condition that would strip a tag, like *Burning*, ticks one off instead.

:::example[Through the hide]
The thing under the bridge is Challenge 5, and it's *Natural Armour ×5 (first)*, *Relentless* and *Hungry*. Nothing touches *Relentless* or *Hungry* until the armour's gone.

Bram goes at the hide first. His Fight is 4 steps, a d8. He's the first to go at it this round, so he rolls its d10 as well, and it comes up 3. His d8 rolls 7, so that's one off, and it's *Natural Armour ×4*.

Ash throws fire at the same hide with her Fight: her Title, *throw fire* and her ember stone, 4 steps with the action, a d8. The thing's 3 still stands, so nobody rolls its d10 again. Ash's d8 comes up 8, its top face, so she gets 1 Aura back, and it's *Natural Armour ×3*. Her 8 doesn't do any more than Bram's 7, because a win ticks one off however high it rolls. Another round or two like that and the crew's into it.
:::

### Hidden tags and weak points {#s-hidden}

Some tags are **hidden**. The GM knows them and you don't, until you find them. A hidden tag still counts. It keeps the threat going, or drives it, whether you know about it or not. [Study](#s-study) finds them, and so do paying attention, asking the right person and [the Oracle](#oracle).

A **weak point** is a tag that holds the whole threat together, like the binding that animates a suit of armour, the true name of a ghost, or the one person a zealot would never hurt. **Break a weak point and the threat falls at once**, however much it had left. Weak points are usually hidden, and finding one is the reward for paying attention.

## What a Win Does {#s-win}

When your die beats a threat's defence, **your intent happens**. Most of the time, that'll be one of these:

-   **Strip a tag**, whichever one your intent went for. Knock the shield away and *Shield* is gone. Get inside the spear and *Reach* is gone. Prove the lie and *Silver-Tongued* is gone. A stripped tag stops counting, and if it was keeping the threat going, the threat's one step closer to falling. A tag with a [multiplier](#s-tough) ticks down one a win instead, and it's stripped when the last one goes.
-   **Take it down.** If nothing's left keeping it going, a win meant to finish it finishes it. Your intent says which: dead, beaten, out cold, fled, surrendered or convinced.
-   **Take it out now.** If you've made an **opening**, one win ends a Mook or a Named threat, whatever tags it's got left. There are three kinds of opening:
    -   **Its weak point.**
    -   **It's helpless:** bound, asleep, or a quiet knife at the throat of a guard who never saw you coming.
    -   **You get around everything that keeps it going:** a thin blade through the visor of a threat whose only keeps-it-going tag is *Armoured*, or fire on a thing that only *Regrows*.

    A Boss takes more than one mistake. An opening against a Boss strips **two** whole tags instead of one, multipliers and all, and only its weak point ends a Boss in one blow.
-   **Put a condition on it**, like burning, pinned, disarmed, blinded or frightened. The condition carries your die, and the crew can spend it later. See [Conditions](#s-conditions).
-   **Move it.** Shove it off the stair, drag it into the light, or drive it back through the door.
-   **Learn something** (that's Study).
-   **Stop a telegraph** (that's answering).
-   **Anything else the intent said.** Grab the key from its belt, cut the rope it's climbing, make it drop the child.

Take a threat down and its telegraphs go with it, which is often the best answer of all.

:::rule
**Threats don't step down.** Beating a threat never makes its Challenge smaller. A few unique threats do work that way, like a crowd that thins as you win it over or a wall that crumbles as you batter it, and their tags say so. For everything else, the Challenge is the Challenge, and the tags are what you fight.
:::

## Stakes, Clocks and Events {#s-stakes}

A scene's rarely just a fight. There's usually something the crew *wants*, like the paintings out of the vault, the witness alive or the ship off the dock, and something it costs them if they don't get it. Those are **stakes**. A good scene has two or three, and not enough actions to do them all.

-   **A stake works like a threat.** It has a Challenge, and tags for everything **in the way**: *Locked, Heavy Load, Someone Must Agree*. Every win strips one, and once you've stripped them all, the stake's yours. A stake with nothing in the way is done with one win.
-   **A clock** is a die on a stake that **steps down at the end of every round nobody works on it**. Working it means rolling against the stake that round. When it steps down past a d2, the chance is gone and the stake's lost. Start it at a d6 for a close call, a d8 for most scenes, or a d10 when there's time.
-   **An event** is something running on its own clock, like the watch arriving, the fire spreading or the ritual completing. Left alone, it steps down one size every round, and when it steps down past a d2, it happens. **Engage it** and beat its die to slow it (step it back up) or drive it (step it down, when you want it to happen sooner).
-   **A scene clock** is an event on the whole scene, face up from the start. When it runs out, the scene turns: the watch arrives, the floor gives way, the ship sails.

The scene ends when the stakes are settled, not when the last enemy falls. A crew that walks out with the paintings while two Wardens are still standing has won.

## Conditions {#s-conditions}

A **condition** is a tag carrying a die. It can be **against you** (*Burning d8, Pinned d6, Frightened d10*) or **for you** (*Warded d8, Unseen d6, Blessed d12*). Good or bad, every condition works the same way.

-   **Its die is the die that placed it.** Beat a Warden with a d10 meant to set it alight, and it's *Burning d10*. When nothing pushes back, like a ward on a friend or a blessing before the charge, there's no roll, and the die you built is the condition's die (see [Wards and blessings](#s-wards)).
-   **Passive: one step, while it lasts.** A condition against you costs you a step on whatever it gets in the way of, and a condition for you gives you a step on whatever it helps. The condition says what fits. It counts once on a roll, however many actions go into it. A few against you make you vulnerable instead, like *Prone* and *Exposed* (see [Vulnerabilities](#s-vulnerable)).
-   **Active: spend it for one big thing.** Every condition has one strong effect. Spend it and it happens, and the condition's gone. Spending it doesn't cost anything: no action, no roll.
-   **It fades.** At the end of every round, every condition steps down one size, and past d2 it's gone. It lasts as many rounds as it has steps, so a d4 lasts two and a d12 lasts six.
-   **Break it.** Spend your action and beat its die: put the fire out, shake off the fear, tear down their ward. A friend can do it for you, if they can reach you.
-   **One of each, once a round.** Nobody carries two of the same condition, and the same condition goes on the same target only once a round, whoever places it. Spent or broken, it can't go back on them until next round. Place it again in a later round and the new one replaces the old one, die and all. Different conditions are fine: a Warden can be *Burning* and *Pinned* in the same round.

### Who spends it {#s-conditions-spend}

-   **A condition for you:** you do, whenever it fits.
-   **A condition on a threat:** anyone in the crew, on their turn.
-   **A condition the GM put on you:** the GM spends it at the end of the round, and she says so when she telegraphs. Until then it's yours to break, so if you don't want it, spend your action and put it out.
-   **A threat's own good conditions:** the GM spends them when they fit, out loud.

A threat can break a condition too. The GM telegraphs it (*the Warden beats at the flames, a d10 against its Burning*) and it rolls at the end of the round. If the crew wants that Burning spent, spend it first.

:::rule
**Conditions on a threat.** A threat doesn't have a wound die, actions or Aura, so read its conditions like this.

-   **A Hurt** is a tag stripped, one of the tags that keeps it going, or one tick off a tag with a multiplier. Whoever spends it picks one the story allows.
-   **An action** is a telegraph. If it loses one, one of its telegraphs is gone. If it gains one, the GM adds a telegraph, free of the budget.
-   **A step against it** is a step for whoever goes at it. Its Challenge never moves.
-   **Anything that weakens its defence**, like a spent *Poisoned*, or drops its Confidence, is a tag stripped, of the kind that fits. A blow takes what protects it, and a word takes what drives it.
-   **A vulnerability** works on a threat the same way it works on you.
:::

### Wards and blessings {#s-wards}

A ward or a blessing reaches **one friend**. Split it, and the second die can reach **another friend you can touch**. Nothing pushes back, so there's nothing to roll: the die you built is the condition's die on that friend, and a split's second die is the condition's die on the second.

:::example[Two wards from one action]
Later in her career, Isolde's Ward is up to 5 steps. She splits it into a d6 and a d4, so Bram gets *Warded d6*, and Vesk, who's right beside her, gets *Warded d4*.
:::

It works the same for any other condition you put on a friend, like *Heartened* or *Rallied*.

### Conditions to start with {#s-conditions-list}

These are examples, not a complete list, so write your own in the same shape: a name, a passive and an active. The passive is one step, for or against, or a [vulnerability](#s-vulnerable). The active is one strong thing that pulls one lever: a Hurt, an action, movement, Defend or Confidence, an item, a telegraph, a clock or a secret.

#### Against you {#against-you}

| Condition | While it lasts | Spend it and… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Burning** | One step less on anything physical. | take a Hurt. |
| **Bleeding** | One step less on anything strenuous. | take a Hurt. |
| **Poisoned** | One step less on anything that needs a steady hand or a clear eye. | your Defend's a d2 until the round ends. |
| **Exposed** | You're vulnerable to every hit. | you lose your next action getting your guard back. Unless you've got an extra one, that's your whole turn. |
| **Pinned** | One step less on moving. | you can't move this round, not even your stride. |
| **Prone** | You're vulnerable to anyone standing over you. | your stride this round goes on getting up. |
| **Staggered** | One step less on keeping your feet. | you go down: you're *Prone*, at the same die. |
| **Grabbed** | One step less on anything but breaking free. | they drag you up to their stride, wherever they like. |
| **Disarmed** | One step less on fighting with what was in your hand. | it's gone: out of reach, or in their hand. Cross it off until you get it back. |
| **Blinded** | One step less on anything that needs your eyes. | one of your dice this round goes wide: it doesn't count. |
| **Deafened** | One step less on anything that needs your ears. | you can't join a friend's roll, or have a friend join yours, this round. |
| **Silenced** | One step less on anything spoken. | you can't speak this round: no orders, no words of power, no talking your way out. |
| **Frightened** | One step less on anything that brings you closer to what you fear. | your next action goes on getting away from it. |
| **Shaken** | One step less on your Confidence. | your Confidence steps down one size. |
| **Stunned** | One step less on everything. | you can't answer telegraphs this round. |
| **Taunted** | One step less on anything not aimed at whoever taunted you. | your next action has to go at them. |
| **Charmed** | One step less on anything against whoever charmed you. | you do one thing they ask. A player can refuse, and steps Confidence down one size instead. |
| **Spotted** | One step less on hiding, and getting away. | whoever spotted you learns one thing: where you are right now, or one of your hidden tags. |
| **Hexed** | One step less on one kind of thing, named when it's placed. | one roll you just won becomes a tie. |
| **Hunted** | One step less on hiding, and resting. | a clock ticks: they're closing in. |

#### For you {#for-you}

| Condition | While it lasts | Spend it and… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Warded** | One step more on your Defend. | it takes a Hurt instead of you. |
| **Heartened** | One step more on your Confidence. | your Confidence comes back up a size, as far as where it started the scene. |
| **Covered** | One step more on your Defend, while whoever covers you is close. | a blow meant for you lands on them instead, and they choose where. |
| **Braced** | One step more on holding your ground. | nothing moves you, pins you or knocks you down this round. |
| **Unseen** | One step more on anything done unnoticed. | take an extra action. |
| **Fleet** | One step more on moving. | move your stride again, free, whenever you like this round. |
| **Blessed** | One step more on the action it blesses. | if that action fails, roll it again. |
| **Inspired** | One step more on one of your set actions, named when it's placed. | gain 1 Aura. |
| **Rallied** | One step more on answering telegraphs. | answer one telegraph without spending an action. |
| **Sighted** | One step more on anything against one target. | the GM tells you one of its hidden tags. |
| **Guided** | One step more on finding your way. | bring the ground in without paying Aura. |
| **Disguised** | One step more on passing as someone else. | one person believes it completely. No roll. |
| **Fortunate** | One step more on anything that's mostly chance. | win a tie without paying Aura. |
| **Surging** | One step more on one of your set actions, named when it's placed. | one of its dice becomes a d12 before you roll. |

*Unseen*'s spend is an extra action, and so is coming out of hiding. If you're doing both at once, they make one extra action between them (see [Extra actions](#s-extra-actions)).

:::example[Fire and a ward, at the table]
**Round 1.** Ash's d8 beats the Warden's defence. Her intent was to set it alight, so the Warden is *Burning d8*. While it burns, anyone who goes at it gets a step. Its spear at Dorrie is still a d10, because its Challenge never moves.

Isolde's got nothing to beat, so she doesn't roll. Her Ward is her Title and her chalk and salt, 3 steps with the action. Dorrie is *Warded d6*, so her Defend gets a step while it lasts, and her d2 is a d4.

**End of Round 1.** Dorrie rolls her Defend once, the d4, and gets a 2. The spear rolls 7. It lands, and the GM names a Hurt. Dorrie spends the ward and it takes the Hurt instead, so the ward's gone. The Burning steps down to a d6.

**Round 2.** The GM telegraphs: *the Warden beats at the flames, a d10 against its Burning.* Bram doesn't wait. He spends the Burning (no action, no roll) and the Warden's *Armoured* blackens and cracks off in plates. Then he swings at it, and since he's the first to go at it this round, he rolls its d10 along with his own die.
:::

### Marked {#s-marked}

***Marked*** is a special condition, and it's the [Hunter](#a-hunter)'s. A Hunter spends their action and rolls against a creature to mark it, and if they win, it's *Marked* with the die they won with. It doesn't work like the rest: it doesn't give a step, and nobody spends it. Instead, anyone who acts against that creature rolls the Mark die too, as one more die against it. It fades a size every round, like any condition.

**The Mark jumps.** When a *Marked* creature falls, the Mark moves to another creature the Hunter can see, with the die it had.

:::example[Marked]
Wren marks the Warden with her Fight, a d10, and wins, so it's *Marked d10*. Bram swings at it with his d8 and rolls the Mark d10 with it. Next round it's *Marked d8*, and when it falls, the Mark jumps to the second Warden, still a d8.
:::

## Vulnerabilities {#s-vulnerable}

A **vulnerability** is something that makes a hit hurt more, like *Vulnerable to fire*, *Prone* or *Opened*. When a win strips a tag or causes a Hurt, **every vulnerability that fits it adds one more tick**. You've still got to win, so a miss is a miss.

-   **A tick** is one Hurt on you, which puts your wound die up a size, or one win off a threat's tag (see [Multipliers](#s-tough)).
-   **They add up, one each.** A goblin that's *Prone*, *Vulnerable to fire* and *Vulnerable to blades* takes four ticks from a burning sword: one for the hit and three more.
-   **The same word never stacks.** The same vulnerability twice counts once. Something that's really weak to fire has two different ones, like *Dry as Tinder* and *Burns Easily*.
-   **Extra ticks stay on the tag your die went for.** If it's only got one tick left, the rest are wasted, so aim the big hit at the big tag.
-   **Some are hidden.** A hidden vulnerability is a hidden tag, and [Study](#s-study) finds it.
-   **Two people count twice.** Two [Warriors](#a-warrior) who both open the same target are two ticks, because they're two people.

A vulnerability only adds to a win that's doing harm: a Hurt, or stripping a tag that keeps a threat going. It doesn't add to a win that puts a condition on something or moves it. It works the same way on the crew: one vulnerability makes a hit two Hurts, so your wound die goes up two sizes. *Prone* and *Exposed* are the usual ones, and the GM keeps the rest rare on the crew.

:::example[A Warden on its back]
The Warden's on its back, *Prone*, and Isolde's Study found it's *Dry as Tinder*. Its *Armoured ×4* is untouched. Bram stands over it and his hammer wins: one tick for the hit and one for *Prone*, so it's *Armoured ×2*. Then Ash throws fire from the doorway and wins too. She isn't standing over it, so *Prone* doesn't count for her, but *Dry as Tinder* does: two ticks, and the armour's gone.
:::

## Wounds, Blaze and Scars {#s-blaze}

Your **wound die** is how hurt you are. It starts at **d2**, and every Hurt you take puts it up a size: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12. It's one die for body and mind, so a spear and a night of horrors both put it up. **Take a Hurt at d12 and you Blaze.**

It's the die a heal has to beat too, so the worse you're hurt, the harder you are to patch up. A hit that's two Hurts, because you were [vulnerable](#s-vulnerable), puts it up two sizes, and if that takes it past d12, you Blaze.

:::example[Wearing Vesk down]
Vesk takes a spear in round one and a knife in round two, so her wound die's gone from d2 to d4 to d6. In round three a guard's blow lands while she's *Exposed*, which is two Hurts, and she's on d10. The next Hurt makes it a d12, and the one after that, she Blazes.
:::

Some of you take more wearing down. Bram's *Unyielding* means the first Hurt he takes each scene doesn't move his wound die, and the Hurt Boon does the same. The Hurt Burden works the other way: you Blaze a size sooner, on a Hurt at d10. See [Boons and Burdens](#s-boons).

**At the end of every scene, you rest.** Roll the Confidence you finished the scene on against your wound die, before your Confidence goes back to d6. Beat it and your wound goes down a size. It's an ordinary roll, so a tie goes to the wound, and 1 Resolve rolls it again. *Tend the Crew* gives everyone in the crew who was in the scene two Confidence dice for it, and the best counts (see [Traits](#s-traits)). **Downtime clears it**: your wound die goes back to d2, or to your Scars.

:::example[Resting]
The job's done, and Vesk walks out on d8 Confidence with a d6 wound. She rolls her d8 against her d6, 5 against 4, so her wound's a d4 for the next scene. In downtime it goes back to d2.
:::

**When you Blaze**, declare something extraordinary and it happens, no roll. From your very first Blaze, **you can choose for your character to die**. If you don't, they'll leave the scene, and the wound becomes a **Scar**.

:::note
**For the GM.** If a player chooses to die, slow right down. Let them tell their last moment in their own words, and make sure what they died for happens: the door holds, the crew gets out, the threat goes down with them. Then give everyone at the table a moment to answer it, in character. The farewell's in [Growing](#s-death).
:::

A **Scar** is your wound, written down as a Catch: *a spear through the shoulder*, *can't stand the dark*. It bites like any Catch, so let it cause real trouble and you take 1 Aura, or 1 Resolve for a big bite. Each Scar also starts your wound die a size bigger, and nothing heals you below it: with one Scar it never goes below d4, with two never below d6, and so on. When you take a Scar, your wound die drops back to your Scars. Where it is on your body is up to you.

**With five Scars you're scarred out.** Your wound die never goes below d12, so the next Hurt Blazes, and that Blaze is your last.

The GM can take a Scar away when the story's earned it, like a miracle, a ritual or a year of rest. Cross it out, Catch and all.

## Healing {#s-healing}

You don't have to wait for downtime to heal someone, but only a [Healer](#a-healer) makes a **heal roll**. It's an action, built like any other roll from what fits how you're doing it, and you roll it against your patient's **wound die**.

-   **Each of your dice that beats it steps it down a size**, so split your roll to bring it down further.
-   **A heal reaches one person.** Split it, and the second die can reach a second person you can touch.
-   **Nothing heals anyone below their Scars.** With one Scar, a d4's as far down as they go.

:::example[A heal]
Say you're a Healer, and your Title's *The field surgeon of the Red Company*. Your Patch up set action is your Title, *stitch a wound*, *keep someone alive till help comes* and your kit, so with the action it's 5 steps, a d10. Vesk's taken a beating, and her wound die's a d8. You roll 7 and her d8 comes up 8, so you spend 1 Aura on your Healer's +2 and make your 7 a 9. Her wound's a d6 now.

Next round she's still on d6, and Bram's right beside you on a d4. You split your 5 steps into a d6 for Vesk and a d4 for Bram. Vesk's d6 rolls 4 and your d6 comes up 5, so she's on a d4. Bram's d4 rolls 2 and your d4 comes up 3, so he's back to d2.
:::

**Quick heal.** Anyone can do this one. Pay 1 Aura to step one person's wound die down a size, no roll. It still takes your action. It's how a crew with no Healer gets by: Dorrie can't stitch a wound, but she can spend the Aura and her action to bring Bram's wound die down from a d6 to a d4.

**Traits and gear that say they heal** still work the way they're written, because none of them is a heal roll. *Field Dressing* is a quick heal without the Aura (see [Traits](#s-traits)), and so is a medical kit.

A few archetypes have their own way past a Hurt. A [Bloodsworn](#a-bloodsworn) can spend Aura and their action to step their own wound die down, a size for each Aura, whoever's holding a [Maker](#a-maker)'s item can break one of its tags instead of taking a Hurt, and a [Shifter](#a-shifter) can strip an animal tag instead. A ward takes a Hurt instead of you as well, and cashing in fired-up Confidence steps your wound die down a size (see [Confidence](#s-confidence)).

**In downtime,** wounds clear for free. When downtime's over, your wound die goes back to d2, or to your Scars. See [Downtime](#recover).

A few Boons and Burdens change how heals land on you, or let you heal on your own. They're in [Boons and Burdens](#s-boons).

## Social and Mental Conflict {#s-social}

Social and mental conflict run on the same engine and the same dice. A threat has one Challenge whether you're trading blows or trading words. There are only two differences: its telegraphs come at your Confidence instead of your Defend, and its *keeps-it-going* tags are the duty, pride and fear that keep it saying no. Strip them all and it gives in. Find what drives it and you might not need to strip anything.

Your Confidence swings in an argument the same as it does in a fight (see [When It Lands](#s-defending)), so keep an eye on Dorrie's in this one.

:::example[A social conflict, played out]
A few sessions in, Dorrie's trying to talk a magistrate into releasing Vesk from a cell before the Vane Estate's lawyers arrive in the morning. Isolde's come along as her clerk. The magistrate is *The Honest Magistrate*, Challenge 5. *Duty-bound* and *Wary* keep him going, and one hidden tag drives him. Dorrie's in her court clothes, and they fit a hearing, so her Confidence starts the scene on d8.

**Round 1.** The GM telegraphs: *"Show me the law that lets her walk."* That's a d10 at Dorrie's Confidence. Dorrie answers it, and Isolde puts her action into the same thing. Isolde digs up a harbour statute nobody's used in forty years, and Dorrie swears the court leaned on it only last spring. Dorrie's Talk is 4 steps and Isolde's Study is 4, so that's 8 steps from two actions, a d12 and a d4. The d12 goes on the answer, and the d4 goes straight at *Wary*.

The telegraph rolls 4 and the d12 rolls 9, so it's stopped. That's everything that came at Dorrie this round, so her Confidence goes up a size, to d10. The magistrate's d10 rolls 7 against the d4's 3, so *Wary* holds. He's not ready to trust her. So she fires *Cold Reader* (free, once per scene, one true thing about his mood), and the GM reveals the hidden tag: *Hates the Vane Estate.*

**Round 2.** *Duty-bound* comes into play, and he reaches for the bell to call the captain of the watch. That's a d10 aimed at the stake, and it's got one answer: if it lands, the hearing's over for tonight.

Dorrie pays 1 Aura to bring *Hates the Vane Estate* in: "You know who wants her kept in here, don't you?" That's her Talk and the tag, 5 steps. Isolde joins in again with her Study, reciting every time the Vane Estate's bent the harbour law, so it's 9 steps from two actions. They share them out as a d10 for the bell and a d8 for *Duty-bound*.

The bell's d10 rolls 5 and theirs rolls 10, a top face, so the bell's stopped and Dorrie gets her Aura back. Against *Duty-bound* he rolls his d10 once for the round, and it's a 3. The d8 rolls 7, so it's stripped, and now only *Wary* keeps him going. Nothing was aimed at Dorrie this round, so her Confidence stays on d10.

**Round 3.** He wavers: *"And why should I trust you?"* That's a d10 at her Confidence. Dorrie lets it ride. She goes at *Wary* instead, with her Talk and the tag again for 1 Aura, 5 steps, a d10. Her d10 beats his, 9 to 8. *Wary* is gone, and with it his last reason to say no. He releases Vesk on her own recognizance.

His question never lands. A threat that gives in takes its telegraphs with it, and Dorrie knew it. She left the d10 alone because ending him was the best answer there was, and if she'd missed, his question would only have met her Confidence, a d10 of her own. It was the only telegraph at her, so her one die only had one thing to stop. Taking him down sends her Confidence up a size, so she walks out on d12, fired up. When the scene ends it goes back to d6.
:::

## A Full Fight, Start to Finish {#s-fight}

:::quote
The rules make a lot more sense once you've seen them in a fight, so here's one, played out round by round.
:::

The crew are in the lower corridor of the Vane manor: Vesk, Dorrie, Bram and Isolde, all as they start in the [six crew](#s-pregens). Bram's just forced the vault door, and it was loud.

:::example
The corridor's thirty feet of polished marble, lit by candles in iron brackets. At the far end, two suits of armour are already on the move, not walking so much as moving the way the vault door moved. They've got spears, and they're aimed at you. Behind you, three paintings hang in the open vault, each one chained to the wall with a padlock. On the servants' stair, a scullery maid has frozen with a tray in her hands. Tell me what you do.
:::

:::rule
**The scene.**

**Stakes:** *Get the paintings out*, Challenge 4. In the way: *Three of Them, Heavy Load*. If they fail, the souls stay in the paint and Vane moves them somewhere harder to reach. *Leave no trace*, Challenge 3, with a clock at d8. In the way: *Watched* and one hidden tag.

**Threats:** Two **Iron Wardens**, only half-woken: Bosses, run a size small at Challenge 5, and too sluggish yet for the multipliers a fully woken Warden has. Keeps it going: *Armoured, Fortified, Old,* and *Bound to a Place*. The GM gives them *Reach* for their spears. One hidden tag. The **scullery maid**: Challenge 2, *Overlooked, Knows the House*.

**Scene clock:** *The household wakes*, at d8.

**Ground:** *Candlelight, Narrow Stair*, and one hidden tag.

**Telegraphs:** about 5 steps a round for each of the four, so about 20.
:::

That's four keeps-it-going tags each, so grinding a Warden down will take four wins. The crew doesn't know yet that there's a faster way. Bram's Defend is a d8 and Vesk's is a d4, and Dorrie's and Isolde's are both d2s. Everyone's wound die is on d2, and Bram's *Unyielding* means the first Hurt he takes this scene won't move his. Everyone's got 5 Aura and 2 Resolve, and they all start the scene on d6 Confidence, apart from Isolde, whose *Iron Will* starts her on d8.

### Round 1: The telegraph {#round-1-the-telegraph}

:::example
**GM:** The left Warden closes in to pin Bram against the wall, that's a d10. As it passes, it sweeps its spear low at Vesk, a d8. The right Warden drives its spear at Isolde, a d10, and plants itself in the vault doorway, another d10. And the maid's going to bolt up the stair and wake the house. That's a d4, and it's only got one answer: if it lands, the household clock drops two sizes.
:::

That's 5 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 2 = 21 steps, about what four players get, and it's five telegraphs against four actions. The sweep at Vesk's a glancing blow as it goes past, so the GM makes it smaller than it could be.

**Bram** goes first. Isolde's Defend is a d2, there's a spear coming at her, and Bram's a [Guardian](#a-guardian).

:::example
**Bram:** Isolde's right beside me, well inside my stride, so I'm intercepting that spear. And I'm Defending.

**GM:** You're a Guardian, so the intercept's free, and it doesn't touch your action. The spear at Isolde comes at you instead, along with the pin. Vesk's too far off, and the doorway's aimed at the stake, not a friend, so those stay put. When they land, every line on your Defend counts twice.
:::

**Isolde** studies the right Warden.

:::example
**Isolde:** My Study is my scholar's Title, *know the history of anything old* and *Iron Will*, so that's 4 steps with the action. This binding's my order's work, though, so I'll bring my other Title in for 1 Aura. That's 5. I'll split it: a d6 for what's holding it together, and a d4 for what else it's hiding.

**GM:** Nobody's gone at it yet this round, so roll its d10 too. Whatever it rolls stands till the round's over.

**Isolde:** It's a 3. My d6 is a 5, and the d4 comes up 4, its top face, so that's my Aura back.

**GM:** Both beat it. *Bound to a Place* is its weak point. There's a sigil cut into the floor under each Warden, and if you break the sigil, it falls. Its hidden tag is *A Person Inside*. Someone was built into that armour a hundred years ago.
:::

That changes the whole fight, because now nobody has to grind through four tags. Isolde isn't done, either.

:::example
**Isolde:** I'm spending 1 Resolve for an extra action, and I'm putting a ward on Vesk. My Ward is my Title and my chalk and salt, 3 steps.

**GM:** Nothing's pushing back on a ward, so there's nothing to roll. Vesk's *Warded d6*, and that's a step more on her Defend while it lasts.
:::

A ward reaches one friend. Isolde could have split it and put the second die on Bram, because she can touch him, so a d4 for Vesk and a d2 for him. Bram's got his plate and his shield, though, so Vesk gets the lot.

**Dorrie** takes the maid.

:::example
**Dorrie:** I'm answering her. "Love, come down. It's only the new locks going in, did nobody tell you?" That's my Talk: my Title, *read a room in a glance* and *make a lie sound like old news*, 4 steps, a d8.

**GM:** She's a d4. Roll it with yours.

**Dorrie:** 6, and her d4's a 3. **GM:** She comes down, shaking, and she's staying.

**Dorrie:** I'll fire *Cold Reader*. What's she feeling? **GM:** Terror. She saw Bram's face on the stair. That's the hidden tag on *Leave no trace*: *A Servant Saw Bram*.
:::

**Vesk** doesn't bother with the Wardens. She goes for the stake.

:::example
**Vesk:** The paintings. Those padlocks are mine. My Locks is my Title, *pick a mechanical lock*, *know how a lock's built* and my picks, with the action 5 steps, a d10. It's all on stripping *Three of Them*.

**GM:** It's Challenge 4, so roll a d8 for it.

**Vesk:** 9, and the d8's a 4. **GM:** Three padlocks open, three canvases cut free, and one roll under your arm. Only *Heavy Load* is left in the way.
:::

**End of Round 1.** The maid was answered, and Bram intercepted the spear at Isolde, so four telegraphs land.

-   **The doorway** (aimed at the stake) lands with no roll. The right Warden fills the vault door, and *Get the paintings out* gains a new tag in the way: *Blocked*.
-   **The pin and the spear at Bram.** He Defended, so the three lines on his Defend, his Title, his plate and his shield, count twice. With the action that's 7 steps, a d12 and a d2, one die for each telegraph. The pin's d10 rolls 10 and the spear's rolls 6, and Bram's d12 rolls 4 and his d2 rolls 1. He spends 1 Aura on his Guardian's +2, so the 4's a 6, and puts it on the spear. The tie goes to him, and it rings off his shield. The 1 can't stop the pin, and the GM names *Pinned*. Bram doesn't want to be stuck against the wall with another Warden coming, so he offers a Hurt instead: it cracks his ribs on the stone, but he's free. It's his first Hurt of the scene, so his *Unyielding* means his wound die doesn't move, and he's still on d2.
-   **The sweep at Vesk.** She rolls her Defend once, a d6 with the ward, and gets 2. The sweep rolls 7, so it gets through, and the GM names a Hurt. Vesk could spend the ward on it, but it's still a step on her Defend and there are more spears coming, so she offers her leathers instead. They split down the side, and she crosses them out until they're mended. That leaves her Defend on 1 step, a d2, or a d4 while the ward lasts.

Nobody worked *Leave no trace*, so its clock steps down to a d6. *The household wakes* steps down to a d6 as well, and Vesk's ward fades to a d4. Nothing came at anyone's Confidence this round, so it hasn't moved for any of them.

### Round 2: The plan {#round-2-the-plan}

:::example
**GM:** The left Warden comes at Bram again, a d10. The right Warden's braced in the doorway, and it drives its spear back into the vault at Vesk, a d8, out at Dorrie, a d10, and across at Isolde, another d8. Upstairs, a door bangs, so somebody heard. And the portraits along the wall have started to whisper. One of them is saying Isolde's name, and that's a d6 at her Confidence.
:::

That's 5 + 4 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 21 steps. A Warden only aims one die at each of you, so the right one spreads its spear around the crew. The portraits were the hidden ground tag: *Portraits That Watch*.

The table talks it through. The sigil under the left Warden is the target, and Bram's right next to it.

:::example
**Isolde:** I'll break the sigil, but not with that thing standing on it.

**Bram:** Then I'll shift it. I'm putting my action in with yours: I heave it off the sigil and hold it, and you cut the line.

**GM:** That's one thing, so it's a combo. What have you got?

**Isolde:** My Ward, 3 steps. **Bram:** Lift and carry, my Title and *lift what two men can't*, 3 as well. **Isolde:** So it's 6 between us, a d12, and the whole lot goes on the sigil.

**Bram:** And the spears going at Dorrie and Isolde, I'm intercepting those. They're both in my stride.

**GM:** Free for a Guardian, so they're yours. You're not Defending this round, though, so when they land you'll roll your Defend once, and one die only stops one.
:::

Isolde rolls for the combo. Nobody's gone at the left Warden yet this round, so its d10 rolls too, and it comes up 9. The d12 comes up 11. The chalk bites through the old line and the left Warden stops mid-step. The armour empties out like a coat coming off a hook, and clatters to the marble, and its d10 at Bram goes with it.

One Warden's down, and three of its four keeps-it-going tags weren't even touched, because the sigil was its weak point. Taking a threat down sends your Confidence up a size, so Bram's on d8 now and Isolde's on d10.

**Vesk** has the bundle, but the doorway's *Blocked* and there's a spear coming.

:::example
**Vesk:** I want out through the skylight in the vault. The vault's three storeys tall and I've got a grappling line.

**GM:** That strips *Blocked*, because you don't need the door. But hauling three canvases up a rope is *Heavy Load*.

**Vesk:** My Move, then: my Title and the grappling line. *Run the rooftops* doesn't come into it till I'm up there, so I'll leave it out. That's 3 steps, a d6, on *Blocked*. ...5, and the stake's d8 is a 4. **GM:** You're up the rope before the Warden turns around.

**Vesk:** Nothing on my sheet's any good for hauling. **Bram:** Stay up there. I'll get it to you next round.
:::

**Dorrie's** action goes on the stake nobody's watching: *Leave no trace*. She sits the maid down and gives her a story to tell. Her Talk is 4 steps, a d8, against *A Servant Saw Bram*. The stake is Challenge 3, so it rolls a d6, and her 5 beats its 2. The maid saw a stranger, and she'll swear to it.

**End of Round 2.** The left Warden's gone, so its d10 at Bram never lands. The rest do.

-   **The spear at Vesk.** She's on the rope when it finds her, because climbing away doesn't stop a telegraph aimed at you. Her leathers are gone, so her Defend's 1 step, and what's left of the ward makes it 2, a d4. She rolls 3, and the d8 rolls 6, so it gets through and the GM names a Hurt. Vesk spends the ward, and it takes the Hurt instead.
-   **The spears at Dorrie and Isolde** come at Bram, because he intercepted them. He rolls his Defend once, a d8, and gets 3. The spears roll 6 and 4, so neither's stopped, and even an 8 would only have stopped one of them. That's two Hurts. His *Unyielding* went on his ribs in round 1, so both of them count, and his wound die goes up two sizes, from d2 to d6.
-   **The portrait's whisper** goes against Isolde's Confidence, a d10 now, and she rolls it once: 2. The d6 rolls 4, so it gets through, and what it costs her is her call. She could bury it and take a Hurt, or spend her last Resolve to hold her ground. She lets it shake her instead, and she's back down to d8. The portrait goes quiet, and keeps looking at her.

The work on *Leave no trace* kept its clock where it was, and *The household wakes* steps down to a d4.

### Round 3: The turn {#round-3-the-turn}

The GM leans in. The right Warden abandons the doorway and comes for Isolde with everything it's got: a d10 at her, a d10 at Bram and a d10 at Vesk's rope. The butler's lantern appears on the main stair, a d8 aimed at *Leave no trace*. Then the GM plays her twist. The portrait that whispered Isolde's name speaks out loud, and it knows the name of the person inside the second Warden.

Isolde doesn't go for the second sigil. She speaks the name instead, which is the other way to end it, because the hidden tag *A Person Inside* is a weak point of its own. Her set action for this is The dead, her first Title and *speak with the recently dead*, 3 steps with the action. A hundred years isn't recent, so speaking to this one is going beyond, and that's 1 Resolve, her last. She brings in *A Person Inside*, a tag she knows, for 1 Aura, and that makes 4 steps, a d8. The right Warden's d10 rolls 6, and her d8 comes up 8, its top face, so she gets 1 Aura back. The right Warden kneels on the marble, and its three telegraphs go with it. Taking it down puts Isolde's Confidence back up to d10.

Bram ties the bundle on and heaves it up the rope, and Vesk puts her action into the same thing, hauling from the top. His Lift and carry is 3 steps, and she's got nothing that fits, so she adds 1 for her action. That's 4 steps, a d8, against the stake's d8: 7 against 3. *Heavy Load* is gone, and the paintings are theirs.

Bram took her spear last round, so Dorrie spends 1 Resolve for an extra action and puts it into him first. She's no Healer, so she can't make a heal roll, but a quick heal's for anyone: 1 Aura and the action, no roll, and his wound die steps down from d6 to d4. Then she meets the butler at the foot of the main stair as the housekeeper, with a smile and a story about the wind and a loose shutter. That's her Pass as someone else: her second Title, *pass as someone I'm not*, *keep my story straight under questions* and her signet pin, 5 steps. She splits it into a d6 to answer the lantern and a d4 to strip *Watched*. The lantern's d8 rolls 4 against her 6, and the stake's d6 rolls 3 against her 4. That's two top faces, which fills her Aura with one to spare, so the spare goes to Bram. *Leave no trace* is theirs too.

At the end of the round the household clock steps down to its last die. When it runs out there'll be lamps lit on every floor, but by then the crew will be three roofs away, and the maid will be swearing she saw a stranger.

When the scene ends, everyone rests. Bram's the only one whose wound die isn't on d2, so he rolls the Confidence he finished on, a d8, against his d4 wound: 6 against 3, and he's back on d2 for the next scene. Then everyone's Confidence goes back to d6, and Isolde and Dorrie get 1 Resolve back each. Vesk's leathers stay crossed out until she gets them mended in downtime.

### What the fight taught {#what-the-fight-taught}

-   **The crew never ground a Warden down.** They studied, found a weak point and a hidden tag, and took both routes.
-   **The stakes mattered more than the enemies.** Vesk spent her whole first round on padlocks instead of spears, and it won the scene.
-   **Every cost was out in the open.** Bram traded a pin for a Hurt his *Unyielding* soaked up, Vesk gave up her leathers to keep her ward, and Isolde chose to let the portrait shake her rather than spend her last Resolve. Nobody lost anything they didn't see coming.
-   **One die stops one.** In round 1 Bram Defended, so he had a die for each of the two telegraphs at him. In round 2 his action went on the sigil, so he had one d8 for two spears, and it cost him two Hurts.
-   **One action each made them work together.** The sigil fell to a combo, Bram intercepted the spears at Dorrie and Isolde for free, and Dorrie spent her extra action patching him up.

Isolde doesn't take a Scar this time. She takes something better: the name of the person who was inside the armour, and the knowledge that there are more of them in this house.
