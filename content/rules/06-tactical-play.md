:::quote
Every round's a puzzle with a clock on it, and you get to see all the pieces before you move.
:::

In most games, tactics means finding a bonus: flank for +2, high ground for +1. Zi doesn't work like that. Before anyone moves, the GM puts everything that's coming this round on the table: what it's doing, who it's aimed at, and how hard. You can see the whole problem. The trouble is, you've got more problems than dice.

So in Zi, tactics comes down to choices. What do you answer, and who answers it? What do you let ride, and onto whose Armour? What do you ignore completely, because the stake matters more than the spear? How do you shape your dice? And where do you stand when the round ends?

This chapter's about those choices. The rules themselves are in [The Core](#the-core) and [Conflict](#conflict), apart from movement and range, which live here. The rest of the chapter is about using them well, whether you're on a grid or playing it in your head.

## The Five Choices of a Round {#s-five-choices}

1.  **Answer.** Which telegraphs do you stop? An answer can stop anything, whether it's a blow, an alarm or a slammed door, but if it fails, you've walked straight into it.
2.  **Let ride.** Which telegraphs do you take on your Armour or Confidence, and whose? A d12 Armour can shrug off a d4, but a d2 Armour shrugs off almost nothing.
3.  **Press.** What do you do with the rest? Stakes, clocks, weak points, the thing the scene's actually about.
4.  **Shape.** One big die, several intents, or advantage? Small dice for small jobs (and Aura)?
5.  **Stand.** Where are you when the round ends? Who can reach you, who can you reach, and who are you guarding?

Make all five on purpose and it's a good round. The great rounds are the ones where one action solves two problems.

## Movement and Range {#s-movement}

:::quote
You play Zi on a grid first, where 1 square is 1 unit, about five feet.
:::

-   **Stride is free.** Every character has a base **stride of 3 units**. Every round you can move your stride without spending an action, whether that's before, between or after your actions.
-   **Move action.** Spend an action to go further. Build a die the usual way from whatever makes you fast here (*Athlete*, *Acrobat*, a Title, a grappling line) plus the action, and roll it. The number you roll is how many extra units you move. Split it into several dice and they add together. Movement's the one roll in Zi where dice add.
-   **Diagonals count as one.** It keeps things quick.
-   **Difficult ground**, like deep snow, a slick floor or a crowd, costs 2 units a square. *Sure-Footed* ignores it.
-   **Traits and items change stride**: *Light on my Feet* adds 2 units, and a *Heavy* item takes 1 away, to a minimum of 0.
-   **Pulling something out of your bag** costs an action, but putting it away is free. See [Carrying and bags](#s-carrying).

:::rule
**No reactions, no opportunity attacks.** You can walk right past a guard and nothing lashes out at you for moving. Threats control space with their *telegraphs*, like *cut off the way to the door* or *grab whoever runs*, so you always know what moving will cost before you move. Moving doesn't get you out of a telegraph aimed at you, though. That one lands wherever you end up, and diving behind cover once it's aimed is a way of answering it, so roll for it. Only a telegraph aimed at a place, like a fire or a falling chandelier, can be walked out of.
:::

### Reach and range {#reach-and-range}

How far a bow, a thrown knife or a spell reaches is the story's call. The bands are a guide, so don't go getting a ruler out.

| Band | Grid | What reaches it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Close** | Next to you (1 unit) | Fists, blades, a whisper, a hand on the shoulder. |
| **Reach** | 2 units | Spears, polearms, whips: anything with *Reach*. |
| **Near** | Across a room, or down a street | Thrown and Ranged weapons: knives, slings, shortbows, pistols. Most magic. A shout. |
| **Far** | Across a town square, or a field | Longbows and crossbows: anything *Long*. Big magic. |
| **Beyond** | Further | The story decides: a siege engine, a sniper's rifle, a working that spans the city. |

**Line of sight** comes from the story. If something solid's in the way, you can't hit through it, and neither can they. That's what cover is. It decides what's possible, and it doesn't add steps. Stand behind a wall and the archer on the roof has to telegraph at someone else, or come down.

:::example[Movement at the table]
Vesk has *Light on my Feet* and *Heavy* leathers, so her stride is 3 + 2 − 1 = 4. In one round she moves those 4 units for free, then spends an action to Move across the rooftops. Her Title and her grappling line both fit, plus the action, so she's on three steps, a d6. She rolls a 5, and that's 9 units in total.

Bram's got a *Heavy* war hammer, so his stride is 3 − 1 = 2. He moves 2 units for free, then spends a Move action. *Athlete* fits, plus the action, which makes two steps, a d4. He rolls a 3, for 5 units in total. He's slow, and that's the price he pays for the hammer.
:::

## Weapons and What They Let You Do {#s-weapons-tactics}

Every weapon is an item with a permission, and it adds its step whenever it fits. On top of that, weapons come with **properties**, free with the weapon. They're the things a weapon lets you do that bare hands can't. The full list is in [Step 4: Items](#s-weapon-props). At the table they sort into four jobs:

-   **Controlling distance**: *Reach, Thrown, Ranged, Long.* You're hitting them from where they can't hit back. A spear holds a doorway, and a longbow holds a whole street.
-   **Getting through**: *Piercing, Breaker.* A crossbow bolt doesn't care about *Armoured*, and a war hammer doesn't care about *Shield*. When everything keeping a threat going is something your weapon ignores, one win takes it out.
-   **Holding and moving**: *Grapple, Sweep, Charge.* Pin one enemy where the others can't help it, or go at a whole line at once.
-   **Surprise**: *Concealed, Quick.* It's the knife nobody found, drawn without spending an action.

Some properties are costs: *Two-Handed* means no shield, and *Reload* means an action between shots. The heavy hitters carry them for a reason.

## Shaping Your Dice {#s-shaping}

How you split your steps is the most important tactical decision you make every round, and it's the one new players rush. Before you roll, look at the defences you're rolling into.

-   **One big die** against a hard defence. A d6 isn't going to beat a d12 Warden very often, but a d12 will, sometimes. Don't split into dice that can't win.
-   **Several intents** against a soft target, or a target with a lot of problems. Three d8s on a guard captain can disarm him, drop him and break his nerve in one round. Each defence is rolled separately, so each die only has to beat its own.
-   **Advantage** when one thing has to happen. Say the sigil has to break this round. Put two dice on it, and the best one counts.
-   **Answer and act** in one roll. A fighter's eight steps can be a d8 to parry the blow coming at her and a d8 to strip the brute's shield, both from the same swing.
-   **A small die for a small job.** Put a d2 on keeping an eye on the door. It'll probably fail, but half the time it lands on its top face, and that's an Aura back.
-   **An area die when there are many of them.** A d6 of fire and a d4 of area beats one d10 on one man when there are five men.

:::rule
**Tags decide your targets.** A threat defends every intent with its Challenge die, so its tags never make your roll harder, unless it's *Layered*. What tags do decide is which win matters most. Strip *Reach* and the spear stops outranging you. Strip what keeps it going and it falls. Go after what drives it and you might not need to fight at all.
:::

## Answer, Guard or Let It Ride {#s-answer-or-ride}

You deal with every telegraph aimed at the crew in one of three ways.

-   **Answer it** when it's big, when your defence is small, or when it does something Armour can't stop, like raising the alarm, grabbing the painting or slamming the door. If a friend's on a d2 Armour, answer for them. You can also answer by taking out whatever telegraphed it, or by taking away what it needs: disarm the crossbowman and his bolt never flies.
-   **Guard** when several things are coming at one person. One action of guarding adds its steps to every defence roll that person makes at the end of the round. When four blades are aimed at the same shield, it's the best trade in the game.
-   **Let it ride** when your defence is bigger than the die, or when you've got something better to do. If it lands, the GM names the cost, and you can offer another one that costs about as much.

Offering a different cost is tactics too. A step off your Armour now makes the next round worse, and a Hurt now brings you closer to a Blaze. A step back on your Aspiration costs nothing in this fight, but it costs you something in the long game. The party Goal belongs to everyone, so ask before you spend it.

## Tags Are the Battlefield {#s-tags-battlefield}

You never need to ask how hurt something is. Read its tags instead. If it's got Tough, that's its hit points, and the GM says what's left every time it drops.

-   **Keeps it going**: how many wins it takes. Four of them makes for a long night, so look for another way.
-   **Comes at you**: what its telegraphs can do. Strip *Reach* and its spear stops outranging you. Strip *Commanding* and every one of its followers telegraphs a step smaller.
-   **Drives it**: how to end the fight without winning it. You can buy the greedy ones, let the frightened ones go, and show the loyal ones what their master really did.
-   **Hidden**: there's always more on a boss than you can see. Spend an action to Study. It's almost never wasted.

### Openings {#openings}

An **opening** is how a big threat falls in one blow, and there are three kinds.

-   **Its weak point.** Find it, then hit it. It's the only opening that ends a Boss in one blow.
-   **It's helpless.** It's bound, asleep, or there's a knife already at its back from someone it never saw coming. Get there first and the next one of you finishes it. Against a Boss it tears two tags away instead. That won't finish a Boss off, but it's a big step towards it.
-   **Getting around everything that keeps it going.** Shove the shielded, fortified captain off a narrow bridge into the river, and neither his shield nor his position does anything for him. Use the ground, use the right weapon, and think about what its tags actually protect it from.

### Conditions are teamwork {#s-conditions-tactics}

A condition is a win you can save for later. Once it's on, decide whether to keep the passive or spend it now.

-   **Stack the passives.** A threat that's *Exposed*, *Blinded* and *Taunted* is up to three steps easier for everyone, on every roll those conditions fit, until they fade.
-   **Spend before it fades, or before it's broken.** The die shrinks every round. When the GM telegraphs a threat shaking one off, that's your cue. Spend it on your turn and it never gets the chance.
-   **Burning, Bleeding and Poisoned strip tags with no roll.** Against a d12 defence, that's worth more than any swing.
-   **Look after each other.** A ward, a blessing or a rallying shout costs you one action and saves a friend's Hurt box. Add an area die and it reaches as many friends as it rolls. Every action you spend on a friend is one you're not spending on the stakes, so pick your moment.

## Stakes Beat Bodies {#s-stakes-tactics}

Most scenes have two or three stakes and a clock, and there are never enough actions to do everything. Crews coming from other games tend to kill everything first and then do the job, and in Zi that loses. While you're grinding through a Warden's fourth tag, the household wakes, the ship sails and the witness bleeds out.

Every round, ask yourself: *what's this scene actually about?* Put your dice there. Send one character to hold the enemies off while the others work the stakes, and let the fight be the distraction.

## Aura and Spark {#s-aura-tactics}

-   **Spend it.** Aura refills every scene, so a full tank at the end of a scene is Aura you never used.
-   **Farm it, and share it.** Every spare d2 is a coin toss for an Aura. When a roll has steps to spare, peel off a d2 with a small intent. If you're already full, the Aura goes to a friend.
-   **Ties matter.** A tie on the roll that ends the fight is worth an Aura, every time.
-   **Stretch at the right moment.** A tag you know, a chandelier cut loose, a friend's permission: one Aura, one more permission on the roll. Spend it when that extra permission changes the result.
-   **Your Spark isn't Aura.** You only ever hold one. Spend it when it changes the story, then earn the next one by being worth watching, or by taking trouble.

## Roles Come From Your Sheet {#s-roles}

Zi doesn't have classes, but every crew finds its roles, and every character plays several:

-   **The wall.** Big Armour, *Defender*, a shield. You answer for others, guard, and hold the narrow place.
-   **The blade.** Big dice on one target. You strip tags, take openings and end things.
-   **The opener.** Lots of small dice that disarm, trip, blind, burn and expose. You put conditions on threats for the blade to spend.
-   **The keeper.** Wards, blessings, a steady voice. You put conditions on friends and keep their Hurt boxes empty.
-   **The eye.** *Scholar, Investigator, Tracker, Empathy.* You study, find weak points and hidden drives, and tell everyone else where to aim.
-   **The voice.** You talk threats down, turn them, buy them, and end fights that were never worth having.
-   **The runner.** You work the stakes and the clocks while everyone else is busy.
-   **The storm.** Fire, a sweeping blade, a shout that carries. You put one roll on the whole room with an area die.

## A Round on the Grid {#s-grid-example}

Bram, Vesk, Ash and Wren are taking a wagon over a toll bridge, and the bandits who hold the bridge have other ideas. There are four characters with one Title each, so the GM's budget is 4 × 10 = 40 steps a round.

![Grid map of the toll bridge at the start of round one](../figures/tactical-play-1.svg)

*The toll bridge, round one. One square is one unit. B Bram, V Vesk, A Ash, W Wren on a rock on the west bank, her line of fire to the captain dashed. 1–3 bandits. C the toll captain in the toll-house doorway. The bandits' cart blocks the east end of the bridge, and the anchor ropes behind it are burning.*

:::rule
**The scene.**

**Stake:** *Get the wagon across*, Challenge 3. In the way: *Blocked*.

**Scene clock:** *The bridge burns*, d8. When it runs out, the bridge falls.

**The toll captain**, Challenge 4. Comes at you: *At Range* (a crossbow), *Commanding*. Keeps him going: *Shield*, *Fortified* (the doorway). Drives him: one hidden tag.

**Three bandits**, Challenge 2. Comes at you: *Belligerent*. Drives them: *One of Many*. Nothing keeps them going, so it's one good win each.

**Ground:** *Narrow Bridge* (two abreast, no more), *River Below*.
:::

:::example
**GM:** The captain levels his crossbow at Ash, a d8. Bandits one and two hack at Bram, a d6 each. That's a step bigger because the captain's watching, and *Commanding* says so. Bandit three winds up to hurl a torch into the wagon's straw, a d6 at the stake, and it's got one answer: if it lands, the wagon's on fire. And the fire's running along the ropes, so *The bridge burns* will tick at the end of the round.
:::

So that's 4 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 13 steps, and the GM holds the rest back. It's round one, and the bandits think they've got this.

The crew looks at the map. There are four problems: the bolt at Ash, two blades at Bram, a torch at the wagon, and the fire. And there's one opportunity nobody's said out loud yet: the river.

**Wren** goes first, from her rock. She hasn't moved, so *Marksman's Eye* is live. She's got *Marksman*, *longbow*, the bow itself and what it does best (*hit anything I can see*), her Title and the trait, plus the action. That's 7 steps an action and 14 for both, so she's rolling two d12s and a d4. The captain's 11 squares away across the water, which is *Far*, and a longbow reaches Far.

:::example
**Wren:** A d12 and the d4 on bandit three, to take him down before he throws. The other d12 goes at the captain's crossbow. I'm shooting it out of his hands.

**GM:** Roll bandit three's d4 with yours, and the captain's d8.

**Wren:** 7 and 2 on bandit three, and 9 on the crossbow. His d4's a 2, and the captain's d8 is a 6. **GM:** Bandit three drops his torch into the river and follows it in. The crossbow spins off the toll-house porch into the water, and his bolt at Ash never flies.
:::

Wren's answered two telegraphs without rolling a single answer. She took away what they needed instead.

**Ash** isn't being shot at any more, so she turns to the fire.

:::example
**Ash:** I don't want to slow it. I want it *out*, all of it, in one go.

**GM:** It's thirty feet of pitch-soaked rope, burning at both anchors, and the fire's a d8. Beat it everywhere it burns and it goes out for good, instead of just slowing down.

**Ash:** *Evocation*, *fire*, the ember stone and my Title, twice, plus the actions. That gets me ten steps. A d12 on the fire, and the d8 for my area, a line along the ropes.

**GM:** Roll the fire's d8 with yours.

**Ash:** 9 on the d12, against the fire's 6. My d8 comes up 4, so that's a line eight squares long, and it covers all of it. **GM:** The flames lean towards you like dogs called to heel, then go out. *The bridge burns* is over.
:::

**Vesk** has the best idea at the table.

:::example
**Vesk:** The captain's *Fortified* because he's in a doorway looking at the bridge. So I go over the side on my grappling line, along the underside of the bridge, and up behind him. I've got my stride of 4, then a Move action with my Title and the line, plus the action. That's a d6.

**GM:** You need 6 squares to get round behind him. **Vesk:** I rolled a 4, plus 4 on the free stride, so that's eight. I'm behind him.

**Vesk:** Second action. I lift his own knife off his belt and put it to the small of his back, with a whisper in his ear. I want him *Exposed*. *Stealth*, *Combat* with my *dagger* specialization, and my Title, plus the action, so I'm rolling a d10.

**Vesk:** 5, and his d8's a 3. **GM:** He goes very still, and he's *Exposed d10*.
:::

**Bram** holds the bridge. *Narrow Bridge* means only two bandits can reach him at a time, and both of them are.

:::example
**Bram:** First action, I guard. *Defender*, my shield and my Title, plus the action. That's four steps on top of my Armour d6, so against both blades I'm rolling a d12 and a d2 and keeping the best.

**Bram:** Second action, bandit one goes in the river. *Combat*, *war hammer*, the hammer and my Title, plus the action. Five steps, a d10.

**GM:** He defends with a d4. **Bram:** 9, and his d4's a 1. **GM:** He goes over the rail. Splash.
:::

**End of Round 1.** Bandit one's telegraph went into the river with him. Bandit two's d6 rolls a 5 against Bram's guarded d12 and d2, which come up 8 and 1, so nothing lands. The fire's out, so nothing ticks. The captain's *Exposed* steps down to a d8.

### Round 2: The opening {#round-2-the-opening}

:::example
**GM:** The captain drops his shield and grabs for the knife in Vesk's hand, a d8 at her. Bandit two, alone on the bridge, swings wild at Bram. It's a d4 now, with his captain in no state to command anyone.
:::

The GM's still got 40 steps, but there's nothing left worth spending them on, which means you're winning.

:::example
**Vesk:** I spend the *Exposed*, so he loses an action getting his guard back. **GM:** Every time he shifts his weight, your knife moves with him. His telegraph at you is gone.
:::

And he never saw her coming. With her knife already at his back he's helpless, and that's an opening. The next win against him takes him out, whatever he's got left. But her d8 against his d8 is a coin toss.

:::example
**Wren:** I'll help. I've got an arrow on the string, aimed at his face. "Don't." *Marksman*, *longbow*, the bow and what it does best, my Title, *Marksman's Eye* and the action. That's seven steps to Vesk.

**Vesk:** *Combat*, *dagger*, my Title and the action make my four. Add Wren's seven and I've got eleven steps, a d12 and a d10. Both go on "yield, now", so that's advantage.

**Vesk:** 11, and his d8's a 7. **GM:** He yields. Sweating, he tells you the toll money's in the strongbox upstairs, and it can all be yours. That's his hidden tag: *Can Be Bought*.
:::

Bandit two sees his captain on his knees and remembers he's *One of Many*, so he jumps. Nobody rolls for it, because the story's already decided it. Bram and Ash put their shoulders to the bandits' cart and strip *Blocked*, and the wagon rolls across.

**Two rounds.** Nobody on the crew got hurt. The captain had two tags keeping him going, and he fell to one win because Vesk got behind him and Wren made it a sure thing. The fire would have dropped the whole bridge in four rounds, and it went out because Ash spent her steps on reaching all of it instead of slowing it down. And the river did more damage than any weapon.

## Playing Without a Grid {#s-theatre}

You don't need a map. Use the bands instead of squares.

-   **Close, Reach and Near** are within your free stride of each other, so moving between them costs nothing.
-   **Far** takes a Move action to cross.
-   **Beyond** takes more than one round.

When it matters where someone is, like who's in the doorway or who can reach the lever, say it out loud and the GM writes it on a card in the middle of the table. A rough sketch on scrap paper counts as a grid too.

## Five Mistakes Every New Crew Makes {#s-tactics-mistakes}

1.  **Killing everything first.** The scene has stakes, so work them.
2.  **Splitting into dice that can't win.** Three d4s against a d10 are three long shots. Look at the defence before you split.
3.  **Answering everything.** A failed answer lands with no defence, so let the small ones ride onto big Armour.
4.  **Never studying.** Every boss has something hidden, and it's usually the way to win.
5.  **Sitting on a full tank of Aura.** It refills, so spend it.
