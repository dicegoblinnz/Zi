:::quote
Everyone's got a soul, and a soul can be spent.
:::

Zi'Dara starts with *Zi*, the Sumerian word for soul. In Zi'Dara, magic is a permission, like a sword or a silver tongue. It runs on the same rules as everything else, and it grows the same way: more steps, bigger dice, and then more dice.

## Magic Is a Permission {#s-magic}

-   **You need a magic permission or a magic item** to do magic at all. *Throw fire*, *change my face* and *speak with the recently dead* are permissions like any other, but magic's written narrow: *throw fire*, not *fire magic*. That's because a magic permission is what lets you do that magic at all, and it does what it says for free. See [Writing permissions](#s-writing-permissions).
-   **A school of magic is a Title.** Ash's first Title is *The last fire-speaker of the Ember Court*, and that's the Fire school: Soul, with Fire, Intimidation and Court lore. Her [archetypes](#s-how-archetypes) are a separate pick, and the Elementalist's what lets her fire fill a room. It's one school per Title, so pick the one you're known for, because *Archmage* is too wide. Every permission has to fit inside its Title, so Ash's fire-speaker can have *throw fire* and *feel every flame in a room*, and her other Title, *The Court's runaway heir*, can't.
-   **A magic item works the same way.** Ash's ember stone is attuned, and it lets her call fire without a word or a gesture. It's 1 more when she throws fire, and its awakening, *Fire without a word*, adds to one of her dice for every Aura she's pumped into it. A magic item lets you do the magic it's for, school or no school. See [Attunement](#s-attunement).
-   **Every permission does what it says, for free.** Ash's fire is her Soul and her Fire, with her ember stone, 10 steps, a d12 and a d8, and no Aura. If *throw fire* covers it, whether it's a bolt at a man or a rope burning through, it doesn't cost a thing.
-   **Going beyond what a permission normally does costs 1 Aura**, the same as it does with a sword. Phasing's free until you phase through a wall. If you have to make a case for why it's normal, it's going beyond, so pay the Aura and keep playing. There's more in [Going beyond](#s-going-beyond).
-   **Areas are an Elementalist's.** A fireball that fills a room is an area, and an [Elementalist](#a-elementalist) makes one for free. Everyone else puts their magic on one target, or splits it. See [Area magic](#s-magic-area), below.
-   **Anything that works on its own is [summoning](#s-summoning)**, and only a Summoner does that. Raising the dead to fight or carry something as part of your own action is your permission doing what it says, and it's free. One of the dead that gets up and works for you on its own is a summon.

It's exactly the same with a sword. A master of combat fights for free, and an aimed shot that takes out the tag you choose costs 1 Aura.

How far magic reaches is the story's call. A fireball can land across the town square, but burning hands need you close. A threat that's *Warded* is protected against magic the way *Armoured* is against steel: it's one more reason the threat's still standing.

Going beyond lets your permission do the bigger thing. If something's in the way, it's still in the way, and you've still got to beat it.

:::example[Magic at the table]
**For free:** A guard levels a crossbow at Ash across the warehouse. She throws fire at him with her Soul and her Fire, and her ember stone, 10 steps, a d12 and a d8. A bolt of fire at a man is what *throw fire* does, so it doesn't cost her anything.

**Going beyond:** In the Vane crypt, Isolde wants a word with the mason who built the vault, a hundred years dead. That's her Soul and The dead, 5 steps, a d10. A hundred years isn't recent, so that's going beyond, and she pays 1 Aura. The old man doesn't want to come, and the GM makes him Challenge 3, a d6. Isolde rolls 5 against his 4, and he starts talking.

**Bigger, with a friend:** The city gate is Challenge 5, a d10, and two tags keep it shut: *Iron* and *Barred*. *Throw fire* doesn't normally make iron give, so Ash pays 1 Aura to go beyond. Bram's going at the gate with his war hammer, and Ash puts her action in with his, so it's a combo, on his Body. His Body, his Lifting and his hammer are 8 steps, and Ash's Fire and her ember stone are 4 more. That's 12 between them, two d12s, one for *Iron* and one for *Barred*, and Bram rolls for both. The gate hasn't been attacked yet this round, so it rolls its d10 once, a 5, and both dice have to beat it. The first comes up 7, so *Iron*'s gone. The second comes up 5, a tie, and the gate keeps a tie unless he pays, so Bram pays 1 Aura, the bar splits, and the gate swings open.
:::

### Area magic {#s-magic-area}

A fireball that fills a room or a storm that rakes a street is an area, and areas are an [Elementalist](#a-elementalist)'s. One of your dice can be your area instead of an intent: a burst (that many squares out from a point), a cone (that long the way you face, and as wide at the end) or a line (twice as far, straight out). It takes one of your splits, and it doesn't cost Aura. Everyone the story puts in it defends against your other die, and you can always make it smaller. If it comes up short, your Push puts +2 on your area die for 1 Resolve. If your friends are in it, *Sculpt* makes it skip one of them for 1 Resolve, and another for each 1 more. There's more on the shapes in [Area](#s-area).

Every other caster puts their magic on one target, or splits it: a d6 at the captain and a d4 at the rope he's cutting, or a ward on one friend and a second ward on another friend you can touch.

:::example
**A line:** Say you're a storm-caller, an Elementalist with a Soul of 4 and Storm 2. You throw lightning down the corridor, 6 steps split into a d8 for the bolt and a d4 for a line. The d4 comes up 2, a line four squares long, but the third guard's six squares back, so you pay 1 Resolve for your Push and it's a 4: eight squares. That's the d4's top face, but the +2 got it there, so there's no Aura back. All three guards defend against your d8.

**A split:** The volley's coming, and Bram and Dorrie are pressed up against Isolde. Her ward's her Soul and her Warding, with her chalk and salt, 6 steps, and she splits it into a d8 and a d4. Nothing pushes back, so nothing's rolled. Bram's *Warded d8*, and Dorrie, close enough to touch, is *Warded d4*. See [Wards and blessings](#s-wards).
:::

## Schools of Magic {#s-schools}

A school of magic is a Title, the same as a role. Here are fifteen to start from. Each one shows the archetypes that make the most of it, a few things its permissions do for free, and what going beyond looks like, for 1 Aura. Pick one, write your Title for it, real and specific, with a Root and three specialties, and choose three permissions that fit inside it. Your archetypes are a separate pick. There's more on archetypes in [How archetypes work](#s-how-archetypes), and you'll find the full ideas for every school, with Titles and permissions, in [Title ideas](#s-title-ideas).

Where a school suits more than one archetype, pick the one that fits how you play it. Warding on a Guardian stands in front of the crew, and Warding on an Oracle sees how the dice will fall and decides who gets the good ones.

| School | You might be | Suits | For free | Going beyond, for 1 Aura |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fire** | a pyromancer or fire-speaker | Elementalist | *throw fire* · *light a room with a word* · *burn through rope or wood* | become a column of flame · see through a fire on the other side of the city |
| **Storm** | a storm-caller or wind-witch | Elementalist | *call lightning down on a man* · *throw a gust that knocks men flat* · *fill a sail* | ride the wind · call a storm out of a clear sky |
| **Illusion** | an illusionist or trickster | Illusionist | *make a false sound* · *change my face* · *hide in plain sight* | duplicate myself · an image that fools the whole street |
| **Enchantment** | an enchanter or mesmer | Face | *calm someone with a word* · *make someone like me* · *put a moment of fear in someone* | make someone forget the last minute · put someone to sleep with a word |
| **Divination** | a seer, oracle or scryer | Oracle | *know if someone's lying* · *find something lost in a room* · *see an object's past* | see a place far away · glimpse what's coming |
| **Wildcraft** | a druid, beast-talker or green witch | Shifter | *calm an animal* · *make a vine hold* · *speak with birds* | wear a beast's shape for the scene · make a plant grow in a moment |
| **Warding** | a warder, sigil-maker or ritualist | Guardian, or Oracle | *ward a friend against harm* · *bar a door with a sigil* · *keep a spirit out of a room* | seal a room so nothing gets in or out · unpick a ward someone powerful made |
| **Mending** | a mender, healer-mage or bone-setter | Healer | *stop bleeding with a touch* · *draw out a poison* · *mend a broken object* | keep the dying alive till dawn · mend a whole wall or a ship's hull |
| **Veil** | a shadow-mage or night-walker | Shadow | *snuff the lights* · *silence my steps* · *see in the dark* | open a shadow door to somewhere nearby · make a room forget I was in it |
| **Phase** | a walker-through-walls or blink-mage | Teleporter | *slip out of any grip* · *pass through a crowd without touching anyone* · *step to a place I can see* | step through a wall · take someone with me when I step |
| **Medium** | a medium, spirit-talker or necromancer | Summoner, or Oracle | *speak with the recently dead* · *sense the dead nearby* · *raise the dead to fight or carry things* | call up someone who died long ago · lay a ghost to rest for good |
| **Hexer** | a hexer, curse-witch or bone-caster | Oracle, or Bloodsworn | *make someone stumble* · *spoil food and drink* · *put bad luck on someone* | break a curse someone else laid · curse a bloodline |
| **Faith** | a priest, paladin or holy warrior | Bard, Healer or Guardian | *steady a friend with a prayer* · *bless a friend before a fight* · *hold holy ground* | drive out a spirit · hallow a place so nothing evil can enter |
| **Kinesis** | a mind-mover or force-mage | Elementalist, or Warrior | *shove a man without touching him* · *catch anything that falls* · *lift something heavy with my mind* | fly · stop an arrow in the air |
| **Artifice** | an artificer, sigil-smith or golem-maker | Maker | *read a sigil* · *wake a lock* · *mend a construct* | make a lock only I can open · bind a spirit into an object |

A beast called up to hunt for you through the scene, or one of the dead brought back to work at your command, works on its own, so it's a summon, and only a Summoner can do that (see [Summoning](#s-summoning), below).

### Writing your own school {#writing-your-own}

A school's a Title, so write it like one: who you are, real and specific, and one school only. Give it a Root and three specialties, and three permissions. Write the magic ones narrow, a doing word and the part that says how, what or where, so it's *throw fire*, not *fire magic*. Then think about what going beyond looks like for your school, so the table knows when it's time to spend the Aura. There's more on permissions in [Writing permissions](#s-writing-permissions).

:::example[A school of your own]
Say you want a sea-witch, and none of the fifteen fits. Your school's the tide, and your Title is *The tide-witch the fishing fleet won't sail without*: Soul, with Tides, Weather and Sailing. For permissions you take *calm a rough sea*, *call a wave onto a deck* and *breathe under water*. Each one's something you'd do a few times a session in a campaign at sea, and each one fits inside the Title. You want your waves to sweep a whole deck, and that's an area, so one of your archetypes is the Elementalist. Going beyond is parting the harbour, or sinking a ship with one wave.
:::

### Magic in the World {#s-ritualist}

Magic in Zi'Dara is a craft, and there's no caste or class of wizards, just people with a magic permission or a magic item on their sheet.

**The Book-Bound** learned it from reading, and Isolde's one of them. **The Quiet Orders** are small religious traditions that do one or two things very well and nothing else. **The Practical Crafters** are the alchemists, ward-makers and sigil-carvers. **The Born** could always do it and never knew why. **The Desperate** can be anyone, once, when they need something enough.

How magic sits in society is up to your world. It can sit quietly, where most people don't believe rituals are real and the ones who do keep their voices down. Decide it in [Session Zero](#session-zero), because it decides what happens when the crew uses magic in the street.

## Summoning {#s-summoning}

Only a [Summoner](#a-summoner) catches souls and summons. A Summoner catches souls, then calls them back out to work for her. A summon is anything that works on its own, like a hound that tracks for you, or a caught courtier who'll talk to people you can't.

**Souls go in attunement.** A summoning attunement holds souls the way any attuned thing holds awakenings, a soul to each awakening, and the souls you catch are its material. It sits in one of your [attunement](#s-attunement) slots and grows through the same three stages:

| Stage | Souls | It can hold more | In all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awakened | 1 | 1 more with a Catch | 2 |
| Tempered | 2 | 2 more with a Burden | 4 |
| Perfected | 3 | 3 more with a Burden and a Catch | 6 |

You start at Awakened, like anything you attune, so that's one soul, or two if you give it its Catch. A starting soul's a d4. You start with one attunement slot, so if your summoning attunement's in it, that's your one attuned thing till you get more in downtime. Like any attuned thing, it gets you Aura its own way, which you write when you attune it.

**Catching a soul.** When a creature falls, spend your action and roll against its Challenge, with a Root and a specialty that fit how you catch it. Beat it and its soul goes into one of your utility slots, with its Challenge die. Miss and that soul's gone. In downtime, put it into your summoning attunement. You can swap a soul in your attunement for one you're carrying whenever you've got the slot.

**Calling them out.** Each soul's an active awakening. Spend Aura on it as part of your action, and every Aura's **a d4 of summons**: build them into one summon or several, as you like, but none bigger than that soul's own die. Three Aura on the harbour Warden, a d8, is a d8 Warden and a d4 one, or three d4s. It needs a reason in the story. Nobody calls up a horde to drink at the tavern.

**A soul is what it was.** A caught courtier talks, a hound tracks and a Warden fights. Each summon rolls its own die for whatever it does, and acts in the round like anyone.

**They fade.** Every summon drops a size at the end of every round, and **every hit that gets through to it knocks a size off**. Past d2, it's gone, and its soul's back in your attunement, ready to call again.

**A horde rolls as one throw.** Roll every summon's die together, with an intent each, so ten of them don't take ten turns.

**Intercepting.** A summon [intercepts](#s-intercept) for free, so it doesn't cost Aura or an action. It can take the blows aimed at someone within its stride, as many as you like, and they come at it instead. Like anyone's intercept, it can't take what's at their Mind or their Soul. When they land, it rolls its die to stop one of them, and it's got to beat it or tie. Every one that gets through knocks a size off it, and once it's gone, the rest go on to whoever they were aimed at.

Summons can't combo, and **their dice never give Aura**, top faces and wins included. They all go back at the end of the scene.

:::example[Summoning at the table]
Sable's a Summoner, and she's got three souls: a rat swarm (d4), a hound (d6) and the harbour Warden (d8). Two dockhands come at her with clubs, so she spends 3 Aura as part of her action: 2 on the hound, which can't be bigger than a d6, so it's a d6 hound and a d2 one, and 1 on the rats, a d4 swarm. The swarm goes at the dockhands' feet.

Next round, the clubs come at Sable, a d6 each. The bigger hound intercepts both for her, free, so they come at it instead. When they land, the clubs come up 4 and 3. The hound faded to a d4 at the end of last round, so it rolls a d4: 4. That stops the 4, and the 3 gets through, so it's a d2. At the end of the round it fades again, and it's gone, back in her attunement.

Later the dock boss falls, and Sable spends her action catching his soul: her Soul and her Necromancy, 7 steps, against his Challenge 3, a d6. Her d12 rolls 7 against his 4, and his soul goes into one of her utility slots, a d6. In downtime she can put him in her attunement if there's room, or swap him for the rats.
:::

## Rituals {#rituals}

:::quote
A ritual is magic that's too big for one moment.
:::

Some workings can't be done in a breath. They need a circle, a price and time, and while you work, something's trying to stop you. A ritual's a **stake** with a **price**, and everything in [Conflict](#conflict) applies.

### How a ritual runs {#s-ritual-runs}

1.  **Name the goal.** *To free a soul from its frame.*
2.  **Pay the cost.** Every ritual has a price, paid *before* the first die's thrown: a name spoken aloud, a memory given up, a drop of blood in someone's tea.
3.  **Work it.** The ritual has a Challenge and tags **in the way**, and every win strips one. Strip them all and it's done. Anyone with a permission that fits can work a tag, or put their action into someone else's roll.
4.  **Something interferes.** Most rituals have an **interference die**: something's coming. The GM telegraphs it every round, at the circle or at whoever's working, from the round budget.
5.  **If it fails** (the interference lands on the working, or the scene's clock runs out), the **failure mode** happens. A failed ritual goes wrong in a particular way, and each ritual below tells you how.

There's no Aura to pay for a ritual. The price is what it costs.

If you're out of danger, with all the time in the world, the GM can just let it take an hour and ask for the cost. The dice come out when something's pushing back.

### Twelve Rituals {#twelve-rituals}

:::rule{#rit-unbinding}
**The Unbinding**: to free a soul from its frame.\
**Challenge 6.** In the way: *Names* · *Mercy* · *Old Ink*. Interference d10.\
**Cost:** You speak your own true name aloud.\
**Failure Mode:** The soul is *loosened*: free of the frame, and of everything else.
:::

:::rule{#rit-quiet-word}
**The Quiet Word**: to make one named person believe one named thing for one day.\
**Challenge 4.** Interference d6 if the target has allies.\
**Cost:** A drop of your blood in their tea.\
**Failure Mode:** They believe the opposite for a week.
:::

:::rule{#rit-iron-door}
**The Iron Door**: to seal a room against entry for one night.\
**Challenge 5.**\
**Cost:** A lock of hair from whoever will sleep inside.\
**Failure Mode:** The seal holds, but only against *you*.
:::

:::rule{#rit-debt-called}
**The Debt Called**: to make a person honour an obligation they've tried to forget.\
**Challenge 6.** In the way: *They Forgot*.\
**Cost:** Something they gave you once and now can't have back.\
**Failure Mode:** The debt transfers to you.
:::

:::rule{#rit-named-blow}
**The Named Blow**: to make a weapon strike true against one specific person.\
**Challenge 3.**\
**Cost:** You write their name on the weapon. Every day you do, you lose something you used to have.\
**Failure Mode:** The strike lands on you instead.
:::

:::rule{#rit-empty-hour}
**The Empty Hour**: to make one room forget that you were in it.\
**Challenge 4.**\
**Cost:** You must leave through a door you haven't used before.\
**Failure Mode:** The room forgets you, but everyone remembers where you went.
:::

:::rule{#rit-long-watch}
**The Long Watch**: to see through a linked token from any distance, for one hour.\
**Challenge 5.**\
**Cost:** You can't eat for a day.\
**Failure Mode:** You see the wrong thing.
:::

:::rule{#rit-quiet-passing}
**The Quiet Passing**: to let one recently dead soul speak, once, for a minute.\
**Challenge 3.**\
**Cost:** A question you've been avoiding, spoken aloud.\
**Failure Mode:** The soul doesn't speak. Something else does.
:::

:::rule{#rit-broken-vow}
**The Broken Vow**: to make two people unable to harm each other for one day.\
**Challenge 5.**\
**Cost:** You break one of your own promises, aloud, in front of a witness.\
**Failure Mode:** They can't harm each other, but they both know you cast it.
:::

:::rule{#rit-waking-stone}
**The Waking Stone**: to wake something sleeping inside a stone.\
**Challenge 6.** In the way: *Deep Sleep*. Interference d8.\
**Cost:** A memory that mattered, spoken and then forgotten.\
**Failure Mode:** The thing inside wakes somewhere near you, and it isn't pleased.
:::

:::rule{#rit-gilded-lie}
**The Gilded Lie**: to make a lie true for one hour.\
**Challenge 4.**\
**Cost:** You must believe the lie yourself while you speak it.\
**Failure Mode:** The lie becomes true for a year instead of an hour, and you can't tell anyone.
:::

:::rule{#rit-long-goodbye}
**The Long Goodbye**: to speak to someone who's been dead for years.\
**Challenge 6.** In the way: *Years*. Interference d8.\
**Cost:** A day of your life, given willingly and never returned.\
**Failure Mode:** You speak to something wearing their face.
:::

### Rituals at the Table {#rituals-at-the-table}

Every ritual worth rolling for has three things: something *interfering*, something *at stake*, and something that'll *break* if it fails. If none of those are true, it's just a lock with a fancy name, so let it take an hour and move on.

:::example[The Unbinding, at the Vane manor]
Isolde kneels in the binding room with the three paintings, and the crew gathers round the circle. She speaks her own true name aloud, and the GM starts the interference: Vane's household is at the door, a d10 each round.

Isolde works *Old Ink* with her Mind and her Dead languages, because this binding's in her order's own script. Bram answers the interference at the door. Vesk works *Names*, since she stole these paintings and knows whose they were, and Dorrie puts her action into the same roll: she knows the old families, and she reads their names off the frames. Three rounds later, *Mercy*'s the last tag standing, and it's Isolde's to strip. She has to mean it.
:::
