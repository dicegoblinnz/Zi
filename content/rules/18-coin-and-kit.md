Money in Zi'Dara is simple: a purse of gold is roughly a month's wages.

## What you can afford {#what-you-can-afford}

-   **Nothing worth tracking.** A meal, a bed for the night or a round of drinks.
-   **A purse of gold.** It's one of your [Resources](#s-resources), about a month's wages, and it covers a bribe to a mid-level official, a horse, or a new item at tier 2.
-   **Several purses of gold.** Passage on a ship, or a tier 3 item.
-   **A small fortune.** A ship, a house, or an item at tier 4, if you can find someone good enough to make it.

## Upgrading gear {#upgrading-gear}

Every item has a [tier](#s-item-tiers), from 1 to 6, and that's how many steps it adds to a roll it fits. Here's how one goes up:

| To | How |
| --- | --- |
| Tier 3 | Grow in downtime puts one of your items up a tier, as far as tier 3. |
| Tier 4 | Money. A small fortune and a maker who's good enough, and it's done in a downtime. |
| Tier 5 | Only out of the story, with a material, the way an attuned thing's stages are. |
| Tier 6 | Only out of the story, with a material, and there's only ever one of it. |

A weapon keeps its properties at every tier, so a tier 4 crossbow is still *Long* and *Piercing*. Attuning something never changes its tier, and Aura never buys one. A [Maker](#a-maker) can give a thing tags as well, which last the scene, and their *Tune it up* puts it a tier higher with a making die, but only till the scene ends and only as far as tier 4.

Replacing a broken item, or mending one, takes a downtime slot and usually a purse of gold. Soaking never breaks armour, so it's broken when the GM names "something breaks" as a consequence, and it's crossed out till then (see [Broken items](#recovering-armour)). Attuned things are magic and can't break. Some things can't be replaced, and shouldn't be. Your mother's blade isn't for sale.

:::example[Wren's knife]
Wren's hunting knife is tier 3, and she wants it better. Grow won't take it past 3, so in downtime she spends a small fortune from the last job with a blade-smith in Thornby, and it's tier 4. Skinning a beast or fighting close with it is her Body's 8 and the knife's 4, 12 steps, two d12s. Tier 5 would take a material out of the story, like the tooth of the thing that took her brother.
:::

## Packs {#s-packs}

A **pack** is an item that gives you more [Resources](#s-resources): half its tier, rounded up. It adds no steps, and it takes one of your six. A tier 2 pack's 1 more Resource, and a tier 4 one's 2, so you've got 5.

| Pack | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Quartermaster's pack** | A soldier's kit, rope and rations, and a bit of everything else. |
| **Bandolier** | Loops and pouches for the things you throw, drink or light. |
| **Surgeon's roll** | Bandages, needles and the bottles that go with them. |
| **Smuggler's coat** | Hiding places sewn into the lining, more than anyone would think to search. |
| **Hunter's pack** | Snares, a fire kit and what you need a long way from anywhere. |

What's in the pack is whatever you say when you tick the Resource. The pack's only what makes it believable that you had it.

## Vessels to use {#s-kit-vessels}

A [vessel](#s-vessels) holds its tier in charge, filled with Aura you'd lose or a point at a time mid-scene, which stays held there till it's spent, and spent on your own abilities and awakenings. Here are a few to use straight off the page. Each is an item of its own, with its tier.

| Vessel | Tier | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A coal horn** | 2 or 3 | A cow's horn of banked coals that never quite go out. |
| **A prayer string** | 2 or 3 | Beads that warm in your hand, one for each charge. |
| **A lantern of captured light** | 3 or 4 | It glows brighter the more it holds, and never lights anything. |
| **A battery-coil** | 3 or 4 | Copper and glass that hums when it's full. |
| **A reliquary** | 4 or 5 | A saint's knucklebone in a silver box, out of the story. |

At the start you can take one at tier 2 or 3, like any item. The higher tiers come the way any item's do.

## Attuned Things from the Story {#s-rare}

Some things come out of the story already grown, like the harpoon they cut out of the thing in the bay, or a skull that won't stop talking. They're attuned things like the one you start with, just further along, and each is an item with a tier that's often past what money buys: a **Tempered** thing has two awakenings of its own and room for two more with a Burden, and a **Perfected** one has three and room for three more with a Burden and a Catch. Like anything attuned, each of its awakenings locks a point of the Root it fits, so taking one on takes a bit of you with it. The rules for attunement itself are in [Attunement](#s-attunement). Here's how materials work, how to write one of these and where they come from, then twenty you can use straight off the page or pull apart for ideas.

### Materials {#s-materials}

Every new stage needs a **material** first, found in the story or bought. It should fit the thing and what it's becoming, like steel from a vault door for a set of picks, or a feather from the bird a flute was carved from. Work out with the GM what it'll take, then go and get it. Once it's in, every new awakening the stage makes room for takes a downtime slot. The twenty below each say what took them to their stage.

### How to write one {#how-to-write-one}

You write one the same way as anything you attune, with a few things to watch.

-   **One line for each awakening**, written the way you'd write a permission, as something the thing itself does, with the Root it fits after it. It locks a point of that Root, and it's 2 steps on any roll it fits, and 2 more for each Aura spent on it as you roll, so the line only has to say what it does, or what it's steps on, or both. *Bite deep* (Body), 2 steps on every roll you fight with it, is the usual kind, and *Up any hull* (Body), its hook taking you up any wall you can reach, does something no plain axe can. Anything past what a line says is [going beyond](#s-going-beyond). An awakening can do something else in place of the steps, and the 30 in [Awakening ideas](#awakenings-30) are the ones to pick from or copy.
-   **No Aura.** It doesn't touch its owner's Aura, however far it's grown.
-   **Write awakenings for different situations**, so the thing does many things rather than one thing very hard. Three awakenings that all mean *fight with a sword* are one awakening written three times, and it's no help anywhere else. The Bosun's Axe fights, and it climbs. In a fight it's *Bite deep*, and halfway up a ship's side it does something no other axe does.
-   **A Catch carries one more awakening at Awakened, and a Burden two more at Tempered.** At Perfected it's three more for a Burden and a Catch. A Catch is trouble, and if the GM couldn't run with it at the table, it's too soft, so write another. A Burden comes off the list in [Burdens](#s-boons) with a name from the story, and it has to cost its owner something they use.
-   **Weapons keep their properties for free.** A Tempered spear is still *Reach* and *Thrown*, and you don't spend an awakening, a Catch or a Burden on that.
-   **Every one's an item**, with its kind and its tier on its Item line, and it takes one of its owner's six. Out of the story they're usually tier 3 or 4 when they're Tempered and 4 to 6 when they're Perfected, but the tier's the thing itself, so write the one that fits.
-   **Attuned armour soaks like any armour** and it can't break. An awakening like *Turn the blow* (Body), 2 steps on your Body when the telegraphs land at it, is 2 more on top of getting out of the way, and the same awakening twice is 4. Armour for your Mind soaks on your Mind and does the same there.
-   **Awakenings that heal.** Only a [Healer](#a-healer) makes a heal roll, so an awakening that fits one is 2 steps on a Healer's heals, like any awakening. A few go further. Sister Hale's Thimble adds 3 to what every heal rolls, before any Toll comes off, and the Surgeon's Casebook's heals ignore a step of [Toll](#s-healing), the way a Healer who's come prepared does.

For Catches, use the [Catch ideas](#catches-12), or write your own in the same shape: a name, and one line for what it means. Here are four more.

| Catch | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Talks** | It has a voice and opinions of its own, and the GM speaks for it. |
| **Honest** | Lie while you carry it and it can give you away. |
| **Sworn** | It comes with a promise, agreed when you get it. Break the promise and none of its awakenings work until you've put it right. |
| **Borrowed** | It belongs to someone else, and they can ask for it back. |

### Where they come from, and how often {#where-they-come-from-and-how-often}

**Only from the story.** A Tempered or Perfected thing turns up because something happened in play, and it's usually one of these:

-   **Taken from a boss**, like the harpoon they cut out of the thing in the bay.
-   **Found at the bottom of a site**, in the last room, or on someone who got there first and didn't make it out.
-   **Inherited** from a bond who dies, or from a crewmate who retires and leaves something behind.
-   **Given by a faction that's with the crew**, one whose threads toward them are mostly bond threads. Handing over something it can't replace is a risk, and it only takes that risk for people it trusts.
-   **Bought from a faction.** If a faction holds the thing as one of its assets and it'll sell, which means it isn't hostile to the crew, the crew can spend an extra resource on it, and the thing changes hands. A material can change hands the same way. See [Extra resources](#s-crew-wealth).
-   **Won** in a duel, like Captain Marrow's rapier, or at cards.

**Taking one on.** It goes in an attunement slot, so whoever takes it needs a free one, the same as when a friend hands over something they've attuned. It comes at the stage it's at, with its awakenings, its Catch and its Burden, and it grows from there like anything else you've attuned.

Go easy with them. I'd give each character about one Tempered thing over an arc. Perfected things shouldn't turn up more than a handful of times in a whole campaign, and only at the moments that matter, like the end of an arc or a boss that nearly broke the crew. Any more than that and they'll stop feeling rare. Go easy with materials too, because each one's a whole stage.

**Their Catches are some of the best trouble you'll get.** The player took each one for an awakening, and letting it bite is up to them. When they do, run with it and make it real: the pilots want their glass back the night before the crew sails, or the skull starts lecturing halfway down the vault stairs. They take 1 Aura for it, or a point of Soul back when it changes the scene or costs them something they wanted (see [Catches at the table](#s-catches-gm)).

### Twelve Tempered things {#twelve-rare-things}

They're written out in full, with what tempered each one. Use them as written, or swap the names and the story for ones that'll fit your world.

:::example[Dorrie and Lady Quell's Ring]
Say Dorrie's got a free attunement slot, and Lady Quell's Ring is in it, her fifth item. The rest of her sheet's the one she started with. The ring's three awakenings lock two points of her Mind and one of her Soul, so with her signet pin's point she can hold 4 of her Mind's 7 and 5 of her Soul's 6, and its Burden takes her Aura maximum down to 3. She's at a wedding at the Vane house, and she wants ten minutes alone in Lord Vane's private rooms. The steward's on the stair, and she's been working the room all evening, so she's down to 2 Aura.

First she wants to know how to play him, so she asks what the Vanes did to the Quells. That's the ring's *Every feud*, and nothing's pushing back, so there's no roll. The GM tells her: when the Quells went under, the Vanes bought the house with the servants still in it, and the steward was one of them.

Now she works on him. She isn't lying to him, so it's her Soul, 6 steps, and the ring's a tool at tier 4 that fits, so it's 4 more. He served the Quells and he knows that ring on sight, so *One of the old families* is 2 more, and she spends her last 2 Aura on it as she rolls, which is 4 more. That's 16 steps, two d12s and a d8.

The rest of the ring sits this one out. There's no letter to seal, and nobody's lying to her, so she stops there.

The steward's Challenge 4, so she rolls his d8 with hers. Hers come up 7, 2 and 5, and his comes up 6. Her 7 takes it, and he takes her up the back stair himself. That's a win, so she's got 1 Aura back.

She gets her ten minutes. On the way down he asks what she was after, and she tells him she wanted to see Lady Quell's portrait. That's a lie, and the ring's *Honest*. Keeping it quiet would cost the 1 Aura she's just got back, so her player lets the Catch bite instead: the ring goes cold on her finger, and the stone clouds over right under his nose. He knows what that means.

The steward's off down the stair to find his master, and the GM telegraphs it with his d8: if nobody deals with it, the house knows somebody's been in those rooms before the toasts. That changes the scene, so it's a big bite, and instead of the Aura Dorrie gets a point of Soul back, on a Soul she'd spent down to 2 rolling again all evening. She's got a new problem with a name and a reason.
:::

### Writing your own {#s-kit-writing-your-own}

Write it in the same order as the sheets above: one line of story, its stage and its own awakenings, then any Burden or Catch with what it carries, and the material that took it there. Do it with the player who's getting it. The GM says what the thing does, because it's come out of her world. The player decides whether to take a Burden or a Catch for more, up to what its stage allows, and you'll work out between you what each one carries.

Then read it back the way you'd use it at the table. Every awakening should be quick to call (it says what it does, or what it's steps on, and the Root it locks), every Burden should cost its owner something they use, and every Catch should give the GM something she can run with when its owner lets it bite. If a line could mean two things, fix it now, so it doesn't come up in the middle of a fight.

## What money cannot buy {#what-money-cannot-buy}

Money buys things, but it won't buy you relationships, reputation, favours or trust. It can buy a material, if somebody's selling, but the work's still yours: every new awakening on an attuned thing takes a downtime slot.

The crew's own money is different again. It comes from the crew's **resources** on the party sheet, like *Odd jobs* when it's starting out. One that isn't paying for an asset is an **extra resource**, and an extra buys what's too big for a purse, like a ship's passage for twenty or a magistrate's goodwill. See [Extra resources](#s-crew-wealth).
