## What you can afford

-   **Nothing worth tracking.** A meal, a bed for the night or a round of drinks.
-   **A purse of gold.** It counts as one Resource, and it covers a bribe to a mid-level official, a good set of tools or a horse.
-   **Several purses of gold.** A quality weapon, real armour or passage on a ship.
-   **A small fortune.** A ship or a house.

## Upgrading gear

The story earns the die, and you can't pay for a step up. Instead of a *+1*, a better sword is a new item with a better permission, or a Catch you were willing to take to get a second one. A weapon's properties come with the weapon, so buying a crossbow gets you *Long* and *Piercing* with it.

Replacing a broken item, or mending one, takes a downtime slot and usually a purse of gold. Some things can't be replaced, and shouldn't be. Your mother's blade isn't for sale.

## Rare and Legendary Things {#s-rare}

A rare or legendary thing is an item with more on it. A rare one starts with two permissions and a legendary one with three, and they've got room for more Catches (see [Rarity](#s-rarity)). Here's how to write one and where they come from, then twenty you can use straight off the page or pull apart for ideas.

### How to write one

You write one the same way as any item, with a few things to watch.

-   **One plain line for each permission**, like *stitch a wound closed* or *find the road, day or night, in any weather*. There's no number on it and no +1. When a permission plainly fits what you're doing it's a step, and when nothing's pushing back you just do it.
-   **Write permissions for different situations**, so the thing does many things rather than one thing very hard. Three permissions that all mean *fight with a sword* are one permission written three times: every swing gets 3 steps out of it, and it's no help anywhere else. The Bosun's Axe fights, and it climbs. In a fight it's 1 step from the axe, the same as any hand axe, and halfway up a ship's side it's a step no other axe gives you. Now and then two of a thing's permissions fit the same roll, and that's fine: both count, like any two permissions that fit.
-   **A Catch is a real downside**, something that changes play when it bites, and each one carries one more permission. A rare thing can take up to two, and a legendary thing up to three. If the GM couldn't ever turn it on you at the table, it's too soft, so write another.
-   **Weapons keep their properties for free.** A rare spear is still *Reach* and *Thrown*, and you don't spend a permission or a Catch on that.
-   **Armour and Confidence work as they do on any item.** A permission that says *Armour* steps your Armour up a size, and one that says *Confidence* steps your Confidence up a size.

For Catches, use the twelve in [Step 4](#s-items), or write your own in the same shape: a name, and one line for what it means. Here are four more.

| Catch | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Talks** | It has a voice and opinions of its own, and the GM speaks for it. |
| **Honest** | Lie while you carry it and the GM can make it give you away. |
| **Sworn** | It comes with a promise, agreed when you get it. Break the promise and none of its permissions count until you've put it right. |
| **Borrowed** | It belongs to someone else, and they can ask for it back. The GM says when. |

### Where they come from, and how often

**Only from the story.** A rare or legendary thing turns up because something happened in play, and it's usually one of these:

-   **Taken from a boss**, like the harpoon they cut out of the thing in the bay.
-   **Found at the bottom of a site**, in the last room, or on someone who got there first and didn't make it out.
-   **Inherited** from a bond who dies, or from a crewmate who retires and leaves something behind.
-   **Given by a faction that's an Ally.** An Ally takes risks for the crew, and handing over something it can't replace is one of them.
-   **Bought from a faction.** If a faction holds the thing as one of its tags and it'll sell (it's Neutral or better), the crew can step its own Wealth down one, and the thing changes hands. See [Factions](#factions).
-   **Won** in a duel, like Captain Marrow's rapier, or at cards.

Go easy with them. I'd give each character about one rare thing over an arc. Legendary things shouldn't turn up more than a handful of times in a whole campaign, and only at the moments that matter, like the end of an arc or a boss that nearly broke the crew. Any more than that and they'll stop feeling rare.

**Their Catches are some of the best trouble you've got.** The player took each one for a permission, so when you turn it on them, offer the Spark the way you would for any trouble: the pilots want their glass back the night before the crew sails, or the skull starts lecturing halfway down the vault stairs. See [Bringing Trouble](#s-trouble-gm).

### Twelve rare things

They're written out in full, Catches and all. Use them as written, or swap the names and the story for ones that'll fit your world.

:::sheet[Old Hobb's Fiddle]
*A fiddle that's played every wake and wedding in the harbour quarter for sixty years. Hobb left it to the first person who could play his last tune back to him.*

**Rare** Play a room into whatever mood I like · Be welcome at any wake or wedding
:::

:::sheet[The Bosun's Axe]
*A short boarding axe with a hook on the back of the head. Its bosun went over the side in a squall, and it didn't turn up again till a year later, in a fishing net.*

**Rare** Fight with an axe · Climb any hull or wall with its hook

**Properties** Breaker, Thrown
:::

:::sheet[Judge Orme's Seal]
*A harbour judge's seal ring. The court kept it as a relic after he'd died, and sold it the year the roof fell in.*

**Rare** Seal a writ that any clerk will take as real · Get a hearing with any official in the city
:::

:::sheet[The Courier's Saddle]
*A post rider's saddle, worn smooth by a lifetime on the coast road. The riders gave it to whoever brought their last courier home alive.*

**Rare** Ride any horse as if I'd raised it · Find the road, day or night, in any weather
:::

:::sheet[Sister Hale's Thimble]
*A silver thimble worn thin at the tip. Sister Hale sewed shrouds for forty years, and she left it to the one who sat with her at the end.*

**Rare** Stitch a wound closed · Sew something into a hem where no search will find it

**Catch** *Fragile*: When a consequence breaks one of your items, it's this one. **Adds** Know who last wore a piece of cloth.
:::

:::sheet[The Ferryman's Lantern]
*A river lantern that's never gone out, found on a ferry that sank at its own landing. The ferryman carried the living by day and the dead by night.*

**Rare** Light the way through fog and dark water · Hold the dead back with its light

**Catch** *Cursed*: Once a scene, the GM can put *Spotted* d6 on you through it, because something dead has seen the light. **Adds** Speak with the dead who come to its light.
:::

:::sheet[The Night Porter's Key]
*A plain brass key off a night porter's belt. One locksmith made the servants' doors for every great house in the city, so it'll open any of them.*

**Rare** Get in by the servants' door of any great house · Pass as staff below stairs

**Catch** *Delicate*: It's soft old brass. A 1 on any die where it's used bends it, and it's no use till the end of the scene. **Adds** Find my way through any house's back stairs and passages.
:::

:::sheet[The Dancing Master's Coat]
*A dove-grey coat cut for the man who taught three generations of the old families to dance. His nephew lost it at cards.*

**Rare** Dance with anyone at any ball, and never put a foot wrong · Confidence (step up)

**Catch** *Conspicuous*: It can't be hidden or passed off as anything plainer. **Adds** Slip through any crowd without anyone stopping me.
:::

:::sheet[Corporal Tench's Brigandine]
*A soldier's brigandine, patched by a dozen hands over thirty years. Tench's old company hands it on to the one who's earned it.*

**Rare** Armour (step up) · Talk soldiers round like one of their own

**Catch** *Heavy*: Your stride is 1 less while you wear it. **Adds** Armour (step up again).

**Catch** *Loud*: It rattles with every step, and everyone can hear where you've got to. **Adds** Hold my ground, whatever tries to move me.
:::

:::sheet[Captain Marrow's Rapier]
*The rapier of a duellist who'd never lost, until the day she did. She handed it over hilt first.*

**Rare** Fight with a rapier · Hold a room's eye whenever I draw it

**Properties** Piercing

**Catch** *Marked*: Every duellist on the coast knows the blade on sight. **Adds** Call anyone out under the old duelling code.

**Catch** *Wanted*: Every young blade who's after a name comes looking for it. **Adds** Read a fighter's training from the way they stand.
:::

:::sheet[The Abbot's Ledger]
*Every debt the old abbey was ever owed, in a hand nobody but its keeper can read. It came up out of the abbey's flooded undercroft.*

**Rare** Know who owed the old abbey, and how much · Read any cipher or code

**Catch** *Wanted*: Half the families in it want it burned, and they know you've got it. **Adds** Call in a debt that's written in it.

**Catch** *Slow*: Looking anything up in it takes two actions, not one. **Adds** Know a forgery on sight.
:::

:::sheet[Mother Gall's Spindle]
*A blackthorn spindle, still wound with a hedge-witch's grey thread. She was buried with it, and it didn't stay buried.*

**Rare** Curse someone with a thread tied round something of theirs · Spin a fever or a poison out of someone

**Catch** *Fussy*: 3 charges. Every curse you lay with it spends one, and a downtime slot spinning fresh thread refills them. **Adds** Untie any curse, if I can find the knot.

**Catch** *Narrow*: It only works between sunset and sunrise. **Adds** Spin a rope out of anything, strong enough to hold a man.
:::

:::sheet[The Weatherwife's Flute]
*A bone flute carved from the wing of a bird nobody's seen in living memory. The old weatherwife played the boats home with it, and she left it to whoever would keep doing it.*

**Legendary** Call up a wind or a fog · Call any bird to me · Play a tune nobody who hears it can walk away from
:::

:::sheet[The Surgeon's Casebook]

**Legendary** Treat any wound or fever, given an hour · Know how anyone died from what's left of them · Find the weak point in anything that bleeds
:::

:::sheet[The Gatekeeper's Plate]
*Plate that held a city's north gate for nine days against a siege. When the siege lifted it was still standing in the gateway, empty, and it stayed in the gatehouse a hundred years until somebody took it down.*

**Legendary** Armour (step up) · Hold a gate or a stair against anyone · Feel it when anything bound by magic is near

**Catch** *Heavy*: Your stride is 1 less while you wear it. **Adds** Armour (step up again).
:::

:::sheet[The Harpooner's Iron]
*A whaler's harpoon, gone black with age. It's the one that finally killed the thing in the bay, and they cut it out of its side when it washed up.*

**Legendary** Fight with a harpoon · Haul in anything I've hooked, however big · Know which way anything I've drawn blood from has gone

**Properties** Reach, Thrown

**Catch** *Conspicuous*: It's eight feet of black iron, and there's no hiding it. **Adds** Get passage on any ship in any harbour.
:::

:::sheet[Lady Quell's Ring]
*The ring of the last Lady Quell, who'd outlived her whole house and left everything she had to the one stranger who kept visiting.*

**Legendary** Be received by the old families as one of their own · Know any old family's history and feuds · Seal a letter so only the one it's for can open it

**Catch** *Honest*: Lie while you wear it and the GM can make it give you away. **Adds** Tell when someone's lying to me.

**Catch** *Marked*: Every old family knows that ring from across a ballroom. **Adds** Confidence (step up).
:::

:::sheet[The Lookout's Glass]
*A brass spyglass from the masthead of a ship that never ran aground in fifty years at sea. The harbour pilots lend it to whoever brings home a ship that should've sunk.*

**Legendary** See to the horizon as clearly as across a room · Find my bearings from the stars or the shore · See through any disguise or illusion

**Catch** *Bright*: It's no use in the dark. **Adds** Signal anyone who can see me, by flashing the sun off it.

**Catch** *Borrowed*: It belongs to the pilots, and they can ask for it back. The GM says when. **Adds** Call on any harbour pilot for help, and get it.
:::

:::sheet[The Fallen Champion's Sword]
*The sword of the best fighter of a generation, who'd fought for the side that lost. They gave it up the day somebody finally sat down and asked them why.*

**Legendary** Fight with a sword · Read a battlefield at a glance · Cut through any chain or binding

**Catch** *Marked*: Anyone who's fought in the war knows it on sight. **Adds** Make old soldiers lower their weapons.

**Catch** *Cursed*: Once a scene, the GM can put *Shaken* d6 on you through it, because it remembers what it did in the war. **Adds** Know a turncoat when I see one.

**Catch** *Sworn*: You promised never to draw it on anyone who's yielded. Break that and none of its permissions count until you've put it right. **Adds** Swear on it and be believed.
:::

:::sheet[Brother Amis's Skull]
*The skull of a scholar-monk who saw no reason to stop teaching just because he'd died. He left the monastery's bone-house in a sack, complaining the whole way.*

**Legendary** Read any language that's ever been written down · Know the history of any old place I'm standing in · Keep watch while I sleep, and wake me if anything comes

**Catch** *Talks*: He's got a voice and opinions of his own, and the GM speaks for him. **Adds** Out-argue anyone who learned it from a book.

**Catch** *Fragile*: When a consequence breaks one of your items, it's this one. **Adds** Know a fake relic or a forged deed on sight.

**Catch** *Wanted*: The monastery wants its brother back, and knows who took him. **Adds** Recite any rite or ritual word for word.
:::

:::example[Dorrie and Lady Quell's Ring]
Dorrie's sheet is the one she started with, plus Lady Quell's Ring with both its Catches. She's at a wedding at the Vane house, and she wants ten minutes alone in Lord Vane's private rooms. The steward's on the stair.

First she wants to know how to play him, so she asks what the Vanes did to the Quells. The ring's *know any old family's history and feuds* plainly covers that, and nothing's pushing back, so there's no roll. The GM tells her: when the Quells went under, the Vanes bought the house with the servants still in it, and the steward was one of them.

Now she works on him. *Influence* covers talking him round, and she's a Novice, so that's 1 step. Her *nobility* specialization doesn't fit, because he's staff. He's never heard of her, so *Reputation Precedes Me* doesn't come into it. Her Title, *The voice that talked a duke out of his own house*, fits: 1 step. The ring's *be received by the old families as one of their own* fits too, because he served the Quells and he knows that ring on sight: 1 step. With one action, that's 4 steps, a d8.

The rest of the ring sits this one out. There's no letter to seal, nobody's lying to her, and its *Confidence* is already in her Confidence die. She could argue that what she's just learned helps, but if she has to make a case for it, it's close, and that's 1 Aura. She keeps her Aura.

The steward's Challenge 4, so she rolls his d8 with hers. Hers comes up 6 and his comes up 4. He takes her up the back stair himself.

She gets her ten minutes. On the way down he asks what she was after, and she tells him she wanted to see Lady Quell's portrait. That's a lie, and the ring's *Honest*, so the GM brings trouble: "The ring goes cold on your finger, and the stone clouds over right under his nose. He knows what that means. Take a Spark?"

Dorrie's already spent her Spark tonight, so there's no turning it down, and it happens. The steward's off down the stair to find his master, and the GM telegraphs it with his d8: if nobody deals with it, the house knows somebody's been in those rooms before the toasts. Dorrie's got a new problem with a name and a reason, and she's got her Spark back to deal with it.
:::

### Writing your own

Write it in the same order as the sheets above: one line of story, then the permissions, then any Catches with the permission each one carries. Do it with the player who's getting it. The GM says what the thing does, because it's come out of her world. The player decides whether to take Catches for more, up to the limit, and you'll work out between you what each one carries.

Then read it back the way you'd use it at the table. Every permission should be quick to call (plainly, close or not at all), and every Catch should give the GM something she can turn on its owner. If a line could mean two things, fix it now, so it doesn't come up in the middle of a fight.

## What money cannot buy

Money buys things, but it won't buy you relationships, reputation, favours or trust.

The crew's own money is different again. Its **Wealth** is a die on the party sheet for what the crew can bring to bear, like a ship's passage for twenty or a magistrate's goodwill, and it's spent a step at a time. See [The Crew](#s-party-faction).
