## What you can afford {#what-you-can-afford}

-   **Nothing worth tracking.** A meal, a bed for the night or a round of drinks.
-   **A purse of gold.** It's one of your utility items, and it covers a bribe to a mid-level official, a good set of tools, a horse or a piece of Common armour.
-   **Several purses of gold.** A quality weapon, a piece of Fine armour or passage on a ship.
-   **A small fortune.** A ship, a house or a piece of Masterwork armour, if you can find a smith who makes it.

## Upgrading gear {#upgrading-gear}

Money buys plain things, and a plain thing is just what it is. A sword's a sword: it's 1 more on any roll you use it in, and it lets you do what a sword does. A finer one is still 1 more, so you can't pay for a step up. A weapon's properties come with the weapon, so buying a crossbow gets you *Long* and *Piercing* with it.

It's the same with armour: each piece you wear is 1 more on a roll it fits, like a Defend, and so is a shield, so Bram's plate and his shield are two of the 14 steps he Defends with. Money buys quality, though: a Fine piece takes two hits for you before it breaks, and a Masterwork one takes three, but it's still 1 more (see [Quality](#s-armour-quality)). Anything more than that is [attunement](#s-attunement), and money can buy the material for an attuned thing's next stage, if somebody's selling. A [Maker](#a-maker) can give a thing tags as well, but they only last the scene.

Replacing a broken item, or mending one, takes a downtime slot and usually a purse of gold. It's usually armour or a shield, because a box on one is a cost you can offer instead of a size off your track (see [Armour](#s-armour)), and a piece with boxes ticked is mended the same way, every box at once. Attuned things are magic and can't break, so it's always something you carry. Some things can't be replaced, and shouldn't be. Your mother's blade isn't for sale.

## Attuned Things from the Story {#s-rare}

Some things come out of the story already grown, like the harpoon they cut out of the thing in the bay, or a skull that won't stop talking. They're attuned things like the one you start with, just further along: a **Tempered** thing has two awakenings of its own and room for two more with a Burden, and a **Perfected** one has three and room for three more with a Burden and a Catch. Like anything attuned, each one puts 2 on your Aura maximum, and that's all the Aura it gets you. The rules for attunement itself are in [Attunement](#s-attunement). Here's how materials work, how to write one of these and where they come from, then twenty you can use straight off the page or pull apart for ideas.

### Materials {#s-materials}

Every new stage needs a **material** first, found in the story or bought. It should fit the thing and what it's becoming, like steel from a vault door for a set of picks, or a feather from the bird a flute was carved from. Work out with the GM what it'll take, then go and get it. Once it's in, every new awakening the stage makes room for takes a downtime slot. The twenty below each say what took them to their stage.

### How to write one {#how-to-write-one}

You write one the same way as anything you attune, with a few things to watch.

-   **It's an item.** When you're using it and it fits, it's 1 more on your roll, the same as any item, whatever stage it's at.
-   **One line for each awakening**, written the way you'd write a permission, as something the thing itself does, and saying whether it's passive or active and what the Aura buys: *Stitch a wound closed* (active), 1 Aura puts someone's Stamina up a size. A passive one's powered, up to 2 Aura off what you've got and off your maximum till the scene ends, and it works for as much as is powering it. The usual one's +1 on one die for each Aura powering it, on the rolls where the thing does its job. An active one spends Aura when you use it, and each Aura more gets you more of it: further, bigger, another target. Either kind's a step on any roll it fits.
-   **No Aura of its own.** It puts 2 on its owner's Aura maximum, like anything attuned, and that's all the Aura it gets them. Its passive awakenings take some of it back when they're powered.
-   **Write awakenings for different situations**, so the thing does many things rather than one thing very hard. Three awakenings that all mean *fight with a sword* are one awakening written three times, and it's no help anywhere else. The Bosun's Axe fights, and it climbs. In a fight it's 1 more and its *Bite deep*, and halfway up a ship's side it does something no other axe does.
-   **A Catch carries one more awakening at Awakened, and a Burden two more at Tempered.** At Perfected it's three more for a Burden and a Catch. A Catch is trouble, and if the GM couldn't run with it at the table, it's too soft, so write another. A Burden comes off the list in [Boons and Burdens](#s-boons) with a name from the story, and it has to cost its owner something they use.
-   **Weapons keep their properties for free.** A Tempered spear is still *Reach* and *Thrown*, and you don't spend an awakening, a Catch or a Burden on that.
-   **Attuned armour can't break**, so it hasn't got boxes and it can't take a hit for you, but it's still 1 more on a roll it fits, like a Defend. An awakening like *Turn the blow*, +1 on one die of your Defend or your Stamina when the telegraphs land for each Aura powering it, is how it holds the line, and the same awakening twice can be powered with up to 4. For your Mind, it's the same on your Confidence.
-   **Awakenings that heal.** Only a [Healer](#a-healer) makes a heal roll. An awakening that heals is like a quick heal you can make bigger: with your action, 1 Aura puts one of someone's tracks up a size, and each Aura more is another size.

For Catches, use the [Catch ideas](#catches-12), or write your own in the same shape: a name, and one line for what it means. Here are four more.

| Catch | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Talks** | It has a voice and opinions of its own, and the GM speaks for it. |
| **Honest** | Lie while you carry it and it can give you away. |
| **Sworn** | It comes with a promise, agreed when you get it. Break the promise and none of its awakenings work until you've put it right. |
| **Borrowed** | It belongs to someone else, and they can ask for it back. |

### Where they come from, and how often {#where-they-come-from-and-how-often}

**Only from the story.** A Tempered or Perfected thing turns up because something happened in play, and it's usually one of these:

-   **Taken from a boss**, like the harpoon they cut out of the thing in the bay.
-   **Found at the bottom of a site**, in the last room, or on someone who got there first and didn't make it out.
-   **Inherited** from a bond who dies, or from a crewmate who retires and leaves something behind.
-   **Given by a faction that's with the crew**, one whose threads toward them are mostly bond threads. Handing over something it can't replace is a risk, and it only takes that risk for people it trusts.
-   **Bought from a faction.** If a faction holds the thing as one of its assets and it'll sell, which means it isn't hostile to the crew, the crew can spend an extra resource on it, and the thing changes hands. A material can change hands the same way. See [Extra resources](#s-crew-wealth).
-   **Won** in a duel, like Captain Marrow's rapier, or at cards.

**Taking one on.** It goes in an attunement slot, so whoever takes it needs a free one, the same as when a friend hands over something they've attuned. It comes at the stage it's at, with its awakenings, its Catch and its Burden, and it grows from there like anything else you've attuned.

Go easy with them. I'd give each character about one Tempered thing over an arc. Perfected things shouldn't turn up more than a handful of times in a whole campaign, and only at the moments that matter, like the end of an arc or a boss that nearly broke the crew. Any more than that and they'll stop feeling rare. Go easy with materials too, because each one's a whole stage.

**Their Catches are some of the best trouble you'll get.** The player took each one for an awakening, and letting it bite is up to them. When they do, run with it and make it real: the pilots want their glass back the night before the crew sails, or the skull starts lecturing halfway down the vault stairs. They take 1 Aura for it, or their Resolve comes up a size when it changes the scene or costs them something they wanted (see [Catches and Resolve at the table](#s-catches-gm)).

:::example[Dorrie and Lady Quell's Ring]
Say Dorrie's got a free attunement slot, and Lady Quell's Ring is in it. The rest of her sheet's the one she started with, so with her signet pin and the ring her Aura maximum's 9, and the ring's Burden takes it to 7. She's at a wedding at the Vane house, and she wants ten minutes alone in Lord Vane's private rooms. The steward's on the stair, and she's been working the room all evening, so she's down to 3 Aura.

First she wants to know how to play him, so she asks what the Vanes did to the Quells. She pays 1 Aura for the ring's *Every feud*, and nothing's pushing back, so there's no roll. The GM tells her: when the Quells went under, the Vanes bought the house with the servants still in it, and the steward was one of them.

Now she works on him. She isn't lying to him, so it's her Soul, her first Title, *talk my way past a door* and Face, and her court clothes fit a wedding. The ring's 1 more, because he served the Quells and he knows that ring on sight, and so's *One of the old families*. That's 10 steps, a d12 and a d8. Before she rolls she powers *One of the old families* with her last 2 Aura, so it's +2 on one of her dice, and her maximum's 5 till the scene ends.

The rest of the ring sits this one out. There's no letter to seal, and nobody's lying to her, and anything else she wanted would cost Aura she hasn't got, so she stops there.

The steward's Challenge 4, so she rolls his d8 with hers. Hers come up 5 and 2, and his comes up 6. The ring's +2 makes her 5 a 7, and he takes her up the back stair himself. A die the ring added to doesn't pay, so she's still on nothing.

She gets her ten minutes. On the way down he asks what she was after, and she tells him she wanted to see Lady Quell's portrait. That's a lie, and the ring's *Honest*. Keeping it quiet would cost her 1 Aura, and she hasn't got any left, so her player lets the Catch bite: the ring goes cold on her finger, and the stone clouds over right under his nose. He knows what that means.

The steward's off down the stair to find his master, and the GM telegraphs it with his d8: if nobody deals with it, the house knows somebody's been in those rooms before the toasts. That changes the scene, so it's a big bite, and Dorrie's Resolve, down to d10 after a reroll earlier in the evening, comes back up to d12 instead of the Aura. She's got a new problem with a name and a reason.
:::

### Writing your own {#s-kit-writing-your-own}

Write it in the same order as the sheets above: one line of story, its stage and its own awakenings, then any Burden or Catch with what it carries, and the material that took it there. Do it with the player who's getting it. The GM says what the thing does, because it's come out of her world. The player decides whether to take a Burden or a Catch for more, up to what its stage allows, and you'll work out between you what each one carries.

Then read it back the way you'd use it at the table. Every awakening should be quick to call (it says what it does, and what each Aura buys), every Burden should cost its owner something they use, and every Catch should give the GM something she can run with when its owner lets it bite. If a line could mean two things, fix it now, so it doesn't come up in the middle of a fight.

## What money cannot buy {#what-money-cannot-buy}

Money buys things, but it won't buy you relationships, reputation, favours or trust. It can buy a material, if somebody's selling, but the work's still yours: every new awakening on an attuned thing takes a downtime slot.

The crew's own money is different again. It comes from the crew's **resources** on the party sheet, like *Odd jobs* when it's starting out. One that isn't paying for an asset is an **extra resource**, and an extra buys what's too big for a purse, like a ship's passage for twenty or a magistrate's goodwill. See [Extra resources](#s-crew-wealth).
