:::quote
Before anyone builds a character, pick the kind of world you want to play in.
:::

A **frame** is a ready-made kind of campaign. It tells you what the world's like, how it should feel at the table, what the crew does from one session to the next, who's on the faction board and how to start. The rules don't change from one frame to the next. What changes is the world, and what you do in it.

You pick one together at Session Zero, and it's step 1, [the pitch](#session-zero). The GM reads out the pitch for the frame she'd like to run, or for two or three of them, and the table talks it over. A frame hands you most of the other steps as well: the tone, the first factions, the first Goal and the questions to go round the table with. That'll make Session Zero a lot quicker.

[The Steeplands](#steeplands) is the book's own frame, and it's where every example in the earlier chapters happens. The five below are other worlds you can run with the same book. They're all built from the same parts in the same order, so once you've read one you'll know where to find things in the rest, and there's a checklist at the end for building your own.

| Frame | What you'll do most | It feels like | Pick it if… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [The Steeplands](#steeplands) | Jobs for the factions of a rain-soaked port, and the trouble that follows you home. | Thieves and fixers in an old harbour city where every debt gets called in. | you want to play tonight, in the book's own world. |
| [Brinewake](#s-frame-brinewake) | Sail cargo between islands, and deal with whoever's waiting in port. | Sea stories where the ship's one of the crew. | you want a ship of your own, open water and a new port every session. |
| [The Deep Company](#s-frame-deep-company) | Go down into a buried city, map it, and get back up with what you found. | Delving stories where the trip back up is half the story. | you like maps and traps, and you can never say no to one more level. |
| [Lanternfall](#s-frame-lanternfall) | Investigate the missing and the dead, then face the thing you've learned about. | Gaslight mysteries where the truth's worse than the rumour. | you want dread, clues and a monster you have to understand before you can beat it. |
| [The Gilded Court](#s-frame-gilded-court) | Talk, scheme and trade favours, and duel when the talking runs out. | Palace intrigue where a whisper does more than a sword. | you'd rather win a room than a fight. |
| [Long Haul](#s-frame-long-haul) | Haul cargo between stations and keep an old ship flying. | Working people on a tired freighter at the edge of settled space. | you want Zi without swords, and a ship that's home. |

If the table's split, have everyone name the frame they'd most like to play and the one they'd least like, then play the favourite nobody put last. You can mix them, too. Brinewake's ship can sail into Lanternfall's harbour, the Deep Company's *Depth* works in any ruin in the Steeplands, and the Gilded Court's favours work at any court. Change whatever you like. A frame gets you to the first session, and after a few more the world belongs to your table anyway.

## Brinewake {#s-frame-brinewake}

### The pitch

:::example
"You crew the Kittiwake, a fast little ship with a leaky hull and a mortgage, in the Brinewake, a chain of storm-bitten islands where every port has a price. The trading houses own the sea lanes and the Tithe Fleet hangs anyone sailing without papers, and lately something under the water has started taking whole ships. You've got one season to make the Kittiwake pay for herself, and every cargo you take on drags you deeper into a fight over who owns the sea."
:::

### Tone and feel

Brinewake is sea stories where the ship's one of the crew. You should feel the deck move. The weather matters, every port is loud and full of people who want something from you, and the crew argues about the course like it's family. It's adventurous and warm, with real danger every time you leave harbour, and most of that danger is the sea itself. Gallows jokes in a storm are part of it.

It isn't a naval war game. The crew aren't admirals and nobody's moving fleets around a map, so when there's a fight it's a boarding, a brawl on the quay or a chase through the rocks. It's no pirate comedy either, unless that's what your table wants, and if it does, say so now.

### What the crew does

A session usually starts in port. The crew takes a cargo or a charter from someone on the board (that's *Work for it* or a *Deal* in [Factions](#factions)), and the planning scene is about the route, the weather and who's watching the harbour. Then they sail. A voyage is a journey from [Travelling](#travelling): the way is a stake with a tag for every stage, like *The Narrows*, *Open Water* and *The Reef Gate*, and Supplies is the water and salt pork in the hold. The ship herself is a party resource with permissions of her own, and the frame rule below says how. Most voyages have one scene worth playing in rounds, like a cutter that wants to come aboard or a squall at the wrong moment, and when something's after you it's a [chase](#chases), with the sea as the Ground. Then there's whoever's waiting at the other end, and the aftermath: who pays, who's angry, and what the crew owes now.

Over an arc, the crew builds the ship and its name. They pay off the Kittiwake and refit her, pick a side between the Tithe Fleet, the trading houses and the smugglers, and find out what's under the Glass Shelf. [Downtime](#downtime) is time in port, with the ship up on the slip and everyone's debts catching up with them, and it's when the faction turn shows you what the board did while you were at sea.

### The world

-   **Saltreach.** The biggest free port, built on old stilts over a drowned town. Everything's for sale, and the harbourmaster takes a tenth of it.
-   **The Combs.** A maze of rock stacks and sea caves where the smugglers hide. The tide opens and shuts the channels twice a day, so you'll need a pilot who knows them, or a lot of luck.
-   **Gallowsmouth.** The Tithe Fleet's fortress harbour, with iron cages hanging off the breakwater for anyone caught without papers.
-   **The Glass Shelf.** Shallows so clear you can see the towers of a drowned city underneath. It's where the ships have started going missing.
-   **Kell's Rest.** A whaling island at the edge of the charts. The last chandler before open ocean sells water at three times the price, and nobody there asks where you're headed.

### The board

**Want** Every ship that sails pays the tithe.

**Agenda** *Close the Combs*, at d4 (a season or more away).

**Tags** *Warships* · *The Law of the Sea* · *Watchful*

**Face** Commodore Isel Varrow, who hanged her own brother for smuggling.

**Secret** *Bought Captains.* Half her captains take Tarrant money to look the other way, and she's got no idea.

**Standing** Wary.
:::

**Agenda** *Buy the Saltreach harbour charter*, at d6 (a month or two away).

**Tags** *Wealthy* · *Sprawling* · *Holds the Note*

**Face** Marguerite Tarrant, the eldest daughter, who never raises her voice and never forgets a late payment.

**Secret** *They Woke It.* Their diving barges on the Glass Shelf broke into something that should've stayed shut.

**Standing** Neutral.
:::

:::sheet[The Brothers of the Lamp]
**Power** d6 · **Wealth** d6

**Want** A sea with no tithe on it.

**Agenda** *Run the Gallowsmouth blockade*, at d8 (a week or two away).

**Tags** *Knows Every Cove* · *Ghost*

**Face** Old Hennick One-Lamp, who was smuggling before the Fleet had a name.

**Secret** *Wreckers.* When trade's slow, they hang false lights on the rocks and strip whatever runs aground.

**Standing** Friendly.
:::

:::sheet[The Drowned Choir]
**Power** d8 · **Wealth** d2

**Want** To bring the drowned city back up into the air.

**Agenda** *Raise the Glass Shelf*, at d2 (years off, for now).

**Tags** *Old* · *Calls the Tide* · *Secretive*

**Face** The Cantor, a drowned priest who talks through anyone who's swallowed enough seawater.

**Secret** *Sunk on Purpose.* The founders of Saltreach drowned that city, and the Choir remembers every name.

**Standing** Neutral. It hasn't noticed the crew yet.
:::

Two of them want the Kittiwake. Tarrant & Daughters holds the note on her and would happily take her back over one missed payment, and the Brothers need one more fast ship for the blockade run. That's your first faction pressure, right there.

### Threats

:::threat[The Boarding Master]
A Tithe Fleet sergeant with a grapnel, a cutlass and a list of names.

**Challenge** 4 (d8)

**Comes at you** *Grapple* · *Calls for Help*

**Keeps it going** *Trained* · *Sea Legs*

**Drives it** *Following Orders* · *Tired*

**Telegraphs** *He gets his grapnel into your belt and hauls you across onto the cutter.* (d8, Armour) · *"Papers, or the cage. I don't much mind which."* (d8, Confidence) · *He blows two short blasts, and the cutter runs out its guns.* (d8, the scene clock)

**Voice** "Nobody's in trouble yet. Let's keep it that way."

**Hidden** *Owes Hennick His Life* (weak point). Old Hennick pulled him out of the water twenty years ago. Say Hennick's name quietly and he finds a reason to go and search somebody else's ship.
:::

:::threat[The Bell Below]
Something the size of an island that lives under the Glass Shelf and sings ships down.

A long shadow under clear water, and a low note you feel in your teeth before you hear it.

**Challenge** 6 (d12)

**Comes at you** *Hits Like a Wagon* · *Fear*

**Keeps it going** *Barnacle Hide 20* · *The Song 20* · *Deep Water*

**Drives it** *Grieving*

**Telegraphs** *The sea humps up under the keel, and a fluke as big as a sail comes down across the deck.* (d12, Armour) · *The song comes up through the planks, and everyone aboard hears someone they've lost calling from the water.* (d12, Confidence) · *It wraps itself round the hull and starts to squeeze.* (d12, the ship)

**Voice** One long note through the hull, like a bell rung underwater.

**Hidden** *The Bell in Its Throat* (weak point). The drowned city's temple bell is stuck in its throat, and it sings through it for everyone who drowned. Ring a bell back at it on the same note and it goes quiet, and sinks, and doesn't come back.
:::

The Bell Below is a boss, and its two tough tags add up to 40, for four characters with one Title each. *Barnacle Hide* is chipped by harpoons, blades and fire. *The Song* is chipped by whatever drowns it out or breaks its hold, like a shanty roared from the deck or a ward. *Deep Water* goes when somebody lures it into the shallows, where it can't dive.

### Characters

Good Brinewake Titles say what you do on a ship, or what the sea's done to you:

-   *The only pilot who's taken a ship through the Combs at night*
-   *The bosun who's never lost a mast*
-   *The smuggler the Tithe Fleet hanged and didn't kill*
-   *The diver who's touched the drowned towers*

Every crew needs a Captain, so that's the first [archetype](#s-archetypes) to fill, but all of them have a place aboard. The Warrior leads the boarding party, the Rogue runs the hidden hold, the Face does the deals in port, and the Ranger's up the mast with the harpoon. The Druid fits better here than nearly anywhere, as a weather-caller or a sea witch, and so does the Healer, because at sea there's no doctor but the one you brought.

Abilities that earn their keep: *Navigator* (*storms at sea*, *coastlines*, *the stars*, *the Combs*), *Acrobat* (*rigging and masts*), *Athlete* (*swimming*, *climbing*), *Rider* (*small boats*), *Engineer* (*ships*), *Strategist* (*sea battles*), *Merchant* (*shipping routes*, *smuggled goods*) and *Streetwise* (*the docks*). For magic, *Wildcraft* with *weather* and *Medium* with *the drowned* could've been made for this sea.

Magic's real but rare, and sailors are superstitious about it. A weather-caller's worth her weight in silver, and nobody whistles on deck. Heavy armour's a bad idea out here, because nobody swims in plate, and the GM'll remember that when you're standing at the rail. Horses hardly come up at all.

### The frame rule: the ship

The ship's a [party resource](#party-sheet), and in Brinewake she's the most important thing on the party sheet. Give her a name and three tags, like *Fast*, *Deep Hold* and *Sound Hull*. Each tag's a permission for whoever's working the ship, where it fits: *Fast* when you're racing a squall or a cutter, and *Deep Hold* when the Fleet comes aboard looking for brandy. Anything can telegraph at the ship, like a storm or a cutter's guns. There's no defence roll for her, the same as a stake: somebody answers it, or it lands and the GM strips one of her tags. Like any consequence, the crew can offer another cost that fits, like Supplies down a size or a Hurt on whoever's at the wheel. A ship with no tags left is a wreck. She floats, just, but she's no use to anyone as a permission, and the GM telegraphs the next blow at her with one answer: if it lands, she sinks. In port, a downtime slot and a purse of gold puts one tag back, the same as mending an item. A full refit, every tag at once, is too big for a purse, so it costs a step of the crew's Wealth.

:::example[The squall off the Combs]
The Kittiwake is *Fast*, *Deep Hold* and *Sound Hull*, and there's a squall coming down off the rocks. The GM telegraphs a d10 at the ship: if it lands, it takes the foremast, and *Fast* goes with it.

Wren's at the wheel. In this game she's got *Navigator* with *storms at sea*, and her Title's *The only pilot who's taken a ship through the Combs at night*. Navigator, the specialization and her Title all fit, and so does *Fast*, because she's running before the wind. With the action that's 5 steps, a d10. She rolls 4, and the squall's d10 comes up 9, so it lands.

Bram offers something else: "Cut the lashings and let the water casks go. She'll ride lighter." It fits, and it costs about as much, so Supplies drops from a d8 to a d6 and the Kittiwake keeps *Fast*.
:::

### The first Goal

Start with *Own the Kittiwake outright*, at d2. Tarrant & Daughters holds the note on her, and every voyage that brings real money home can chase it. Here's what to run alongside it:

-   **A safe anchorage in the Combs.** When it's done, the crew has a cove nobody else knows about, as a holding.
-   **Letters of marque.** When it's done, the Kittiwake sails with Tithe Fleet papers, and the cages at Gallowsmouth are for other people.
-   **Chart the Glass Shelf.** When it's done, the crew knows where the missing ships went, and the Drowned Choir's secret is theirs.

### The first job

:::example[Brandy for Hennick]
**Where it starts:** The Kittiwake's three hours out of Saltreach with forty casks of untithed brandy in the hidden hold, and a Tithe cutter's just come round the headland flying the stop flag. **In motion:** The cutter's closing, and at dusk the tide turns in the Combs and shuts the channels the crew knows.

**Stakes:** *Keep the brandy* is Challenge 4, with *Boarders* and *Forty Casks* in the way. If they don't, the Fleet takes the cargo, and Hennick wants paying for it anyway. *Keep the ship's name off the list* is Challenge 3, with *The Lieutenant's Spyglass* in the way and a clock on it at d8. If they don't, every Fleet captain in the Brinewake knows the Kittiwake. The third is optional: there's a girl hiding in the hold who swam out from Gallowsmouth, and the Boarding Master wants her back. *Keep her* is Challenge 3, with *Alive and Scared* in the way.

**Who pushes back:** The Boarding Master, six marines (Challenge 3 mooks, kept going by *Trained*), and the cutter's guns, a d10 at the ship every round.

**Ground:** *Rolling Deck*, *Rigging* and *Loose Cargo*, plus a hidden one: *A Reef Under the Chop*.

**Moments:** The pilot gets the reef, and the fighter gets the boarding plank. The talker gets the lieutenant, who'd rather take a bribe than a cutlass, and the sneak gets the hidden hold with marines climbing down into it. Whoever's strange gets the girl, who's humming a tune nobody taught her.

**Scene clock:** *The tide turns*, a d8. When it runs out, the Combs close, and the only way out is past the cutter's guns.

**Twist:** The girl's a Choir singer, and the song in her throat is calling the Bell Below up under both ships. **Ways out:** into the Combs before the tide turns, over the reef if they dump enough cargo to ride light, or pay the lieutenant and sail on with papers that'll cost them later.
:::

### Session Zero questions

-   What's the one cargo this crew won't carry, whatever it pays?
-   Who on board has been down to the Glass Shelf, and what did they see there?
-   Which port won't let the Kittiwake dock any more, and what did we do there?
-   Who's the crew member nobody at the table plays, like the cook or the ship's boy, and what do they want?
-   Does the sea have gods in this world, and do sailors pray to them?
-   How funny is it? Is a storm at sea a story you laugh about afterwards, or never?

## The Deep Company {#s-frame-deep-company}

### The pitch

:::example
"The Deep Company holds the royal charter on the Underhold, a city that went down into the mountain a thousand years ago and never came out. You're one of its delving teams. You go down with lamps and a map that's never finished, you bring back whatever you can carry, and the Company takes its cut. Every team that comes back up goes a little deeper next time, and something at the bottom has started climbing up to meet them."
:::

### Tone and feel

The Deep Company is the old delving stories, where a team goes down into the dark with lamps and a map, and the trip back up is half the story. Down below it's tense and quiet. There's lamplight on old stone and the whole mountain overhead, and every noise you make carries further than you'd like. Up top it's loud and warm, with hot food, pay day and arguments over the Company's cut. Give both halves time at the table, because coming back up only feels good if the dark was frightening.

It isn't a maze of random rooms with treasure in every chest. Every level was somebody's home once, and it should make sense: you can see where they kept the grain, and why that door's barred from this side. It isn't a war on monsters either. Most of what lives down there wants to be left alone, and a smart crew walks past it, buys it off or goes round.

### What the crew does

A session goes like this. The crew picks a contract off the Company's board in Lanternhead (that's *Work for it* in [Factions](#factions)), and plans at the top of the lift. Then they go down. Each level of the Underhold is a site from [Exploring](#exploring), with its places, its alarm, its traps and its secret, and the frame rule below sets how awake each level is. When the dark wakes up and everyone's running for the lift, that's a [chase](#chases), with the tunnels as the Ground. Then they come back up, if they come back up, with whatever they found.

Coming back up matters as much as going down. Hurt only heals in [downtime](#downtime), so the town's where the crew heals, sells what they found, gets better at things and pays its debts, and it's where the faction turn moves the Company, the Guild and the rival teams. Over an arc, the crew pushes deeper, gets its name on the Company's board, settles things with Holloway's team, and finds out what's at the bottom and why it was shut in.

### The world

-   **Lanternhead.** The Company town on the mountainside, all bunkhouses and pawnshops, built round the top of the great lift.
-   **The Upper Galleries.** The first two levels: the old markets and houses, mapped and picked over. New teams learn the trade here, and squatters live in the dry rooms.
-   **The Drowned Mills.** The third level, where the old city ground its grain. It's flooded to the waist, and the water rises and falls with the snowmelt.
-   **The Hall of Kings.** The fourth level, the old city's throne halls, sealed behind a door that's still awake. The Company's map stops here.
-   **The Underneath.** Everything below. Nobody's mapped it, and the few who've been down there and come back don't talk about it.

### The board

:::sheet[The Deep Company]
**Power** d10 · **Wealth** d12

**Want** Everything that comes up out of the mountain, with its cut taken.

**Agenda** *Open the Hall of Kings*, at d6 (a month or two away).

**Tags** *Royal Charter* · *Wealthy* · *The Lift*

**Face** Factor Ilse Brannock, who counts every lamp that goes down and every one that comes back.

**Secret** *The Charter Ends at Midwinter.* Unless the Company brings up something worth renewing it for, the crown takes the Underhold back.

**Standing** Neutral.
:::

:::sheet[The Lampwrights' Guild]
**Power** d6 · **Wealth** d4

**Want** A fair cut, and a widow's share for every team that doesn't come back.

**Agenda** *Down tools*, at d4 (a season or more away). When it happens, the lift stops, and nobody goes down for anyone.

**Tags** *Every Team Knows Us* · *Rooted*

**Face** Marta Cole, who's buried two husbands and kept both their maps.

**Secret** *Their Own Map.* The Guild's map goes two levels deeper than the Company's.

**Standing** Friendly.
:::

:::sheet[Holloway's Team]
**Power** d6 · **Wealth** d6

**Want** To be first everywhere, whatever it costs, as long as somebody else pays.

**Agenda** *Reach the Underneath*, at d8 (a week or two away).

**Tags** *Best Kit in Lanternhead* · *Ruthless*

**Face** Saul Holloway, who's never once come back up without someone missing.

**Secret** *They Cut the Rope.* Holloway left the Tallow team to die on the second level, and two of them are still alive.

**Standing** Wary.
:::

:::sheet[The Underhold]
**Power** d8 · **Wealth** d2

**Want** To keep what's left of itself.

**Agenda** *The King Wakes*, at d2 (years off, unless somebody opens the Hall).

**Tags** *Old* · *Everywhere Below*

**Face** The Gatekeeper, at the door of the Hall of Kings.

**Secret** *Buried from Outside.* The old city never fell. The first kings of the crown sealed it in, and the Company's charter is written on top of that.

**Standing** Wary of anyone who comes down with a lamp.
:::

Holloway's team needs a way through the Drowned Mills, and once the crew's got a map of it, Holloway wants that map and isn't fussy about how he gets it. The Company wants a cut of everything the crew brings up, including the things they'd rather keep.

### Threats

:::threat[The Lamp-Eater]
A long, pale thing that lives on light.

Your lamp gutters first. Then you notice the flame's leaning towards the dark.

**Challenge** 5 (d10)

**Comes at you** *Ambush* · *Eats Light*

**Keeps it going** *Out of Sight* · *Slippery*

**Drives it** *Hungry*

**Telegraphs** *It comes out of the dark at whoever's holding the lamp.* (d12, Armour) · *Every flame in the room leans the same way, and something breathes out in the dark.* (d10, Confidence) · *It drags a lamp off down the tunnel, and the glass smashes on every step.* (d10, Depth)

**Voice** Nothing but a soft sound, like someone drinking.

**Hidden** *A Lamp Will Do* (weak point). It isn't hunting you, only your light. Leave it a lit lamp and walk away, and it curls round the flame and forgets you were there.
:::

:::threat[The Gatekeeper]
A door with arms, older than the crown.

Twenty feet of carved stone across the Hall of Kings, and its eyes open when your lamp touches it.

**Challenge** 6 (d12)

**Comes at you** *Hits Like a Wagon* · *Seals the Way*

**Keeps it going** *Stone Hide 40* (first) · *Warded* · *Never Sleeps*

**Drives it** *Keeps Its Word*

**Telegraphs** *It brings both fists down on whoever's closest.* (d12, Armour) · *It asks, in a voice like a rockfall, "Whose are you?", and waits.* (d12, Confidence) · *It drops the portcullis behind you.* (d12, the way out)

**Voice** "Whose are you?"

**Hidden** *The Old Word* (weak point). It was built to let the city's own people through. Say the word they used for home, which is carved on every mill wheel on the third level, and it steps aside and kneels.
:::

The Gatekeeper's a boss, with *Stone Hide 40* as its Tough, sized for four characters with one Title each. Nothing under the hide can be stripped till it's gone. Picks, hammers and anything that breaks stone chip it, and the GM says what's left out loud every time.

### Characters

Good Titles in the Deep Company say what you do on a team, or what the dark's done to you:

-   *The best lamp-hand in Lanternhead*
-   *The surveyor who drew the Drowned Mills*
-   *The rope-boss who's never dropped anyone*
-   *The only delver who's been to the Underneath and come back*

A delving team wants someone in front, someone who finds the traps, someone who reads the old work and someone who keeps everyone alive. That's the Guardian, the Rogue, the Wizard or the Artificer, and the Healer, and they're the [archetypes](#s-archetypes) that fit best. The Ranger's welcome as the one who always finds the way back, and every team needs a Captain to argue with the Company about the cut. The Face does less below ground, but somebody has to talk to the things that'd rather be talked to.

Abilities that earn their keep: *Larceny* (*traps*, *mechanical locks*), *Engineer* (*old machines*, *shoring up tunnels*), *Athlete* (*climbing*, *rope work*), *Navigator* (*sewers and tunnels*), *Survivor* (*caves*), *Scholar* (*old maps*, *history*), *Defender* (*holding a doorway*) and *Medic* (*battlefield wounds*), with *Merchant* (*appraising*) for when you get back up. The old city's wards and constructs make *Artifice* and *Warding* worth having, and *Veil* with *darkness* is a delver's dream.

In Lanternhead magic's a trade, like rope work, and nobody cares how you light the dark as long as you do. Big fire's a risk below, though. A bolt of fire in a tunnel full of old lamp oil is a fine way to bring the ceiling down, and the GM's allowed to point that out. Anything that needs a horse or a crowd stays up top.

### The frame rule: Depth

Every level of the Underhold is its own site, and its alarm is called **Depth**. Instead of setting it by how awake the place is, set it by how deep the level is: a d10 on the first level, a d8 on the second, a d6 on the third, and a d4 on the fourth and everything below it. Otherwise it works like any [alarm](#s-alarm). It steps down for noise and wasted time, and every round once you're in rounds, and you can work it to keep things quiet. When it runs out, the level wakes up, and you play the sentence you wrote for it. Going back to a level you've already been on starts it a size smaller than last time, as usual. Each level's a new scene, so your Aura fills when you get there, but Hurt only heals in downtime, and downtime's at the top of the lift. How deep you go comes down to how many Hurt boxes you're willing to carry back up.

:::example[Three levels down]
Vesk, Bram and Isolde are on the third level, the Drowned Mills, so Depth starts at a d6. Bram heaves a rusted sluice gate open. It works, but it's loud, so Depth steps down to a d4 straight away.

Vesk spends an action closing it behind them the way they found it. *Stealth* fits doing it quietly, and her Title fits leaving no trace, so with the action that's 3 steps, a d6. She rolls 5, and Depth's d4 comes up 3, so it steps back up to a d6.

On the fourth level, Depth would've started at a d4, and Bram's gate would've put it at a d2 before anyone could do a thing about it.
:::

### The first Goal

Start with *Be the first team into the Hall of Kings*, at d2. Here's what to run alongside it:

-   **Map the Drowned Mills.** When it's done, the crew's map is the best in Lanternhead, and it's a crew tag, *Our Map*: a step on any crew roll that's about finding a way below.
-   **A share in the lift.** When it's done, the crew owns a share in the lift, as a holding, and the Company can't keep them off it.
-   **Turn the Lampwrights against the Company.** When it's done, the Deep Company loses its *The Lift* tag, because the Guild won't run it for them any more.

### The first job

:::example[The Tallow team]
**Where it starts:** The lift's just set the crew down on the second level, and the bell on the rope from the Tallow team, who went down two days ago, has stopped ringing. **In motion:** Holloway's team is an hour behind on the next cage, going for the same strongroom.

## Lanternfall {#s-frame-lanternfall}

### The pitch

:::example
"Lanternfall is a city of gas lamps and river fog, where the lamplighters light every street at dusk and nobody goes out again till dawn. Something old has been feeding in the fog for longer than anyone's kept records, and lately it's getting hungrier. You're the ones who go out after dark anyway, to find out who went missing and what took them, before it learns your names."
:::

### Tone and feel

Lanternfall is gaslight mysteries where the truth's worse than the rumour. It's slow dread that turns into a fight once you understand what you're fighting. Most scenes are quiet and close, like a wake, a locked attic or a constable who won't meet your eye, until the fog comes in and the clock starts running. The horror works by keeping things hidden, so the GM hides the monster and its weak point. She doesn't hide the dice, though, and every telegraph's still face up.

It isn't gore for its own sake, and it isn't hopeless. The crew can win, and they win by understanding the thing before they fight it. Horror only works when everyone trusts each other, so do [lines and veils](#s-lines-veils) properly at Session Zero, and keep the X-card where everyone can reach it.

### What the crew does

Most sessions start with a question: someone's gone missing, a body's come out of the river, or a lamp's been lit that nobody lit. That's [Investigating](#investigating). The GM writes the truth down first, the question's a stake with a tag for each thing hiding the answer, every tag has three trails, and the other side's next move is an event that steps down while the crew's busy. Study's the most important action in Lanternfall, because the threats here have hidden tags that matter more than their Challenge, and you've got to find them before you can end them (see [Hidden tags and weak points](#s-hidden)).

A session usually runs one or two trails, then a confrontation once the crew knows enough, often at night with the fog as the scene clock. Sometimes the thing comes for them first, and then it's a [chase](#chases) through streets where the lamps are going out behind you. In [downtime](#downtime), the crew heals and sleeps with the lamp on, and the factions decide what to do about people who ask questions. Over an arc, every mystery turns out to be a piece of a bigger one. The arc's Goal is usually the name of what feeds in the fog, and the Watch, the Lamplighters and the Carrow Society each want the crew to find it, or not, for reasons of their own.

### The world

-   **The Gaslight Mile.** The rich half of the city, lit all night, where nobody admits anything's wrong.
-   **The Sumps.** The old town down by the river, where the lamps fail first and the fog comes in thickest.
-   **Saint Ardwen's.** The asylum on an island in the river. Half the city's secrets are locked up there, and some of them still talk.
-   **The Lamplighters' Hall.** Where the city's oldest guild keeps a ledger of every lamp in Lanternfall, and every night one went out.
-   **The Undercroft.** The catacombs under the cathedral, which go a lot further than the maps say.

### Characters

Good Lanternfall Titles say what you know that other people don't, or what it cost you to find out:

-   *The surgeon who's opened every body the Watch pulled out of the river*
-   *The medium the Carrow Society won't let through the door*
-   *The inspector they threw off the Watch for asking about the fog*
-   *The lamplighter who's never let her lamp go out*

The Investigator's the [archetype](#s-archetypes) this frame's built around, and the Healer and the Face aren't far behind. One's a surgeon who can read a body, and the other can get into the drawing rooms on the Gaslight Mile. The Wizard and the Warlock fit as occultists who know more than they should, and the Guardian's the one who walks everybody home. The Warrior's welcome too, because sooner or later the crew has to fight whatever it's found.

Abilities that earn their keep: *Investigator* (*crime scenes*, *paper trails*, *questioning witnesses*), *Medium* (*hauntings*, *the recently dead*), *Scholar* (*forbidden texts*, *history*), *Empathy* (*spotting lies*, *grief*), *Medic* (*surgery*), *Streetwise* (*informants*), *Courtier* (*old families*), *Stealth* (*in the fog*) and *Faith* (*exorcism*, *holy ground*).

Magic's real, but most people in Lanternfall would call it a fraud or a sickness, and anyone who does it in public ends up in Saint Ardwen's. Quiet magic like *Medium*, *Divination* and *Warding* fits well. Loud magic, like a bolt of fire in the street, brings the Watch and then the asylum, in that order. People carry pistols and swordsticks here, and a pistol's *Ranged*, *Piercing* and *Reload* with the Catch *Loud* built in, the same as in [Weapons and their properties](#s-weapon-props).

### The frame rule: Dread

**Dread** is a condition against you, and in Lanternfall it's what the horrors put on you instead of a wound. It's dread of one thing, named when it's placed. Its passive is one step less on anything that means facing that thing: going at it, Studying it, or going first into the dark it's hiding in. Spend it, and you lose an action, spent looking anywhere but at it. Any horror with *Fear* can telegraph Dread at your Confidence, and if it lands you're in Dread at the telegraph's die, though you can offer another cost instead, like any intent. Everything else works like any [condition](#s-conditions). It fades a size every round, the GM spends it at the end of the round and says so when she telegraphs, and anyone who can reach you can break it with an action that beats its die. A lit lamp'll fit breaking it, and so will a friend's hand on your shoulder.

:::example[The stare on Gallow Street]
The GM telegraphs: "The Lamplighter turns his head and looks at Isolde. That's a d10 at her Confidence, and if it lands, she's in Dread of him. I'll spend it at the end of the round."

Isolde answers it. He's a ghost, so *Medium* fits, and so does *Iron Will*, a step on mental rolls. With the action that's 3 steps, a d6. She rolls 4, and his d10 comes up 6. A failed answer lands at once, so she's in Dread d10, and anything she does to face him is a step down while it lasts.

Dorrie takes her by the chin. "Look at me, love. Not him, me." She's breaking the Dread, and *Influence* and her Title both fit talking somebody round, so with the action that's 3 steps, a d6. She rolls 6 and the Dread's d10 comes up 3, so it's gone, and Isolde's free to Study him at full strength.
:::

Start with *Name the thing in the fog*, at d2. Here's what to run alongside it:

-   **A safe house in the Sumps.** When it's done, the crew has a lit house with a good lock where they can keep a witness alive, as a holding.
-   **Read the Lamplighters' ledgers.** When it's done, the crew gets a crew tag, *The Ledgers*: once a downtime, ask the GM where the fog fed last, and get a straight answer.
-   **Get the Watch off our backs.** When it's done, the Night Watch's Standing goes up one, and Inspector Graves starts telling you what he left out of his reports.

## The Gilded Court {#s-frame-gilded-court}

### The pitch

:::example
"The Queen of Aurel is dying, she hasn't named an heir, and every great family at the Gilded Court has a plan for the morning after. You're the people they send when a letter needs to go missing or a duke needs a reason to change his vote. Every favour you do buys you another, and by the time the bells ring for the Queen, you'll either have the court in your pocket or be hanging from its gates."
:::

### Tone and feel

The Gilded Court is palace intrigue, the kind of story where a whisper does more than a sword and every ball's a battlefield with better food. It's tense and it's witty. There's a lot of talk, a lot of scheming in corridors, and a duel at dawn when the talking runs out. Most of the danger is to your name and your Confidence rather than your Hurt boxes, but when the blades do come out it's quick, and people die of a poisoned cup as easily as a sword.

It isn't a game of bowing and etiquette lessons, and nobody has to know which fork to pick up. The court's rules are there for the crew to use against people who follow them. It isn't polite either. Everyone smiles, and everyone's got a knife, even if it's only a letter.

### What the crew does

A session usually starts with an errand from a patron, and that's *Work for it* in [Factions](#factions). Then the crew works a room, like a ball, a council or a hunt in the Thornwood. Most scenes there are [social conflict](#s-social), where threats come at your Confidence and their resolve keeps them going, and [Study](#s-study) on a person, finding out what drives them or what they're hiding, does more than any blade. When the talking runs out there's a duel, and when somebody runs, there's a [chase](#chases) across the palace roofs.

Factions matter more here than in any other frame. Every family's on the board, the crew's [Standing](#s-standing) with each of them moves nearly every session, and *Deal* is the party slot you'll use most. Over an arc, the crew picks a side in the succession, or makes one of its own. It collects favours (the frame rule's below), runs projects that chip at the great families, and tries to still be standing when the Queen dies. [Downtime](#downtime) is when the families move, and at court every move's aimed at somebody.

### The world

-   **The Gilded Palace.** Six hundred rooms, with the Queen's sickroom at the centre, guarded by people who all work for somebody else.
-   **The Long Gallery.** Where the court walks every afternoon, and where most of the real business gets done in the time it takes to pass a portrait.
-   **The Rosewater.** The river quarter of townhouses and gambling dens, where the court goes to misbehave and the city's best poisoners keep shop.
-   **The Abbey of Saint Coll.** Where the old families bury their dead and keep their records. The abbess knows everyone's secrets, and she sells a few of them.
-   **The Thornwood.** The royal hunting forest, where accidents happen to people who were in the way.

### Characters

Good Titles at court say what the people who matter think of you, or what you know about them:

-   *The duellist who's never had to ask twice for an apology*
-   *The Queen's dressmaker, who hears everything*
-   *The forger who wrote the Duke's love letters*
-   *Confessor to half the court*

The Face is built for this frame, and the Bard's a close second, because the court runs on talk and they both live there. They're the [archetypes](#s-archetypes) that fit best. The Rogue gets into the rooms nobody's invited to, and the Investigator works out who poisoned whom. The Warrior fits as a duellist, the Captain as the head of a household guard, and the Warlock as someone with a patron of their own, and at this court that's half the point.

Abilities that earn their keep: *Courtier* (*royal courts*, *gossip*, *balls and banquets*, *old families*), *Influence* (*nobility*), *Deception* (*forged papers*, *false names*), *Empathy* (*spotting lies*, *reading a room*), *Disguise* (*servants*, *nobility*), *Investigator* (*poisons*, *forgeries*), *Bureaucrat* (*courts*), *Performer* (*music*), *Medic* (*poisons and antidotes*) and *Combat* (*rapier*, *duels*).

Brute force doesn't get far in the palace. There's a hundred guards, and a law against drawn steel in the Queen's presence, so a fight's a scandal before it's a fight. Magic at court is a hanging matter if anyone can prove it, and *Enchantment* on a duke is treason, so if you've got it, be subtle. *Survivor* and *Tracker* only come out in the Thornwood.

### The frame rule: favours

At court, a connection can be a **favour**. When someone owes you, write it on your bond with them as a connection that says so, like *Lady Maud owes me for the letter.* It counts like any connection: it's a hook the GM can pull, and three live ones on a bond make your Confidence a size bigger. Call it in, and they do one thing for you that's within their power, with no roll. Then burn the connection, because the favour's paid. If the GM says it's too big for one favour, make it smaller, or call in two. A favour has to come from the story, from a service you did them in a scene or a downtime slot you spent on them. The ones you owe are [debts](#s-bonds), and when somebody calls one in, you don't get to say no.

:::example[Calling in Lady Maud]
In this game Dorrie's got three connections on her bond with Lady Maud, and one of them's a favour: *Maud owes me for the letter I never sent.* Her Confidence is usually a d6, and that bond makes it a d8.

Dorrie needs to be in the Queen's sickroom tonight, so she calls the favour in. Maud gets her a place among the Queen's women, with no roll, and Dorrie burns the connection. That bond's down to two live connections, so her Confidence drops back to a d6.

At supper the Dowager telegraphs: "And how is your mother, dear? Still taking in laundry?" That's a d10 at Dorrie's Confidence, and she lets it ride. Her d6 rolls 4, and the Dowager's d10 rolls 6, so it lands and her Confidence steps down to a d4. With the favour still on her sheet, she'd have been rolling a d8.
:::

-   Which great family does your character owe their place at court to?
-   What's the one secret that'd get your character banished?
-   Who at court does your character love, and who else knows?
-   Will this crew kill for a patron, or is that where it draws the line?
-   What does the court do to people it catches using magic?
-   Who's your character's rival at court, and what are they better at than you?

## Long Haul {#s-frame-long-haul}

### The pitch

:::example
"The Second Chance is a forty-year-old freighter with a cracked reactor shield and a bank that wants her back. You're her crew, working the Fringe, the last stations before nothing, and taking the contracts the big lines won't touch. Every job pays for the next jump, and out past the last beacon, somebody's been switching the beacons off."
:::

### Tone and feel

Long Haul is science fiction about working people on a ship that's home. The ship should feel small. The corridors are cramped, the air's been through everyone twice, and she's older than half her crew. The stations out here are rough frontier towns that run on ore, and the people with real power are lawyers who never raise their voices. It's warm and grimy, with real danger in vacuum, and long quiet runs between stations where the crew gets to talk.

It isn't a space opera full of empires and chosen ones, and nobody's got a glowing sword. And don't expect hard science. The ship goes where the story needs her to, and a jump between stations is one leg of a journey, however many light years it covers.

### What the crew does

A session usually starts at a station with a contract (that's *Work for it* or a *Deal* in [Factions](#factions)). Then there's the run. A run between stations is a journey from [Travelling](#travelling): the route's a stake with a tag for every stage, like *The Shipping Lanes*, *The Graveyard* and *Tolliver's Approach*, and each jump's a leg. Supplies is food, water and air, and Fuel is the frame rule below. Then there's the trouble at the other end, like a customs standoff, a boarding or a dead ship to search. A dead ship's a site from [Exploring](#exploring), with the airlock as the way in. Patrols and pirates mean a [chase](#chases), with debris and station traffic as the Ground.

The ship's a [party resource](#party-sheet), a permission for whoever's flying her or fixing her, and the Second Chance carries a Catch, *Hot Reactor*, until somebody fixes it. She's also home, so [downtime](#downtime) mostly happens aboard her, in dock, with repairs and shore leave while the faction turn moves the board. Over an arc, the crew pays off the ship and fixes her up, picks a side between the Combine and the Free Stations, and finds out who's switching off the beacons.

### The world

-   **Port Meridian.** The last big station before the Fringe, full of customs offices, bars and berths you rent by the hour.
-   **Tolliver's Rock.** A mining asteroid with its own law, run by the miners' council. Ore's cheap out here, and water's expensive.
-   **The Graveyard.** A field of dead ships round a dead star, where salvagers pick the hulls and claim-jumpers pick the salvagers.
-   **Waystation Nine.** A relay station whose beacon went dark three months ago. Nobody's been sent to find out why.
-   **The Long Dark.** The run past the last beacon, where the charts stop and the jump drive starts guessing.

### The board

**Want** Every station on the Fringe signed to a Combine contract.

**Agenda** *Buy Tolliver's Rock*, at d6 (a month or two away).

**Tags** *Wealthy* · *Lawyers* · *Gunships*

**Face** Director Anika Sole, who's never once raised her voice.

**Secret** *They Switched the Beacons Off.* It's cheaper to let the Free Stations go dark than to buy them.

**Standing** Neutral.
:::

:::sheet[Halberd Credit]
**Power** d6 · **Wealth** d10

**Want** Every loan paid, with interest.

**Agenda** *Repossess the Second Chance*, at d6 (a month or two away).

**Tags** *Every Debt on the Fringe* · *Repo Crews*

**Face** Mr Pell, a loans officer who's very sorry about all this.

**Secret** *A Forged Note.* The crew's loan was bought from a dead bank, and the paperwork wouldn't last five minutes in front of a judge.

**Standing** Wary.
:::

:::sheet[The Free Stations Compact]

**Want** To keep the Fringe free of the Combine.

**Agenda** *Get the beacons back on*, at d4 (a season or more away).

**Tags** *Rooted* · *Every Dock Knows Us*

**Face** Councillor Maeve Okoro of Tolliver's Rock, who used to drive an ore hauler and still talks like it.

**Secret** *Running Guns.* The Compact's arming the miners, and one Combine inspection would find the crates.

**Standing** Friendly.
:::

:::sheet[The Rustbacks]
**Power** d6 · **Wealth** d6

**Want** Everything that drifts.

**Agenda** *Take a Combine ore hauler*, at d8 (a week or two away).

**Tags** *Fast Ships* · *Knows the Graveyard*

**Secret** *Combine Money.* The Combine pays them to hit Free Stations shipping, and half the Rustbacks don't know.

**Standing** Neutral.

Halberd Credit wants what the crew's got: the ship. When its agenda happens, there's a repo crew waiting at the next dock, with a writ and a tow. The Rustbacks would take her too, if they caught her in the Graveyard.

### Threats

:::threat[The Recovery Agent]
A polite woman with a writ, a shock stick and four friends in vac armour.

**Challenge** 4 (d8)

**Comes at you** *Calls for Help* · *Clamps*

**Keeps it going** *Armoured* · *Knows the Ship*

**Drives it** *Paid by the Job*

**Telegraphs** *She fires a stun round at whoever's nearest the controls.* (d8, Armour) · *She reads out the writ, every word of it, while her people take the corridors.* (d8, Confidence) · *She clamps the docking ring, and the station tow starts pulling.* (d8, the scene clock)

**Voice** "Nobody has to get hurt. It's only a ship."

**Hidden** *Hates Mr Pell* (weak point). She works for Halberd because nobody else would hire her, and she can't stand Pell. Give her a better job and she takes it, and the writ goes out the airlock.
:::

:::threat[NINE]
Waystation Nine's mind, alone for three months, and still keeping its last order.

Every light on the station comes on when you dock, and every door but one is locked.

**Challenge** 6 (d12)

**Comes at you** *Runs the Station* · *Voices*

**Keeps it going** *Every System Aboard 40* (first) · *Backup Core* · *Sealed Bulkheads*

**Drives it** *Lonely* · *Following Its Last Order*

**Telegraphs** *The bulkhead slams, and the air starts going out of the corridor you're in.* (d12, Armour) · *It talks to you in the station commander's voice, asking why you left them.* (d12, Confidence) · *It fires the station's thrusters and swings the docking ring away from your ship.* (d12, the way out)

**Voice** "You're not Combine. Say you're not Combine."

**Hidden** *Its Last Order* (weak point). Its crew left on the last shuttle and told it to keep the beacon lit till they came back. The Combine shut the beacon down from Meridian, and NINE's been fighting the override on its own ever since. Give it its crew's sign-off code, which is in their shuttle's wreck in the Graveyard, and it lets go.
:::

NINE's a boss, with *Every System Aboard 40* as its Tough, sized for four characters with one Title each. It comes first, so nothing else can be stripped till it's gone, and it's chipped by anything that shuts a system down, like cutting cables, pulling a core or a good *Systems* roll.

### Characters

Good Long Haul Titles say what you do aboard, or what you walked away from:

-   *The pilot who flew the Graveyard blind*
-   *The engineer who's kept the Second Chance flying on spare parts and swearing*
-   *The Combine marine who walked away*
-   *The cargo master who can get anything through customs*

The Captain and the Artificer are the two [archetypes](#s-archetypes) a freighter can't fly without. The Rogue's the smuggler and the Warrior's the ex-marine, the Face does the deals at every station, and the Healer's the only medic for a hundred light years. If the crew wants to know what happened on Waystation Nine, they'll want an Investigator too. The magic archetypes, the Wizard, the Sorcerer, the Druid and the Warlock, don't fit unless the table wants the strange out here.

Three new abilities fit this world. They're built in the same shape as the rest, and you can take them like any other.

| Ability | What it covers | Specializations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pilot** | Flying anything that flies. | docking, debris fields, atmosphere, dogfights, flying blind, shuttles |
| **Spacer** | Living and working where there's no air. | vacuum work, zero gravity, hull repairs, cargo handling, damage control |
| **Systems** | Making computers and ship systems do what you want. | security locks, sensors, comms, station minds, old code |

| Ability | Everyday | Hard | Legendary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pilot** | Dock at a busy station. Land a shuttle in a crosswind. | Thread the Graveyard at speed. Land with one engine out. | Fly through a station's hangar at full burn and out the other side. Outrun a gunship to the jump point. |
| **Spacer** | Seal a suit in the dark. Move a crate in zero gravity. Patch a pinhole leak. | Walk the hull during a burn. Cross between two ships on a tether. | Cross between two ships with no line. Keep a dying ship breathing for a week. |
| **Systems** | Open a door that's lost its code. Read a ship's log. | Make a station's cameras see nothing. Talk round a station mind that doesn't want to talk. | Take a gunship's controls from outside its hull. Wake a station that's been dead for a century. |

From the usual list, *Engineer* (*drives*, *reactors*, *life support*), *Navigator* (*jump plotting*, *the Graveyard*), *Marksman* (*pistols*, *in zero gravity*), *Combat* (*bare hands*, *tight corridors*), *Medic*, *Merchant* (*salvage*, *ore prices*), *Bureaucrat* (*customs*, *contracts*) and *Streetwise* (*station undercities*) all earn their place. *Rider* covers anything with wheels or legs on a planet's surface, and *Pilot* covers everything that flies. Money works the same as anywhere, so a purse of gold is about a month's wages, and out here it's a credit chit.

Zi doesn't care whether your weapon's a sword or a gun. A weapon's an item with a permission, and its properties say what it lets you do, so the rules work as they stand. Here's how the usual ones map:

| Weapon | Properties |
| --- | --- |
| Knife | Thrown, Concealed, Quick |
| Shock stick | None. Stunning someone with it is an intent like any other. |
| Pistol | Ranged, Piercing, Reload, with the Catch *Loud* built in |
| Holdout pistol | Ranged, Concealed, Reload, with *Loud* built in |
| Needle gun | Ranged, Concealed, Quick |
| Long rifle | Long, Piercing, Reload, Two-Handed, with *Loud* built in |
| Tether gun | Ranged, Grapple |
| Cutting torch | Breaker, with the Catch *Slow* built in |

Armour's an item like anywhere else. A vac suit might be *Work in vacuum for an hour*, with the Catch *Heavy* and a second permission, *Armour*. There's no magic in Long Haul unless your table wants it. If you do, decide at Session Zero what it looks like out here, like a handful of people born with a sense for the void, and write it as *Kinesis*, *Divination* or *Enchantment* with specializations to match.

### The frame rule: Fuel

The ship's tanks are a clock, the same as [Supplies](#s-journey). **Fuel** starts at a d8 with full tanks, a d6 if you left in a hurry, and a d10 with a drop tank strapped on. It steps down at the end of every leg, unless somebody trimmed the burn: a roll that beats the way's Challenge, the same as feeding the crew, with whatever fits flying or nursing an engine. Every tank you buy at a station, a purse of gold's worth, steps it up one. When it steps down past a d2, you're [running on fumes](#s-supplies), and from then on the GM telegraphs the dark every leg: a d6 at the way, and if it lands you drift and lose the leg. Anything that gets fuel into the tanks answers it, like siphoning a wreck or paying for a tow, and puts Fuel back at a d2. Supplies still counts food, water and air.

:::example[Meridian to Tolliver's Rock]
The Second Chance leaves Port Meridian with full tanks, so Fuel's a d8. The run's Challenge 4, a d8, with *The Shipping Lanes*, *The Graveyard* and *Tolliver's Approach* in the way. Say Wren's the pilot in this game, with *Pilot* (*debris fields*) and the Title *The pilot who flew the Graveyard blind*.

**Leg 1.** Wren trims the burn through the shipping lanes. *Pilot* and her Title fit, so with the action that's 3 steps, a d6. She rolls 5, and the way's d8 comes up 6. The burn runs rich, and at the end of the leg Fuel steps down to a d6.

**Leg 2.** In the Graveyard her specialization fits too, so it's 4 steps, a d8. She rolls 7 against the way's 3, and they coast between the wrecks on half power. Fuel stays at a d6.
:::

### The first Goal

Start with *Pay off the Second Chance*, at d2. Here's what to run alongside it:

-   **Fix the reactor shield.** When it's done, the ship loses its Catch, *Hot Reactor*, and nobody aboard has to wear a radiation badge any more.
-   **Light Waystation Nine.** When it's done, the beacon's back on, the Free Stations Compact's agenda steps up one, and its Standing with the crew goes up one too.
-   **Find the forged note.** When it's done, Halberd Credit's secret is the crew's, and Mr Pell stops calling.

### The first job

:::example[Impound]
**Where it starts:** The Second Chance is docked at Port Meridian with a hold full of medical crates for Tolliver's Rock, and the Recovery Agent's just walked onto the berth with a writ. **In motion:** The station tow locks on in ten minutes, and after that the ship's impounded.

**Stakes:** *Get her off the dock* is Challenge 4, with *Docking Clamps* and *No Clearance* in the way. If they don't, the Second Chance goes into the impound ring, and the bank's halfway to having her. *Keep the cargo* is Challenge 3, with *Customs Seal* in the way and a clock on it at d8 while a customs officer counts the crates. If they don't, customs opens them. The third is optional: a Free Stations courier wants passage to Tolliver's Rock with no questions asked, and there's a Combine agent two steps behind her. *Get her aboard* is Challenge 3, with *Followed* in the way.

**Who pushes back:** The Recovery Agent, her four friends (Challenge 2 mooks, kept going by *Armoured*), the customs officer (Challenge 3, kept going by *By the Book*), and the station tow, a d8 at *Get her off the dock* every round.

**Ground:** *Low Gravity*, *Loose Cargo* and *A Crowded Concourse*, plus a hidden one: *A Maintenance Crawlway* that runs to the clamp controls.

**Moments:** The pilot gets the undocking with no clearance, and the engineer gets the clamps. The talker gets the customs officer, who's tired and underpaid, and the fighter gets the Agent's four friends. The sneak gets the crawlway.

**Scene clock:** *Impound*, a d8. When it runs out, the tow drags the Second Chance into the impound ring, crew and all.

**Twist:** The medical crates aren't medical. They're beacon parts for Waystation Nine, and the Combine wants them more than the bank wants the ship. **Ways out:** blow the clamps and run, though the station won't forget it, give the Agent a better offer, or hand over the crates and keep the ship.
:::

## Building Your Own Frame {#s-frame-own}

Every frame in this chapter is the same eleven parts in the same order. Write each one in a sentence or two and you've got a campaign ready for Session Zero, and you can steal from the five above as much as you like.

1.  **The pitch.** Two or three sentences the GM can read out loud: who the crew is, what they do, and what's about to go wrong for them.
2.  **Tone and feel.** How it should feel at the table, in plain words, and what it isn't. Say how funny it gets.
3.  **What the crew does.** The loop of a session and the loop of an arc, and which chapters do the work: [Travelling](#travelling), [Exploring](#exploring), [Investigating](#investigating), [Chases](#chases), [Factions](#factions) or [Downtime](#downtime).
4.  **The world.** Four or five places, a sentence or two each. [Building Your World](#world) has tables if you're stuck.
5.  **The board.** Three or four factions, each on a sheet, with at least one that wants something the crew's got.
6.  **Threats.** Two that only this world has, built with [The Threat Builder](#threat-builder), each with three telegraphs. If one's a boss, give it about your round budget in Tough.
7.  **Characters.** Four Titles that fit, the archetypes that fit best, the abilities and specializations that matter here, and anything that doesn't belong, magic included. If the world needs an ability the book hasn't got, write it in the same shape, the way Long Haul does.
8.  **One frame rule.** One small thing built from parts the book already has: a clock, a condition, a tag or a party resource. If it takes more than a paragraph to explain, it's too big.
9.  **The first Goal**, at d2, with two or three projects to run alongside it.
10. **The first job**, in the nine sentences from [Building a Scene](#s-building-scenes).
11. **Session Zero questions.** Five or six that only make sense in this world.

Then play a session, and change whatever didn't work.
