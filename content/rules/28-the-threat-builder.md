:::quote
You don't need a page of numbers for every monster. A threat is a Challenge and a few tags.
:::

This chapter's just the procedure. [Running the Game](#s-building-threats) has the thinking behind it, and [The Bestiary](#bestiary) has forty threats built this way.

## Building a threat {#building-a-threat}

1.  **Decide what it is.** Roll on the table below, or pick one. That gives you a Challenge and a couple of tags to start with.
2.  **Choose its size.** A mook has one tag that keeps it going, or none. A named threat has two, and a boss has three or more, plus a weak point. A boss that should last gets [multipliers](#s-tough-gm) on its big tags, like *Armoured ×6*, and a special encounter can be [Layered](#s-layered).
3.  **How it comes at you.** Give it one or two. They're what its telegraphs will look like.
4.  **What keeps it going.** Give it as many as its size says. That's its health, and a tag with a multiplier counts as that many wins.
5.  **What drives it.** Anything with a name gets at least one. It's how the crew talks to it, buys it, scares it or turns it.
6.  **What it's weak to, if anything.** A [vulnerability](#s-vulnerable), like *Vulnerable to fire* or *Dry as Tinder*, adds a tick to every win it fits, so Ash's fire on a *Dry as Tinder* Warden takes two off its armour. Extra ticks are wasted on a plain tag, so save them for threats with multipliers. They're often hidden, and [Study](#s-study) finds them.
7.  **Hide one.** Every named threat and every boss gets a hidden tag. On a boss, make it the weak point, or make the weak point something they can see but can't reach yet.
8.  **Give it a power, if it has one.** That's an impossible thing it can do, written as a tag and telegraphed from the round budget. Most threats don't need one. There's more in [unique mechanics](#s-unique-threats). If it has Forms, write *Forms (2)* or *Forms (3)*, and a Forms list of about ten tags to pick its new shapes from.
9.  **Name it, and give it a voice.** Write one line for it to say in character, and the first thing the crew notices.

## Building a Threat, Start to Finish {#building-a-threat-start-to-finish}

The GM needs a boss for the end of the arc. She rolls 13, which is **the champion**: Challenge 6, starting with *Hits Like a Wagon*, *Armoured* and *Proud*. She wants him quicker than a wagon, so she swaps that one for *Reach*: he fights with a long-hafted axe.

For how he comes at you, she rolls a 9, *Calls for Help*, and decides that's his wolf. A boss needs three things keeping it going. She's already got *Armoured*. She rolls a 10, which is *Relentless*, and she writes one of her own, *Feeds*: he gets stronger on blood. *Proud* drives him, and he isn't weak to anything. Then comes the hidden tag, and the weak point: the wolf's a curse, not a pet, and it has a name.

:::rule
**Redhand**: Challenge 6.\
**Comes at you:** *Reach* · *Calls for Help*.\
**Keeps him going:** *Armoured ×3 (first)* · *Relentless* · *Feeds ×3*.\
**Drives him:** *Proud*.\
**Hidden:** *The Wolf Is the Curse* (weak point). His real name is written in Vane's ledger. Say it to his face and the wolf leaves him, and so does everything that kept him standing.\
**Voice:** *"You are the last one standing. That's not a compliment to you. That's a fact about me."*\
**What you notice:** The wolf arrives first. It always does.
:::

Three plain tags would be three wins against his d12, and a crew of four can manage that in a single round. So she gives two of them [multipliers](#s-tough-gm). A Challenge 6 boss gets about ×6 between its tags, and he's got two that matter, his armour and his feeding, so she splits it: *Armoured ×3*, which comes first, and *Feeds ×3*. Her table's a starting crew of four, a [threat budget](#s-threat-budget) of 40, so they stay as they are. With three Titles each it'd be 50, and they'd be ×4s. Now he's seven wins the long way: three through the armour, one for *Relentless* and three to stop him feeding. A crew of four lands about two wins a round, so that's three or four rounds, or one win if the crew has read Vane's ledger. That makes him a boss worth a session, because he's hard to grind down and he's got a secret worth hunting for.

At the table, Redhand telegraphs from the round budget like everything else. His axe is a d12, and *Reach* lets it pin someone at the end of it. His wolf goes for the scene's clock with a d12: *it howls, and the pack answers.* Write three like that on his card before the session, the way every threat in [The Bestiary](#bestiary) has them, and you'll never be stuck for what he does next.
