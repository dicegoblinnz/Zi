# Abnu: the canon

Abnu's lore, put together on 30 Oct 2026 for its whole world (stages 15 and 16 of `notes/builder-plan.md`). Those stages write Abnu from this file and nothing else, so it holds all of it: Mo's picks, what his sources agree on, the calls Claude made where they don't, and what stays open. Nothing from his old systems' rules is here: no dice ladders, no Gates or soul abilities as rules, no Flow permissions, Consequences, Truth or cards. On a card or a page, what something is at the table is said in Zi'Dara's terms.

- Everything here is canon unless it's marked **Claude's call** or **open**.
- A call is Claude's reading where the sources disagree or say nothing. They're all listed at the end for Mo's yes or no, and they stand till he answers.
- Open stays open. No card, page or line of history answers it. It can be a hook, a question someone asks, or a Secret that says nobody knows.
- Where a stage needs more than this (a ruler's name, a district, a line of history), it writes it in the shape of what's here, and lists what it made up in its message to Mo.

## Mo's picks (30 Oct 2026)

- **The Ezkian Union's the scholars' steam republic.** Scholars running from Alysta founded it, and it has steam, clockwork, Ezkia Prime and the Council of Nine. The Ezki hunters of the cold, the fastest people alive, aren't in it.
- **Xurux is the city of the dead, and only that.** It isn't the molefolk kingdom, and who keeps it was left open. Claude's calls say how the world reads it.
- **The molefolk are their own faction, and secret.** They live in a special place underground between Alysta and the Soths, near the coast, and most of the world doesn't know it's there. If the world has a secret faction, it's them.
- **Abnu starts in its own now.** A thousand years after the Sundering, the Queen stirring and Alysta's ban on the Seven fresh, with nothing on the board that needs one adventure. The Six Souls can still be run in it.
- **Abnu's stages come after Night City 2077**, ahead of 2045.

## The feel

Tragic-heroic, and hopepunk if the table wants it. High fantasy with grit: soul-eyes glowing, ancient wounds, floating relics and drowned ruins, gods who aren't gods and monsters who aren't monsters, next to muddy streets, bent officials, hungry kids and people making hard calls with half the facts. Nobody's chosen. The world's broken, the odds are bad and the truth's heavy to carry, and people get up and do the work anyway.

It raises big questions (what a soul is, what we owe the dead, what's worth keeping when everything ends) and never answers them for the table. Four kinds of story run under everything: who you are when your memories aren't all yours, what you leave behind, the ties that hold people up, and what you do once you know the truth.

On top, the world's doing well. Nations keep a watchful peace, trade moves, craft gets better every year, and people build, love, bury their dead and get up the next morning to do it again. That's the real answer to what's under the water, and the board should feel it.

## The myth

The same sky over every table that plays here.

### Abnu

Abnu's the name of the world, of the stone and of an empire that once ruled most of it, and the ancients didn't tell the three apart. To them the world was the stone: a living thing, a sleeping god or a prison, depending on who you asked and how scared they were that week. Every nation still says the name, and fewer remember what it meant first. The few who know what an amulet does call a stilled soul abnu too, stone.

### The shape of the world

A globe of wide oceans and scattered lands. People began in the Cradle, where the Anunnaki first opened their eyes (Claude's call: the Cradle's the western continent, where the oldest nation, the Soths, still stands).

### The ten moons

Ten moons hung over Abnu once, each its own colour and each tied to a different side of the soul, and every new life drew its soul from one. The Anunnaki watched them from observatories, honoured them in temples and, in texts most nations would rather burn than read, drew on them for weapons.

Nine are left. The tenth fell into the sea, and it lies at the bottom of the Sundering Deep, a trench that could swallow a nation, still shaking against what holds it and sinking a little further every century.

Most people don't know there were ten (Claude's call). Every Alystan doorway has a small brass moon over it, and nobody can say why there are nine. The Etweil hum the tenth verse of their songs instead of singing it. Thelisian carvings show ten. Children sing a counting rhyme that doesn't scan, because a line's gone. Sailors know the sea's wrong over the Deep, and that's all they know.

### The Garden of Souls

A soul doesn't end at death. It rises into the Garden of Souls, a realm laid over the sky that soul sight can see: the souls of plants low down, close to the world, animals and people higher, and the Anunnaki near the top, at the edge of whatever's past it. It isn't paradise. It's a cycle, souls resting and coming back, and it's how the world's always renewed itself.

It's failing. Fewer souls come back, the Garden's crowded with souls that can't move on, and every rebirth costs more memory than the last. Some of the souls that can't rise end up in Xurux. The Seven are about the only ones who know how bad it's got, and they've never stopped trying to hold it together.

### The soul and the Gates

Soul's the living energy in everything, from a blade of grass to a beast to a person. It isn't magic, which is a lazy word for it. It's what makes the living different from the dead. Soul-work is working what's around you: a torch's fire made into a blast, a river sent over a road. It takes years of training, and it's rarer than the empire admits.

How far a soul's opened is counted in Gates, reached through discipline and honest self-knowledge, never through blood:

- **Asar, the First Gate.** The eye opens. Your eyes glow, and you see souls, the Garden and other people's soul-work, and you can put soul into one small point, a fist or a word. Most who train stop here. To anyone without soul sight it's a faint shimmer in the eyes, if they look hard.
- **Mul, the Second Gate.** The body opens, and soul runs through a whole limb, which glows with it. It's the mark of someone who's put in the years.
- **Kashurra, the Third Gate.** The self opens. The whole body glows, and the elements bend. It's as far as anyone gets in one life, and those who get there are remembered.
- **Shi, the Fourth Gate.** The soul leaves the body, which lies still till it's back. The Anunnaki did it like breathing, and the Queen did it and wouldn't stop. Nobody living has reached it in a thousand years, and the Seven watch closely to keep it that way.

The First Gate takes a year of training, unless someone grew up with it. Monasteries take no coin, only favours, trials and a share of the work, and you owe them after. Schools charge, and a year's board and teaching costs a fortune. Someone raised to it might train for ten years and open the Second.

At Zi'Dara's table the Gates are lore: how people talk about a soul-worker, never a rule. Soul-work's played with the book's archetypes and permissions, like an Elementalist bending a river, a Healer mending or an Oracle seeing what's coming.

### The Anunnaki

The first souls, the first people, when the world was young. They raised cities of impossible geometry, passed through the Gates like breathing, lived for centuries and came back with every memory, and their civilisation covered the world. They weren't gods. They were closer to the world's first dream of itself. They gave mortals the means to work the soul. Then they died, slowly, over generations, for a very human reason: some of them wouldn't let go.

### The Queen

Her name's lost from every text that's left. She's the Queen, the First Usurper, the One Who Would Not Die: the greatest of the Anunnaki, a ruler, a thinker and a soul-worker of nearly impossible power. When she died, she refused it. She forced her soul back into the world, took someone else's body and kept ruling, and her followers copied her. That refusal broke a cycle that had always worked, and every soul born since is weaker for it, cut off from the fullness the Anunnaki had.

### The Seven

Abnu's own spirit rose up as seven guardians to stand against her: Courage, Wisdom, Compassion, Justice, Humility, Integrity and Resilience. People treat them like gods, and they aren't. They aren't infallible either. They've made mistakes that cost people, let things happen they could've stopped, and stopped things that maybe should've happened. What makes them the Seven is that a thousand years on, hidden, tired and imperfect, they're still at their posts: watching the Garden, watching for a second Fourth Gate, watching nations they can't openly rule, and keeping secrets they've judged the world isn't ready for. Hardly anyone ever sees one, bar the rare soul-worker who reaches far enough.

People pray along with them, not to them. A Montian picks a virtue at breakfast and tries to be it till evening, and nobody expects an answer. The Seven are honoured openly in Montia and the Soths, as decoration in Alysta, and not at all in the empire's schools.

**The seventh door.** Every Montian temple's built low and wide with seven doors. Six are used. The seventh's kept shut, never locked or sealed, just never opened, and a priest will tell you plainly why: because one of them isn't here. Push and you'll hear that's all anyone knows, which isn't quite a lie. Which of the Seven isn't here is open.

**The Traditions.** A school of thought has grown round each of the Seven, training its students toward that one virtue rather than toward power. They're strongest in Montia, tolerated in the Soths and quietly gone from Alysta.

### The war and the Sundering

The Queen's refusal started a war, not between nations but between two ideas of what a soul owes the world: that a soul's owed forever, or that death has to mean something. It lasted centuries, broke continents and shattered the Anunnaki's cities. At the end she bound her soul to every Anunnaki still loyal to her and used them all to drag the moons out of the sky. The sky burned, the oceans boiled and mountains fell, and her own people died fuelling it. The Seven caught nine and hauled them back, at a cost nobody's written down. Then they sealed her and her loyalists inside the tenth moon and bound it with everything they had left.

The world calls it the Sundering. She battered at the moon for generations, till it broke from its course and fell, slowly, into the deepest sea. She's still in it, and she's getting stronger as it sinks. She'll get out. The only real question's when, and what the people standing here then will do about it.

### After the Sundering

Nine moons weren't enough. The Seven brought rebirth back for every soul, not just the strong, but the fallen moon pulls at every soul that passes, and each rebirth strips memory. Coming back became a second death.

### Alysta

The Seven set a soul called Alysta to guide people toward virtue, and he was the first to lose his memory to rebirth. An amulet was forged to hold his memory across lives, and the moon's fall weakened it too. Over centuries he forgot and remembered, built monuments and pulled them down, and banned the worship of the Seven he'd served. Worn out, he buried the amulet and asked that nobody ever find it.

A hundred and fifty years later a merchant found it and gave it to the emperor of Alysta, who was Alysta again and didn't know. Every life came back to him at once. That was about 380 years ago. He took the throne, ended the wars, rebuilt a ruined empire, restored order and abolished slavery, the first ruler in the world to do it. Then he chose never to bring back the Seven's worship, never to encourage awakening, and to make his own weaker amulets the fashion: the same suppression, worn by choice, generation after generation.

He's taken his own sons' bodies down the centuries, believing it necessary. He can't take his amulet off without dying, and anyone else who puts it on is taken over by him: the body changes and he doesn't. The amulet won't let his soul open a single Gate, and he's the most dangerous person in Abnu anyway, because anything he faces is something he set up years before, in a building he built, through an institution he wrote, against people whose names he had long ago. For 380 years he's been trying to find another way through that door, and his own notes keep count (*Attempt eleven. Not yet.*).

He's stolen every awakened future in Alysta for centuries out of fear, and he's tired, and he isn't exactly the villain. Both are true, and a table should have to decide what to do about it.

### The amulets

Abnu amulets are worn across the empire and well past it. They're fashionable, cheap, reassuring and said to keep the wearer safe from dangerous spiritual influence. Xurux makes the finest. They're graded like any craft good, stamped with a maker's mark, and handed out free in poorer districts by the empire's charity, through temples and almshouses, which everyone counts as one of the emperor's kindnesses, and it is. The charity ones are crude, the standard ones cost little, and a fine one in worked silver costs more and does exactly the same thing, which tells you what they're really for.

| Who | Their amulets |
| --- | --- |
| Alysta | Nearly everyone, nearly always. Not wearing one says something, and in some districts it says something ugly. |
| Montia | Worn at sea for the look of it and taken off ashore. Nobody says why. |
| The Soths | Refused outright. They find the fashion faintly obscene. |
| Etweil | Never offered any. |
| The lizardfolk nations | Never asked. |
| Thelis | Won't discuss it. |
| Xurux | Makes them. |

While one's on, your soul's still. You feel steady, respectable and nobody's problem, which is why the fashion caught on. Soul sight reads you as ordinary, and so does a Soulless hunting for the tells. And your soul does nothing at all. Take it off and whatever's waiting in you can wake, and some people awaken the moment theirs comes off.

Nobody questions them. The smiths who stamp them, the charities that hand them out and the emperor's own clerks can't say what the etching's for. The ward patterns are old: nobody at the workshops designed them, they came with the tools and the rule that nothing leaves unetched. The emperor's own amulet is a different thing entirely and does the opposite, holding his memory in.

The charity runs on a standing order: amulets from Xurux, off the books, renewed for longer than the paper should've lasted, signed *A.*, at a volume a clerk would call industrial. A whole people who can never open a Gate, handed their amulets by kind people at the door. He wears one too, which is either the most honest thing about him or the worst, and nobody should be told which.

### The five truths

The world hides five things, and they land best in this order:

1. Amulets do something other than protect. Someone awakens the moment theirs comes off.
2. Someone's hunting awakened souls on purpose. A Soulless, and the fact that it can't be reasoned with.
3. There were ten moons, and there are nine. An Etweilian elder, a Thelisian carving, a rhyme that doesn't scan.
4. The emperor's older than the empire. A portrait, a signature, a witness who should be long dead.
5. He isn't the villain, exactly. Meeting him, and finding him tired.

Every Secret and every line of history that's hers sits somewhere on that ladder, and none gives a later truth away before an earlier one has a way in.

### The ways it can end

Five endings, none of them right, and every one of them costs someone:

- **Break the amulets.** Deepshaft Kel, its ward patterns and someone who understands them. It costs an empire of people who can suddenly awaken and were never taught, which proves his point.
- **Free him.** A way to take off an amulet that kills its wearer, or someone who'll wear it knowing they'll be taken over.
- **Find the seventh.** The shut door and whatever's behind it. Nobody knows which of the Seven is missing, whether they can be found, or whether they should be.
- **Raise the moon.** Thelis, the Fourth Gate and something that's been working at it for six hundred years. She's still in it, so raising it starts a problem rather than ending one.
- **Tell everyone.** Fragments, witnesses and a way to be believed. It's the slowest ending and maybe the only one people survive, and he's outlasted the truth before.

The board's agendas and Secrets leave every one of them possible.

## Now

A thousand years after the Sundering. The world rebuilt, nations rose, and the Garden still works, barely. The Seven still watch from wherever they watch. The cycle's thinning: fewer souls return, the Garden's crowding, and memory frays a little more with every turn of the wheel. In Alysta the emperor's just banned the Seven's worship outright. Someone with more curiosity than sense is running experiments they don't understand (Hollowgate's, and the Ezkian Union's race). Under all of it, in the dark of the Sundering Deep, the Queen's waking up. And above the water, against every reasonable expectation, the world's thriving.

## The peoples

Four peoples, by their old names:

- **Nisa Matati, the People of the Land.** Humans: the Alystans, the Soths, the Orzaini and the Montians, and most of the Ezkian Union, whose scholars came from everywhere.
- **Nise Masku, the People of the Hide.** The Etweil under their green hair, and the four lizardfolk peoples of the east under their scales.
- **Nisa Apsu, the People of the Deep Sea.** The merfolk of Thelis.
- **Nise Buzur, the People of the Deep Mines.** Molefolk, with claws that go through soil and soft stone, feet turned back for digging, whiskers that map a dark room from the moving air, and eyes that bright light hurts. They live underground between Alysta and the Soths, near the coast, in a place most of the world doesn't know is there.

Ancestry isn't destiny. Someone raised somewhere else carries where they grew up, and a Thelisian raised on Verrant Docks is more interesting than either half.

## The nations

Each is on Mo's map (The map, below). Where the canon's silent on a nation's ruler, what it wants or what it hides, the stage writes it in the shape of what's here.

### Alysta

The pink heart of the western continent and the biggest nation by far, with its capital, Alysta, on a bay of the south-east coast near the Montian border. People of the Land.

The largest and richest nation, and the one that stopped asking questions: busy cities, proud people, good roads and honest courts, and souls quietly dulled by amulets worn with pride. Everything's written down and nothing moves quickly, and the civil service has outlasted a dozen emperors before this one, in every sense but the literal. *My word is my bond* runs through all of it, held sincerely. *Stronger together* is taught in every school and meant, and Alystans work together better than anyone alive, in crews, watches, gangs and choirs. It's also the handiest belief there is for a ruler who'd rather nobody grew too strong alone, and nobody's ever put those two things side by side out loud.

White bread and soft cheese for breakfast, eaten standing. A small brass moon over every door, nine of them, and nobody can say why. *Long years to the throne*, said fast, the way people say bless you. Everything that matters is from this reign, and the empire's very proud of how it rebuilt.

Lately the emperor's banned the Seven's worship outright and turned against them, and soul sight's a crime. The statue of the Seven in the capital was left standing in ruins where everyone has to walk past it.

It hides the emperor's secret, the Soulless, Hollowgate and what the charity's amulets do. The Order of the Seven still meets in secret, inside the palace itself.

### The Soths

The yellow north of the western continent and the second biggest nation. Mo's map calls it the *Savages of the Hinterlands*, which is what Alystan maps call it (Claude's call). Its capital's Mornhold (Claude's call: the map's Cata is another Soth town). People of the Land.

The oldest nation in the world, older than the Sundering, and the one Alysta's never absorbed. Warriors above all: blunt, honourable and terrifying, two centuries behind on everything made and entirely unbothered. Survivors rather than conquerors, who've never needed more than their own mountains and valleys. *Strength is earned, not claimed.* A Soth warrior clears a chasm other nations would bridge, then complains about the bridge. They learn young that a promise is a debt and a debt's a wound, and they solve problems by force because it's always worked, though it hasn't always been right.

Cold meat and hot bitter tea for breakfast, in company, since eating alone is what you do when you're ashamed. Scars are shown, never hidden, and asking about one's a courtesy. The worst insult they've got means *you would be believed*. Every adult's armed and trained, by custom rather than law. No amulets.

They've got the finest iron ore in the world in the Iron Valleys, and Mornhold on top of the Flat-Top Mountain, which a hero's said to have torn free with bare hands. The ruling family runs the Ironhand Forge, whose marked blades are respected in every nation, and every one's tested against the smith's own hand before it's sold. The Standing Stones are in their highlands. The Seven are honoured openly and the Traditions tolerated.

They're watching Alysta's war on the Seven with real unease, because they remember what the last one cost. Queen Signe Ironhand keeps an old peace with the Seven and means to honour it, quietly, whatever Alysta does, which could put her against her own people if it ever has to be said aloud.

### Orza

The Principality of Orza, the lilac north-west of the western continent, with Aron its capital. People of the Land.

A nation of thinkers, running on a plant that's killing them slowly. Jak's Dream grows thick there, and it's eaten, smoked, or burned as incense to fill a whole room. It drives the mind into vivid visions, now and then true ones, then takes a full day of crushing tiredness for it. Some Orzaini really do see things before they happen. Most treat it as a nuisance. A few have tried to change what they saw, and how that went is most of Orzaini literature. Generations of it have left their minds always running and their bodies fragile. An Orzaini's cleverer than the room and knows what it cost, and the day after's always coming.

The leaf for breakfast, steeped grey and drunk fast, and nothing else till evening. Cities run on alternating days, half the people bright and talking too fast and half grey-faced and silent in darkened rooms, so *which day are you on?* is the first thing anyone asks a stranger. Orza has more libraries than beds.

### Montia

The Kingdom of Montia, the cyan of the middle sea: its capital, Pera, on the main island, the big island south of it and the islands between, the eastern tip of the western continent across a border from Alysta, and a holding on the western shore of the eastern continent. People of the Land.

A faith that travels, where the seafaring and the devotion are one fact. Montian pilgrims kept the Seven's texts alive through centuries of quiet suppression by carrying them: copied by hand, taken aboard, spread through every hold in every fleet, because a book in one library can be burned and a book in two hundred ships can't. Generations at sea gave them eyes that see a kilometre clean, a feel for the earth's pull so they always know north, and bodies that keep going on short rations. They call Alysta a heretic king, out loud, in his own ports. Montians always say the thing.

Barley porridge for breakfast and a virtue picked for the day. Temples low and wide with seven doors, the seventh always shut. *Which one are you carrying?* is hello. They copy books by hand on purpose, since a text nobody bothered to copy was one nobody needed, and they go out because staying still is how a thing gets lost. Amulets on at sea, off ashore.

The Traditions are strongest here. Sethilan Harbour's where the pilgrim fleets take on supplies, and books leave it in every hold, illegally and constantly, and nobody's ever managed to stop it. Wayside shrines of the Seven have seven alcoves, and the seventh's always empty and swept clean (*you don't put anything in that one*).

### The Ezkian Union

The blue-grey west of the western continent, between Orza and the Etweil, with Ezkia Prime its capital (Claude's call: the map's Waslach is another Union town). People of the Land, from everywhere.

Founded three centuries ago by scholars running from Alysta's persecution of soul-workers. Small next to Alysta or the Soths, and the most advanced nation in Abnu by a long way: steam, clockwork, and the first stirrings of something like electricity. Ezkia Prime's the City of Towers, lit by gas lamps after dark. A Council of Nine governs it, elected through the Grand Library and never born to it. Scholars from any nation are welcome, whatever they're running from, so the awakened who flee Alysta come here (Claude's call). *Everything has a price, including speed.* An Ezkian smith's quick if you pay extra, brilliant, easily distracted, and halfway through commissions for three clients at once.

It's racing to master something the Anunnaki left half finished, before a rival gets there first, and it isn't sure, inside, that the race is a good idea.

### The Etweilian Tribes

The white south of the western continent, with Niebali its chief town. People of the Hide.

Small, hairy and gentle, among the kindest people in the world, hidden in mountains cold enough to kill, which they walk through naked because their bodies handle it. Their hair's a green that darkens with age, and a still Etweilian gets taken for a bush, again and again. One meal a day keeps them going, and in a land with almost nothing that's the whole reason there are still Etweil. They're patient in a way that unsettles everyone else, and they're never in a hurry.

Whatever grew nearest for breakfast, eaten slowly, outdoors and usually in silence. They greet each other by standing very still till the other one notices. Their songs run to eleven verses, and the tenth's always hummed, because their grandmothers did it that way. Nobody's ever offered them an amulet.

The Green Silence is in their mountains, and nobody's mapped it. People go in, and the Etweil bring them out again, politely, and never talk about what happened. The Etweil remember what everyone else agreed to forget, and they won't tell you till they trust you completely.

### Xurux

The small pale green land between Orza and the Soths, with Xunan beside it (Claude's call: Xunan's the living town at Xurux's gate).

The city of the dead. Souls that can't ascend, with the Garden too crowded to take them, live in Xurux, and when one's ready to cease, it chooses to become essence, and the city and everything it makes are built from it. Xurux makes the finest amulets in the world, stamped with its mark, and Deepshaft Kel's the finest workshop of them all. Its master's proud, decent, and lately asking questions, which is the last thing anyone in that trade should do. The amulet houses are the most respected in the city, and nobody in Xurux knows what the etching does.

Claude's calls for Xurux:

- The dead run it themselves, so its people who turn up are the dead.
- The living come no further than Xunan, where the amulets go out and the empire's buyers come in.
- The amulets are built from the essence of the dead, like everything else the city makes.

### Thelis

The green island in the north of the middle sea, and the kingdoms in the water round it and down toward the Deep. People of the Deep Sea.

Merfolk kingdoms older than any surface empire, in water deeper than ships can sound. No nose, only a blowhole. Webbed hands and feet on two arms and two legs, four hours on one breath, and a way of speaking to sea creatures that isn't language and works anyway. They're raised to watch rather than act, and they feel older than everyone round them.

They eat when the current turns, not in the morning. Their names are three notes long and can't be written in any surface script. Their halls face down, toward the deep, and no Thelisian will tell you what's down there, though every one of them knows. They won't discuss amulets.

The Ledge is the shallowest Thelisian hall, and the only one a surface-dweller's ever been invited into. Their carvings show ten moons. They know what fell into the sea and are raised never to say it, and one who tells has betrayed their people.

### The lizardfolk nations

The eastern continent. People of the Hide: four peoples, scattered and unalike, who share a tie to Abnu the empires have long forgotten. None of them wear amulets, and none were ever asked.

**Cobria**, the long green north of the eastern continent, with Gustburg its capital. The rainforest nation, dangerous and crowded with creatures, and closer to the world's energies than almost anyone. Cobrian souls run deep and open early. A share of the world's awakened far beyond their numbers comes from here, and Cobrian children are watched for the glowing eyes from young rather than trained toward them. A venomous bite, a stomach that shrugs off poison, and real trouble in the cold. They awaken more easily than anyone, and somebody's certainly noticed.

**The Free City of Inkhal**, the salmon south-east of the eastern continent, with Inkhal its city. The heat. Nocturnal by need, they sleep through days that'd kill anyone else and live in a cool the other nations never see. Leathery skin, night eyes, and colour that shifts to match whatever they lie against, so a still Inkhalian simply isn't there. They keep other hours than everyone they deal with, and it shows.

**Echily**, the grey-green middle of the eastern continent, with Neuzon its capital. The high places. Pseudo-wings to glide down from a height and talons to climb back up, and shorter lives than anyone would like. Disease moves through an Echilian town fast, so they build spread out, high and always ready to move: an Echilian town's built to be left. Every Echilian knows exactly how much less time they've got.

**The Polythusian Marches**, the olive east of the eastern continent, with Molythia its capital. The mountains. Masters of thin air, with lungs that recover where others fail and legs that clear what others need a run-up for. They hibernate twelve hours to rest, deeply and not negotiably, which makes them poor travelling companions and superb at holding a wall, and they know both.

### Lubro

The tiny bright green land on the Soths' east coast. The canon has nothing on it (Claude's call: a free port the Soths let stand on their coast, since somebody has to sell Soth iron to the world).

### The molefolk

Underground, where the Soths and Alysta meet the east coast, by the inlet just south of Lubro (Mo's pick). There's nothing of theirs on the map, since all of it's under the ground. People of the Deep Mines.

Their own faction, and Abnu's secret one: most of the world doesn't know they're there, and the few who do don't say. The finest craftsmen in the world, with claws that go through soil and soft stone, feet turned back for digging, and whiskers that map a room in total darkness by how the air moves. They're slow above ground and bright light hurts, and neither has ever seemed to them worth complaining about. They look after the creatures and the ground round them in a way surface folk find strange.

Mushroom bread for breakfast, and mineral water so hard it squeaks on the teeth. Every one of them carries a maker's mark and stamps everything they finish, meals included, and *who made it?* is how they ask the price. They find the surface unbearably loud. They put their mark on their work, and they answer for it (Claude's call: these are the old book's ways, from when Xurux was their kingdom).

Their name for themselves, their home's name, who leads them, what they want, what they make and trade, and who knows they're there are the stage's to write, in the shape of this.

## The orders and the rest

- **The Order of the Seven** still meets in secret inside the emperor's own palace: a handful of rebels who know what he is.
- **The Soulless** came out of the emperor's research, staffed by the empire's brightest to break his curse. It's never done that. It found a way to copy how his amulet works in others, making undying agents who claim a body the way he claims his own. A Soulless can never awaken, because there's no self left in it to awaken. They hunt the unawakened who show the tells of a strong soul (strength of will, a sharp edge, whatever makes someone memorable), and most of those they take never come back. They're loyal to the emperor himself, not the throne, trained to move unseen, and sure they're saving the empire from a second Sundering. They've got agents in every big institution, a headquarters that officially doesn't exist, and a founding charter in the emperor's own hand from an incarnation three centuries back. He denies they exist.
- **The empire's charity** hands out the free amulets through temples and almshouses, run by people being kind.
- **The Sealed Archive**, in the capital, catalogues every banned text instead of burning it. Its Keeper's quietly sympathetic to the Seven. It holds the only complete copy of the Seven's texts from before the ban, a locked section even the Keeper can't open, and a visitor log the Soulless read.
- **The Standing Stones**, in the Soth highlands, is a monastery that takes no coin. Half the awakened in the north trained there, and every one of them still owes it something.
- **The Grand Library**, in Ezkia Prime, elects the Council of Nine.
- **The pilgrim fleets** are Montia's ships, every hold carrying copied texts.
- **The Traditions** are the Seven's schools, one to a virtue.

## Past the board

Two powers in Abnu have no seat to pin on a map, so like what's past the Blackwall in Night City they live in Secrets, history and places, never on a card of their own:

- **The Seven**, wherever they are, with the seventh door and the Soths' old peace among the ways toward them.
- **The Queen** and her loyalists, inside the tenth moon at the bottom of the Sundering Deep.

## Places

The canon's places. Every faction's seat is a place too.

- **Alysta**, the capital, in white stone on the south-east coast. In it are the palace, the ruined statue of the Seven, the Sealed Archive, and:
  - **Verrant Docks**, the capital's lower city, where everything enters the empire, including crates that shouldn't exist. Working, unromantic and cold, with lamps in the rain. Most campaigns start on this dock.

## People

The canon's people. Everyone else, every ruler the canon doesn't name among them, is the stage's to write.

- **The emperor**, Alysta, the same soul for a thousand years and all of it remembered for 380, in a body that was his son's. Tired, and always already prepared.
- **Queen Signe Ironhand** of the Soths, who keeps the old peace with the Seven.
- **The Keeper of the Sealed Archive**, quietly sympathetic to the Seven.
- **The master of Deepshaft Kel**, proud, decent, and asking questions.
- **Merren Vast**, dock-master at Verrant, who signs manifests nobody reads. Middle-aged, tired, and decent the way people who've made a lot of small compromises are decent. He knows every crew and every crate, and he's the easiest way into anything, but every favour he does gets noticed by someone, and he's the one who pays. Two nights ago he read a manifest instead of signing it, and he hasn't slept properly since.
- **Sister Ilva**, a Montian pilgrim keeping an unlicensed shrine in a room above a laundry. She's got almost nothing and will risk almost everything, feeds anyone who turns up, and asks questions about virtue that people answer honestly and think about for weeks. She'll forgive one betrayal. She knows one true thing about the Seven she's never said out loud.
- **Old Tekk**, a Xurux smith exiled for asking about the amulets. He can still make anything, but he's got no workshop, no house and no mark he's allowed to use, and he never says why he left. Something in him's going wrong that nobody's asked about (Claude's call: he's one of the dead, fading outside the city).
- **Cassia Brenne**, a magistrate's clerk with expensive habits, beautifully dressed, very well placed and terrified nearly all the time. She can get you a signature, a name, a date or a copy of something, and she won't risk a hair for anybody. Somebody already pays her more than you do, and she'd tell you if you asked.
- **The Widow Ashgrove**, who owns half the district's debts and all its gossip. She'll grant almost anything and risk nothing, every conversation's a negotiation she enjoys enormously, and she's never once been unfair, which is why people keep borrowing.
- **Juno**, twelve, who sees everything and charges in food. She runs errands nobody asked her to and knows which doors are watched. She isn't brave exactly, she just hasn't worked out yet that she can be hurt. She's never hurt casually, and never untouchable either.

Cassia, the Widow and Juno live in the capital (Claude's call, since the old book only says the district).

## History

### What everyone agrees happened

What anyone in Abnu will tell the crew, and the first thing How it came to be shows them:

- **Before counting**, the Seven walked, and souls reached further than they do now.
- **The Sundering**: something was fought and sealed, and the Seven weren't seen again.
- **The long middle**: empires rose and fell, the records are poor, and historians blame fire.
- **About 400 years ago** the empire nearly collapsed, and nobody agrees why.
- **About 380 years ago** Alysta took the throne, ended the wars and abolished slavery.
- **Since**: a golden age, with amulets the fashion and awakening a curiosity.
- **Now**: nine moons, and everything's fine.

A four-hundred-year gap with poor records and a reign that starts right at its edge is the fourth truth, for anyone who lays it beside the emperor's portrait and squints.

### What happened

The true order, for the three ages.

**Long ago**, from the Anunnaki to about eighty years back:

- The Anunnaki opened their eyes in the Cradle, built their cities and passed the Gates like breathing.
- The Queen died, refused it and took another body, and her followers did the same.
- The Seven rose from Abnu's spirit, and the war lasted centuries.
- The Soths were a people already, and the kingdoms of Thelis older still.
- The Sundering: she pulled the moons down, the Seven caught nine and sealed her in the tenth.
- She battered at it for generations, till it fell into the Sundering Deep.
- The Seven brought rebirth back for every soul, and the fallen moon began stripping memory.
- Alysta forgot and remembered across lives, banned the Seven's worship, buried his amulet and asked it never be found.
- The long middle: empires rose and fell, the records burned, and about 400 years ago the empire nearly collapsed.
- About 380 years ago a merchant gave the buried amulet to the emperor, who was Alysta, and every life came back to him.
- He took the throne, ended the wars, abolished slavery and rebuilt the empire, and never brought back the Seven's worship.
- His amulets became the fashion, and the Soulless's charter was signed in his hand.
- Three centuries ago, scholars running from his persecution of soul-workers founded the Ezkian Union.
- Montian pilgrims carried the Seven's texts through the centuries, ship to ship.

**In living memory**, the last eighty years or so: the stage writes this age in the shape of the rest, from the board as it stands, with the charity's order growing to industrial volume, the Union's towers and steam, the Cobrian awakened being noticed, Old Tekk's exile, and whatever puts each card's agendas part way up.

**This last year**:

- The emperor banned the Seven's worship outright, and the statue of the Seven in the capital was left in ruins.
- The Soths watched with unease, and Queen Signe kept her peace quiet.
- Deepshaft Kel's master started asking questions.
- Two nights before the crew turn up, Merren Vast read a manifest instead of signing it.
- The Deep's been shaking harder, and Thelis knows why.

The stage fills both later ages out to a running board and keeps every line inside the record's 80. A line that gives one of the five truths away is hers.
