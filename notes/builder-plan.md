# The world builder plan

A builder that deals a whole world, faction by faction, from tables Mo's approved, so a GM can build one alone in minutes, or play its making with the crew as session zero. It comes from the research in *Worldbuilding that feels like play* (27 Oct 2026, a doc in Mo's Claude account) and Mo's answers on 27 and 28 Oct. It goes ahead of the worlds plan's last two stages (the crew's pins and notes, and timelines and family trees), which carry on after it.

In code it's the world builder, never just the builder, since `builder.js` is the character builder: its files are `world-` names.

It also holds Night City (stages 12 to 14 and 17 to 19, from 30 Oct): a whole campaign world written from the official Cyberpunk universe rather than dealt, and the first world that comes into a campaign whole. It's Mo's one exception to CLAUDE.md's rule about naming other games, and A whole world, in the brief, says what it is and what it may never do. And it holds Abnu (stages 15 and 16, from 30 Oct), Mo's own setting written whole the same way, from `notes/abnu-canon.md`.

## Decisions

Mo's calls (27, 28 and 30 Oct):

- **No AI in the builder.** Nothing's generated when someone builds. Every word it shows comes from tables Mo's approved, or is typed by someone at the table. The fun's in gamified randomness and guided writing.
- **The GM picks who builds.** Alone, she builds the world and presents it to the crew. With the crew, they found it together in a session-zero game, so they're invested from day one and play in what they made.
- **The app first.** Built for the site, on phones. A paper version comes later.
- **Region, then genre.** A world's region is Town, City, Country, World or Stars. Its genre gives its seats, resources, assets, factions and names their words: Fantasy, Medieval, Renaissance, Modern, Steampunk, Cyberpunk and Futuristic, plus the ones Claude added below. Genres replace doing the Steeplands first.
- **Educated guesses, never generic.** Each step reads what's already on the board, so a river city trades and a mountain hold needs grain, and a result says why when it isn't obvious.
- **All four kinds of randomness:** pick one of three, draft from a dice pool, answer questions, and spend points like gods.
- **Tables:** Claude drafts them in Mo's voice, and nothing goes in till he's said yes.
- **Alone:** guided by default, three cards at every step, with a Deal it all tap that plays the same steps for her.
- **With the crew:** a whole session, with a short played history that ends with every conflict still live. Each player founds a faction and speaks for it, and the GM takes them all over when play starts.
- **Secrets** are drawn hidden, and only her phone ever sees them.
- **Every character** leaves with a friend and a foe among the factions.
- **Maps:** the faction web, a map sketched during the build, and her own picture with pins.
- **Power comes only from people who turn up** (28 Oct). Stage 1 makes it true across the book.
- **Fantasy next** (28 Oct, once he'd judged Medieval). Magic's tables come before building alone, so Fantasy deals as soon as it can.
- **Magic's a known craft, and it's people only** (28 Oct). Everyone knows magic's real: wards, charms and healing are trades, rituals and bound souls stay behind closed doors, and about a faction in three runs on it. There are monsters, spirits, the dead and old things like dragons, and no other peoples.
- **Building by answering** (30 Oct): a better way to build alone, the way a reign goes. She goes through a deck of cards, swiping each one way or the other, and how she answers shapes the factions. Going faction by faction and Deal it all both stay.
- **As many cards as it takes** (30 Oct): enough for interesting dealings between the factions and to form each one whole, never a set number, and never a form filled in line by line. In Mo's words: "different card amount so it is not just 12 cards", and "it needs to help form the entire faction without it asking to fill out line by line".
- **An answer can ask for a name or a detail** (30 Oct): a field to write in, with suggestions off the tables to tap, never filled in for her ("the suggestions needed will be recommended but it is not given").
- **The deck comes next** (30 Oct), ahead of the founding, which uses it later.
- **How it came to be's shown an age at a time** (30 Oct, stage 8): long ago, in living memory and this last year each have their own Show the crew, so this last year's agendas and grudges can wait till the crew's found things out, and a line whose answer made a Secret never leaves her phone.
- **The tension's a turn of its own** (30 Oct, stage 9): in the room, right after what holds it together, the next player rolls the d12, so the asking's four turns and a fourth player gets one too.
- **The asking goes into Build the world till the ground's built** (30 Oct, stage 9): once the crew's answered it, one tap on her phone takes it into Build the world, the crew's answers locked, so the same session carries on with the ground and the deck there.
- **Renaissance names are a wide mix** (30 Oct, stage 10): Italian, French, Spanish, Dutch, German and English on every board, Town to World.
- **A seat's kept as it comes, or in their own words** (30 Oct, stage 11): in the ground, whoever takes a die keeps one of the three seats it deals, its name and what a stranger notices as they are, or writes either themselves, so a turn's one tap when a card suits.
- **Night City, 2077, a whole example campaign** (30 Oct): the official Cyberpunk universe, lore accurate, starting in Night City in 2077, made into a finished Zi'Dara world, the way a campaign looks once it's set up and running. Every faction as the rules make one (the corps, the gangs, the fixers, the law and the rest), each card whole and adding up with its agendas, Face and Secret, and the web between them: rivals, allies, debts, blocked needs, bonds, threads and mini factions. Places and people with everything a page holds (the districts, the places that matter and the people who run them), How it came to be across the three ages from the setting's own history, and a map of the city with a pin on every place and seat. Other years of the same universe come later, so it's built for another year to be added.
- **Naming the game, here only** (30 Oct): Night City breaks CLAUDE.md's rule about naming other games, by Mo's call, and the rule stays everywhere else.
- **Cyberpunk is CD Projekt's and R. Talsorian Games'** (30 Oct): the lore's checked on the web, and every word's written fresh in Mo's voice, never copied (no text, maps, art or logos). The map's drawn in code, a map of the districts of our own, never traced from the game's and never from an image generator. It stays out of the book, the printed sheets and search, like `/deal/` and `/kit/`, with a line saying it's fan-made and who owns Cyberpunk, and it isn't linked from anywhere public till Mo says so.
- **The builder's tables stay Zi'Dara's** (30 Oct): Near future and Chrome are their own stage, with names of their own, and nothing from Night City goes into them.
- **2077 starts before the Konpeki job** (30 Oct): Saburo Arasaka's alive and Dex DeShawn's setting up the job of the year, so everything up to it is settled, and the game's story is hers to let happen around the crew or never.
- **Only Mo brings it in** (30 Oct): CD Projekt's guidelines want to be asked first when fan work goes inside another game's app, so Night City's offered on the World screen to Mo's account alone, at his own table, till he says otherwise.
- **2045 comes next** (30 Oct): Cyberpunk RED's own year, the city rebuilding after the war.
- **A campaign jumps, and the year it left is kept** (30 Oct): going to another year brings the new year in as the campaign's world, and the year it left stays on the World screen to look back on.
- **Abnu, a whole world of Mo's own** (30 Oct): his setting written whole the way Night City is, from his lore (the peoples doc, the old book's nations, the Seven and the amulets, places and contacts, the Six Souls and *The World of Abnu*) and his own map, with every mechanic from his old systems left out. The lore's put together in `notes/abnu-canon.md`, which is all its stages read for it.
- **The Ezkian Union's the scholars' steam republic** (30 Oct, Abnu): Ezkia Prime, the Council of Nine, steam and clockwork, and not the Ezki hunters of the cold.
- **Xurux is the city of the dead only** (30 Oct, Abnu): not the molefolk kingdom, and who keeps it left open.
- **Abnu starts in its own now** (30 Oct): a thousand years after the Sundering, the Queen stirring in the Deep and Alysta's ban on the Seven fresh, with nothing that needs one adventure.
- **Abnu comes after Night City 2077** (30 Oct): stages 15 and 16, ahead of 2045.
- **The web on a map** (30 Oct, stages 13 and 14): a map, Night City's or her own picture of a world or a city, can have the web on it: each faction's token where it's pinned and its threads drawn to the others, every faction faint till one's tapped, then its threads and the factions at their ends light up, and a tap on another walks to its ties. The look's the web's own, and a pin she moves takes its lines with it.

## Calls Claude made

For Mo to say otherwise.

- **A genre's an era and a twist** behind the scenes, so the genres Mo didn't name cost almost nothing. The eras are Ancient, Medieval, Renaissance, Industrial, Modern, Near future and Far future. The twists are Magic, Steam, Chrome, Ruin and Dread. The menu shows genres by name, and Mix your own takes any era with any twist:

| Genre | Era and twist |
| --- | --- |
| Fantasy | Medieval and Magic |
| Medieval · Renaissance · Modern | The era on its own |
| Steampunk | Industrial and Steam |
| Cyberpunk | Near future and Chrome |
| Futuristic | Far future |
| Mythic | Ancient and Magic |
| Gothic horror | Industrial and Dread |
| Urban fantasy | Modern and Magic |
| Post-apocalyptic | Modern and Ruin |
| Dark fantasy | Medieval and Dread |
| Space fantasy | Far future and Magic |

- **Each frame says its mix** (Long Haul is Far future at Stars), so Add a faction fits a frame she brought in. A frame's mix goes in with the last pack it needs.
- **Stars needs the Far future era** at first.
- **The sizes keep their names in the rules,** and the builder shows each region's words for them (on a Town board, Great reads as the whole town).
- **Board size:** the players plus two, at least four and at most twelve, hers to change at the start. Seats are two more than the factions, so there's always one worth fighting over.
- **In the founding,** the old powers she deals are City or Great and the founders start Local, so upstarts face old money. Every agenda in the founding starts at d4. Building alone uses the agendas table's own dice.
- **The founding's three ages** (the ground, the founding and the strife) each have a clock she sets at the start, 30, 40 and 40 minutes to begin with. When one runs out, the turn that's on finishes, the engine deals whatever's left, and the next age starts.
- **The strife:** 3 tokens a round each, and Scheme pushes an agenda a size once a round. It ends with the round that takes an agenda to d10, after 4 rounds, or when its clock runs out, so it runs three or four rounds. These are the dials to tune at the table.
- **After the founding,** what the table built in the open is shown to the crew. Secrets, the old powers' agendas and everything from her pass stay hidden.
- **Names** are dealt from each genre's lists, three at a time, or typed.
- **No builder skill.** Writing tables, below, does the job one would, so it doesn't take a slot.
- **A card's people always reach its die** (stage 2). If they can't, because it's too few resources to pay for them, the die comes down to the one they reach and the size with it.
- **There's always a trade to block** (stage 2). When nobody's got a resource to spare, one faction's dealt as many resources as its size holds, and one's left over.
- **Deal again** never gives a step the card it had while there's another, never touches a locked step, and a new seat deals the card on it again from its size, bar what's locked. A changed answer moves the dials for what's dealt next and leaves what's dealt alone.
- **The core keeps the book's rows** (stage 3): Building a Faction's wants, how it works and its secrets, and the tension, word for word, with two agendas to each of the book's, at its die. A Secret's written the way the book's cards write one, a hidden tag in capitals and a line, and resources and assets are named the way the cards name them.
- **Plain Medieval's strange things are the deniable kind** (stage 3): saints, curses, old graves, things nobody can explain. The Magic pack makes them real.
- **What fits counts** (stage 3): a row's weight goes up three times over for each thing on its card it fits and half that for each on the board, a type that leans to its seat's keep counts eight times over (so about three factions in four live off their seat), and a faction's next resource after its seat's, its first people and its first asset are ones it leans to.
- **A step dealt again reads only the steps before it** (stage 3), so a type's three cards never lean on the method and Want it had.
- **A Face is one of the threats core gives its kind of faction** (stage 3), never the pack or the swarm for a guild.
- **A specific name needs its kind's tags** (stage 3): the Hammermen need `metal`, the Wool Staplers `cloth`, Coyle's Barges a river, so names and types agree. `metal`, `cloth` and `stone` are core's tags for that.
- **A magic row that only makes sense for a magic faction needs magic** (stage 4): its seat or kind has to carry the `magic` tag already, so a world's magic gathers on the factions that run on it, and the trades anyone could profit from stay open.
- **A kind of faction can say its Face** (stage 4): a type row's `faces` are the three threats its Face is likeliest to be, instead of core's for its kind, so a coven's is likeliest a sorcerer.
- **The Steeplands are Fantasy at Country with how strange at 0, and the Deep Company's Fantasy at Town** (stage 4): the Steeplands are an archipelago of trade cities where magic sits quietly, and the Deep Company's a company town over the old city. A frame's keyed by its board's id in the book and can set the dials the book gives it, and Add a faction deals at them.
- **A twist's rows are written for the eras that deal it so far** (stage 4): Magic's are Medieval's. The stage that brings the next era to deal with Magic (Renaissance in a mix, then Modern for Urban fantasy) writes Magic's rows for it, and gives a row that only fits some eras a field saying which, a change to the tables and the engine.
- **Every step arrives dealt** (stage 5): a screen deals what it needs in the engine's order, so each step's three cards come with the one dealing picked already on, and Next always works. The factions are all dealt, round twice, the moment she reaches them, so an agenda or a Secret can aim at any faction, and she goes through them a faction at a time, in four screens. The asking puts the tension after the first question, the way the book asks it.
- **Picking a card deals what's after it again** (stage 5), the way the engine reads the card so far: the rest of that faction's card that isn't locked or hers, and the screen says what changed. Lock keeps a card through anything that's dealt, Deal this faction again deals what she hasn't locked or written, and her own change to a set locks it.
- **Add a faction ties in** (stage 5, told to Mo): a rival, and an ally or a debt, among the factions on the board, a thread each way, each one to keep or swipe away. Its agenda only aims at what's on the board, it sits on a seat nobody holds or a new place, and mini factions aren't held. With no build kept, it deals at the mix of the frame most of the factions came in from, or asks, and Done keeps the mix in the record so it doesn't ask again.
- **Where a faction sits goes on its card** (stage 5, Mo to judge): "It sits on the Old Bridge." after what it is, the because line's words, as `templates.seat` in `core.yaml`, so the place's name links. Each seat goes in as a place with what a stranger notices as what it's like, and its page lists the faction that sits there where it comes up.
- **Breaking the logic is hers too** (stage 5): one card in six still breaks it when dealt, and she can deal one onto a card or take it off.
- **One build on a phone for each campaign** (stage 5), kept till it's on the board or she drops it, and Undo takes back its last 25 changes till a reload. Undo on Done's note takes it all back off the board and brings the build back.
- **What a faction's after of the crew's** (stage 6, Mo's pick: either): its agenda aims at one of the characters by name (`{who}`, like Get back what Vesk took) from session zero, or at something on the party sheet worth taking (`{thing}`, the crew's Ship) once there's something there, never the new crew's card, people who turn up, or what the crew's like rather than something it's got (Rooted and Influential, `crew.qualities` in `core.yaml`). An agenda aimed at the crew comes up half as often for each faction already after it, and the crew check deals one in when nobody is.
- **A friend and a foe for every character** (stage 6): a screen each after the board, three cards a side, each a different faction and never the same one for both, spread round the board (a faction that's someone's friend already is a quarter as likely to be another's), with the words off `core.yaml`'s crew tables, named for them, as that faction's thread toward the crew. A foe's likeliest the faction that's after them, then their friend's rival, and the card says so. They go on hidden like the rest of the build, so the crew finds out when she shows that faction's threads.
- **Adding a faction ties it to the crew too** (stage 6): one thread toward the crew, named for someone the builds say hasn't got a friend or a foe on the board yet (anyone, when there's no record), as a foe when its agenda's after them, swiped away like its other ties.
- **The web** (stage 6): the factions go round the ring so the ones with threads between them sit close (each mini faction after its maker), the board's first at the top, the crew in the middle. Two threads of one kind going both ways are one line, anything else has an arrow at whoever it's toward, a strained thread's dashed and a snapped one dotted and faint, and a line between two factions bows round the crew. A tap on a faction lists its threads each way, and on the crew how every faction sees it. The GM's has Everything and What the crew sees, with what's hidden faint, and the builder's board screen draws the web it's dealt.
- **This last year's voice is the Face** (stage 7, told to Mo): whoever brings a faction's cards this last year is its Face, by name, so she's met the Face on the cards. An answer that makes someone a faction's Face (`face`) puts them there instead.
- **The deck deals who's who first** (stage 7): before the first card, the asking and the seats, and each faction's seat, size and kind, so the dials are set and a card can say who's on it. Till a faction's named it's called by what it is up to its first comma, and where it sits (the millers on the Abbey Orchards).
- **The next card's for whatever's least formed** (stage 7): the faction with the most still to form in the age that's on, with the one whose parts the card forms best and that it's met least, or a seat nobody holds. The board's own needs count too: a need someone blocks in living memory, and this last year a Secret naming another faction, something strange, a faction after the crew, and each of the crew by name. A part an age runs out of cards for carries into the next. A later card leaves the only one of those needs alone, and the end deals whichever the answers didn't make.
- **The dials weight a card by what its answers do** (stage 7): how tense brings feuds and agendas, how rich deals and debts, how ruled the law (houses, offices and soldiers, the crown and the law), each 0.6 times at 0 and 1.6 times at 2, and how strange the strange ones, a third as often at 0 and two and a half times at 2. A seat nobody holds comes up less for each of the last three cards that was about one.
- **What a card names is theirs** (stage 7): a resource, an asset or people a card names is one the faction holds, or one dealt for it that it holds once the card's answered. People who turn up keep its size and die in step, a lot more being a step more and a lot fewer a step less. A thread that named a resource a later card took names one it's got at the end.
- **An answer's first thread is in its own words** (stage 7): `thread` from the faction doing it and `back` from the other side, so a tie both ways needs both. The rest come off core's ties.
- **Chains** (stage 7): the next card lands two to four cards later, a chain never starts after card 65 so every one lands by 70, and a chain's card can't be passed over.
- **A prompt she skips** (stage 7): a skipped name's dealt at the end, and a skipped person, place or detail is the first suggestion, so the cards after it in its chain can say it. A name she picks or writes is locked, since it's what happened.
- **Names come first at the end** (stage 7), so every line of history and every because reads with the names the factions end up with.
- **A mini faction's Loyalty** (stage 7): core's `loyal` lines, its Loyalty thread toward the faction that made it.
- **`/deal/` keeps every answer in the address** (stage 7), so a link plays the same deck, and a new deck goes into the browser's history so Back brings the last one back. Answer the rest at random takes either answer at even odds and a suggestion two times in three.
- **The deck's the way after the ground** (stage 8): arriving there starts it, with Go faction by faction instead till the first answer, and Deal it all on the set up as it was. Once she's gone faction by faction, Answer the deck instead deals the factions again from who's who, a tap again, since what she's locked or written past it goes.
- **The ground holds once the deck's played on it** (stage 8): once she's answered a card, the asking and the ground are what the cards were played on, so they stay as they are, while the deck's on and after it, with Start the deck again to change them. Before the first answer, a change there starts the deck again from them, and a new seat deals only who's who on it. The factions' own screens stay hers to change once the deck's done, round what it locked.
- **Deal the rest on the deck's a tap again** (stage 8), since it ends the deck there. Undo's the build's own, the last 25 till a reload, and after a reload it plays the deck's last answer back off the deck itself. A tile opens its faction's card so far on the card's place, and the arrow keys answer a card too.
- **The deck's last screen is How it came to be** (stage 8), and Next is the board. The faction screens come once it's done, a tap from the board. Done puts the mini factions the answers made on the board under their makers, and the record keeps the deck without the world it started from, so a big board fits.
- **A line's hers when its answer made a Secret** (stage 8): it's stamped on the card when it's answered, marked on her phone, and it never reaches a player, whatever's shown, the tables change or a phone on older code sends. On the World screen she can change a line or take it out before she shows its age, and showing one tells the crew's phones to look again, the way a map does.
- **The founding's offered on an empty board** (stage 9), on her campaign page, like Build the world on the World screen. Opening the room puts everyone playing in the campaign in, in the order they joined it, and they join from their World tab. She can move someone up before it starts, and take anyone out (their turn goes on to whoever's after them, and they can't put themselves back) or put them back at the end, any time.
- **The asking's part of the ground** (stage 9), so the first age's clock starts with it. A turn's one of the book's four parts in its order, so with more than four players the rest start the ground, and with fewer it goes round again.
- **Every phone deals a turn's three cards** (stage 9), the same from the shared seed and the answers before it, so nothing has to come from the server to deal. A tap on a card answers it, and whoever answered, or she, can take the last answer back till someone answers after it. She can put one in for anyone without a phone, with a second tap so a look never answers for them, and it says it was hers. Everyone sees the cards of whoever's turn it is, so the table can talk it over.
- **Out of time** (stage 9): the turn that's on finishes, then her phone deals the rest the way the engine would, from the seed. She can stop the clock, run it, and take five minutes off or put five on, and more time once it's out sends the turns round again.
- **The room's seed stays in the room** (stage 9): every player has it, to deal the room's cards, so carrying the asking into Build the world deals the rest from a seed of its own, and nobody can deal her factions and Secrets for themselves.
- **Leaving the campaign leaves the founding** (stage 9), so the turns never wait on someone who's gone, and someone she's taken out can't take their last answer back to win their turn again.
- **Brinewake is Renaissance and Magic at Country with how strange at 0, and the Gilded Court's Renaissance at City with how tense at 2** (stage 10, told to Mo): Brinewake's islands have magic that's real but rare, as its frame says, and the Gilded Court's a dying queen's court where magic's a hanging matter, so it's plain Renaissance and anything strange stays deniable.
- **Renaissance magic's a learned art** (stage 10): astrologers, alchemists, a mages' academy, printed charms and weather-callers, still a craft everyone knows is real, with the big magic behind closed doors like Medieval's.
- **Plain Renaissance's strange things are the deniable kind** (stage 10), like Medieval's: a comet, miracle cures, a brass man who plays cards, an alchemist who might be a fraud.
- **The far side of the world is trade** (stage 10, told to Mo): ships, spices, dye and maps, and never the conquest of whoever lives there.
- **A twist says the eras it's written for** (stage 10, told to Mo): `eras` on its pack, and on a row or a moment that only fits some of them, the way stage 4 said it would go. A twist only deals with an era it's written for, so Magic's Renaissance rows never come up in Fantasy.
- **`print` and `powder` are core's newest tags** (stage 10), so presses, pamphlets and guns find each other.
- **Two frames brought in by as many factions** (stage 10, told to Mo): Add a faction deals at the first one's, so Tuesday Lair, with Brinewake's four and the Steeplands' four, deals Brinewake's mix.
- **The ground's dice are rolled on the server** (stage 11): her tap rolls a d6 for every seat in the Table object, so every phone gets the same faces and no phone picks them. A die's three seats are of the keep its face picks and none already in the ground, and once a keep's run out they're whatever comes next. Anyone can tap a die to see its three, and only whoever's turn it is keeps one.
- **The ground's turns go on round the table** (stage 11) from whoever gave the asking's last answer, a die a turn, till the pool's empty.
- **The ages wait for her** (stage 11, told to Mo): the clock stops at the end of each age and she starts the next, so she can say what's coming. Out of time, the turn that's on finishes and her phone deals the rest of that age.
- **How many old powers** (stage 11, told to Mo): the factions she set less the players, and always one at least, since a founder's first question needs a faction on the board to owe, so eleven found one at most and twelve factions is the most. The pool's the factions and two more seats, with room for everyone in to found one beside an old power.
- **The old powers are dealt in the open** (stage 11, told to Mo): she picks each from three whole cards (its name, what it is and how it works, where it sits, City or Great, and its Want), each a different kind of faction on a stream of its own, and its resources and people come with it. The first old power holds a resource to spare, so there's always a trade to block. Its agenda, Face and Secret wait for her pass.
- **What's dealt with a card is kept** (stage 11): an old power's resources and people come up with its card, a founder's size with its seat and its resources, people and agenda with its Want, dealt on the phone that answers and kept in the room, so the board the crew saw stays what it was when the tables change between sessions (the world keeps what was dealt, never just the seed).
- **A founder's turn is its whole faction** (stage 11, told to Mo), a part at a time in the brief's order, its name last (three off the tables, or their own), and they can take their answers back one at a time while they're the last. Its resources and people at Local and an agenda at d4 are dealt in the open once it's got its Want. Before it's named, its lines call it the newcomers (`templates.newcomer`).
- **The founding's questions are lines off core's `founding` table** (stage 11, Mo to judge): three for who had the seat before (the old holder's grudge toward the newcomers) and three for who they owe (saying what's owed), once they've picked the faction, weighted to the seat and that faction, or their own words. What they'll never do is a bond thread on their own Loyalty, a promise their people hold them to. Dealt by the tables, nobody had the seat before.
- **Someone who leaves mid-founding** (stage 11): their turn goes on to the next founder, what they made stands, and a faction they hadn't finished waits for her phone, which deals the rest once nobody in's left to take it.
- **Till the strife's built, the founding goes into Build the world** (stage 11, told to Mo, on from his stage 9 pick): from the end of any age, everything the crew made locked, on a seed of her own. After the asking it's the ground's first seat, after the ground the deck, and after the founding the board, where her phone deals the old powers' agendas, every Face and Secret, the web round what the founding wrote, the needs (one can land on a faction the room made, beside its set), the checks and the crew's friends and foes.
- **A whole world is files of its own** (stage 12): `content/worlds/`, read by a script of its own into `public/worlds/`, never into `book.json`, the chapters or the builder's tables, so keeping it out of the book is how it's built, not something to remember.
- **Every faction that acts in the city** (stage 12): the corps with a hand in Night City's streets, every gang, every district's fixer, the law, and the rest (the medics, the net's police, the news, and what's past the Blackwall), around thirty cards. A brand that only sells in the city isn't a faction. A board that big runs on the book's own rule: only telegraph the factions the crew can reach, and the rest move off-screen.
- **V's left out** (stage 12): V's the game's player's own, so the world's people are everyone else, and nothing on it needs V to happen.
- **Running, not fresh** (stage 12): the board's in the middle of things, with agendas part way up, threads strained, a need or two unpaid and mini factions already made, the way a campaign looks once it's running.
- **It fits with room to spare** (stage 12): brought in whole, `was` copies and all, a year's world is under 450,000 of the 700,000 characters a world holds, so she's got room for her own. If it won't fit, small places go before any faction does.
- **Both companies' notices go on it** (stage 12): R. Talsorian's Homebrew Content Policy asks for its notice on fan work, and CD Projekt's fan content guidelines ask for theirs, so both go under the line saying whose it is, in the companies' own words, the only words on it that aren't Mo's. The title's *Night City, 2077*, since R. Talsorian's policy keeps its games' titles out of a fan work's title.
- **A whole city's web is a faction's ties at a time** (stage 12): thirty-odd factions round one ring is too much for a phone, so the page draws one faction, everyone it's got a thread with either way and the threads between them, picked from a list or walked to with a tap. Past twelve factions the ring's lines and arrows also thin in step with its width. Bringing Night City into a campaign (stage 14) gives the World screen's web the same way of walking it.
- **Fixers are their outfits, and brands aren't factions** (stage 12): a fixer's faction is named for their outfit (the Afterlife, DeShawn's Outfit, Padre's Flock), so it never shares a name with the fixer's own page and links stay plain. Kiroshi, Zetatech, Orbital Air, SovOil and Petrochem only sell in the city, the FIA isn't in it before the Konpeki job, and what's past the Blackwall has no seat to pin on a map, so they live in Secrets, notes and history instead.
- **The map's drawn on every phone, never a picture** (stage 13): shapes in the map's own coordinates, drawn as SVG in the site's tokens, so it follows the light switch, stays sharp at any zoom and needs nothing put up. In a campaign (stage 14) it's a new kind of map with its shapes kept beside it, and the sketch (stage 25) can be one too.
- **Abnu's lore calls live in its canon** (30 Oct): where Mo's sources disagree or say nothing, `notes/abnu-canon.md` makes the call and lists it for his yes or no, and Abnu's stages read those calls as canon till he answers.
- **Abnu's kept like Night City till Mo says** (30 Oct): offered to his account alone and linked from nowhere, though it's his own, since he judges it first.
- **Abnu deals at Renaissance and Magic at World, how strange at 0** (30 Oct): the nearest recipe to it (the Union's steam is the leading edge, the Soths two centuries behind, and soul-work rare and quiet), so Add a faction on an Abnu board deals Brinewake's kind of magic.
- **Abnu's year is `1000`** (30 Oct): a thousand years after the Sundering, the only date its lore gives.

## The brief

### Tables, roles and packs

- **Roles** are what every genre has in common. A seat's and a resource's role is where the keep comes from: passage, land, ground, craft, trade, belief, office, knowledge, labour, crime or wonder. An asset's role is one of Appendix B's assets, its mechanics as they stand after stage 1. A faction type's role is one of Building a Faction's twelve (a ruling house, traders, crooks, faith, office, a secret society, soldiers, makers, a family, rebels, keepers of old things, something old).
- **The core** holds everything that isn't genre: the regions, the roles, Building a Faction's tables (what it is, what it wants, how it works, its secret), the agendas, Building Your World's three region questions and its tension table, the ties between factions, the founding's questions and acts, the because lines, the checks' fallbacks and the seed words.
- **A region** says what a faction, a seat and a mini faction are at that size, the words for the four sizes, which seat role each face of a d6 picks (for the founding's dice pool), and what pressure from one size up looks like.
- **A pack** gives the roles their words for one era or one twist: seats for each region it fits, resource lines, every asset's names (and a word or two of what it does where the genre needs it), faction types, methods, Secrets, exceptions and names. A twist pack adds its own rows to the same roles, and the "how strange" dial sets how often they come up.
- The tables are YAML in `content/world-tables/` (`core.yaml`, `regions.yaml`, `genres.yaml`, `eras/*.yaml`, `twists/*.yaml`). `scripts/build-content.mjs` reads them into `public/world-tables.json`, fetched by the World screen when someone builds and kept by the service worker like `world-book.json`. The build stops on a row it can't read, a tag it doesn't know, a slot nothing fills, or a role with too few names for a region its pack says it fits.
- **Every row** has its words, its roles, the regions and sizes it fits, a weight (common rows 10, rare rows 1), and what it needs or rules out. A type row also says which roles it leans to. A Secret row can name another faction with `{rival}`, and has two ways in.

### The engine

`src/companion/world-engine.js`, pure, with no page: the same code on the phone, in the tests and in the dealing script. Given the tables, the recipe (region, era, twist), the dials, a seed and the board so far, it deals.

- **Seeded and repeatable.** A seed's three words from `core.yaml` (*ash-lantern-ford*), and the same seed and tables deal the same world. The world keeps what was dealt, never just the seed, since the tables change most weeks.
- **A shuffled bag for each table** in each world, so no two factions share a type, a method or a Secret till the bag's empty. Rare rows weigh 1 against 10, and every board gets at least one strange thing.
- **Each step reads the card so far,** in this order: seat, size, type, how it works, Want, resources, assets, agenda, Face, Secret, name. A row's weight goes up for what fits (a ford gives passage, so a toll-taker's likelier), and a row comes out for what it rules out.
- **The because** comes from templates in `core.yaml` (*because it sits on the ford*), shown only when the reason isn't obvious.
- **About one deal in six breaks the logic on purpose,** from the pack's exceptions, with its reason (*a river town that won't trade, because the last barge brought the plague*).
- **Power** comes only from people who turn up. Size picks a die in the book's range, people who turn up are dealt to reach it, and the other assets are dealt so each has a resource paying for it, bar the needs. A faction never holds two of the same people.
- **Bonds start empty,** as the book says, unless something's already happened. When the card says something has (a Want to keep everything, and guards who haven't had a rise in years), the builder offers a thread or two as cards to keep or swipe away. The founding's history always has.
- **A Face** is a name from the pack and one of the Threat Builder's threats with its tags, at the Challenge its Power sends (a d8 sends Challenge 4).
- **The web.** The factions sit round a ring. Each points a rival at the next one round, an ally or a debt at the one after, and a wildcard at the one after that (skipped on boards of four or five, where it comes back round). A rival's a hostile thread each way, an ally or a debt a bond thread each way (a debt's words say who owes what), and a wildcard one thread from the first toward the other, its kind and words dealt off the ties table.
- **Need and extra.** An asset with nothing paying for it is a need, and an extra resource is what's left over. One faction in three is dealt a need, never people who turn up (so it keeps its size), and a faction with the extra resource it needs gets a hostile thread across it, so the obvious trade's blocked.
- **The checks,** before anyone sees the board: every faction has a rival, one need's blocked (on boards of five and up), one Secret names another faction, no two factions hold the same asset that does one thing, every agenda aims at something on the board, and there's one strange thing. Once there's a crew, one faction wants something the crew's got. Only what fails is dealt again, up to three times, then a fallback from `core.yaml` goes in.

### The build, kept

The build's record is kept with the world as `doc.build`: the recipe, the dials, the seed, what was dealt and the history. `src/companion/world-record.js` is the record and its `wb-` changes, handed through `board.js` the way `p-` and `m-` are, and `buildForPlayer()` is the only part a player gets: How it came to be, the history, once she's shown it, and never the seed or anything dealt. Only the GM changes it, checked on the Worker.

### Building alone

On the World screen: Build the world on an empty board, and Add a faction on any board, one brought in from a frame included. `src/companion/world-make.js` draws it, and a build that isn't finished is kept on her phone (`zi-make`, by campaign, forgotten when the campaign leaves the phone, like `zi-world`) till Done sends it as one batch. On a board with no build kept and no frame mix for it yet, Add a faction asks the region and genre first.

1. **Set up:** the region, the genre (or Mix your own) and how many factions.
2. **The asking:** Building Your World's three questions, each with three answers dealt off the tables or her own words, and the tension rolled. The answers set the dials: how tense, how strange, how rich and how ruled.
3. **The ground:** the seats, three cards at a time, each with its name and what a stranger notices there.
4. **The factions,** answered off the deck (Building by answering, below) or each in a few screens: where it sits and its size, then what it is, how it works and its Want, then its resources and assets, then its agenda, Face, Secret, name and any bonds. Every screen has three cards or a dealt set, with Lock, Deal again, Write my own, the because under each card, and Undo. Deal the rest finishes the board.
5. **The web and the checks,** then **the crew:** a friend and a foe for each character in the campaign.
6. **Done:** the factions go onto the board and the seats into the pages as places, hidden from the crew, and the record into `doc.build`. A faction's seat is written on its card by name, so it links itself.

**Deal it all** plays every step by the weights in one tap, and she goes through what it dealt faction by faction, locking what she likes and dealing the rest again.

### Building by answering

A third way to make the factions in Build the world, after the ground, beside going faction by faction and Deal it all. She goes through a deck of moments a card at a time, the way a reign goes, and how she answers forms the factions, so she never fills one in line by line. It runs on her phone like the rest of building alone, kept in `zi-make` till Done.

- **Who's who.** The factions' seats, sizes and what they are come with the ground, dealt the way they are now, so a card can say who's who. Till a faction's named it's called by what it is and where it sits (*the bell-founders on the Old Bridge*), off `templates` in `core.yaml`, and everything else on its card is open for the deck.
- **A card's a moment** from before the crew turned up. Someone from one faction brings it, named off the pack's people, about another faction or a seat nobody holds, in a line or two (*Old Maud of the Wool Staplers: "The bridge men want a toll off every bale."*), with two answers as short as a choice gets (*Pay it*, *Run the bales at night*). She swipes it one way or the other, or taps an answer at the foot. As she leans it toward an answer, the factions it touches light up along the top, and once it's gone, what it did comes up in a line.
- **An answer's one of the six outcomes** between the card's factions, or a mini faction made: a resource or an asset gained, taken or lost (people who turn up change Power), a thread written or strained, a grudge or a debt that becomes an agenda, a Secret that names the other faction, and a lean (tags on the faction, so how it works, its Want, its Secret and its Face come out to fit what it did). Both answers cost someone something, so every answer leaves a faction with a reason to act.
- **Every answer's a line of history** in the past tense (*The Wool Staplers paid the toll, and never forgave the bridge.*), kept in `doc.build`'s history as How it came to be, hidden till she shows the crew.
- **Three ages**, each done when its part's formed for every faction: long ago (where each faction came from, what it took and what it's like, so how it works and its Want), in living memory (deals, feuds and debts, so the web, the needs and the rivals), and this last year (what's live now, so its agenda, its Face and its Secret). Each line of history keeps its age (1 to 3, the record's `age`), the way the founding's will.
- **As many cards as it takes.** The next card's the one that does most for whatever's least formed in its age, so how many it takes depends on the board and the answers: a small board might be done in a dozen and a big one in forty. The dials weight it (how tense brings feuds, how rich deals and debts, how ruled the law, how strange the strange ones). It never runs past 70, so the history fits, and the last card's how things stood when the crew turned up.
- **Chains** make the dealings more than questions: an answer can start a story whose next card comes a few cards later (*Forbid the wedding*, then *The ferry-master's daughter ran off with the Vane groom anyway*), and a word she's written comes back on the cards after it.
- **A prompt** comes with some answers: a name (the faction's, its Face's, a new place's or someone's) or a detail (what was taken, what's under the chapel). The card turns over to the question, a field and three suggestions dealt off the tables to fit, none of them filled in. She taps one, writes her own or skips it, and what's skipped is dealt at the end. What she writes is hers, so nothing deals over it.
- **Not this one** deals another card for the same need and changes nothing. Undo takes an answer back (the last 25, as building alone does), and a faction's tile opens its card so far.
- **The crew**, once there's one, can come in this last year by name: a card whose answer makes a faction a friend or a foe of a character, and their screen after the board only deals the side the deck didn't.
- **The end.** What's still open on every card is dealt to fit what the answers made, a step the answers steered saying which moment did it in its because. She's on the board screen with the web, the checks and each faction's screens, then the crew and Done, as building alone goes. What the answers made is locked, since it's what happened, and the checks only deal what fails around it.
- **Seeded** like the rest: the same seed and the same answers deal the same deck and the same board, and Not this one, Undo and a prompt leave everything else as it was.
- **The engine** is `src/companion/world-deck.js`, pure like `world-engine.js` and playing on top of it. The deck's kept in the world in progress, so `recordOf()`, `resume()` and `zi-make` keep it. An answer becomes the engine's own picks, locks, tags and threads, the end's `dealRest()`, and the record's `dealt` keeps which way each card went.
- **The cards' words** are a table in `core.yaml` (`deck`), written for every genre with slots the packs fill (a seat, a resource, an asset, someone's name), and a pack can add its own. Add a faction keeps its screens for now.
- **The founding uses it later:** in the strife, each old power's act comes to her as a moment off the deck, and her answer's the act.

### The founding (a working name)

The crew builds the world with her, live on everyone's phone, as session zero. It's kept on the campaign's live table the way the faction turn is (`doc.found`): `src/companion/found-rules.js` is the rules both sides apply (`fd-` changes, and `foundView()`, the only part a player gets), wired into `table-rules.js`, and `src/companion/found.js` draws it on her campaign page and every player's World tab. Nothing touches the board till she hands it over, and Secrets never go into the room.

1. **Set up** (hers): the region, the genre, how many factions, who's in, and each age's clock.
2. **The asking** (answering questions): each player answers one of the region's questions in turn, from three dealt answers or their own words, and the tension's rolled.
3. **The ground** (drafting from a dice pool), the first age: her phone rolls a d6 for every seat. Taking turns till the pool's empty, each player takes a die, and its face picks the seat's role off the region's table. They keep one of the three seats it deals, name it, and say what a stranger notices there.
4. **The founding** (pick one of three), the second age. First she deals the old powers onto seats, picking one of three for each. Then, in reverse order, each player founds a faction on a free seat. Its three founding questions write its first threads, and the factions they can name are the ones already on the board: who had this seat before (a hostile thread from them toward it, or nobody), who it owes (a bond thread from it toward them, its words saying what's owed), and what it'll never do (a Loyalty thread). Then three cards each for what it is, how it works and its Want, and the rest dealt at Local.
5. **The strife** (spending like gods), the third age. Each round, every founder spends 3 tokens:
   - **Claim** a free seat (2): its resource goes on the card (Establish).
   - **Raise** people who turn up (2), paid for by an extra resource (Establish).
   - **Pact** (1): a bond thread each way, and both say yes (Influence).
   - **Feud** (1): strain a thread the other faction has toward it, or write a hostile one (Undermine).
   - **Raid** (3): Power against Power, ties to the defender, and a win takes one asset or resource (Acquire). Win or lose, the defender gets a hostile thread toward the raider.
   - **Scheme** (1, once a round): its agenda up a size, working its agenda the way the faction turn's d6 has it.
   - **Found a mini faction** (3), the way Mini Factions says: one resource and one asset handed over, two outcomes, a Loyalty thread toward its maker, and three at most (Establish).

   Pacts, feuds and raids touch another faction, and the rest build the founder's own. Every act writes a line of history, and every act but a scheme is one of the six outcomes, so the founding teaches the faction turn. Each round her phone deals each old power's one act off its agenda as a moment off the deck, and her answer's the act.
6. **Her pass,** on her phone alone: Secrets and the old powers' hidden parts are drawn there, never from the shared seed, then the Faces and the checks.
7. **The crew:** each player takes a friend and a foe for their character, with their own faction's ally and rival offered, and the hottest feud's named as the first job, for her to write.
8. **Hand-over:** one tap from her phone writes it into the world: the factions and their mini factions onto the board, the seats into the pages as places, and the record and its history into `doc.build`, shown on the World screen as How it came to be. What the crew built in the open is shown, and everything else is hidden. The agendas keep the dice they reached.

### Maps

- **The faction web** comes first: the factions round a ring on the World screen and tab, their threads drawn between them, hostile and bond, with the crew in the middle. A player sees only what they've been shown.
- **The sketch** comes later, in the ground of both ways of building: seats placed on a blank map, with coast, river, mountain, forest and road strokes. When the build's done, her phone draws it into a picture that goes up the way her pictures do, as one of the world's maps with a pin at each seat opening its page.
- **Her own picture** already takes pins to the seat pages.

### A whole world: Night City

A world written whole rather than dealt, the way a campaign looks once it's set up and running, that a GM brings into a campaign in a tap. The first is Night City in 2077, from the official Cyberpunk universe (Mo's call, 30 Oct). A universe can hold more than one year, and each year's a world of its own.

- **Whose it is.** Cyberpunk belongs to CD Projekt and R. Talsorian Games. The lore's checked on the web, and every word's Mo's voice, written fresh: nothing copied or closely reworded, and no text, maps, art or logos of theirs. That line, in Mo's voice, says it's fan-made, free and nothing to do with them, and the two notices go under it in the companies' own words (R. Its top says how a world's written.

A place has its name and other names, what it is, its Challenge, what it's like, who's here, the ground, the stakes, a scene clock, a complication, telegraphs, hooks, Elsewhere, its Secret and her note. Factions that share a pin fan out round it, a mini faction sits by its maker's, and a faction with no pin waits in a strip under the map, a tap from its ties. Nothing's lit till one's tapped. A player's is only the factions, threads and pins they've been shown, and a pin under the fog stays off it. ## Writing tables

For every stage that writes or changes tables.

- Mo's voice through `zi-voice`: plain, concrete and specific, contractions, NZ spelling, and never em dashes, semicolons or exclamation marks. Names one to four words, lines up to twelve.
- Every entry earns its place: it gives someone a reason to act, a hook or a rule, never just colour.
- No two entries in a table say the same thing, no phrase repeats across a pack, and nothing's named after a real company, church, person, place or game.
- Every role has at least four names for each region its pack fits (faction names for each kind of faction too), people who turn up at least twelve, faction names and people's names at least twelve each, every type at least two for each size, and three strange Secrets with core's. The build checks it.
- Six exceptions to a pack, each breaking the logic with a reason. One Secret in three names another faction, and every Secret has two ways in a player could find.
- Historical eras get invented names with a real feel, and the modern ones everyday names from many places.
- A line with a faction's name in a slot reads right whether the name's one or many, so the name never does something in the present tense: past tense, or the name after the verb (*the Hill Men stole our barges*, *we owe the Hill Men*, never *the Hill Men laughs at us*).
- Draft the whole pack in one go, deal at least twenty boards on `/deal/`, and fix what repeats or reads flat before Mo sees it.
- **The deck** (from stage 7): a moment's up to 25 words, an answer 1 to 4, and an answer's line of history up to 16, in the past tense, so How it came to be reads straight through. Both answers are things a faction would really do, and neither's plainly right. At least 60 moments in core and a dozen in each pack, a third in each age, every outcome on at least eight, every kind of faction on at least six, one in four starting or carrying on a chain, and one in eight strange. The build checks the counts. Play at least twenty decks on `/deal/` before Mo sees it.
- **A world** (from stage 12): everything above that fits, in Mo's voice through `zi-voice`, and every word written fresh. The lore's checked on the web, the year's own source first where they disagree (the game for 2077, the tabletop books for the years they cover), and nothing's copied or closely reworded from anywhere. The setting's own names are the one exception to naming nothing from another game, and still no real company, brand or person goes in (CD Projekt's fan rules ask that too). A card or a page says what it is at this game's table: its dice, Challenges, tags and telegraphs in Zi'Dara's terms, never the other game's rules or numbers. Lines run the way the tables' do, a place's look or a Secret's rest is a short paragraph at most, and every entry earns its place with a reason to act, a hook or a rule. Read the whole year through on its page at 390 before Mo sees it, and fix what repeats or reads flat. Abnu's the exception to the web: its lore comes from `notes/abnu-canon.md` alone, and its names are its own.

`/deal/` (from stage 3) shows every table and deals sample boards from the current ones, a tap at a time, for any region and genre, kept out of search and off phones like `/kit/`. It's where Mo reads and judges a pack.
