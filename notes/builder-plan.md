# The world builder plan

A builder that deals a whole world, faction by faction, from tables Mo's approved, so a GM can build one alone in minutes, or play its making with the crew as session zero. It comes from the research in *Worldbuilding that feels like play* (27 Oct 2026, a doc in Mo's Claude account) and Mo's answers on 27 and 28 Oct. It goes ahead of the worlds plan's last two stages (the crew's pins and notes, and timelines and family trees), which carry on after it.

In code it's the world builder, never just the builder, since `builder.js` is the character builder: its files are `world-` names.

## Decisions

Mo's calls (27 and 28 Oct):

- **No AI in the builder.** Nothing's generated when someone builds. Every word it shows comes from tables Mo's approved, or is typed by someone at the table. The fun's in gamified randomness and guided writing.
- **The GM picks who builds.** Alone, she builds the world and presents it to the crew. With the crew, they found it together in a session-zero game, so they're invested from day one and play in what they made.
- **The app first.** Built for the site, on phones. A paper version comes later.
- **Region, then genre.** A world's region is Town, City, Country, World or Stars. Its genre gives its seats, resources, assets, factions and names their words: Fantasy, Medieval, Renaissance, Modern, Steampunk, Cyberpunk and Futuristic, plus the ones Claude added below. Genres replace doing the Steeplands first.
- **Educated guesses, never generic.** Each step reads what's already on the board, so a river city trades and a mountain hold needs grain, and a result says why when it isn't obvious.
- **All four kinds of randomness:** pick one of three, draft from a dice pool, answer questions, and spend points like gods.
- **Tables:** Claude drafts them in Mo's voice, and nothing goes in till he's said yes.
- **Alone:** guided by default, three cards at every step, with a Deal it all tap that plays the same steps for her.
- **With the crew:** a whole session, with a short played history that ends with every conflict still live. Each player founds a faction and speaks for it, and the GM takes them all over when play starts.
- **Secrets** are drawn hidden, and only her phone ever sees them.
- **Every character** leaves with a friend and a foe among the factions.
- **Maps:** the faction web, a map sketched during the build, and her own picture with pins.
- **Power comes only from people who turn up** (28 Oct). Stage 1 makes it true across the book.
- **Fantasy next** (28 Oct, once he'd judged Medieval). Magic's tables come before building alone, so Fantasy deals as soon as it can.
- **Magic's a known craft, and it's people only** (28 Oct). Everyone knows magic's real: wards, charms and healing are trades, rituals and bound souls stay behind closed doors, and about a faction in three runs on it. There are monsters, spirits, the dead and old things like dragons, and no other peoples.

## Calls Claude made

For Mo to say otherwise.

- **A genre's an era and a twist** behind the scenes, so the genres Mo didn't name cost almost nothing. The eras are Ancient, Medieval, Renaissance, Industrial, Modern, Near future and Far future. The twists are Magic, Steam, Chrome, Ruin and Dread. The menu shows genres by name, and Mix your own takes any era with any twist:

| --- | --- |
| Fantasy | Medieval and Magic |
| Medieval · Renaissance · Modern | The era on its own |
| Steampunk | Industrial and Steam |
| Cyberpunk | Near future and Chrome |
| Futuristic | Far future |
| Mythic | Ancient and Magic |
| Gothic horror | Industrial and Dread |
| Urban fantasy | Modern and Magic |
| Post-apocalyptic | Modern and Ruin |
| Dark fantasy | Medieval and Dread |
| Space fantasy | Far future and Magic |

- **Each frame says its mix** (Long Haul is Far future at Stars), so Add a faction fits a frame she brought in. A frame's mix goes in with the last pack it needs.
- **Stars needs the Far future era** at first.
- **The sizes keep their names in the rules,** and the builder shows each region's words for them (on a Town board, Great reads as the whole town).
- **Board size:** the players plus two, at least four and at most twelve, hers to change at the start. Seats are two more than the factions, so there's always one worth fighting over.
- **In the founding,** the old powers she deals are City or Great and the founders start Local, so upstarts face old money. Every agenda in the founding starts at d4. Building alone uses the agendas table's own dice.
- **The founding's three ages** (the ground, the founding and the strife) each have a clock she sets at the start, 30, 40 and 40 minutes to begin with. When one runs out, the turn that's on finishes, the engine deals whatever's left, and the next age starts.
- **The strife:** 3 tokens a round each, and Scheme pushes an agenda a size once a round. It ends with the round that takes an agenda to d10, after 4 rounds, or when its clock runs out, so it runs three or four rounds. These are the dials to tune at the table.
- **After the founding,** what the table built in the open is shown to the crew. Secrets, the old powers' agendas and everything from her pass stay hidden.
- **Names** are dealt from each genre's lists, three at a time, or typed.
- **No builder skill.** Writing tables, below, does the job one would, so it doesn't take a slot.
- **A card's people always reach its die** (stage 2). If they can't, because it's too few resources to pay for them, the die comes down to the one they reach and the size with it.
- **There's always a trade to block** (stage 2). When nobody's got a resource to spare, one faction's dealt as many resources as its size holds, and one's left over.
- **Deal again** never gives a step the card it had while there's another, never touches a locked step, and a new seat deals the card on it again from its size, bar what's locked. A changed answer moves the dials for what's dealt next and leaves what's dealt alone.
- **The core keeps the book's rows** (stage 3): Building a Faction's wants, how it works and its secrets, and the tension, word for word, with two agendas to each of the book's, at its die. A Secret's written the way the book's cards write one, a hidden tag in capitals and a line, and resources and assets are named the way the cards name them.
- **Plain Medieval's strange things are the deniable kind** (stage 3): saints, curses, old graves, things nobody can explain. The Magic pack makes them real.
- **What fits counts** (stage 3): a row's weight goes up three times over for each thing on its card it fits and half that for each on the board, a type that leans to its seat's keep counts eight times over (so about three factions in four live off their seat), and a faction's next resource after its seat's, its first people and its first asset are ones it leans to.
- **A step dealt again reads only the steps before it** (stage 3), so a type's three cards never lean on the method and Want it had.
- **A Face is one of the threats core gives its kind of faction** (stage 3), never the pack or the swarm for a guild.
- **A specific name needs its kind's tags** (stage 3): the Hammermen need `metal`, the Wool Staplers `cloth`, Coyle's Barges a river, so names and types agree. `metal`, `cloth` and `stone` are core's tags for that.
- **A magic row that only makes sense for a magic faction needs magic** (stage 4): its seat or kind has to carry the `magic` tag already, so a world's magic gathers on the factions that run on it, and the trades anyone could profit from stay open.
- **A kind of faction can say its Face** (stage 4): a type row's `faces` are the three threats its Face is likeliest to be, instead of core's for its kind, so a coven's is likeliest a sorcerer.
- **The Steeplands are Fantasy at Country with how strange at 0, and the Deep Company's Fantasy at Town** (stage 4): the Steeplands are an archipelago of trade cities where magic sits quietly, and the Deep Company's a company town over the old city. A frame's keyed by its board's id in the book and can set the dials the book gives it, and Add a faction deals at them.
- **A twist's rows are written for the eras that deal it so far** (stage 4): Magic's are Medieval's. The stage that brings the next era to deal with Magic (Renaissance in a mix, then Modern for Urban fantasy) writes Magic's rows for it, and gives a row that only fits some eras a field saying which, a change to the tables and the engine.
- **Every step arrives dealt** (stage 5): a screen deals what it needs in the engine's order, so each step's three cards come with the one dealing picked already on, and Next always works. The factions are all dealt, round twice, the moment she reaches them, so an agenda or a Secret can aim at any faction, and she goes through them a faction at a time, in four screens. The asking puts the tension after the first question, the way the book asks it.
- **Picking a card deals what's after it again** (stage 5), the way the engine reads the card so far: the rest of that faction's card that isn't locked or hers, and the screen says what changed. Lock keeps a card through anything that's dealt, Deal this faction again deals what she hasn't locked or written, and her own change to a set locks it.
- **Add a faction ties in** (stage 5, told to Mo): a rival, and an ally or a debt, among the factions on the board, a thread each way, each one to keep or swipe away. Its agenda only aims at what's on the board, it sits on a seat nobody holds or a new place, and mini factions aren't held. With no build kept, it deals at the mix of the frame most of the factions came in from, or asks, and Done keeps the mix in the record so it doesn't ask again.
- **Where a faction sits goes on its card** (stage 5, Mo to judge): "It sits on the Old Bridge." after what it is, the because line's words, as `templates.seat` in `core.yaml`, so the place's name links. Each seat goes in as a place with what a stranger notices as what it's like, and its page lists the faction that sits there where it comes up.
- **Breaking the logic is hers too** (stage 5): one card in six still breaks it when dealt, and she can deal one onto a card or take it off.
- **One build on a phone for each campaign** (stage 5), kept till it's on the board or she drops it, and Undo takes back its last 25 changes till a reload. Undo on Done's note takes it all back off the board and brings the build back.

## How a stage goes

- One stage a chat, with the prompt at the end of this plan. The stage is the first in The stages not marked built.
- A stage is marked built by ending its first line with the date it went to `main`, like *Built 30 Oct 2026.*, in the same merge.
- A stage that writes tables or changes a rule sends Mo what to judge in one message (the `/deal/` link on the Preview, for tables), changes only what he names, and waits for his yes in the same chat before it merges.
- Every stage writes what it built into CLAUDE.md the way the worlds stages did: a paragraph under The site for what it built and where, the Live since and Still to come lines, and any new part of the tests in the list that must pass.
- A call about how the builder works goes in Calls Claude made, here. A call about a rule goes in `notes/to-confirm.md`.
- The founding's stages use `durable-objects` for the live table, and every stage with a phone test uses `webapp-testing`.

## The brief

### Tables, roles and packs

- **Roles** are what every genre has in common. A seat's and a resource's role is where the keep comes from: passage, land, ground, craft, trade, belief, office, knowledge, labour, crime or wonder. An asset's role is one of Appendix B's assets, its mechanics as they stand after stage 1. A faction type's role is one of Building a Faction's twelve (a ruling house, traders, crooks, faith, office, a secret society, soldiers, makers, a family, rebels, keepers of old things, something old).
- **The core** holds everything that isn't genre: the regions, the roles, Building a Faction's tables (what it is, what it wants, how it works, its secret), the agendas, Building Your World's three region questions and its tension table, the ties between factions, the founding's questions and acts, the because lines, the checks' fallbacks and the seed words.
- **A region** says what a faction, a seat and a mini faction are at that size, the words for the four sizes, which seat role each face of a d6 picks (for the founding's dice pool), and what pressure from one size up looks like.
- **A pack** gives the roles their words for one era or one twist: seats for each region it fits, resource lines, every asset's names (and a word or two of what it does where the genre needs it), faction types, methods, Secrets, exceptions and names. A twist pack adds its own rows to the same roles, and the "how strange" dial sets how often they come up.
- The tables are YAML in `content/world-tables/` (`core.yaml`, `regions.yaml`, `genres.yaml`, `eras/*.yaml`, `twists/*.yaml`). `scripts/build-content.mjs` reads them into `public/world-tables.json`, fetched by the World screen when someone builds and kept by the service worker like `world-book.json`. The build stops on a row it can't read, a tag it doesn't know, a slot nothing fills, or a role with too few names for a region its pack says it fits.
- **Every row** has its words, its roles, the regions and sizes it fits, a weight (common rows 10, rare rows 1), and what it needs or rules out. A type row also says which roles it leans to. A Secret row can name another faction with `{rival}`, and has two ways in.

### The engine

`src/companion/world-engine.js`, pure, with no page: the same code on the phone, in the tests and in the dealing script. Given the tables, the recipe (region, era, twist), the dials, a seed and the board so far, it deals.

- **Seeded and repeatable.** A seed's three words from `core.yaml` (*ash-lantern-ford*), and the same seed and tables deal the same world. The world keeps what was dealt, never just the seed, since the tables change most weeks.
- **A shuffled bag for each table** in each world, so no two factions share a type, a method or a Secret till the bag's empty. Rare rows weigh 1 against 10, and every board gets at least one strange thing.
- **Each step reads the card so far,** in this order: seat, size, type, how it works, Want, resources, assets, agenda, Face, Secret, name. A row's weight goes up for what fits (a ford gives passage, so a toll-taker's likelier), and a row comes out for what it rules out.
- **The because** comes from templates in `core.yaml` (*because it sits on the ford*), shown only when the reason isn't obvious.
- **About one deal in six breaks the logic on purpose,** from the pack's exceptions, with its reason (*a river town that won't trade, because the last barge brought the plague*).
- **Power** comes only from people who turn up. Size picks a die in the book's range, people who turn up are dealt to reach it, and the other assets are dealt so each has a resource paying for it, bar the needs. A faction never holds two of the same people.
- **Bonds start empty,** as the book says, unless something's already happened. When the card says something has (a Want to keep everything, and guards who haven't had a rise in years), the builder offers a thread or two as cards to keep or swipe away. The founding's history always has.
- **A Face** is a name from the pack and one of the Threat Builder's threats with its tags, at the Challenge its Power sends (a d8 sends Challenge 4).
- **The web.** The factions sit round a ring. Each points a rival at the next one round, an ally or a debt at the one after, and a wildcard at the one after that (skipped on boards of four or five, where it comes back round). A rival's a hostile thread each way, an ally or a debt a bond thread each way (a debt's words say who owes what), and a wildcard one thread from the first toward the other, its kind and words dealt off the ties table.
- **Need and extra.** An asset with nothing paying for it is a need, and an extra resource is what's left over. One faction in three is dealt a need, never people who turn up (so it keeps its size), and a faction with the extra resource it needs gets a hostile thread across it, so the obvious trade's blocked.
- **The checks,** before anyone sees the board: every faction has a rival, one need's blocked (on boards of five and up), one Secret names another faction, no two factions hold the same asset that does one thing, every agenda aims at something on the board, and there's one strange thing. Once there's a crew, one faction wants something the crew's got. Only what fails is dealt again, up to three times, then a fallback from `core.yaml` goes in.

### The build, kept

The build's record is kept with the world as `doc.build`: the recipe, the dials, the seed, what was dealt and the history. `src/companion/world-record.js` is the record and its `wb-` changes, handed through `board.js` the way `p-` and `m-` are, and `buildForPlayer()` is the only part a player gets: How it came to be, the history, once she's shown it, and never the seed or anything dealt. Only the GM changes it, checked on the Worker.

### Building alone

On the World screen: Build the world on an empty board, and Add a faction on any board, one brought in from a frame included. `src/companion/world-make.js` draws it, and a build that isn't finished is kept on her phone (`zi-make`, by campaign, forgotten when the campaign leaves the phone, like `zi-world`) till Done sends it as one batch. On a board with no build kept and no frame mix for it yet, Add a faction asks the region and genre first.

1. **Set up:** the region, the genre (or Mix your own) and how many factions.
2. **The asking:** Building Your World's three questions, each with three answers dealt off the tables or her own words, and the tension rolled. The answers set the dials: how tense, how strange, how rich and how ruled.
3. **The ground:** the seats, three cards at a time, each with its name and what a stranger notices there.
4. **The factions,** each in a few screens: where it sits and its size, then what it is, how it works and its Want, then its resources and assets, then its agenda, Face, Secret, name and any bonds. Every screen has three cards or a dealt set, with Lock, Deal again, Write my own, the because under each card, and Undo. Deal the rest finishes the board.
5. **The web and the checks,** then **the crew:** a friend and a foe for each character in the campaign.
6. **Done:** the factions go onto the board and the seats into the pages as places, hidden from the crew, and the record into `doc.build`. A faction's seat is written on its card by name, so it links itself.

**Deal it all** plays every step by the weights in one tap, and she goes through what it dealt faction by faction, locking what she likes and dealing the rest again.

### The founding (a working name)

The crew builds the world with her, live on everyone's phone, as session zero. It's kept on the campaign's live table the way the faction turn is (`doc.found`): `src/companion/found-rules.js` is the rules both sides apply (`fd-` changes, and `foundView()`, the only part a player gets), wired into `table-rules.js`, and `src/companion/found.js` draws it on her campaign page and every player's World tab. Nothing touches the board till she hands it over, and Secrets never go into the room.

1. **Set up** (hers): the region, the genre, how many factions, who's in, and each age's clock.
2. **The asking** (answering questions): each player answers one of the region's questions in turn, from three dealt answers or their own words, and the tension's rolled.
3. **The ground** (drafting from a dice pool), the first age: her phone rolls a d6 for every seat. Taking turns till the pool's empty, each player takes a die, and its face picks the seat's role off the region's table. They keep one of the three seats it deals, name it, and say what a stranger notices there.
4. **The founding** (pick one of three), the second age. First she deals the old powers onto seats, picking one of three for each. Then, in reverse order, each player founds a faction on a free seat. Its three founding questions write its first threads, and the factions they can name are the ones already on the board: who had this seat before (a hostile thread from them toward it, or nobody), who it owes (a bond thread from it toward them, its words saying what's owed), and what it'll never do (a Loyalty thread). Then three cards each for what it is, how it works and its Want, and the rest dealt at Local.
5. **The strife** (spending like gods), the third age. Each round, every founder spends 3 tokens:
   - **Claim** a free seat (2): its resource goes on the card (Establish).
   - **Raise** people who turn up (2), paid for by an extra resource (Establish).
   - **Pact** (1): a bond thread each way, and both say yes (Influence).
   - **Feud** (1): strain a thread the other faction has toward it, or write a hostile one (Undermine).
   - **Raid** (3): Power against Power, ties to the defender, and a win takes one asset or resource (Acquire). Win or lose, the defender gets a hostile thread toward the raider.
   - **Scheme** (1, once a round): its agenda up a size, working its agenda the way the faction turn's d6 has it.
   - **Found a mini faction** (3), the way Mini Factions says: one resource and one asset handed over, two outcomes, a Loyalty thread toward its maker, and three at most (Establish).

   Pacts, feuds and raids touch another faction, and the rest build the founder's own. Every act writes a line of history, and every act but a scheme is one of the six outcomes, so the founding teaches the faction turn. Each round her phone deals each old power's one act off its agenda, hers to keep or change.
6. **Her pass,** on her phone alone: Secrets and the old powers' hidden parts are drawn there, never from the shared seed, then the Faces and the checks.
7. **The crew:** each player takes a friend and a foe for their character, with their own faction's ally and rival offered, and the hottest feud's named as the first job, for her to write.
8. **Hand-over:** one tap from her phone writes it into the world: the factions and their mini factions onto the board, the seats into the pages as places, and the record and its history into `doc.build`, shown on the World screen as How it came to be. What the crew built in the open is shown, and everything else is hidden. The agendas keep the dice they reached.

### Maps

- **The faction web** comes first: the factions round a ring on the World screen and tab, their threads drawn between them, hostile and bond, with the crew in the middle. A player sees only what they've been shown.
- **The sketch** comes later, in the ground of both ways of building: seats placed on a blank map, with coast, river, mountain, forest and road strokes. When the build's done, her phone draws it into a picture that goes up the way her pictures do, as one of the world's maps with a pin at each seat opening its page.
- **Her own picture** already takes pins to the seat pages.

### Like the rest of the site

One-handed on a phone at the table, in both themes, from 320 wide, and offline for anything already seen. Everything kept per campaign stays in it, checked on the server, and nothing crosses between campaigns. Building alone runs on her phone, so it works with no signal and goes up when there's one.

## Writing tables

For every stage that writes or changes tables.

- Mo's voice through `zi-voice`: plain, concrete and specific, contractions, NZ spelling, and never em dashes, semicolons or exclamation marks. Names one to four words, lines up to twelve.
- Every entry earns its place: it gives someone a reason to act, a hook or a rule, never just colour.
- No two entries in a table say the same thing, no phrase repeats across a pack, and nothing's named after a real company, church, person, place or game.
- Every role has at least four names for each region its pack fits (faction names for each kind of faction too), people who turn up at least twelve, faction names and people's names at least twelve each, every type at least two for each size, and three strange Secrets with core's. The build checks it.
- Six exceptions to a pack, each breaking the logic with a reason. One Secret in three names another faction, and every Secret has two ways in a player could find.
- Historical eras get invented names with a real feel, and the modern ones everyday names from many places.
- A line with a faction's name in a slot reads right whether the name's one or many, so the name never does something in the present tense: past tense, or the name after the verb (*the Hill Men stole our barges*, *we owe the Hill Men*, never *the Hill Men laughs at us*).
- Draft the whole pack in one go, deal at least twenty boards on `/deal/`, and fix what repeats or reads flat before Mo sees it.

`/deal/` (from stage 3) shows every table and deals sample boards from the current ones, a tap at a time, for any region and genre, kept out of search and off phones like `/kit/`. It's where Mo reads and judges a pack.
