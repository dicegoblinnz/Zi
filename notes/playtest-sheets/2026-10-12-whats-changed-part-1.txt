1 Defending
   WAS   Guard put your Guard set action’s steps on your Armour or Confidence, or a friend’s in reach, for the round. And you rolled
   your defence against each die aimed at you, one at a time.

One defence roll a round
Anything being attacked rolls its defence once, and that roll stands against everything that comes at it. When the
telegraphs land at the end of the round, you roll your Armour once against all the blows, and your Confidence once
against all the fear and pressure. Every die aimed at you has to beat that one number.

Threats work the same way. The first time one’s attacked in a round, roll its Challenge, and everyone who goes at it that
round has to beat that roll. Ties still go to the defender.

Defend
Defend replaces Guard, and it takes your action.
    When the telegraphs land, roll your Armour once for every die coming at you, then choose which roll blocks which. It
    works the same with Confidence against fear, lies and pressure.
    You can pull one enemy’s whole attack onto yourself. Everything it aimed at friends within your stride comes at you
    instead.
    That’s one enemy, unless you’ve got the Boon that pulls from two.
    Defend doesn’t use steps, so your Guard set action has nothing left to do. Turn that row into another set action.

   E X AM P LE
   A brute telegraphs three d8s at Wren. Wren’s within Bram’s stride, so he Defends and pulls all three onto himself.
   When they land, the d8s come up 7, 5 and 2, and Bram rolls his d6 Armour three times: 6, 3 and 1. He puts the 6 on the
   5 and the 3 on the 2, so only the 7 gets through. On a single roll, his 3 would only have stopped the 2.

Answering a telegraph hasn’t changed.

2 Hurt, Catches and Scars
   WAS   Three Hurt boxes, and filling the last one was a Blaze. Each Hurt took a downtime slot to heal. A Scar crossed out a box,
   and in downtime you could write it as a permission or a passive.

    Your Hurt track is now three bars of three boxes, nine in all. It’s one track for body and mind.
    Fill a bar and write a Catch in it, an injury like cracked ribs or can’t stand the dark. It works like any Catch, so you can
    let it bite. A bar that already has its Catch doesn’t get another when it fills again.
    At the end of every scene the loose boxes clear. You drop back to 3 boxes for each Catch on your Hurt track.
    Take a Hurt with every box full and you Blaze: declare something extraordinary and it happens, no roll.
    From your very first Blaze, you can choose for your character to die. If you don’t, they leave the scene and take a
    Scar.
    A Scar crosses out one box for good. Scars are for the story, and it’s up to you where it is on your body. A Scar
    doesn’t do anything else, and it doesn’t turn into a permission any more.
    When every box is crossed out, your next Blaze is your last.

  E X AM P LE

  Vesk takes her third Hurt, fills her first bar and writes twisted ankle in it. She takes two more before the scene ends.
  Those two clear, and she starts the next scene with her first bar still full.

   FO R T H E G M If a player chooses to die, slow right down. Let them tell their last moment in their own words, and make sure what
   they died for happens: the door holds, the crew gets out, the threat goes down with them. Then give everyone at the table a
   moment to answer it, in character.


3 Healing
   WAS   Mid-job, only a trait like Field Dressing cleared a Hurt, and magic didn’t. In downtime, Hurts healed one a slot.

    In a scene, a heal is an action rolled against the patient’s heal die. That’s a d6, a size bigger for each Catch on their Hurt
    track: a d8 with one, a d10 with two, a d12 with three. Each die that beats it clears one box, so split it to heal more.
    A heal can clear any box, even one in a bar with a Catch, but the Catch stays. At the end of the scene you’re back to 3
    boxes for each Catch on your Hurt track.
    Quick heal: pay 1 Aura to clear one box on one person, no roll. It still takes your action.
    Healing a group: make a die your area, the same as a ward (1 Aura and a split). Its number is how many friends it
    reaches, and each of them clears a box if your heal beats their heal die.
    In downtime, each Catch on your Hurt track takes a downtime slot to heal, and none heal for free. A trait that says so
    can spend one slot to clear a Catch on everyone in the crew.

  E X AM P LE
  Healing Vesk, who’s got one Catch on her Hurt track, you roll against a d8. Split your roll into two dice, and if both beat
  it, she clears two boxes.


4 Catches, Sparks and Aura
    Let a Catch bite as often as you like. It used to be once a scene. Each time, it has to bring real trouble, and each time
    you take 1 Aura.
    You get a Spark for each Title, and they all come back every downtime. It used to be one Spark, back each session.
    The GM can give you 1 Aura when a great setup doesn’t land: you planned it, it was cool, and the dice said no.

5 Summoning
   WAS    Calling up a creature that works on its own for the scene was going beyond, for 1 Aura.

     Calling up summons takes your action and costs Aura. Each Aura gives you 3 steps to build with, and you can spend
     as much as you’ve got.
     Spend the steps on summons’ dice or their tags. A summon’s die is a d4 at least, and a tag costs 2 steps.
     A tag is something it’s good at, like great axe. It adds a step whenever it fits what the summon’s doing, and it’s one more
     hit the summon can take.
     A summon goes down in one hit, plus one more hit for every tag it has.
     A summon can’t have more tags than its die has steps.
     Summons last the scene and act from the next round, each doing what you tell it. They can’t combo, and their top
     faces don’t give Aura.

6 Permissions
     Write each one with a doing word and what you do it to. It should finish “I can…”: fight with a sword, spot a lie, read
     dead languages. A name, like Combat master, is a Title, and a describing word, like Deceptive, is a trait.
     The quick test: it finishes “I can…” with a doing word, you can picture the moment you’d use it, it’d come up a few
     times a session, and it fits inside your Title.
     Lines that sound alike both count, but you can’t take the same permission twice.
     Swapping: in downtime you can swap any of your permissions for others that fit your Title, as long as it makes sense
     for your character. Boons swap the same way.

7 Boons and Banes
   NE W   Permanent upgrades and downsides you build into your character.

     A Boon is a permanent upgrade. It costs two permission slots on one of your Titles, and you can swap it back for
     permissions in downtime.
     A Bane is a permanent downside, and it pays for one Boon or two permissions.
     Give them names from the story: Iron Lungs (+1 bar), Bad Knee (stride −2).
     A Bane is always on, and it has to cost you something you actually use. Nothing goes below its smallest size, so you
     can’t take an Armour Bane on a d2.

Boons that get you Aura back
Each of these is its own Boon, with no Bane.

    Step in: when a friend within your stride fails their defence, you can take the hit instead. It doesn’t use your action, you
    don’t roll Armour, and you take 1 Aura.
    Beat a threat: take 1 Aura whenever a threat of any kind falls to you, whether it’s a monster, a locked door, a puzzle or
    an argument.
    In your element: start a scene somewhere your Title belongs and take 1 Aura, even past your maximum.
