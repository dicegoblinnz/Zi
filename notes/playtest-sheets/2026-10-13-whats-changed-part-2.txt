1 Items
   R E P LAC E S   The items and resources on your sheet.

    Plain items are just what they are. A sword’s a sword. It counts as a line when it fits, so it’s a step on your roll, and it
    lets you do what a sword does. Each piece of plain armour makes your Armour a size bigger.
    Resources are just items now.
    What you carry: 3 equipment (what you wear or wield, like weapons, armour and shields), 3 utility items (tools and
    things you use up, like rope, a lantern or a smoke bomb) and a keepsake, something that matters to your character.
    Your bag holds 10 more. Getting something out of it still takes your action.
    Attunement is on top of all that. You start with 2 attunement slots, and attuned things don’t count towards what you
    carry. If both of yours are equipment, you’ve got 5 pieces of equipment.
    When something breaks, it’s something you carry. Attuned things are magic, and they can’t be broken.


2 Attunement
   NE W     How an attuned item grows.

An attuned item has permissions of its own, and it grows in three stages. Each new stage needs a material, found in the
story or bought, and every new permission takes a downtime slot. Each stage can also take on more, for a price:

  S TA GE                     PE RMI S S I ON S   I T CA N A DD                                                      IN ALL

  Awakened                    1                   1 more with a Catch                                                2

  Tempered                    2                   2 more with a Burden (the Catch becomes the Burden)                4

  Perfected                   3                   3 more with a Burden and a Catch                                   6

    That’s six permissions at most, with one Catch and one Burden. You can’t take a Burden before Tempered.
    You start with your attuned things at Awakened, and you can give each one its Catch when you make your character.
    You can hand an attuned item to a friend with a free attunement slot. Its permissions, its Catch and its Burden all go
    with it, and so does a Boon its Burden paid for.

3 Titles and Catches
   R E P LAC E S   The Catch on each Title.

    Titles don’t have Catches any more. A Title is its trait and its permissions.
    Catches come from attunement and from Hurt, the Catch you write when you fill a bar.
    Letting a Catch bite works the same: as often as you like, real trouble every time, and 1 Aura each time.
    You don’t need a Catch on your sheet to let trouble bite. Bring real trouble on yourself in the story and take 1 Aura. A
    written Catch just gives you trouble that’s ready to go.


4 Taking the hit
   NE W   For everyone, summons included.

    When an attack’s aimed at someone else within your stride, you can step in front of it and take it instead. That goes for
    players, NPCs and summons alike. It’s free, and you don’t roll to defend: you just take it.
    There’s a Boon and a Bane for it. Boon: when you take the hit, you can spend 1 Aura to roll your Armour against it, like a
    Defend that doesn’t use your action. Bane: taking the hit costs you 1 Aura.
    This replaces the Step in Boon from the last sheet.


5 Summoning
   R E P LAC E S   Summoning in the last sheet.

    Souls go in attunement. A summoning attunement holds souls the way an item holds permissions, and the souls you
    catch are its material: 1 at Awakened (2 with a Catch), 2 at Tempered (4 with a Burden) and 3 at Perfected (6 with a
    Burden and a Catch). Starting souls are d4s.
    Catching a soul: when a creature falls, spend your action and roll your Summon dice against its Challenge. Beat it and
    its soul goes into one of your utility slots, and miss and that soul’s gone. In downtime, put it into your summoning
    attunement. You can swap a soul in your attunement for one you’re carrying whenever you’ve got the slot.
    Summoning takes your action, and it needs a reason in the story. Nobody calls up a horde to drink at the tavern. Roll
    your Summon dice against your souls’ dice, and each of your dice brings out one soul it beats. Split to go for more.
    Aura: 1 Aura adds +2 to one of your dice when you’re catching or summoning. A tie still costs 1 Aura to win.
    A soul is what it was. A caught courtier talks, a hound tracks, a Warden fights. Each one rolls its own die for whatever
    it does.
    Summons act in the round like anyone. One hit takes a summon out, and its soul goes back to its slot, so you can call it
    again. Like anyone, a summon can take the hit for someone.
    Blocking: as its action, a summon can pull the attacks aimed at someone onto itself and roll against each. Each one it
    beats is stopped. The first one it loses kills it, and any others it loses go through to whoever it was protecting.
    At the end of a scene, roll each summon’s die. On 3 or lower it goes back to its slot. They all go back at downtime.
    Still true: summons can’t combo, and their top faces don’t give Aura.
    The summoning Boon now adds +1 to that Aura bonus each time you take it: +3, then +4.

  E X AM P LE
  Sable’s Summon action is a d10, and she’s got three souls: a rat swarm (d4), a hound (d6) and the harbour Warden (d8).
  She splits into a d6 and a d4 and goes for the two small ones. Her d4 comes up 2 against the rats’ 3, so she spends 1
  Aura to make it a 4, and the rats pour out. Her d6 beats the hound.

6 Boons and Banes
   R E P LAC E S   Parts of Boons and Banes in the last sheet.

    Banes only come on attunement now, as a Burden. Titles never take them. A Burden is worth two permissions on
    that attuned thing, or a Boon for you.
    Boons still cost two permission slots on a Title.
    The Bane column in the last sheet is now the list of Burdens an attuned thing can carry.
    On the menu, Take the hit is new (see section 4), Summons is now +1 to your summoning Aura bonus, and Step in is
    gone. So three summoning Boons now make your bonus +5, not 6 steps an Aura.


7 Permissions
   R E P LAC E S   “Write each one with a doing word and what you do it to.”

    Every permission has two parts, and it isn’t finished until it’s got both: the doing word, and the part that says how,
    what or where.
    Deceive on its own is only half a permission. Deceive through actions is finished. It covers feints and misdirection when
    you fight or sneak, but not talking your way past someone or digging for information.
    More finished ones: fight with a sword, talk my way past a guard, climb sheer walls.


8 Splitting past a d12
   M AD E C LE AR E R Not a change, just easier to read.


Going past 6 steps gives you a split on top of the one your action gives. So 8 steps on one action can be three dice, like a
d6, a d6 and a d4.
