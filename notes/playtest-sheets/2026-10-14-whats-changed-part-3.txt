What’s changed, part 3
Here’s what’s changed since part 2. The big one is archetypes: every Title is one now, and each has
something only it can do. A few things everyone used to do have gone to one of them. Where this says it
replaces something, go with this one. Everything else in parts 1 and 2 still stands.

1 Archetypes
   R E P LAC E S    Titles as they were, and the trait on each one.

Every Title is an archetype now, like Guardian, Healer or Shadow. An archetype gives you permissions the way a Title did,
and it has a mechanic of its own, something only that archetype can do.

    Your Title is the name you give your archetype. Bram’s a Guardian, and his Title is The wall that held the harbour gate. It
    still counts 1 on any roll it fits.
    Got a character already? Pick the archetype that fits each of your Titles, and write it next to the Title on your sheet. If
    none of them fits, tell me.
    You start with two archetypes, each with three permissions. A new one comes when your Aspiration climbs past d12,
    and it starts with one permission.
    Permissions haven’t changed. They have to fit inside your Title, and you can have six on each at most.
    The mechanic takes the place of your Title’s trait. Keep the traits you’ve got for now, until we’ve sorted out where
    traits go (see section 5).
    On most archetypes, 1 Aura adds +2 to one die on its mechanic. Spend it after you roll, once you know you need it.
    A die the +2 takes to its top face, or past it, doesn’t give you Aura, though a Warrior ’s still explodes.
    Take the same archetype twice and its +2 becomes +3, three times and it’s +4, and so on.
    You still get a Spark for each archetype, and for now each one still makes your Confidence a size bigger.

Bloodsworn                    Every Hurt you take adds 3 steps to your action. Take one on purpose as you act and the steps go on
                              that roll. Take one any other way and they go on your next action.
                               AU R A     There’s no +2 on this one. Spend as much Aura as you like, plus your action, and clear that
                                          many of your own Hurt boxes.
                                Two telegraphs land on you this round and you take two Hurts, so your next action’s got 6 more steps.


Elementalist                  Areas are yours now. One of your dice can be your area instead of an intent: a burst (that many squares
                              out from a point), a cone (that long the way you face, and as wide at the end) or a line (twice as far,
                              straight out). It takes one of your splits, and it doesn’t cost Aura any more. Everyone the story puts in it
                              defends against your other die, and you can always make it smaller.
                               1 AU R A    +2 to your area die.
                                Ash splits her 5 steps of fire into a d6 and a d4, and the d4’s her burst. It rolls 2, but the five guards are
                                three squares out, so she spends 1 Aura and it’s a 4. All five defend against her d6.


Face                          Spend your action and build your dice the usual way, but don’t roll them. Put them on a threat as
                              influence, and they stay until you use them or the scene ends. At any point before one of its telegraphs
                              lands, roll your influence against it. Each of its dice that your best influence die beats is yours: say what
                              it does instead, as long as it fits the story, like hitting one of its own or the ground. Then the influence is
                              spent.
                               1 AU R A    +2 to one of your influence dice.
                                Dorrie’s got a d8 of influence on the bandit captain. He telegraphs two d6s at Bram, and she rolls her d8
                                against them: 5. They come up 3 and 6. The 3’s hers, so that cut lands on his own sergeant, and the 6
                                still goes at Bram.


Guardian                      When you Defend, every attack aimed at friends within your stride comes at you, whichever enemy it’s
                              from. Roll your Armour once for each die, or your Confidence against fear, lies and pressure, then choose
                              which roll blocks which. To get past you, threats have to pull the crew apart.
                               1 AU R A    +2 to one of your Armour dice.
                                Three goblins telegraph a d6 each at Vesk, Wren and Isolde, all within Bram’s stride. He Defends and
                                takes all three. They land 6, 4 and 1, and his d6 Armour rolls 5, 4 and 2. The 4 holds the 4, because ties
                                go to him, and the 2 holds the 1. He spends 1 Aura to make the 5 a 7 against the 6, and nothing gets
                                through.


Healer                        Only a Healer can make a heal roll, and it works the way it did: an action rolled against the patient’s heal
                              die, and each die that beats it clears a box. A heal reaches one person. Split it to clear more boxes, or to
                              reach a second person you can touch. Quick heal is still for everyone (see section 3).
                               1 AU R A    +2 to one of your heal dice.
                                Say you’re healing Vesk, who’s got one Catch on her Hurt track, so her heal die’s a d8. You roll 7 and her
                                d8 comes up 8, so you spend 1 Aura and make your 7 a 9. She clears a box.


Hunter                        Spend your action and roll against a creature to mark it. Win and it’s Marked, a special condition carrying
                              the die you won with. It doesn’t give a step and nobody spends it. Instead, anyone who acts against it
                              rolls the Mark die too, as one more die against it. It fades a size every round like any condition.
                               1 AU R A    +2 to the Mark die when it’s rolled. It’s your Aura, whoever’s rolling.
                                Wren marks the Warden with a d10 and wins, so it’s Marked d10. Bram swings at it with his d8 and rolls
                                the Mark d10 with it. Next round it’s Marked d8.

Illusionist                   Spend your action and roll your illusion dice against the threats’ Challenge. Meet or beat it and they
                              believe your illusion until they interact with it or try to get through it: a clone of you for them to hit, a wall
                              to run around, a crate to hide under. To see through it before then, a threat has to beat what you rolled.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to one of your illusion dice.
                                Say you throw a wall across the corridor. Your d8 rolls 6 and the guards’ d6 rolls 6, which is enough. They
                                run the long way round, and if one stops to look hard at it, he has to beat your 6.


Leader                        Only a Leader leads now. An order or a rallying song is an action: build it from your set action, then hand
                              its steps out however you like to friends who can hear or see you. They add them to their own rolls this
                              round, and the steps don’t have to fit.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to one of a friend’s dice, on a roll your steps went into.
                                Bram barks orders from the doorway, 4 steps off his Talk, and gives 2 to Vesk and 2 to Wren. Vesk’s
                                Sneak goes from a d8 to a d12. She rolls 6 against the guard’s 7, so Bram spends 1 Aura and she’s on 8.


Maker                         Spend your action and roll against the item’s size: a d4 for something tiny like a ring, a d6 for something
                              small like a dagger, a d8 for something medium like a shield, a d10 for something large like armour, and a
                              d12 for anything intricate. Each die that beats it gives the item a tag for the scene.
                              A tag is a special property, and it follows the same rules as a permission. A ring that gives you an extra
                              step to dodge is fine, and a ring that gives you haste isn’t. Whoever’s holding the item can break a tag
                              instead of taking a Hurt.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to one of your making dice.
                                Vesk hands you her ring, a d4. Your d8 rolls 6 against its 3, so the ring gets a tag for the scene, an extra
                                step when she dodges, and you hand it back. When a blade gets through, she can break that tag instead
                                of taking the Hurt.


Oracle                        Spend your action and build it from your set action, but don’t roll. Take its steps off the threats’
                              telegraphs however you like. Every step takes a telegraph’s die down a size, so 2 steps turn a d8 into a
                              d4, and a die with no steps left is gone.
                               1 AU R A   2 more steps to take off. Take Oracle twice and it’s 3 more, three times and it’s 4, and so on.
                                Say you’ve got 4 steps. A brute telegraphs a d8 at Wren and a d6 at Bram. You take 2 off the d8, so it’s a
                                d4, and 2 off the d6, so it’s a d2. Then you spend 1 Aura for 2 more, and the d4’s gone.


Scholar                       Everyone’s Study finds a hidden tag or a straight answer now (see section 3). When you win a Study, you
                              can introduce a truth instead: something that wasn’t obvious to anyone else. Say it, and it’s true and it
                              matters here. It counts as a permission for the crew this scene.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to one of your Study dice.
                                Isolde wins her Study on the Warden and says the chalk circle it’s bound to has a crack in it. Now it
                                does, and anyone in the crew can use that this scene.


Shadow                        When you get into hiding, the dice you rolled to hide go into your shadow pool. They ’re the things you
                              noticed while nobody was looking. Add them to any roll after that, talking and Study included, as their
                              own dice or as steps (a d8 is 4 steps). A die’s gone once you use it, and the next time you hide, your pool
                              starts again with the new dice. Coming out of hiding gives you an extra action, the same as anyone.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to your roll to hide, or to a shadow die when you use it.
                                Vesk slips into the rafters with her Sneak, a d8, and gets away with it, so the d8 goes in her pool. Later
                                the steward’s lying to her face, and she adds it to her Talk, because she saw him pocket the letter from
                                up there.

Shifter                       You collect animal tags, the way a Summoner catches souls. Spend your action, pick one and roll your
                              Shifter dice against its die: most are a d4, swimming’s a d6, flying’s a d8 and a breath attack’s a d12. Beat
                              it and the tag’s yours until downtime. It lets you do what the animal does, and it counts as a line when it
                              fits. Split to go for more than one, and strip one of your animal tags instead of taking a Hurt if you want
                              to.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to one of your Shifter dice.
                                Say you want wings. Flying’s a d8, and your d10 rolls 7 against its 5, so you’ve got wings as an animal tag
                                until downtime. When an axe gets through, you can strip the wings instead of taking the Hurt.


Summoner                      Catching souls and summoning are the Summoner’s now, and nobody else can do them. Everything else
                              about summoning in part 2 still stands: your souls live in your summoning attunement, you catch one
                              when a creature falls, and you summon against your souls’ dice.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to one of your dice when you’re catching or summoning, the same as before.
                              The summoning Boon’s gone. Take Summoner twice for +3, three times for +4, and so on.


Teleporter                    Your stride can be a teleport of up to 10 units. If you’re a Guardian as well, your Defend reaches every
WO R K I NG NA M E            friend within those 10 units, because you jump to each of them.
                               1 AU R A   Open a portal and link it to another spot within 10 units. It lasts the round, and anyone who
                                          steps in comes out the other end.


Warrior                       Your dice explode when you fight. A die that lands on its top face gives you 1 Aura as usual, then you roll
                              it again and add the new number on, the only time dice add apart from moving. It keeps going as long as
                              it keeps landing on top, so a d8 that comes up 8, then 5, is a 13.
                               1 AU R A   +2 to any one of your dice in a fight. If that takes it to its top face or past, there’s no Aura for
                                          it, but it still explodes.


3 Changes for everyone
   R E P LAC E S   The rules each line names.

    Leading is the Leader’s now. Nobody else can hand their steps out to friends, so lead or inspire comes off everyone
    else’s list of actions. Working together hasn’t changed: anyone can still put their action into the same thing as a combo.
    Areas are the Elementalist’s. Nobody else can make a die their area, so that’s off the Aura list. Sweep weapons still go
    at everyone in your reach, free.
    Wards and blessings reach one person. Warded or Blessed goes on one friend, and the second die of a split can put it
    on another friend you can touch. A Healer ’s heals work the same way, and that replaces healing a group with an area.
    Only a Healer can make a heal roll. Quick heal’s still for everyone: 1 Aura clears one box on one person, no roll, and it
    still takes your action. Regen, and traits that say they heal, still work.
    Study finds a hidden tag or a straight answer. The third choice, something that helps, is gone, and a Scholar can
    introduce a truth in its place. A Spark can still make an opportunity, for anyone.
    Coming out of hiding gives you an extra action, whoever you are. It’s one extra action, even if you’re Unseen as well.

4 Boons and Banes
   R E P LAC E S   Two of the Boons.

    Boons and Banes stay. A Boon still costs two permission slots, now on one of your archetypes, and a Burden on
    attunement still pays for one.
    Summons is gone. Taking Summoner twice does that job now.
    Defend’s pull from two is gone. Pulling more than one enemy’s attack onto you is the Guardian’s now. Its Burden, you
    can’t Defend, stays.
    Everything else on the list, and the Burdens, works the same.


5 Still to come
These are next on the list, so play them as they stand for now.

    Traits: where they go, now that your archetype’s mechanic has taken their place. Keep yours until then.
    Confidence: what makes it bigger, along with traits.
    The Teleporter needs a better name.
    Taking the Teleporter or the Bloodsworn twice: neither has a +2 to grow, so what you get is still to come.
    The Shifter: where you find your animal tags in the first place.
    Permissions like teleport, take an animal’s form or cast illusions: whether they still work without the archetype.

Have a go with your archetypes and tell me how they play, especially the new ones.
