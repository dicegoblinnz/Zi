"Stamina, confidence and resolve" in his first messages are the three tracks of part 1. By the end of the chat they're gone, and each Root's points do their job: "a sword slash is a stamina" means a hit at Body.

## Settled

- **Titles give 5 Root points**, spread across Body, Mind and Soul however the player likes, and come with their one permission, so a Title is 6 points on the Roots. Each permission still puts 1 on its Root.
- **Steps stop being counted after that.** An action picks an approach, Body, Mind or Soul, and rolls that Root. Permissions are purely permissions: what you can do, and what justifies bringing a Root in.
- **A Root's points are its dice, its health and what you spend to push.** Getting stronger makes you healthier. The three tracks (Stamina, Confidence and Resolve at d12) are gone.
- **Rolls always use the full Root.** Points spent, lost or locked never shrink the dice.
- **Bringing in a second Root needs a permission that justifies it and one clear cost** (Claude offered 1 point from the Root you bring in, or 3 Aura, and Mo agreed it needs one clear cost, then said he was happy with all of it).
- **Pushing never hurts you.** You can't spend points you don't have, so spending down to 0 just leaves you exposed. The book says so plainly.
- **Splitting has no limit.** Full control, any way you like. The cost of farming Aura with small dice is failing.
- **No extra actions.** Each player acts once a round, and every die is an intent, so a die is an action.
- **Archetypes** keep their Trait and spend Aura on their special things.
- **Attunement locks Root points** instead of touching Aura: an awakening locks a point of the Root it fits (Resolve 5 to 4 in his example) for its steps (+2 on what it covers), and each Aura spent on it adds 2 more steps.
- **A hit takes 1 point from its Root** (a sword slash at Body). You can take it on another Root for 2 points instead. Vulnerabilities and deadly monsters do 2, 3 or more hits for each intent.
- **Wounds, one per Root:** **Bloodied** (Body), **Frayed** (Mind) and **Hollow** (Soul). Damage on a Root at 0 wounds it. A wounded Root heals only to half its maximum till downtime or a story moment clears the wound.
- **Scars:** damage on a Root at 0 that's already wounded is a Scar, and you leave the scene. While wounded you can choose the **Blaze**: take the Scar, do something significant, then leave.
- **Two rests, a scene and downtime.** At a scene's end you roll each Root unsplit (Body 8 is a d12 and a d4) and recover what you roll. Downtime recovers everything.
- **A heal recovers what's rolled** (a d10 heals up to 10), spread across any Roots.
- **Toll:** healing fatigue on each patient. The first heal is full, the second loses 5, the third 10, the fourth 15 and so on. It drops a step after each scene. Some Traits and attunements ease it (a prepared healer ignores a step), and some attunements add a flat bonus to heal rolls. "There is a lot to do here."
- **Aura and recovery:** the amount healed never gives Aura, but top faces on a recovery roll still do. Recovery rolls are never split.

## Claude's suggestions he took

He said "I'm happy with all that you said" after these, so they're his:

- Damage carries past 0: a ×3 hit on Body 1 empties it, then wounds it, then Scars it. It's fair because the telegraph showed it coming face up.
- Taking a hit on another Root needs the points there. You can't take it on an empty Root.
- Toll counts only heals from someone or something else. Scene recovery never adds Toll, and Toll can't take a heal below 0.
- No heal goes past a Root's maximum, or past half while it's wounded.
- At the table, note the round the first Bloodied lands in a big fight. Round 3 or 4 of a 45-minute fight feels right. Earlier means telegraphs are too heavy, later means heals are.
