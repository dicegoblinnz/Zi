## 27 Oct: a good Aspiration and a good Goal

You asked for examples after a player wrote an Aspiration and a Goal too big to say yes to (*stop the spymaster and stop the return of the queen*, and *be the center of the world and create treaties that can't be broken*). They're in [A good Aspiration](#s-good-aspiration) and [A good Goal](#s-good-goal), and these are the calls in them:

1. **The four tests.** It's one thing (a few steps are fine, the way Wren finds the thing before she puts it down), you'd know the day it's done, it's about someone or something in the world, and the story goes on after it, so no *never* or *can't*.
2. **The size.** An Aspiration's about Vesk's size (seven sessions), and a Goal's one arc, four to six sessions. A Goal should be something everyone at the table has a reason to chase.
3. **Who decides.** When one's too big or too vague, the GM says so, and the table works it out before it goes on the sheet, usually by keeping the first piece. The rest is the next Aspiration, or where the campaign's heading.
4. **The examples** are the crew's own Aspirations, each next to a version that's too big (*be the best thief there's ever been*, *be a hero again*, *bring my order back*, and Wren's with *make sure it never happens again* on the end), and for Goals, *rule the city*, *keep the harbour safe*, and *bring peace to the Steeplands, with treaties nobody can break* next to *get the Harbour Guild and the Vane Estate to sign a truce*. Your player's own words aren't in the book.

## 26 Oct: the ones that matter most

1. **When Confidence moves.**
   - An action moves it once, however many dice it rolled: up a size if any of them won, down a size if they all missed. A roll with nothing to beat, like a ward with nothing pushing back or a quick heal, doesn't move it.
   - Each telegraph your defence stops when the round ends is a size, so Bram stopping three blows with his Defend is three sizes.
   - Answering a telegraph counts once: win and it's up a size (it's a win and a stopped telegraph, but only one size), lose with every die and it's down a size and the telegraph lands straight away.
   - In a combo, everyone who put an action in moves with the roll.
   - A hit that's two Hurts (a vulnerability) drops it two sizes. *Unyielding*, or anything else that stops the wound die moving, means no drop.
   - Fear with an intent like *Frightened* still does that when it gets through, unless you take the size off your Confidence instead, or hold your ground for 1 Resolve.
   - The Bard's song: each fear telegraph it stops for a friend puts that friend's Confidence up a size, as if their own defence had stopped it. Her own Confidence moves for her action like anyone's.
   - *Take a breath* stays (your action, up a size as far as d6), and at d2 it's how you pull yourself together, back to d4.
   - On d2, only fear, lies or pressure getting through are a Hurt (or 1 Resolve to hold your ground). A miss or a Hurt leaves it on d2, so being shaken can't spiral into more Hurts.
   - The names stay: **shaken** at d2 (you said "feared"), **fired up** at d12, and you **cash it in** (you said "surge", and *Surging* is already a condition).
2. **Vulnerabilities.** Where each extra tick goes has to make sense: the hit has to be able to reach what it takes, and nothing under a *first* tag can go till that tag's gone.
3. **Threads.**
   - Straining a hostile thread: doing right by them strains it, doing right by them again snaps it and the grudge is over, and wronging them again before it snaps makes it whole.
   - A hostile thread can't be borrowed through, and you can't strain it for Resolve.
   - With two players, one of your two bond threads goes to someone outside the crew. Playing alone, both do.
   - The crew each write two bond threads and one hostile one now. New truths: Bram to Wren *We held the same wall in the border war.*, Isolde to Bram *He has guarded me since I was twelve.*, Isolde to Dorrie *I am the only one who knows her real name.*, and Wren's hostile thread to Isolde, *She knows more about my brother than she has said.* Their Connect answers changed to match.
   - Their hostile threads: Vesk to the fence (*I owe a debt I would rather not discuss.*), Dorrie to Bram (*He thinks I sold him out on the harbour job.*), Bram to Dorrie (*I do not trust her, and she knows it.*), Isolde to Vesk (*She stole the book that started all this.*), Wren to Isolde, and Ash to Wren (*We have never agreed on anything.*).
4. **One attuned thing.** The crew kept Vesk's grappling line, Dorrie's signet pin, Bram's war hammer, Isolde's chalk and salt, Wren's longbow and Ash's ember stone. Their other attuned things (Vesk's picks, Dorrie's marked cards, Bram's coin from home, Isolde's book, Wren's hooded cloak, Ash's scorched coat) are ordinary items now, still on the same set actions, so every number's the same. Examples later in a career keep more.
5. **The crew's one action.**
   - It replaces the two party slots, and it can be any of the old party moves.
   - A player's personal slot can go into it, and two slots on one move count everything twice, as before. Going at a bigger faction needs the crew's action and a personal slot.
   - A personal slot can chase the Goal, work a project or back an Aspiration on its own, but it's never a second move on a faction.
6. **Extra resources.** What the book called a spare is an **extra resource** now: a resource that isn't paying for an asset. It's spent each downtime, not saved. It can hire another faction, pay for a new asset, go into a trade, or, for the crew, take a tie on a downtime roll.
7. **Hirelings.** A company the crew hires for good is a mini faction, so it sits out the downtime it's made like any other.
8. **The assets list.** Appendix B has about thirty assets now, grouped, each saying what it does. People who turn up are +1 Power, and the rest do one thing when the story fits. Every one of them is Claude's, so cut or change any.
9. **Friends at Court and Influential** each gave the crew a free move on a faction every downtime, which your "one action, no exception" rules out. Now *Friends at Court* is a step on any crew roll to Influence a faction's face, and *Influential* is a step on any crew roll at a faction whose threads toward the crew aren't mostly hostile. They're the only crew assets that add a step, so if you'd rather they didn't, they could be once-a-session favours instead.
10. **Power from assets.** The new list gives Power only to people who turn up, and says an asset does whatever its card says. So the Frames' and the Steeplands' faction cards keep their Power dice, though some of their Power assets aren't people (*Watchful*, *Old*, *Royal Charter*, *The Law of the Sea*, *Sprawling*, *Secretive*, *Ghost*, *Many Souls*). If you want Power only from people, those cards get redone and their Power dice drop, the Guild's d10 among them.

## 24 Oct: still waiting

Your 26 Oct answers settled how big a multiplier is, Defend and Confidence, intercepting fear, where a vulnerability's ticks go, the crew's extra party slot and what crew assets do, so those are gone from here.

1. **Multipliers.** The bestiary's numbers stayed (Redhand's *Armoured ×6 (first)* and *Feeds ×3*, the Wandering Giant's *Armoured ×6* and *Mournful ×3*, the Iron Sergeant's *Armoured ×5*, and the rest). A threat with Forms gets smaller ones, about ×3 with two forms and ×2 with three, because they come back each time. They're written for a starting crew of four: add 1 to each for every 10 the threat budget's over 40, and take 1 off for every 10 under, so playing alone *Armoured ×6* is *Armoured ×3*.
2. **Hiring another faction.** The hired faction spends its own action that downtime where the payer says, rolled with its own dice, and the payer keeps its own action. The money buys the action, not the win.
   - Hiring comes first: the crew says who it's paying before the GM telegraphs, and a hired faction's telegraph is the job it was paid for.
   - A faction that's hostile to the payer won't take its money or sell to it. One that does business or is friendly will.
   - A faction paid to come for the crew rolls on the table for the payer's threads toward the crew.
   - The "don't know what it does?" 6 spends an extra resource to hire someone and rolls again for the faction's own action.
3. **Defend.**
   - Every line on your Defend counts twice, and you can split it once.
   - A defence past 6 steps is several dice, and each one stops one telegraph.
   - With nothing on your Defend it's a d2 either way, so Defending doesn't help.
   - A condition's step on your Defend, like *Warded*, counts once, and Defending doesn't double it.
   - *Poisoned*'s spend is "your Defend's a d2 until the round ends".
4. **Intercept.**
   - 1 Aura for each friend you step in for, and you can take as many of their dice as you like, from as many enemies.
   - The GM's people intercept for free, like a bodyguard, and she says so before you roll.
   - A summon intercepts for free, for one person in its stride, and one hit takes it out. Not said yet: whether one summon can cover several friends.
5. **Vulnerabilities.** One only adds to a win that's doing harm, a Hurt or stripping a keeps-it-going tag, and not to a win that places a condition or moves something. On the crew, one vulnerability makes a hit two Hurts, so be sparing with them.
6. **Wounds.**
   - The rest roll's an ordinary roll: a tie goes to the wound, and 1 Resolve rolls it again.
   - *Unyielding* and the Hurt Boon: the first Hurt each scene doesn't move your wound die. Playing alone gives you the same, so with *Unyielding* it's the first two. A crew of your own doesn't get it.
   - The Hurt Burden: you Blaze a size sooner, on a Hurt at d10.
   - *Tend the Crew* is once per scene: when the scene ends, everyone in the crew who was in it rolls two Confidence dice for their rest, and the best counts.
   - Heals, quick heals, *Field Dressing*, *Healer's Hands*, the medical kit and the Bloodsworn's Aura all step the wound die down a size, and nothing heals anyone below their Scars.
   - The Healer's *Pull them back*: someone who Blazes stays in the scene, still takes the Scar, and their wound die goes to d10 (or their Scars). Their extraordinary moment and the choice to die still happen.
7. **Factions.**
   - A big asset takes as many wins as its multiplier to destroy or to take, and the taker gets it whole.
   - A hostile thread only adds a tick to a Destroy or an Acquire.
   - With its one action, the crew can go at one asset or resource out on its own, guarded by 1 step plus whatever Power assets are really there.
   - Writing an agenda, Establishing with nobody in the way, and coming for the crew don't roll. A faction coming for the crew rolls its d12 on the table when it's telegraphed, and never against the crew.
   - "A die at its agenda" is the crew's *Set back its agenda* move: beat the agenda's die and it steps down one, and the faction's action still lands.
   - *Work for it* pays a resource for a big job, and the GM says so when she offers it.
   - Influence on a faction's face is always open to one slot, and trading costs no slot.
   - No threads toward the crew, or as many hostile as bond, means it does business. A faction the crew's already crossed starts with one hostile thread.
   - New agendas start at the table's die, without the old extra step.
   - In a war, each side splits its one action: one die at its war agenda, one Destroying an enemy asset.
   - The "don't know what it does?" d6: 1–2 works its agenda, 3 Establishes (or writes a new agenda), 4 Destroys or Undermines its worst rival, 5 comes for the crew, 6 spends an extra resource to hire someone (or Acquires).
8. **The crew's card.**
   - A new crew starts with one resource, *Odd jobs*, paying for one asset, *Four or five of us and a back room* (+1 Power), so it's Power d4 with nothing extra.
   - *Fences*, *Friends at Court*, *Influential*, *Rooted* and *A Ship* were reworded to do one thing each.
   - The crew's Establish can't make a brand-new resource. It builds an asset with an extra resource, makes a mini faction, or builds back what the crew lost. New resources come from a Goal, a big job, a project, or off a faction.
   - The crew can make mini factions too.
   - Holdings don't need a resource paying for them. *A Ship* is an asset, so while nothing's paying for it, it stays tied up in harbour.
   - Crew assets do what they say on the crew's card, even when a faction has an asset of the same name (*Rooted*, *Influential*).
   - *Healers* is once a session: between two scenes, everyone in the crew steps their wound die down a size. It used to heal a Catch each in downtime.
   - A faction's threads toward the crew aren't in its bonds: a bond thread's a step for the crew on a roll it fits, and a hostile one adds no ticks.
   - The crew doesn't have the three faction bonds: its people are the characters, and its Reputations do the job of Public Sentiment.
   - Party resources are called **party holdings**, so they don't clash with a faction's resources.
9. **Archetypes.**
   - The Bard's song can split as often as it likes. What it misses still has to get past each friend's own Confidence, the Bard's included.
   - The Oracle's swapped numbers don't give Aura and don't explode, and Isolde's foresight comes off The dead.
   - The Guardian's +2 goes on any Defend or Confidence die, so it works when he lets it ride too.
   - Resolve spends go with their mechanic, so when the mechanic takes your action, the spend does too.
   - The Warrior's *Opened*: the hit that opens the target ticks once more as well.
   - *Masterwork* covers the tags you make with one making roll. The Bloodsworn's Aura never heals below your Scars.
   - Not said yet: whether *Vanish*, *Become it*, *Call the horde* and *Swap* take your action, and who picks where the Hunter's Mark jumps.
