# The website brief

Mo's answers from 16 Oct 2026, given before the redesign. Everything built for zidara.world follows this. When Mo changes his mind, change it here.

## The look

- A modern game screen: dark by default with a light switch, crisp type, cards, glowing highlights, dice as icons.
- Signature colours: arcane violet and radiant gold.
- What was wrong with the first look: it read like a document, the colours were wrong, and it was too plain. It worked fine on phones.
- Mo likes the feel of D&D Beyond (the sheet front and centre), World Anvil (rich lore), Demiplane (dark, glossy cards with big art) and Alchemy (cinematic and atmospheric).
- It has to look good with no art, with spaces that get better as art arrives.
- Fully separate from Dice Goblin: no shop branding and no credit. The home page gets its own intro about the game. The Welcome chapter keeps Mo's words, Dice Goblin line included.
- The home page depends on who's there. Newcomers get what Zi'Dara is and why it's great. Signed-in players go straight to their characters and games.
- Silent unless someone turns sound on. A telegraph landing buzzes the phone.

## The look Mo chose: Sigil

- Picked on 16 Oct from three looks (Obsidian, Astral and Sigil) drawn on a design canvas.
- Type: Cinzel for headings and Alegreya Sans for everything else. The site serves both itself, so they work offline.
- Colours: plum-black (#120d18) under a faint gold diamond lattice, gold (#d9b45a) for buttons, corner brackets and highlights, and violet (#b596f5) for links and the mark. Light mode is a pale lilac page with darker gold and violet.
- Shapes: gold corner brackets on cards, hexagon dice, a gold button with clipped ends, and a diamond mark (a violet outline round a gold core).
- It starts dark. The switch in the top bar flips to light, and the browser remembers the choice.

## Who and where

- For Mo's playtest groups, GMs running it at home, brand-new players and groups playing online.
- Players use the website in their phone's browser. No app store app.
- Also used on the GM's laptop and tablet, and on a TV or a screen shared on a video call.
- Sheets and rules keep working offline. Live play needs internet.
- Sign-in with Google, Discord, Apple or an emailed link. Invite-only at first.

## The character companion

- Four ways in: a guided builder with info bubbles, start from a crew member, a blank sheet, or random in one tap.
- Its number one job is building a roll fast: tap an action and see its steps and dice ready, with the lines that need Aura marked. Playtests found rolls take 1 to 3 minutes while players hunt for permissions.
- Dice: roll in the app, with a proper animation, or roll real dice and tap in the result. The player chooses.
- A sheet is seen by the player and their GM, nobody else.
- What was wrong with the old companion apps: rolls took too long, they were hard to use at the table, they fell behind the rules, and sheets got lost or stuck.
- When a rule changes, a saved character's numbers recount themselves, and anything the player has to choose gets flagged.
- 17 Oct: the first version lets players pick one of the six crew (no blank sheets yet), saves on the phone and works offline. Sign-in comes after it, to keep the first version quick and light on usage. The plan is in `notes/companion-plan.md`.

## The GM companion and live play

- Prep: build threats (Challenge, tags, tough tags, the budget), plan scenes (objectives, stakes, the twist), keep campaign notes and NPCs that players see once revealed, and use generators (names, places, the Oracle).
- Live: set the round's telegraphs and send them to phones, see the party's Aura, Resolve, Confidence and Hurt as they change, track the round's step budget and each threat's tags, and push a picture, a map or the telegraphs to the table screen.
- A telegraph shows on the target player's phone and they answer it there. The threat's die and their answer roll together, in the app or with real dice, and the GM sees the result straight away.

## Worlds

- Everything: linked wiki pages for places, people and factions, maps with pins, timelines and family trees.

## Order

- The new look first, then the character companion. Each piece goes to Mo's playtests as soon as it's ready, and what the groups say shapes the next.

## Already decided, 13 Oct

- The rules are open to everyone. Accounts are invite-only at first.
- The site is the one master copy of the rules, and the book, references and character builder are built from it.
- Worlds hold official content and GM-built worlds.
- Rule changes come four ways: Mo tells Claude, Mo edits in the browser, playtesters leave notes, and trusted co-writers suggest edits for Mo to approve.
- Live telegraphs run on every phone and, when wanted, on a shared table screen.

## The app rework, 17 Oct

Mo's answers in the drill from `notes/app-rework.md`, round by round.

- At playtests so far, players used a mix of phones and paper.
- The slow part of a round is working out a roll.
- Players asked for the phone to roll and say what happened, rules on tap, making their own character, and seeing the party.
- Amazing at the table means all four: it looks like a game, there's no counting, new players learn from it, and the crew plays together live.
- During a round the phone opens on a play screen: big set actions and the trackers, usable one-handed, with the rest of the sheet and the rules a tab away.
- The phone rolls the threat's die with yours, or you tap in the GM's number, and it calls beat, tie or under.
- It counts everything as you play (Aura, Resolve, Hurt, Armour and conditions), with an undo.
- Each die's intent is an optional label: tap hurt, pin or disarm, or type your own.
- Real dice: tap in each die's number, and the phone calls the result and spots the top faces.
- The look: Sigil hex dice that spin and land with weight, and plenty of motion: cards flip, pages slide, Aura pips glow as they fill.
- With sound on, these get a sound or a buzz: dice landing, a top face (a chime and a buzz as Aura comes back), and Hurt or a Catch filling a bar.
- Defend: the app matches the rolls so the most dice get stopped, and the player can tap to swap two (call 5 in `notes/companion-plan.md`, confirmed with the swap added).
- Making your own: the guided builder comes first (info bubbles, teaching as you build), then a blank sheet, from the crew and random.
- The builder follows the Quick Build by default, and any step can open into the full build.
- Permissions: pick from the Title idea's permissions or write your own, with the quick test beside it.
- The builder warns but never blocks: it flags a missing permission or steps that don't add up, and saves anyway.
- Downtime on the phone: pick your slots, and what they bring lands on the sheet, with an undo.
- The party sheet and the Party Goal wait for sign-in, so everyone shares one copy and there's no throwaway version first.
- When a rule changes, each changed line on a saved character gets a gold mark. Tap it to see old and new. Typo fixes stay quiet.
- Calls 1 to 4 and 6 to 8 in `notes/companion-plan.md` are confirmed.
