# The worlds plan

The next piece of the website after telegraphs on phones: worlds and maps, with the faction board. It comes from the brief (`notes/website-brief.md`, under Worlds, and the 13 Oct call that worlds hold official content and GM-built worlds) and Mo's answers on 27 Oct 2026. Mo said go on 27 Oct.

## Decisions

- **A world's in a campaign.** Each campaign has its own, and everything kept in it (the board, pages, maps, and what the players have been shown) stays in that campaign, checked on the server. Nothing crosses from one campaign to another, so a GM running four campaigns keeps four worlds.
- **Official content next to her own** (Mo, 27 Oct). A campaign's world starts empty, or she brings in a frame or the Steeplands in a tap. What she brings in is hers to change from then on. When the book changes something she brought in, she gets a note saying what changed, and she picks: take the book's, or keep hers.
- **What players see, and when** (Mo, 27 Oct). Everything starts hidden. A tap reveals a faction, a place or a person, and its Secret, its agenda and its Power each take their own tap.
- **Maps and pins** (Mo, 27 Oct: all three). Her own pictures as maps, photos of hand-drawn ones too, several to a world, with pins that open pages, and the crew sees a map and the pins she's revealed. Players drop their own pins and notes as well, and the whole crew sees them. A map can start covered, and she uncovers it as the crew explores.
- **Where the board lives** (Mo, 27 Oct). A World screen off each campaign's page: the board first, with the crew's own card from the party sheet at the top, then places, people and maps as they come. Players get a World tab beside Party.
- **Order.** The board first, since it's the bookkeeping that eats table time and the book already says exactly how it runs. Then the faction turn, places and people, maps, and timelines and family trees.
- **Like the rest of the site.** One-handed on a phone at the table, in both themes, from 320 wide up, and it works offline for anything the phone's already seen.

## Calls Claude made

For Mo to say otherwise.

- Showing a faction shows its name, what it is, its Want and its Face, the things anyone in town could tell you. Every other part of its card takes its own tap: Power (with the assets that add it), its resources, its other assets, its agendas, its bonds, its threads and its Secret. Showing the Secret takes a second tap, so it can't go out by accident. A thread toward a faction the crew hasn't been shown stays hidden with it.
- What the players haven't been shown never leaves the server. A player's phone only ever gets what's been revealed, so there's nothing hidden on it to dig out.
- Only the GM changes the board. The crew's own card is the party sheet, which everyone in the campaign changes, the way it does now.
- Power's counted, never typed: 1 step, plus every asset that adds Power and has something paying for it, made into dice off the steps-into-dice table, the same as the crew's. The build checks every Power the book prints for its factions adds up, and stops if one doesn't.
- A faction with more assets than resources says so, and she taps the asset that goes unpaid, since the faction picks. That's the book's Vane example: burn the vineyards, take the tolls, and *Hired Swords* goes unpaid, so the Estate's a d10.
- A multiplier keeps its ticks. Each win ticks one off, and the last one takes it off the card. A resource or an asset can go to another faction in a tap, the way Acquire moves them.
- Agendas climb like the Goal, two at most. Past d12 it's happened: the card says so, with the book's three steps for when an agenda happens, and she writes the next one.
- A faction's threads go strained and snapped the way a character's do. A strained one stops counting till it's mended, straining it again snaps it, and a snapped one stays on the card crossed out till she takes it off.
- How a faction sees the crew (friendly, doing business or hostile) is worked out from its whole threads toward them, as the book says, and shows on its card.
- Mini factions sit under the faction that made them, three at most, each with its two outcomes and its Loyalty thread toward its maker. Making one hands over one of the maker's resources and one of its assets, so the maker's card changes too. The crew's own mini factions come with the faction turn.
- The note when the book changes a card she brought in ignores typo fixes, the same as the gold marks. Taking the book's changes only touches what the book changed, so her ticks, threads and agendas' dice stay where play's put them.
- The Steeplands' Vane Estate comes in with its whole card from Factions, since that's where the book keeps it.
- A faction can have a line saying what it is (*A merchant guild that works through money*), from Building a Faction's first tables, and a note only the GM sees. Where a book card says something the board can't hold, like *None yet. It hasn't noticed the crew*, it goes in that note.

- From the independent check (27 Oct): changes go up in batches the Worker takes, and after one it can't take, one at a time, so only that one's lost. Changes left waiting on a phone go up as soon as any page of the companion opens. A phone still on an older build never offers its older book as news. An agenda's shown with what happens when it does, since that's how the book writes it.

### The faction turn (stage 2)

Mo's answers (27 Oct): phones roll and real dice are welcome (her phone rolls the factions' dice, the crew's phone rolls the crew's and whatever it's going at, and anyone can tap in real dice instead), the crew sees it live as she goes (her telegraphs the moment she sends them, then each roll and what it changed as it lands, and off-screen moves only as what changed), and telegraphing an action by or at a faction the crew hasn't been shown shows the crew its card, and nothing else on it.

- The turn's kept on the campaign's live table, beside the rounds, so it reaches every phone in seconds and a phone keeps the last of it for no signal, the same way a round does.
- Only the GM changes the factions' side and the board. Anyone in the crew can pay a faction, pick the crew's action and roll it, and each player puts in their own personal slot. One slot doubles everything, so a second one's turned away with why.
- She says what the crew's up against, unless it's a telegraphed action's dice or a die on the party sheet, since a die can give away a Power the crew hasn't been shown. For the same reason a player never gets what a faction's action is up against till it's rolled, who paid a faction unless it was the crew, or an action that moves off-screen.
- A faction's Power, once rolled, stands for everything that goes at it that downtime, and an action's dice are rolled once, for an Obstruct against it and its landing alike. An action someone's trying to stop can't land till that's rolled.
- Each outcome's change is a tap on her phone, with Undo: the board through the World screen and the party sheet through the Party one. When the crew takes something it comes off the faction's card and onto the party sheet, and when a faction takes the last tick of something with a multiplier, it's theirs with the multiplier whole.
- Every landing against a faction offers "they know who did it", a hostile thread from that faction toward whoever did it, since that's what the second die in Two things in one job decides.
- When an agenda happens, her sentence goes on the party sheet under What has changed (Appendix A's downtime page has one, so the party sheet has it now too), it's the line everyone gets unless she rewrites it, and its next agenda takes the place of the one that happened.
- The crew's own mini faction is made from a resource and an asset off the party sheet, and the crew sees all of it.
- A player spends 1 Resolve off their own character to roll the crew's dice again. An extra resource takes a tie, and paying a faction and buying a tie each spend one of the crew's extras for that downtime.
- The d6 for when she doesn't know what a faction does, the agendas table, and both d12 tables for when one comes for the crew are read off the chapter. The d12's table goes by the faction's threads toward the crew, or the payer's when it's been paid to.
- What changed stays on every phone as the last downtime until the next one ends.
- From the independent check (27 Oct): when the crew pays a faction another faction's paid already, the crew isn't told, and she sees both payers on that faction. An Obstruct's target keeps its dice and stays in sight while anything's trying to stop it, two that would only stop each other are turned away, and a player never gets an Obstruct's target when it's hidden. The Goal's and a project's die are the only ones a player says, and she says whether anyone's in the way of the crew's Establish. An action whose Obstruct was stopped can still land, and one Obstruct that stopped an action stands whatever another one rolls. Resolve only rolls again what lost, it's only spent on a roll the table will take, a tie bought for one roll doesn't carry over to the next, and nothing rolls again once what it was stopping has landed. An action that moved off-screen starts with no line, so it's only news if she writes it.
- Still to do: the downtime card's "a slot toward the crew" doesn't fill itself in from the faction turn, so a player still marks it on their own downtime.

### Places and people (stage 3)

Mo's answers (27 Oct): names link themselves (any page's or faction's name written on another page is a link, with nothing to tap, and a page can have other names the crew might call it, like Orla for Orla Strand). Showing the crew a person shows their name, who they are and what you notice first, and what they want, their Challenge and tags, and their Secret each take their own tap, with the hidden tag, telegraphs and her notes staying hers. Showing a place shows its name, what it is, its Challenge and what it's like inside, and who's here, the ground, the stakes and the scene clock each take their own tap, with telegraphs, hooks and Elsewhere staying hers and the Secret on its own tap.

- Places and people are pages kept in the world beside the board, in the same document, so they're checked on the server, numbered per phone and kept offline the same way, and only the GM changes them.
- A person's Secret is their hidden tag, the way a faction's is (Hide one, in Building a Threat), so the hidden tag goes to the crew with the Secret, on a second tap, the same as a faction's Secret.
- A place brought in from Twenty Places brings every named person in its Who's here as a page of their own, with their Challenge and tags. A group (four bath-men) stays a line in Who's here, and someone named with their group (*Mags Tolley* and four smugglers) is a page of their own, with four smugglers as who they're with. Someone the place says is the Bestiary's (Mister Quill, the Debt Collector) comes with that threat's tags, telegraphs, voice and hidden tag. Someone already in the world by that name isn't brought in twice.
- The Bestiary's threats can be brought in too, each as a person written as a threat, since that's the quickest way to one, and the Steeplands' Harbour Guild's Face is the Bestiary's Guildmaster.
- A faction's Face offers a page for its person in a tap: the Bestiary's, if it has one by that name, or a new page named from the Face, with the rest of the Face as who they are.
- A name that starts with The also links without it when what's left is two words or more (Harbour Guild), and The, A or An can start lowercase in a sentence. Each name links the first time it's in a part and never to the page it's on. A link to a page the crew hasn't been shown stays plain words on a player's phone, since their phone doesn't know the page is there. Every page and faction lists where else it comes up.
- A person's voice is hers, like their telegraphs, since it's her line to say as them.
- A place's complication, off Building Your World's table, is a part of its own with its own tap. Hooks can go on any page, rolled off the d20 table, and are hers.
- A telegraph on a page that's aimed at someone's Defend or Confidence has Telegraph it, which opens the live table's card with its die, what it's doing and whose it is filled in, for her to aim and send. A place's telegraph that starts with someone in it is theirs ("Orla raps her cane" is Orla Strand's).
- A place she's written goes onto the live table's scene from its page, in place of the scene that's there (a second tap if there's anything there): each piece of ground written as a tag with what it does, each stake with its Challenge, what's in the way and what it's for, and the scene clock, the way Twenty Places writes them, so her changes go on the table and not the book's. It doesn't show the page's parts to the crew on their World tab, since that's still her tap.
- What each of a person's tags does, off Appendix B, sits under their tags.
- The book's places, people and tables are in their own file (`public/world-book.json`) that the World screen fetches when it needs it and the service worker keeps, since a player's phone doesn't need them on every page.
- A world holds 240 pages, and the whole of Twenty Places and the Bestiary fit with room to spare.
- From the independent check (27 Oct): the build stops on a named person in a Who's here it can't read, a change that isn't words and numbers where they belong is turned away, the Worker skips a change it can't read so it can't hold up the ones behind it, and names link without a lookbehind, so the links (and a faction's page) work on older iPhones. Hiding a page and showing it again brings back the parts the crew had been shown, the same as a faction.

### Maps and her pins (stage 4)

Mo's answers (27 Oct): a map's picture goes up at 4096 pixels on its longest side, and a pin she's shown the crew shows them its name (the page's, or a name she gives it for the crew, like smoke on the ridge), and opens its page once she's shown them that too.

- A map's picture is kept in the R2 bucket `zi-maps`, under its campaign, and the map itself (its name, its size, its pins and what the crew's been shown) is kept in the world beside the board and the pages, so it's checked on the server, numbered per phone and kept offline the same way. Only the GM changes it.
- Her phone shrinks the picture before it goes up, makes a small copy for lists, and turns it a quarter at a time, since a photo of a paper map shot flat on a table often comes out sideways. Putting one up needs a signal. Everything else works without one and goes up when there's a signal.
- The Worker only hands a map's picture to that campaign's GM, and to a player once she's shown the crew that map, and only by the picture's own key, so a phone that's behind gets nothing rather than the wrong picture. Hiding a map again stops its picture going out, though a phone that's seen it has seen it.
- A world holds 20 maps, a map 60 pins, and a picture's up to 8 MB (one at 4096 is about 1 to 3).
- She drops a pin by moving the map till the spot's under a cross in the middle, which is easier to get right than a thumb that covers what it's pointing at, then picks what it opens: a place, a person or a faction, or just a name. A pin that's only a name becomes a place of her own in a tap. A pin's moved the same way.
- A pin can open a faction as well as a page, since a faction's hall or its ground belongs on a map too.
- A map can be a map of one of her places, so that place's page shows its map. Every page and faction lists the maps it's pinned on, and that opens the map at its pin.
- The crew's phones keep the pictures of the maps they've been shown as soon as the world comes in, not only once they've opened them, so a map opens at the table with no signal. A picture they're no longer shown comes off their phones the next time the world comes in, and signing out takes them all.
- A picture that comes off (its map taken down, or a new picture put on it) stays a day so Undo can put it back, then goes from the bucket. Ending a campaign takes all its pictures.
- Players are told when she shows them a map, or a new pin on one.

## The stages

Every stage passes `tests/check-site.mjs`, `tests/check-companion.mjs` and `tests/check-accounts.mjs`, keeps each campaign's world to itself on the server, works offline for whatever the phone's seen, in both themes, from 320 wide up, and ends with one independent check.

1. **The board.** Built 27 Oct on the `website` branch. The World screen off a campaign's page, and the World tab. The crew's card from the party sheet at the top, then every faction as a card in the kit's look, a glance when it's shut: Power, its agenda's die and how it sees the crew. She brings in a frame's board or the Steeplands in a tap, or adds factions herself, rolled or picked off Building a Faction's tables, with Appendix B's assets to pick from. Power counted from the assets paying for it, resources and assets with their multipliers ticked, unpaid assets, up to two agendas climbing, bonds and threads strained, mended and snapped, the Secret, and up to three mini factions each. Showing a faction and each part of it to the crew is a tap, and players see only that. The note when the book changes a card she brought in. Builds on stage 7 of the app plan (campaigns and the party sheet). Checked by Brinewake brought in and its four cards matching the chapter, the Steeplands with the Vane Estate's whole card, the Vane example (vineyards burned, tolls taken, *Hired Swords* unpaid, a d10), a ×2 asset taking two ticks, an agenda climbing past d12, a mini faction made, a change made with no signal going up once, a player seeing nothing till it's shown and never getting the Secret till then, a player's change turned away, a made-up book change taken, nothing reaching another campaign, and the World screen and tab at 390 in both themes with axe (`tests/check-accounts.mjs` part 7).
2. **The faction turn.** Built 27 Oct on the `website` branch. Each downtime from the board, the way the book runs it: hiring first, then every faction's action telegraphed to the crew's phones (an outcome or work on its agenda, at what, with its dice and a split), the crew's action, Obstructs settled first, then the actions roll and land, and each outcome changes the cards in a tap: Destroy ticks, Undermine strains or writes, Influence writes or mends, Acquire moves, Establish adds. An agenda that happens goes on the party sheet, and the crew can make mini factions of its own. Builds on stage 1 and telegraphs on phones (stage 8 of the app plan). Checked by the book's Two things in one job (the Guild's d6 and d4 against the Estate's d12 rolling a 4) and a downtime played on three phones (`tests/check-accounts.mjs` part 8): a hire, six actions with one off-screen and one coming for the crew on a real 7, the crew's Obstruct with a slot put in with no signal, the Estate's Power rolled once for two actions, an agenda happening onto the party sheet, the crew's own mini faction in a second downtime, a player's change to the factions turned away, nothing crossing campaigns, and the screens at 390 in both themes.
3. **Places and people.** Built 27 Oct on the `website` branch. Linked pages for places, people and factions, written by the GM with links between them, a part of each kept for her alone, and each one shown to the crew in a tap. Twenty Places and the places, people and hooks from Building Your World to bring in, the Bestiary's threats, and a person written as a threat. Builds on stage 1. Checked by the Copper Baths brought in with its three people matching the chapter, Orla's name linking to her page, the Vane Estate's Face getting a page and the Harbour Guild's coming from the Bestiary, a person written as a threat, the Copper Baths shown with its GM part kept back and its Secret on a second tap, a link that waits till its page is shown, a player's change turned away, a telegraph off a page opening the round's card, the Copper Baths onto the live table's scene from its page, the World tab with no signal, a made-up book change and a typo fix, nothing reaching another campaign, and the screens at 390 in both themes (`tests/check-accounts.mjs` part 9).
4. **Maps and her pins.** Built 27 Oct on the `website` branch. Her own pictures as maps, several to a world, pinched and dragged in one hand, with pins she drops that open pages. Players see a map and the pins she's shown, and their phones keep it for offline once they've seen it. The pictures are in the R2 bucket `zi-maps` in Mo's Zi Cloudflare account, bound to the Worker as `MAPS`. Builds on stage 3. Checked by the Steeplands put up from a sideways 6000 by 4500 phone photo, turned and shrunk to 4096 with its small copy in the bucket under the campaign, a player getting neither till it's shown, a pin on the Copper Baths opening its page and back, one that's only a name kept off players' phones, Lord Vane's pin by name only, a double tap, a double tap held and slid, a pinch and a drag, the Copper Baths' page opening the map at its pin, a map seen once opening with no signal, a map taken down and put back by Undo with its picture and gone from the bucket a day later, nobody but the GM putting a picture up, nothing reaching another campaign, and the screens at 390 in both themes (`tests/check-accounts.mjs` part 9b).
5. **Fog she lifts.** A map can start covered, and she uncovers it with a finger as the crew explores, on every player's phone. Builds on stage 4. Checked by a covered map, a part uncovered reaching a player's phone, and what's still covered never reaching it.
6. **The crew's pins and notes.** Players drop their own pins and notes on a map, and the whole crew sees them. Builds on stage 4. Checked by a pin dropped on one phone reaching the others, the GM taking one off, and nothing reaching another campaign.
7. **Timelines and family trees.** From the brief. Builds on stage 3.

The table screen, on the same Durable Object as the live table, was built 27 Oct on the `website` branch, then stage 4 (maps and her pins), so stage 5 (fog she lifts) is next.
